| junction_profile | `junction_profile.caption.txt`, `junction_profile.csv`, `junction_profile.pdf`, `junction_profile.png` | The vertebral column as the tables describe it. A, the column in anteroposterior projection, drawn to Panjabi's published means: each body a box of its end-plate width and posterior height, the canal width hatched over it, the disc spaces at one height throughout (5 mm) since the tables give none; the scale is millimetres from the C3 upper end-plate. B onward, each measure down the column, the mean and one population standard deviation (the printed standard error times the square root of twelve), the two junctions as dotted lines. The cervicothoracic step is large in every measure; the thoracolumbar step is small beside the spread of one level. C2 has no upper end-plate row, so the profile starts at C3. Panjabi MM et al., Spine 1991;16:861 and 888; Spine 1992;17:299. No patient data. |
| junction_posterior | `junction_posterior.caption.txt`, `junction_posterior.csv`, `junction_posterior.pdf`, `junction_posterior.png`, `junction_posterior_window.csv` | How the template decides. One synthetic typical column drawn from the tables (seed 11), a window of eight bodies around each junction: A, the cervicothoracic; B, the thoracolumbar. Above, the window as a reader sees it, each body a box of its measured end-plate width, named by the count. Below, the template's posterior probability over every cut the window allows, each naming the column could have scored against the profile with one free offset per measure and averaged under a flat prior over the cut; the true junction's bar is filled, the arrow marks the call. The inset gives the log-likelihood gap, per measure, between the best naming and the runner-up: at C7/T1 the canal width carries the call by itself; at T12/L1 no measure decides strongly. |

| junction_blend | `junction_blend.caption.txt`, `junction_blend.csv`, `junction_blend.pdf`, `junction_blend.png` | The thoracolumbar transitional body, two truths one level apart. Profile template, 250 synthetic windows per point, measures end-plate width, posterior body height, canal width. Bars are 95% Wilson intervals. The shape cannot choose between the two conventions; only the count from C2 settles it, which is the case a full-spine corpus meets. |
