CORRECTED: Do not report the (1 − 1/e) guarantee for a greedy Bayes-error or class-separability objective — Krause and Guestrin (JAIR 35:557–591, 2009), Proposition 9 (stated p. 584, proved p. 588) does prove that "decision-theoretic value of information is not submodular, even in Naive Bayes models", and their Theorem 3 goes further, making mere evaluation of that objective #P-complete in Naive Bayes models and "hard to approximate to any factor unless P = NP" — but the two proposed escapes are both weaker than advertised: Das and Kempe's (1 − e^(−γ)) bound (JMLR 19, paper 16-534, 2018, Theorem 6) holds only for a non-negative *monotone* objective, γ is a minimum over exponentially many set pairs that is only ever lower-bounded (in print only for R² via the smallest 2k-sparse eigenvalue, never for Bayes error), and γ is exactly 0 for a plain Naive Bayes Bayes-error instance with a skewed prior — P(Y=1)=0.98 with two conditionally independent 90%-accurate features gives V(∅)=V({X₁})=V({X₂})=0.98 but V({X₁,X₂})=0.9864, so 1 − e^(−γ) = 0 and the bound is vacuous rather than merely weak — while log-determinant/D-optimality carries (1 − 1/e) only in its normalized Bayesian *gain* form log det(P₀ + Σ_{i∈S} aᵢaᵢᵀ/σ²) − log det P₀ with a positive-definite prior precision and independent homoscedastic noise, since the raw log det Σ_S is submodular but not monotone (marginal gain = log var(Xᵢ|X_S), so the guarantee switches on and off with the measurement units while submodularity never changes), non-Bayesian log det(Σ_{i∈S} aᵢaᵢᵀ) is −∞ for |S| < d, and Krause, Singh and Guestrin (JMLR 9:235–284, 2008), Remark 14 states outright that "the analysis of Nemhauser et al. (1978) applies to neither of Bayesian A-, D-, and E-optimality in general"; separately, maximizing the minimum pairwise Mahalanobis distance is definitively non-submodular (not unknown) for two independent reasons that have nothing to do with Proposition 9 — squared Mahalanobis distance for a single fixed class pair is monotone but non-submodular through the classic suppressor-variable effect (δ=(1,0), Σ=[[1,ρ],[ρ,1]]: gain(2|1)=4.26 vs gain(2|∅)=0 at ρ=0.9), and the minimum over pairs destroys submodularity by itself, even for modular components — with measured γ of only 0.017–0.086 on random instances, and the general robust max-min problem admitting no polynomial-time multiplicative guarantee at all (Krause, McMahan, Guestrin and Gupta, JMLR 9:2761–2801, 2008, Theorem 3), only SATURATE's bicriteria guarantee.

CORRECTION: Three defects, one of them the same kind of overclaim the claim itself is warning against.

(1) The attribution is right but the inference step is missing. Proposition 9 is real and verbatim, but its proof uses a three-action utility with asymmetric costs (-3 for a misassignment) and an abstain action. A proposition that a *family* is non-submodular is established by one member's counterexample and does not transfer to a different member. I checked: at Krause and Guestrin's own parameters (prior 0.5, two 75%-accurate features), plain 0/1-loss accuracy IS submodular. So citing Proposition 9 alone leaves open the obvious objection "my objective is plain accuracy, not an asymmetric-cost utility with a reject option." The objection fails, but only because of a separate counterexample (prior 0.98, two 90%-accurate conditionally independent features: V(empty)=V({X1})=V({X2})=0.98 < V({X1,X2})=0.9864), which the report should carry instead of leaning on Proposition 9's. Also, the claim lumps "Bayes-error or class-separability" into one family: Bayes error is a decision-theoretic VOI, but a separability criterion like minimum pairwise Mahalanobis distance has no utility or action structure and is not in that family at all -- it is non-submodular for a different reason (suppressor variables plus the min operator), and attributing it to Proposition 9 is a misattribution. Finally, the claim understates the paper: Theorem 3 of the same paper makes *evaluating* the objective #P-complete in Naive Bayes models and hard to approximate to any factor unless P = NP, which is a stronger obstruction than non-submodularity.

(2) "Das and Kempe (1 - e^(-gamma)) with gamma shown" is honest but, for these objectives, reports essentially nothing -- and the claim should say so rather than presenting it as a working alternative. Theorem 6 requires a non-negative *monotone* objective (Definition 2 defines gamma only for monotone f, and the authors say the non-monotone extension is unlikely to carry over). Bayes accuracy and min-pairwise Mahalanobis both happen to be monotone as population quantities, so the theorem formally applies -- but gamma is a minimum over exponentially many set pairs (about 7.5e19 evaluations at n=50, k=5), so it can only be bounded, and the published bounds (smallest eigenvalue, smallest 2k-sparse eigenvalue, RIP) are for the R^2 linear-regression objective, with Elenberg et al. (2018) extending them to restricted-strongly-convex objectives -- never to Bayes error. Worse, I measured gamma = exactly 0 for the Bayes-error instance above, so 1 - e^(-gamma) = 0: the guarantee is vacuous, not merely weak. This is precisely the mechanism Krause and Guestrin name ("we need to make several observations in order to convince ourselves that we need to change our action"). For min-pairwise Mahalanobis I measured gamma = 0.017-0.086, i.e. guarantees of 2-9% of optimum, falling to 0.002 as two features approach collinearity.

(3) "log-determinant / D-optimality IS genuinely submodular and does carry (1 - 1/e)" is itself an overclaimed guarantee and must be qualified. log det of a principal submatrix is submodular -- confirmed from primary sources and by exhaustive enumeration -- but submodularity alone does not give (1 - 1/e): Nemhauser, Wolsey and Fisher need normalized, non-negative AND monotone. Raw log det Sigma_S is submodular and NOT monotone (marginal gain = log var(X_i | X_S), negative whenever a conditional variance falls below 1; I found 56-80 violations per random instance). It gets worse: whether the objective is monotone depends on the measurement units. Rescaling the data by c leaves submodularity untouched at every c but flips monotonicity on and off (non-monotone at c = 0.3, 1, 2; monotone at c = 5, 20). A guarantee that appears or disappears depending on whether you measure in millimetres or centimetres is exactly a "wrong but plausible" number. And Krause, Singh and Guestrin (JMLR 9, 2008) Remark 14 says flatly that Nemhauser applies to none of Bayesian A-, D- or E-optimality in general: under dependent or heteroscedastic noise D-optimality equals information gain and is not even submodular. The form that genuinely carries (1 - 1/e) is narrower than "log-determinant": the normalized Bayesian gain log det(P0 + sum_{i in S} a_i a_i^T / sigma^2) - log det P0, with a positive-definite prior precision and independent homoscedastic noise. I verified that form is normalized, non-negative, monotone and submodular with gamma = 1. Non-Bayesian D-optimality, log det(sum_{i in S} a_i a_i^T), is -infinity for |S| < d and does not qualify at all. The report should state the gain form explicitly, with its two conditions, rather than saying "log-determinant / D-optimality".

On the subsidiary points the brief asked about: A-optimality genuinely is not submodular (explicit counterexample in Krause, Singh and Guestrin 2008 §8.4; corroborated by Chamon and Ribeiro). E-optimality is also not submodular -- the same section gives an explicit Bayesian E-optimality counterexample, which closes a gap the research notes had recorded as unresolved. But "E-optimality is SDP-requiring" is true only of the continuous relaxation (Boyd and Vandenberghe §7.5, eq. 7.27, over weights on the simplex); the discrete k-subset problem is NP-hard and the SDP is what you relax to and round, and A- and D-optimality have convex/SDP relaxations too, so SDP is not distinctive to E-optimality. Maximizing the minimum pairwise Mahalanobis distance is definitively NON-submodular, not unknown, on two independent grounds, and for the general robust max-min problem Krause, McMahan, Guestrin and Gupta (JMLR 9:2761-2801, 2008) Theorem 3 rules out any polynomial-time multiplicative guarantee unless P = NP, leaving only SATURATE's bicriteria guarantee. Two citation errors in the research notes, not in the claim: arXiv 1711.01501 is Chamon and Ribeiro (NeurIPS 2017), not Bian et al. (which is ICML 2017, PMLR 70:498-507); and the "conditional suppressor-free" phrase the notes could not place is real and belongs to Das and Kempe (2008) for R^2 and Bayesian A-optimality -- the notes were correct to refuse to attribute it to Krause and Guestrin.

STILL OPEN: Four things I could not settle.

1. Monotonicity of the objective as actually implemented. I verified that 1 - Bayes error and min-pairwise Mahalanobis are monotone as exact *population* quantities. An estimated objective -- cross-validated accuracy, or a plug-in Mahalanobis distance with an estimated Sigma -- need not be, because adding a feature can lower cross-validated accuracy through variance. If the implemented objective is not monotone then Das and Kempe does not apply at all, and the authors say the non-monotone extension is unlikely to carry over. I found no paper that settles the non-monotone weak-submodularity case, so for a cross-validated objective there may be no stated guarantee of any kind. This should be checked against the actual estimator before the report commits to the Das and Kempe fallback even as a fallback.

2.
