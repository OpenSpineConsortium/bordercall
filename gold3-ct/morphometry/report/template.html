The measures, drawn where they are taken

Each render cuts one vertebra's own mask in four planes of its frame: sagittal through the body's middle, coronal through the body, coronal through each pedicle's isthmus, and axial through the pedicles. Every line is rebuilt from the measuring code itself and checked against the value the run wrote for that vertebra. A line that disagrees by more than the rows' rounding is left off and listed as withheld. On study 0004, all 77 lines at L3, T8 and C5 matched their rows. Calipers mark lengths. Dashed lines are the end-plates drawn rim to rim (the wedge), and the dotted line is Eisenstein's line from one articular apex to the other. A key entry marked (p) is a line projected onto a plane it does not lie in.

Tested against truth

Each benchmark holds the measures to a truth of its own kind. The phantom's shapes are exact. The bone's own edge comes from the CT. Thinned slices are compared with the same patient's thin scan. Published means come from adults measured by people. VerSe has human fracture grades on masks we did not make. No open CT set gives per-case canal, pedicle or facet dimensions measured by people, so those are held to the phantom and to published means only.
