# CT intensities measure vertebrae; radiographs still need priors

Prior art supports a fully deterministic, training-free route from raw CT intensities to vertebral dimensions, but every published validation stops one step short of the dimensions themselves. Each piece of the CT route exists and carries numbers: the spinal canal found as an enclosed -50 to 50 HU region seeded from the spinous-process tips and joined into a curved centreline (the Radnostics patent, whose fully heuristic pipeline detected **96.25 percent of 8,080 vertebrae in 1,044 CTs** ([US8891848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8891848B2/en))), a geometry-based centreline within **1.8 +/- 1.1 mm** and body and disc centres within **2.8 +/- 1.9 mm** ([Stern 2010](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1088/0031-9155/55/1/015)), discs found as valleys in a one-dimensional bone-weight profile, and bone surfaces placed either by a relative threshold near **49 percent** of the step between adjacent tissues ([Hangartner 2007](https://www.ismni.org/jmni/article/7/9)) or, for anything thinner than about 3 mm, by least-squares fitting of a Gaussian-blurred step, which cut femoral cortical thickness error from 0.90 +/- 0.92 mm (half-maximum) to **-0.01 +/- 0.58 mm** ([Treece TR634](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/reports/abstracts/treece_tr634.html)). No study read reports training-free CT end-plate widths, depths, posterior body heights or canal dimensions against manual measurement in millimetres, so that agreement is the first thing a new pipeline must measure. Single radiographs invert the picture: training-free methods reliably find the spine axis, its boundaries and candidate end-plate lines, but every published millimetre-level corner result (about 0.6 to 2.1 mm) uses a statistical shape model trained on 75 to 670 annotated images, and grey level alone cannot tell a body from its disc. Scale is the second hard limit: a CT's Pixel Spacing and slice positions give exact geometry, whereas a radiograph's Imager Pixel Spacing is defined at the detector and is never corrected, DICOM's magnification factor is the source-to-detector distance over a distance to the table rather than to the spine, and measured lateral magnification of vertebrae is **1.21 cervical and 1.31 lumbar, ranging 1.06 to 1.63** ([Ravi and Rampersaud 2009](https://boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/0301-620X.91BSUPP_II.0910251d)). Turning FleXray masks or four-corner annotations into widths and depths is simple arithmetic once corners exist, but FleXray's 256 x 256 canvas imposes a quantisation floor near 1.7 mm per pixel on a 43 cm film, and the minimum-area-rectangle shortcut common in public code cannot represent a wedged body.

## CT supports a complete training-free chain, validated only piece by piece

### Relative edges beat fixed Hounsfield values, and blurred-step fits beat both below 3 mm

No single Hounsfield value defines bone, and the training-free pipelines that work avoid asking one to. Radnostics uses voxels above **100 HU** per sagittal plane (a "bone intensity frequency") to find the mid-sagittal spine planes, voxels above **300 HU** on a coronal maximum-intensity projection for its inferior crop, a canal-contents band of **-50 to 50 HU**, and eleven density classes in which water is -10 to 10 HU and bone 300 to 1100 HU ([US8891848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8891848B2/en)). A PME IP patent separates body from air at **-1000 HU**, thresholds bone and vessels together at **130 HU**, then raises the threshold "in steps of 1 HU up to 400 HU", assigning to bone each connected component larger than the largest vessel diameter possible in that body region, and warns that fractures can violate its criteria ([US10706538B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10706538B2/en)). These floors collide with osteoporosis. In Pickhardt's cohort of 1,867 adults, L1 attenuation of 160 HU or less was 90 percent sensitive for osteoporosis and 110 HU over 90 percent specific, and 97 percent of patients with moderate-to-severe fractures sat at 145 HU or lower ([ACP Internist, 2013](https://immattersacp.org/weekly/archives/2013/04/23/1.htm)); Burns, Yao and Summers measured mean T12 to L4 attenuation of **146 +/- 29 HU in fracture patients against 173 +/- 42 HU in controls** ([Burns 2017](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiol.2017162100)). The 2016 multi-centre segmentation challenge adds that intravenous contrast "may cause problems in osteoporotic cases" because bone density can approach contrast density ([Yao 2016](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5dj0636d/qt5dj0636d.pdf)). The design lesson is that a fixed threshold can find the cortical shell and end-plate rims, which stay at hundreds of HU even in osteoporosis, but cannot be trusted to fill the trabecular core; the body interior should be reached by connectivity inside a closed rim, and every chord should be measured on the rim.

Where an edge lies matters more than which voxels count as bone. Hangartner showed on a pQCT bone phantom that "the threshold for accurate geometric segmentation was 49% of the difference of the density between the adjacent tissues", while accurate density needed 95 percent of bone's maximum, and warned that "these specific thresholds are valid only for the scanner tested" ([Hangartner 2007](https://www.ismni.org/jmni/article/7/9)). That relative rule fails once a structure is thinner than the scanner blur: Treece and Gee state that cortex under about 3 mm is blurred in clinical CT so that "thresholding and full-width half-maximum methods" cannot determine its width ([TR691](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/reports/abstracts/treece_tr691.html)). On 16 cadaveric femurs with cortices of 0.3 to 4 mm, thickness errors were **0.25 +/- 0.69 mm for simple thresholding, 0.90 +/- 0.92 mm for a 50 percent relative threshold, and -0.01 +/- 0.58 mm for model fitting**, and below about 1 mm thresholding "increasingly fails to detect the cortex at all" ([TR634](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/reports/abstracts/treece_tr634.html)). Vertebral end-plates and canal walls live in exactly that thin regime.

Cortical bone mapping solves it by modelling each intensity profile along the surface normal as piecewise-constant tissue levels convolved with a Gaussian point spread function. A successor patent writes the model out: y(x) = y0 + (y1 - y0)/2 [1 + erf((x - (x1 - t/2)) / (sigma sqrt 2))] + (y2 - y1)/2 [1 + erf((x - (x1 + t/2)) / (sigma sqrt 2))], with y0, y1 and y2 the soft-tissue, cortical and trabecular levels, t the cortical thickness, x1 its centre and sigma the blur, fitted by Levenberg-Marquardt ([US10360681B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10360681B2/en)). Because blur and cortical density trade off when cortex is thin, every variant pins one of them. The first method fixed cortical density per bone; the 2012 revision estimated that constant more rigorously and still saw about **20 percent thickness error at 0.5 mm cortex** ([TR665](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/reports/abstracts/treece_tr665.html)); the 2015 version uses local blur estimates to correct density and reports thickness "accuracy 0.12 +/- 0.39 mm" for 1 to 3 mm cortices against HR-pQCT, with local density estimable only above 800 mg/cm3 ([Treece and Gee 2015](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/6d6be37d-98f8-479a-8450-30df363c10f7)), although Turmezei and colleagues attribute the same figure to the 2012 paper ([Turmezei 2016](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/75866/1/Published_Version.pdf)). Humbert's patent instead ties density to thickness through a look-up table built from 23 micro-CT femurs, reaching 0.08 +/- 0.21 mm at 0.33 x 0.33 x 1.0 mm voxels and 0.10 +/- 0.25 mm at 1 x 1 x 3 mm in simulated leave-one-out tests ([US10360681B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10360681B2/en)); that table is a learned prior, so it fails a strict no-training rule, while the Treece variants estimate density from the scan itself. Treece's free Stradview documentation supplies the practical settings: the measurement line runs along the outer normal for "approximately three times the thickness of the thickest cortex", with zero line width for clinical CT, from a starting surface within "a couple of mm" of the bone; density and blur are estimated globally first, in-plane and out-of-plane blur separately for the 2015 method, and a cortical density "much less than 1000 HU" signals overestimated thickness ([Stradview thickness](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/~gmt11/stradview/thickness.htm); [Stradview how-to](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/~gmt11/stradview/howto_cbm.htm)). Kang, Engelke and Kalender's alternative, 3D region growing with local adaptive thresholds followed by boundary correction and adjustment to local cortical density, reached cortical-thickness accuracy of one to 2.5 voxel side lengths on the European Spine Phantom and precision errors under 1 percent for volumes on nine pelvic CTs ([Kang 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12846428/)), but its local threshold formula is only in the unread full text. Every one of these accuracy figures comes from femur, pelvis or phantom; none was validated on vertebrae.

### The canal is the most reliable training-free axis

Three published canal finders need no trained model, and all exploit the same cue: a dark, roughly round hole enclosed by bone behind the body. Radnostics takes the most posterior high-density voxel on each axial slice of the central sagittal planes as a spinous-process tip, filters tips for redundancy, rib origin and vertical alignment, and searches a cuboid **25 mm either side, 10 to 80 mm anterior of the tip and 30 mm craniocaudally**; after a mean filter of radius 1 it marks a pixel when more than 50 percent of the voxels along z fall in -50 to 50 HU, spirals outwards with flood fill until a region passes size, aspect-ratio and bounding-box occupancy tests, and takes its centroid as the foramen centre; seeds are joined into a polyline, close seeds merged, seeds whose two adjacent segments both cross bone dropped, outliers removed by linear regression with a scoliosis tolerance, and the line extended to the image boundary ([US8891848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8891848B2/en)). Yao, O'Connor and Summers threshold the spine and extract the canal with a watershed and directed graph search on routine 5 mm CT, extracting 69 of 71 columns ([Yao 2006](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FISBI.2006.1624935)). Banik, Rangayyan and Boag detect the canal with a Hough circle transform on a cropped edge map whose centres guide opening-by-reconstruction, reaching a canal Hausdorff distance of **1.6 +/- 0.5 mm** and mean closest-point distance of 0.6 +/- 0.1 mm against a radiologist, though on only 21 paediatric slices ([Banik 2010](https://www.springermedizin.de/automatic-segmentation-of-the-ribs-the-vertebral-column-and-the-/8777810)). Stern and colleagues' "completely automated" geometry-based detector gives the only centreline accuracy in millimetres, 1.8 +/- 1.1 mm on 29 CT and 13 MR lumbar images ([Stern 2010](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1088/0031-9155/55/1/015)), and Vrtovec's thesis fits polynomial spine curves by optimisation on the premise that bodies are "the locally largest bony structures", then estimates each vertebra's pose by registering mirror-image halves of an elliptical-cylinder mask with six rigid parameters ([Vrtovec 2007](https://www.dlib.si/stream/URN:NBN:SI:DOC-5XZ0NEQ7/49874a04-5289-47f5-994a-1c6424203dd1/PDF)). The canal's centroid does not depend on trabecular density, which makes it the most osteoporosis-proof axis available, but its bony ring opens at the foramina and between laminae, so per-slice flood fill needs an area guard that none of the sources specifies. No training-free study measured canal width or anteroposterior depth on CT against manual values; the automated canal-measurement papers found were all MRI.

### Discs appear as valleys in a bone-weight profile along that axis

Every training-free partitioner finds intervertebral discs as intensity valleys between bony peaks along a curved axis. Radnostics publishes the most reimplementable rules: along a curve through the expected body centres, each cross-section gets a weight of Frequency(200 <= HU < 400) + 4 x Frequency(HU >= 400), a formula the fetched HTML flagged as garbled and that must be checked against the patent's PDF figures; moving average, maximum and minimum are taken over a window of about **40 mm**, "the height of a vertebra plus adjacent discs"; samples are classed as extreme spikes above about 60 percent of the moving maximum, spikes above about 33 percent, highs above twice the moving average and depressions within about 5 of the moving minimum; ordered rules mark "a depression between two spikes" as a disc; a gap analysis flags gaps over **150 percent of the local average gap** (75 percent from the border) and repeats until no new disc appears, and of two discs closer than about 40 percent of the window the wider is dropped; each disc is then flood-filled at min[100 HU, mean + 2 SD] of its seed region with one-nearest-neighbour voting ([US8891848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8891848B2/en)). On 1,044 abdominal CTs from 15 sites this detected **7,777 of 8,080 vertebrae (96.25 percent) with 80 false positives**, and an observer judged the single mid-sagittal segmentation adequate for height assessment in 83.3 percent, at 75 to 439 s per study ([US8891848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8891848B2/en)). Yao 2006 analyses aggregated intensity profiles on curved reformations with two partition errors in 69 columns, one a missed T1-T2 level ([Yao 2006](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FISBI.2006.1624935)), and Kim and Kim build 3D "deformable fences" on valley-emphasised Gaussian images from detected discs, correct trapped curves with a minimum-cost path and label vertebrae by fence-limited region growing, but report no accuracy figure for their 50 patients ([Kim and Kim 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19328651/)). Klinder 2009, Korez 2015 and all five methods of the 2016 challenge rely on trained shape or appearance models ([Yao 2016](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5dj0636d/qt5dj0636d.pdf)), so they serve only as benchmarks: healthy-case Dice 0.868 to 0.947, falling to 0.472 to 0.897 on osteoporotic spines.

### No training-free CT study reports chords against calipers

The literature measured detection, overlap and fracture grade, not the chords this project needs. Stern's superquadric, started as an elliptical cylinder and deformed under 25 clinically meaningful shape parameters and 6 pose parameters, was fitted to 75 CT and 75 MR bodies with no millimetre error in the abstract ([Stern 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22080628/)); its follow-up read body height and end-plate inclination and concavity off the fitted parameters to classify 682 bodies with 92.5 percent accuracy, the classification thresholds being learned while the measurement fit is not ([Stern 2013](https://open.uns.ac.rs/handle/123456789/9029)). The NIH "height compass", which divides each body into Genant-style zones, detected 95.7 percent of 210 fractures but graded them correctly only 68 percent of the time and is an SVM-based "machine learning" system ([Burns 2017](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiol.2017162100)). Radnostics computes no numeric heights at all. The one structural hint is that bodies are the most reliably delineated part (challenge Dice 0.936 against 0.852 for other substructures) ([Yao 2016](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5dj0636d/qt5dj0636d.pdf)), which favours body chords over posterior-element chords. Panjabi's landmark definitions for end-plate width and depth, posterior body height and canal width and depth were not retrieved ([Shangloo 2021](https://ijrabms.umsu.ac.ir/article-171.xml) did not define them), so the chord definitions in the build section below are conventional, not cited.

### Osteoporosis, contrast and the column ends break intensity rules

The documented failures cluster where intensity cues weaken. All five challenge methods lost accuracy on osteoporotic spines, with maximum surface distances up to 44 mm, one leaking into the disc space of fractured bodies; mean Dice for T1 to T6 was 0.867 healthy and 0.652 osteoporotic against 0.933 and 0.854 lumbar, because the costovertebral junctions complicate interfaces ([Yao 2016](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5dj0636d/qt5dj0636d.pdf)). Radnostics' inferior crop may remove L5, end discs need a separate border rule, rib-origin tips must be filtered out, and abutting aortic calcification is cut away by a ray-based connectivity test toward the body centre ([US8891848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8891848B2/en)). Doubling or quintupling noise changed Kang's cortical thickness by one voxel side ([Kang 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12846428/)), and blurred-step fits fail or overlap inner surfaces on highly curved surfaces with thick cortex ([Stradview thickness](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/~gmt11/stradview/thickness.htm)). Diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis, ankylosis, metal, low-dose noise, the sacrum and C1-C2 were not quantified for any training-free method; bridging osteophytes will fill the disc valley, true fusion leaves no disc to find, and the sacrum and atlas break the body-plus-disc periodicity, so each needs a special-case rule that no source supplies.

## Radiographs give the spine axis without training, but corners only with shape models

Whether any classical method works on a single film with no trained shape model has a split answer. For the spine's position, orientation and outline, yes. Long and Thoma at the National Library of Medicine blur a cervical film with a 100 x 100 Gaussian of sigma 100, subsample it to a 6 x 7 matrix, grow regions for the two brightest blobs (skull and shoulder) with a tolerance of **0.05 of the dynamic range**, join them as a coarse axis, cast 20 transverse lines and use dynamic programming with **lambda = 0.05** to trace the posterior edge, ridge, anterior edge and anterior valley; thresholds were set "experimentally" and no trained model is involved, and on 48 NHANES II films the curves were computed on 46 with orientation within 15 degrees of the expert slope in all but one, though no millimetre error is given ([Long and Thoma](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/system/files/pub2001001.pdf)). Dinesh Kumar and Thomas use a top-hat transform, H-maxima and a Radon transform on 100 cervical films, reaching a mean orientation error of **4.6 degrees** and a posterior-boundary point within **+/- 5.2 mm** ([Dinesh Kumar and Thomas 2005](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10278-005-5150-4)).

For end-plates, three expired Siemens patents describe fully automatic, training-free designs but publish no accuracy. The first builds intensity ridge maps from the curvature of profiles in at least two directions and picks one axis point per equally spaced cutting line by dynamic programming over segment orientation, inter-segment angle and ridge strength, then a "dual" programme picks left and right boundary points jointly ([US6608916](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6608916)). The second computes second-order derivatives horizontally, vertically and diagonally, keeps ridges nearly orthogonal to the axis, fits a boundary-to-boundary line segment at each axis point over a range of angles, scores it by summed curvature with outliers excluded, reads maxima as end-plates and minima as discs, and tells an upper end-plate from a lower one by the sign of the summed directional gradient between peaks ([US6608917](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6608917)). The third fits parabolas y = kx^2 + b and runs dynamic programming over end-plate position and disc thickness, constrained by vertebral height within [hmin, hmax], neighbour height ratios, a disc-to-height ratio, a maximum angle between neighbouring end-plates and a global height model **h_v = k a^v + b with a <= 1**, pruning candidates whose residual exceeds a tolerance; it assumes the first and last end-plate ranges are known and states no numeric parameter values ([US7010151](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7010151)). Lunar's lateral DXA patent reads disc spaces as minima of a superior-inferior profile, orients each vertebra by rotating a rectangle to maximise its enclosed sum, and places borders at the first row crossing **30 percent of the histogram peak**, giving six morphometry points with no operator input but also no validation ([US5228068](https://patents.justia.com/patent/5228068)).

Region intensity cannot carry the job on its own. Long and Thoma measured an interior C4 region at mean 197.4 (SD 2.2) against 198.1 (SD 1.6) for the adjacent disc space ([Long and Thoma](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/system/files/pub2001001.pdf)), and Zamora's team found the plain grey-level profile model of an active shape model "invalid" on digitised films until multi-scale edges were blended in ([Zamora 2003](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf)). Edges and ridges, not grey levels, are the training-free signal.

Seeds help but leave the millimetre question open. With the user selecting the top and bottom vertebrae, Zhang and colleagues' fuzzy Hough transform under "vertebral shape constraints" matched manual Cobb angles with **ICC above 0.95 and mean absolute difference under 5 degrees** on 76 films, though the abstract does not say whether the shape prior was learned ([Zhang 2009](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s10278-008-9127-y&resultType=core&format=json)). A generalized Hough transform built from the mean of 25 vertebra shapes detected 64.5 percent of cervical vertebrae automatically with 35.5 percent false detections, rising to 89.0 percent with two user points ([Benjelloun, Mahmoudi, Larhmam 2012](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/38131)). Every millimetre-level corner result found uses a trained statistical model: Smyth's active shape model on lateral DXA, trained on 77 images with three seeds, gave 0.98 to 1.61 pixel point-to-line error against 1.11 to 1.66 for human operators ([Smyth BMVC 1996](https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/1996/smyth_1.pdf)); Roberts' linked appearance models reached about 0.7 mm on normal DXA vertebrae ([Cootes project page](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/timothy.f.cootes/Projects/VertFrac/vert_frac.html)) and, trained on 670 lumbar radiographs, **0.82 mm for normal vertebrae, 1.19 mm for grades 1-2 and 2.12 mm for grade 3 fractures** ([Roberts 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21431411/)); Benjelloun's cervical ASM, trained on 75 images, reported about 0.6 mm ([Benjelloun 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3149802)); and the hierarchical Hough, shape-model and deformable-model pipeline on NHANES II put only **75 percent of cervical and 49 percent of lumbar films** within a 3 mm mean landmark error ([Zamora 2003](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf)).

The failure modes repeat across groups. A template matched along a repeating column produces shifted, off-by-one matches that the template cannot resolve ([Zamora 2003](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf)); even a trained detector can be unsure whether the lowest vertebra is L4 or L5 ([Roberts MICCAI 2012](https://www.springerprofessional.de/automatic-location-of-vertebrae-on-dxa-images-using-random-fores/3978140)); C7 detection fell to 32.5 percent automatic ([IntechOpen 2012](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/38131)); C3 and C4 crowd C2 near the jaw and light leakage misplaced landmarks on two films ([Long and Thoma](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/system/files/pub2001001.pdf)); and analog radiographs carry 10 to 15 percent variable magnification with cone-beam distortion at the field edges ([US5228068](https://patents.justia.com/patent/5228068)). The double contour of a tilted end-plate, ribs and bowel gas are named by no source read.

## DICOM fixes CT scale exactly but gives films only detector-plane scale

### Projection images: Imager Pixel Spacing is at the detector and never corrected

On projection radiographs three spacing attributes compete, and the standard's wording decides which to trust. Imager Pixel Spacing (0018,1164) is the "physical distance measured at the front plane of the detector housing", whose value "shall never be adjusted to account for correction for the effect of geometric magnification"; it is **Type 1 in DX but only Type 3 in CR and in the XA and XRF acquisition module** ([PS3.3 C.8.11.4](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.11.4.html); [PS3.3 C.8](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.html); [PS3.3 C.8.7.2](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.7.2.html)). Pixel Spacing (0028,0030) is the distance "in the Patient", Type 1C, "required if the image has been calibrated"; if uncalibrated it "shall be the same as" Imager Pixel Spacing or Nominal Scanned Pixel Spacing (0018,2010), and if it differs "the image has been corrected for known or assumed geometric magnification or calibrated with respect to some object of known size" ([PS3.3 10.7](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_10.7.html)). Pixel Spacing Calibration Type (0028,0A02) says how: **GEOMETRY** values correspond "to some unspecified depth within the Patient" and **FIDUCIAL** values to the depth of a known object, both valid only "close to the central ray". When Calibration Type, Imager Pixel Spacing and Nominal Scanned Pixel Spacing are all absent, "it cannot be determined whether or not correction or calibration have been performed", and every spacing attribute lists row spacing first ([PS3.3 10.7](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_10.7.html)). Viewers implement this ladder: Cornerstone3D prefers GEOMETRY, then FIDUCIAL, then Imager Pixel Spacing divided by the Estimated Radiographic Magnification Factor or SID/SOD, falling back to detector-plane PROJECTION or UNKNOWN ([Cornerstone3D](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cornerstonejs/cornerstone3D/main/packages/metadata/src/utilities/getPixelSpacingInformation.ts)), while InteleViewer labels measurements "corrected at modality", "not corrected. Measured size is at detector" and so on, though its paraphrase of the magnification factor inverts the standard's ratio ([InteleViewer](https://inteleviewer.documentation.intelerad.com/iv-help/PACS-5-4-1-P2/en/Content/Topics/IV_Measurement_Calibration.html)).

Magnification correction is defined but weak. The Estimated Radiographic Magnification Factor (0018,1114) is the "ratio of Source Image Receptor Distance (SID) over Source Object Distance (SOD)", Type 3 in DX, XA and XRF positioning and absent from the CR Image module ([PS3.3 C.8.11.5](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.11.5.html)). The crucial catch is that SOD (0018,1111) means different things per module: in DX it is the distance "to the table, support or bucky side that is closest to the Imaging Subject", which "does not take into account any air gap", whereas CR measures it to the centre of the field of view and CT and cardiovascular XA to the isocentre ([PS3.3 C.8.11.5](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.11.5.html); [PS3.3 C.8.2](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.2.html); [PS3.3 C.8.7.5](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.7.5.html)). A DX factor therefore describes the patient surface on the table side and under-corrects a spine lying centimetres above it, and since the DX Positioning module is user-optional and CR has none, many files carry no factor at all ([PS3.3 A.26.3](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_A.26.3.html); [PS3.3 A.2.3](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_A.2.3.html)). Clunie notes that size from Imager Pixel Spacing alone "will be an overestimate", gives Magnification = SID / (SOD - Thickness x depth) for an object above the support, and observes that in the installed base "neither the SID and SOD nor Estimated Radiographic Magnification Factor are encoded" in many cases ([Clunie 2007](https://wiki.ihe.net/uploads/e/e6/DigitalMammoSizePaper_20070318a.pdf)). Enhanced XA and XRF come closest to a patient-plane scale for fluoroscopy: Object Pixel Spacing in Center of Beam (0018,9404) is computed per frame from Distance Source to Isocenter and Distance Object to Table Top, with automatic calculation advised only within **+/- 60 degrees** of beam angle ([PS3.3 C.8.19.6.9](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.19.6.9.html)), and the standard's worked example derives patient spacing as detector spacing x SOD / SID ([PS3.17 FFF.2.4](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part17/sect_FFF.2.4.html)).

### CT: Hounsfield units and the affine are exact if slices are sorted by position

CT intensity is unambiguous: output = Rescale Slope x stored value + Rescale Intercept, and when Image Type Value 1 is ORIGINAL, Value 3 is not LOCALIZER and the acquisition is not multi-energy, the output "shall be" Hounsfield units even when Rescale Type is absent ([PS3.3 C.8.2](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.2.html)); projection DX images by contrast carry an identity rescale of slope 1, intercept 0 and type US ([PS3.3 C.8.11.3](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.11.3.html)). Geometry comes from the mandatory Image Plane module: Image Position (Patient), Image Orientation (Patient) and Pixel Spacing are Type 1, Slice Thickness is a Type 2 "nominal" value, Spacing Between Slices is Type 3 and Slice Location is relative to "an unspecified implementation specific reference point"; the mapping from pixel (column i, row j) to patient coordinates uses the second Pixel Spacing value for columns and the first for rows, with zero-based indices ([PS3.3 C.7.6.2](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.7.6.2.html)). Nibabel builds the volume's third axis from the actual positions, (T_N - T_1)/(N - 1), which absorbs gantry-tilt shear ([nibabel](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nipy/nibabel/master/doc/source/dicom/dicom_orientation.rst)), and Clunie advises computing the interval "from the distance along the normal", since the encoded interval "may be unreliable" and some vendors stored the gap instead ([Clunie 2013](http://dclunie.blogspot.com/2013/10/how-thick-am-i-sad-story-of-lonely-slice.html)). Gantry/Detector Tilt is "not intended for mathematical computations", and Patient Position is "for annotation purposes only" ([PS3.3 C.8.2](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.2.html); [PS3.3 C.7.3](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.7.3.html)). Enhanced CT moves the same attributes into shared or per-frame functional groups ([PS3.3 C.7.6.16](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.7.6.16.html)). KVP (Type 2) and Convolution Kernel (Type 3) are the acquisition covariates most likely to shift edge profiles ([PS3.3 C.8.2](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.2.html)).

### Covariates are optional and de-identification removes most of them

Patient's Sex and Birth Date are Type 2 (present, possibly empty), while Age, Size (in metres) and Weight (in kilograms) are Type 3 in a user-optional module, so a conformant file may lack all three ([PS3.3 C.7.2.2](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.7.2.2.html)). Age uses the fixed four-byte form nnnD, nnnW, nnnM or nnnY ([PS3.5 6.2](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part05/sect_6.2.html)). The Basic Application Level Confidentiality Profile empties Sex and Birth Date and removes Age, Size and Weight unless the Retain Patient Characteristics Option keeps them; it does not touch Pixel Spacing, Imager Pixel Spacing, KVP or kernel ([PS3.15 Annex E](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part15/chapter_E.html); [PS3.15 E.3.7](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part15/sect_E.3.7.html)). TCIA applies that option, keeping sex, age (listed as 90+ above 90), size and weight while emptying birth date and offsetting dates ([TCIA](https://wiki.cancerimagingarchive.net/x/MpJXAQ)). View Position is Type 2 in CR and Type 3 in DX, and Body Part Examined Type 2 in CR and Type 3 in the general series ([PS3.3 C.8](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.html); [PS3.3 C.7.3](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/current/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.7.3.html)).

### NIfTI keeps the grid and the rescale, and loses everything else

NIfTI-1 holds dimensions, voxel sizes, a quaternion qform and a general sform, one linear scaling (scl_slope, scl_inter, ignored when slope is zero) and units, in a right-handed RAS+ world where DICOM is LPS+, so x and y change sign ([nifti1.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NIFTI-Imaging/nifti_clib/master/niftilib/nifti1.h)); NIfTI-2 only widens the field types ([nifti2.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NIFTI-Imaging/nifti_clib/master/nifti2/nifti2.h)). There is no field for any patient attribute, Rescale Type, calibration type, magnification, kVp or kernel. Dcm2niix keeps the stored integers and copies Rescale Slope and Intercept into scl_slope and scl_inter, does not carry Rescale Type, sets qform_code and sform_code to scanner-anatomical, and by default (`-ba y`) **strips PatientSex, PatientAge, PatientSize and PatientWeight** from its JSON sidecar; `-ba n` keeps them, PatientAge is written only when the age string ends in Y, and SliceThickness and SpacingBetweenSlices are skipped whenever an exposure tag marks the series as X-ray, which covers most CT ([dcm2niix README](https://github.com/rordenlab/dcm2niix/blob/master/BIDS/README.md); [nii_dicom.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rordenlab/dcm2niix/master/console/nii_dicom.cpp); [nii_dicom_batch.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rordenlab/dcm2niix/master/console/nii_dicom_batch.cpp)). It writes a resampled "_Tilt" volume for gantry tilt and an equalised "_Eq" volume when "interslice distance varies" ([nii_dicom_batch.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rordenlab/dcm2niix/master/console/nii_dicom_batch.cpp)), and which spacing tag it uses for CR or DX pixdim was not traced. Nibabel prefers the sform, then the qform, applies the scaling in `get_fdata()` and marks the header's scaling consumed as NaN ([nibabel](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nipy/nibabel/master/doc/source/nifti_images.rst)). PNG's pHYs chunk states "the intended pixel size or aspect ratio for display" ([PNG](https://www.w3.org/TR/png-3/#11pHYs)), JFIF stores print densities ([JFIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG_File_Interchange_Format)) and Exif designates 72 dpi when resolution is unknown ([FreeImage](https://freeimage.sourceforge.io/fnet/html/BD344C00.htm)), so none of them can set a vertebral scale.

## Corners convert to widths in one line; magnification of 1.06 to 1.63 dominates the error

Quantitative morphometry places six points per vertebra on a lateral film, "the four corners of the vertebral body, as well as the mid-point of the superior and inferior endplate", and takes anterior, middle and posterior heights between the paired points ([Oei 2013](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/2910/html)); Genant's grades are 20 to 25, 25 to 40 and at least 40 percent height reductions ([La Rhumatologie](https://www.larhumatologie.fr/outils/index-de-genant/)). From those points the end-plate depth on a lateral film is the distance between the posterior and anterior corners of one end-plate, the posterior body height is the distance between the two posterior corners, and on an AP film the end-plate width is the distance between the left and right corners of one end-plate; no readable source reports the reproducibility of widths or depths in millimetres, only of heights and ratios. For heights, SpineAnalyzer's semi-automated six points reached ICCs above 0.95 with RMS coefficients of variation of 2.5 to 4.4 percent on 1,246 vertebrae ([Kim 2011](https://www.springermedicine.com/intra-and-inter-reader-reliability-of-semi-automated-quantitativ/20634906)), a six-landmark network reached a **height MAE of 0.61 mm** and ICC 0.98 against three radiologists without saying how pixels became millimetres ([Yao 2024](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/127000/html)), an AP four-point method fell to ICC 0.53 at L5 ([Chen 2024](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/110366/html)), and corners placed by maximising quadrilateral area on a shape-model contour erred by 0.82 to 2.15 mm, with mid-points worst at 2.57 to 2.81 mm ([Franchini 2014](https://imeko.org/publications/tc13-2014/IMEKO-TC13-2014-06.pdf)).

Corner datasets fix the arithmetic. AASCE stores 68 landmarks per AP film, four per vertebra for 17 vertebrae, and the widely used reference code reads them in groups of four, averaging points i and i+2 and points i+1 and i+3 into the left and right side mid-points, which implies a top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right order; the Cobb angle is the largest angle between any two of the resulting vertebra lines, a tilt measure rather than a true end-plate Cobb ([Yi, cobb_evaluate.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yijingru/Vertebra-Landmark-Detection/master/cobb_evaluate.py)). Under that order the upper end-plate width is the distance from corner 1 to corner 2 and the lower from 3 to 4. The project holds 609 labelled AASCE films ([aasce.json](file:///home/claude/SchwingNet/data/sources/aasce.json)) and 4,963 lateral cervical CSXA films with corner JSON whose convention was not verified because the paper could not be read ([csxa.json](file:///home/claude/SchwingNet/data/sources/csxa.json)). BUU-LSPINE's automatic corner extraction erred by **4.63 mm on AP and 4.91 mm on lateral films** ([BUU-LSPINE](https://doaj.org/article/07d0825c5b2c444aa717d93b1396e6fb)).

Masks become corners in three published ways, none compared with the others on the same data. Public scoliosis code fits OpenCV's minimum-area rectangle to each contour and reads end-plates from its long sides ([LijunRio](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LijunRio/Spine-cobb-angle-measurement/master/coronal_plane_cobb.py)), which forces upper and lower end-plates parallel and equal and so cannot express wedging or biconcavity. A more careful extractor approximates the contour to four vertices, validates the quadrilateral, merges points within 6 pixels, falls back to the rectangle on the convex hull, orders corners top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left from the previous vertebra and refines them with sub-pixel corner search on a blurred mask, or takes end-plate endpoints as the extreme contour points within a band of 12 percent of body height ([Radimsky, extraction.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JaroslavRadimsky/vertebra_segmentation_and_keypoint_extraction/main/Src/Atlas/inference/extraction.py)). The only peer-reviewed mask-to-landmark result found had median landmark errors of **1.7 to 4.1 mm** ([Song 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5354/10/10/1229)), several times the 0.61 mm of direct six-point regression, and the strongest morphometry pipeline uses its mask only to locate and crop a body before regressing the points, with agreement collapsing for bodies over 40 percent collapsed ([Arakawa 2025](https://academic.oup.com/jbmrplus/article/9/4/ziaf017/7979261)).

Magnification then dwarfs the corner error. Against CT or MRI, vertebral bodies on lateral digital films were magnified **1.21 +/- 0.01 at the cervical spine (range 1.06 to 1.57) and 1.31 +/- 0.01 at the lumbar spine (range 1.09 to 1.63)**, rising with BMI from 1.28 in non-obese to 1.38 in obese patients ([Ravi and Rampersaud 2009](https://boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/0301-620X.91BSUPP_II.0910251d)). A statistical correction reduced the variance of magnification differences between serial films by 14 percent, "considerably more" than correction from the spine-film distance ([Lunt 2001](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s001980170018)), and long-leg CR overstated length by 8.7 percent against EOS ([Birkenmaier 2024](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/123378/1/s00256-024-04592-9.pdf)). A spread of 1.09 to 1.63 is a factor of 1.5 between patients, so an uncorrected lumbar end-plate depth of 35 mm could correspond to anything from about 21 to 32 mm; ratios such as anterior-to-posterior height or depth-to-height cancel uniform magnification and are the only magnification-free outputs. Simple geometry adds two further effects that no source quantified: an end-plate viewed 5 degrees off its plane projects a band about 45 x sin 5 deg = 3.9 mm tall, and a vertebra 15 cm off the central ray at 80 cm source distance is seen about 10.6 degrees obliquely.

FleXray's outputs fix the conversion details. For each input it writes channel-first arrays of shape 61 x 256 x 256: thresholded uint8 masks at 0.5, float32 sigmoid probabilities and logits, plus a label_names.json; the model is multilabel, so channels can overlap, with background at 0, ribs 1 to 12 at channels 17 to 28, C1 to C7 at 30 to 36, T1 to T12 at 37 to 48, L1 to L5 at 49 to 53 and the sacrum at 55, and no L6 or T13 channel ([FleXray README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorButoi/FleXray/main/README.md); [model labels](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorButoi/FleXray/main/src/fxr/configs/models/all_structures_flexray_v4.yml)). Every input is zero-padded centrally to a square, with pad_left = (side - width) // 2 and pad_top = (side - height) // 2, then resized with antialiased bilinear interpolation to 256; DICOM is rescaled by slope and intercept, MONOCHROME1 inverted and the image min-max scaled to [0, 1] ([inference.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorButoi/FleXray/main/docs/inference.md); [preprocessing.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorButoi/FleXray/main/src/fxr/inference/preprocessing.py)). Its training renders span magnifications of 1.28 to 2.5 with a quarter rescaled 0.8 to 1.2 in plane, so its masks carry no physical scale of their own ([FleXray audit plan](file:///home/claude/symphony-template/docs/research/flexray-audit-plan-2026-10-25.md)). A 43 cm film side maps to 430/256 = 1.68 mm per canvas pixel at the detector, so a 30 mm lumbar body spans about 18 pixels. The code is MIT and the weights CC BY-NC 4.0 ([FleXray README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorButoi/FleXray/main/README.md)).

## Conclusion

The prior art changes where the risk sits. Finding and separating vertebrae in CT without a trained model is a solved engineering problem at the 96 percent level, and sub-voxel surface location has femoral validation better than 0.6 mm; what nobody has done is validate the resulting chords, so a pipeline built from these parts is defensible only once its end-plate, posterior-height and canal chords are compared with calipers or manual readings on the project's own scans, starting with a phantom where truth is exact. On radiographs the honest training-free claim covers the axis, the column outline and candidate end-plate lines; any millimetre corner from a single film today comes from annotation, a seed, a trained model or a network such as FleXray, and its millimetres inherit a magnification uncertainty of tens of percent that dwarfs sub-millimetre corner precision. That makes ratios the robust radiograph output, makes CT the only modality where absolute chords are physically meaningful from intensities alone, and makes the DICOM reader, not the image algorithm, the place where absolute radiograph measurements most often go silently wrong, because the default conversion to NIfTI discards both the covariates and any record of whether a spacing was ever corrected.

### Steps no prior art validates

The following steps have no validation in any source read and must be measured before their numbers are trusted. In CT: the agreement of every chord (end-plate width and depth, posterior body height, canal width and AP depth) with manual or caliper measurement for any training-free method; the blurred-step fit applied to vertebral end-plates and canal walls rather than femoral cortex, including whether the outer-surface position is better conditioned than thickness; the canal flood-fill leak guard; the vertebral symmetry frame's accuracy; the metal exclusion threshold; rules for fused or DISH-bridged levels, the sacrum and C1-C2; Kang's local threshold formula; and the Radnostics disc-weight equation, which must be checked against the patent drawings. On radiographs: every Siemens and Lunar end-plate step and its parameter values; corner localisation of any kind without a trained model or seed; handling of the end-plate double contour; and level naming without an anchor. In the header: how often clinical files carry Pixel Spacing different from Imager Pixel Spacing or any magnification factor, which tag dcm2niix uses for radiograph pixdim, and magnification factors for AP and full-spine views. For masks and corners: any comparison of rectangle, polygon and band corner extraction on the same masks against manual corners; FleXray mask-derived corner and width accuracy; whether upsampling probabilities before thresholding beats canvas-resolution corners; the effect of axial rotation and lateral bending on apparent width and depth; and how FleXray's published 5.13-degree AASCE Cobb result was computed from its masks, since its code carries no Cobb routine ([FleXray audit plan](file:///home/claude/symphony-template/docs/research/flexray-audit-plan-2026-10-25.md)).
