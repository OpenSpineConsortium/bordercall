## The census in brief (synthetic, from the printed tables)

Of the reread's 106 keys, 97 are measures of a vertebra; 68 are ranked (Panjabi's 42, 26 more from the 39 other papers' 46), 10 are printed everywhere without a spread, 19 are named but tabled by no paper, 9 are not measures. Over C3 to L5 a source can give at most 68.0 nats (22 log 22): Panjabi's upper end-plate area alone gives 22.79 and names 19.2% of lone bodies, the first five of the census order 44.73 and 56.5%, all 32 61.56 and 88.8%; 22 of the 32 add under a tenth of what they give alone; none of the seven a SPINEPS mask gives is among the first five (the lower end-plate width is sixth, the posterior height last). Tan 2004's 18 measures at every level name 85.9%. The lumbar-only tables (80 groups of five levels) name a lone lumbar body 23.8% to 69.4% of the time, 43.5% at the median, against a chance of 20%.
