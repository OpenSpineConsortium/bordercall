Naming Vertebrae From Every Measure

The naming rule scored with every measure the four Panjabi papers print with a spread from C3 to L5, against the seven a SPINEPS mask gives today: the build-up, the chain of levels, a lone vertebra and the sampler review. bordercall pull requests 18 to 33, merged on main at 3722785.

What this page shows

  • Of the 49 measures the four Panjabi papers print, 32 carry a mean and a spread at every level from C3 to L5, 18 lengths, 6 areas and 8 angles; the other 17 are printed with no spread or in one region only. The rule takes those 32, each with its own measurement error and its own power of the patient's size.
  • Adding measures in a greedy order names more fields at every step, and the answers at 0.99 keep their promise throughout. The first seven of that order share none of the seven measures in use today and name 89.2% of three-body fields against 73.1%.
  • The chain of levels, which sums out a missed body and gives an L6 and a T13 names of their own, names 97.5% to 99.0% of nine-body fields exactly where the rule before it names 70.0% to 74.0%, and its answers at 0.99 are right 99.4% of the time or more.
  • A lone vertebra is named 86.8% of the time with all 32 and 46.2% with the seven; T4 to T8 are named least; a lone L6 is named one time in eight and a lone T13 half the time, where the seven can name neither.
  • NUTS with every level known is the closed form within 1.9 Monte Carlo errors. PyMC's proportional categorical proposal samples the wrong law, so the discrete step was written here with Liu's acceptance; the PyMC issue is drafted.

Every number here is synthetic. The columns are drawn by bordercall's generator from the table the rule assumes, Panjabi's four papers on twelve fresh autopsy spines, with the measurement error the rule assumes (1 mm for a length, 3 degrees for an angle). The rule is scored against the truth it was built from. What a real spine does, and what a mask or a radiograph measures with what error, is not measured on this page; the gold tier 3 CT morphometrics and the comparison with the 39 other papers are the pieces that measure it.

The 32 measures, in the order that adds most

The forward greedy order was chosen on 100 selection columns of seeds of its own by the log loss of the true place summed over 1, 3 and 9 bodies, and every prefix of it was then scored on columns that did not choose it. Each measure's place is what it adds given the measures before it, so the order separates the information the measures carry: a measure that repeats an earlier one lands late however strong it is alone. This is the census of these 32 as the lone-body and the three-body problem see it; the census per level, with the groups of independent information and the gold tier 3 table beside this one, follows as schwingcheck census.

The build-up

One measure names 42.0% of three-body fields exactly; the seven, 73.1%; the first seven of the greedy order, 89.2%; 14 measures, 94.8%; 25, 97.6%; all 32, 98.3%. From 25 measures on every nine-body field of the 2,000 columns is named exactly. The share named at a posterior of 0.99 grows from 2.5% to 87.0% at three bodies, and of the fields named at 0.99, 99.59% or more were right at every count and every width scored; the least, 99.59%, is 9 wrong of 2,196, at two measures and three bodies.

The errors move the share named and not the promise. With every measure's error doubled, in the columns and the rule alike, the seven name 63.0% of three-body fields and all 32 name 95.3%; halved, 76.8% and 99.2%; with the offsets correlated at 0.4 between measures, which the rule takes as independent, 73.8% and 98.3%, against 73.1% and 98.3% as assumed. In every cell the answers at 0.99 are right 99.77% of the time or more.

What the table costs

A table estimated from 12 people, as many as Panjabi measured, names 13.2 points fewer three-body fields exactly with the seven and 5.2 fewer with all 32 than the table as printed, and its answers at 0.99 are right only 95.75% and 98.05% of the time, below the 99% the posterior promises. From 50 people on they are right 99.36% of the time or more, with all 32 99.82% at 50 people; from 200 people the share named is within 1.25 points of the printed table's. With the seven alone, tables from 50, 200 and 2,000 people stand 1.1 to 1.6 standard errors above 99%, and the table as printed is itself 99.54% on these columns, so with the seven these columns cannot tell whether a table from 50 people keeps the promise.

The axis's people all have one size and are measured without error, so these rows give what the people's own variation costs a table and nothing more; whether real spines vary from person to person as the generator's people do is not measured here.

The chain of levels

The chain reads a field as a walk down the column, with pymc-extras' DiscreteMarkovChain as its PyMC form and an exact sum as its answer. Each step may miss a body, and a six-lumbar spine or a spine with a T13 is a state of the chain rather than an error, so an L6 and a T13 have names of their own. Against the consecutive rule of step d, on synthetic columns where a step misses a body with probability 0.167, the chain names more at every width and its answers at 0.99 keep their promise where the consecutive rule's do not.

With the seven, 46.2%, and almost never at 0.99. Knowing the patient's height adds 1.9 points with the seven and 0.2 with all 32; the size itself adds 9.5 and 2.9. Speed

One three-body field, single-threaded, with each engine's table built once: the rule takes 0.31 ms with one measure, 1.12 ms with the seven and 9.68 ms with all 32 on the trapezoid's nodes (5.25 ms on the Gauss-Hermite nodes); NUTS on the same field takes 166 to 712 times as long, and at 16 measures was once silently wrong at R-hat 1.00. The chain, built once, takes 2.4 ms at three bodies, 6.6 ms at five and 79 ms at nine with all 32; the path form that rebuilds its blocks in every call takes 16 s at nine. These are batch means with the setup inside, not latency numbers by SchwingNet's rule, which needs ONNX parity and a p95 for the same file; the budget there is 97 ms.

The sampler review

With every level known, NUTS on the size is the closed form: 8 fields, |z| at most 1.9 Monte Carlo errors, R-hat 1.002, effective samples 2,941 and 6,698 for the seven and all 32. With the place free, PyMC's uniform categorical proposal switches between two likely places 0.15% to 2.9% of the time and its chains disagree (R-hat 1.314); its proportional proposal mixes (R-hat 1.001) but samples the stationary law of its own kernel, not the target, 3 to 37 Monte Carlo errors off: its acceptance ratio takes one less the renormalised proposal weight where Liu's takes one less the value's own probability. Summing the level out, as pymc-extras does, is exact except where NUTS leaves a chain in a far mode, which R-hat shows. The discrete step bordercall uses is schwingcheck.categorical.MetropolisedGibbs, Liu's acceptance written out; the report to the PyMC project is drafted with a reproduction that stands alone (pull request 34) and waits for the owner to post it.

Against the 39 other papers

The 39 other papers of the owner's list give 209 paper-measure pairs, 106 distinct measures (57 not in Panjabi) and 4,477 series, nearly all lumbar or thoracolumbar; three papers reach C3 to L5 with a spread at every level. By the owner's rule of 28 October each paper is its own population and technique unless its text says otherwise, so they do not enter this table. Set beside it (the clinical reread's draft pull request 26, not yet on main): on three-body fields drawn from 25 papers' own series, Panjabi's rule names 23.7% exactly against 47.2% on fields from his own table, and its answers at 0.99 are right 88.8% of the time, 92.0% when the rule is widened by the papers' spread. That gap, not the mathematics, is where the rule is weak: the population behind the table.

Limits. Synthetic truth throughout. One table of twelve spines; no correlation between measures is known, and the rule takes them as independent. Most of the 32 are measured by no code on a mask or a radiograph yet, and the measurement errors are stated assumptions, not measured ones. The six-lumbar and T13 columns are the generator's construction. No latency number by SchwingNet's rule exists for any of this.
