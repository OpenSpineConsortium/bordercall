## The classical chain priors and the four references of the first answer

### Glocker et al. 2012, regression forests and a hidden Markov model

- Claim: Glocker et al. 2012 (MICCAI), 'Automatic localization and identification of vertebrae in arbitrary field-of-view CT scans': regression forests giving a noisy landmark map routed through a hidden Markov model (also described as 'a separate, discrete generative Markov Random Field') solved by Viterbi, credited with 'Voxel Grid Search (Median < 6mm)'.
- Status: real but misdescribed
- Record: Glocker B, Feulner J, Criminisi A, Haynor DR, Konukoglu E. Automatic Localization and Identification of Vertebrae in Arbitrary Field-of-View CT Scans. MICCAI 2012, LNCS 7512 (Part III), pp. 590-598. DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-33454-2_73; PMID 23286179. Open full text: http://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~bglocker/pdfs/glocker2012miccai.pdf; poster: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/glocker2012spine.poster.pdf. Already resolved in SN docs/research/autoanatomist-search/prior-art.md (vertebra labelling group, entry 6) and prior-art-records.tsv row 52.
- What it does: Stage 1: regression forests (50 trees, depth 20, long-range contextual intensity features) regress the 26 centroids C1 to S2 from every image point, with a global confidence per vertebra. Stage 2, which the paper itself calls 'Hidden Markov Model': a joint prior of per-vertebra appearance (mean and variance template sub-volumes of 11x11x5 cm, Gaussian likelihood) and spinal shape (a Gaussian on the inter-centroid offset divided by a global scale s), energy E(C;s) with lambda 0.1; 'Given a value for s and multiple sampled location candidates MAP inference can be achieved via dynamic programming. Several thousand candidate locations are sampled from the vicinity of the forest prediction using a normal distribution N(ci, sigma^2) with sigma = 30mm', and s is searched over 7 values in [0.85, 1.15]. Data: 200 CT scans, 'mostly trauma patients' (poster), 2,595 vertebrae, fields of view from 4 vertebrae to whole body, slice spacing 0.5 to 6.5 mm, two-fold cross-validation. Results: median 5.31 mm after the HMM (15.91 mm from the forest alone), mean 9.50 mm, std 10.55; identification 81 percent (2,089 of 2,595; cervical 72, thoracic 78, lumbar 85), cervical median about 7 mm; identification means within 20 mm with the closest centroid the correct one.
- Where the transcript errs: (1) The paper's own words are 'regression forests and probabilistic graphical models' and 'an HMM with hidden states for each vertebrae position'; it never says Markov random field. An HMM is a chain-structured graphical model, so 'discrete generative MRF' is loose rather than false, and 'discrete' holds only because the states are sampled candidate positions. (2) There is no 'voxel grid search': inference is dynamic programming (the Viterbi MAP) over several thousand Gaussian-sampled candidates around the forest prediction (sigma 30 mm) plus a 7-value search over the global scale. (3) 'Median < 6 mm' is right (abstract: 'overall median localization error of less than 6mm'; table 5.31 mm) but it is the HMM stage's median over all vertebrae, with a 9.5 mm mean and only 81 percent identification. (4) The forest output is a set of centroid votes with a confidence per vertebra, not a heatmap.
- What bears on the plan: This is the ancestor of every chain-of-centroids prior: a Gaussian on adjacent offsets scaled by one global size variable, which is the same device as schwingcheck's shared patient size s, plus a per-level appearance template. It has no shape manifold over intra-vertebra landmarks (26 centroids only) and a fixed 26-state alphabet with no L6 or T13, so it is a baseline for the chain, not a precedent for the L-PDM arm; its 20 mm identification convention is what the plan's naming metric should keep.
- Searched: grep of SN prior-art.md and prior-art-records.tsv (already resolved there); Crossref record for DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-33454-2_73; WebSearch 'Glocker 2012 vertebrae localization ... median'; MGH publication listing (abstract sentences); the poster PDF extracted with pdftotext; the full paper PDF from doc.ic.ac.uk extracted with pdftotext and read at the dataset, model, inference and results sections.

### Glocker et al. 2013, the follow-up on pathological spines

- Claim: The 2013 follow-up by Glocker on pathological spines.
- Status: real
- Record: Glocker B, Zikic D, Konukoglu E, Haynor DR, Criminisi A. Vertebrae Localization in Pathological Spine CT via Dense Classification from Sparse Annotations. MICCAI 2013, LNCS 8150, pp. 262-270. DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-40763-5_33. Open full text: http://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~bglocker/pdfs/glocker2013miccai.pdf. Mentioned inside SN prior-art.md labelling entry 6.
- What it does: Replaces the 2012 pipeline with classification forests (20 trees, depth 24) trained on dense probabilistic labels made from sparse centroid annotations, with no explicit parametric appearance or shape model. Evaluated two-fold on 224 pathological spine CTs (high-grade scoliosis, kyphosis, surgical implants) and on 200 mostly abdominal 'normal' CTs, against the 2012 RF+HMM retrained on the same data. Table 1, pathological spine CT: this method median 8.8 mm, mean 12.4 mm, std 11.2, identification 70 percent (cervical 80, thoracic 62, lumbar 75); RF+HMM median 14.8 mm, mean 20.9 mm, identification 51 percent. Normal CT: this method 7.6 mm median, 11.5 mm mean, 76 percent; RF+HMM 5.4 mm, 9.7 mm, 80 percent. About 1 minute per scan.
- Where the transcript errs: None in what little the transcript says; it omits that the 2013 paper drops the HMM and shape prior and shows the parametric prior degrading on pathology (51 percent) while helping slightly on normal anatomy (80 versus 76 percent). The SN record's '74.0 percent with 13.2 mm' is Liao 2018's figure for it on the public 302-scan split, a different dataset; the paper's own numbers are 70 percent and 12.4 mm on its 224 pathological scans.
- What bears on the plan: Direct evidence that a restrictive shape-and-appearance prior helps on normal spines and hurts on abnormal ones, which is the risk of a tight latent shape prior; the plan's comparison of the L-PDM and hybrid arms against the explainable rules should be stratified by anomaly and pathology, and report both strata, for this reason.
- Searched: Crossref record for DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-40763-5_33; JHU MICCAI 2013 proceedings page; WebSearch for its results; the full paper PDF from doc.ic.ac.uk extracted with pdftotext, abstract and Table 1 read.

### Zheng et al. as a co-pioneer of the chain prior (not supported)

- Claim: 'Zheng et al.' as a co-pioneer of the same regression-forest-plus-HMM strategy (which Zheng, which paper?).
- Status: uncertain
- Record: No first-authored 'Zheng et al.' paper on regression forests with an HMM for vertebra localisation was found. Best candidate: Chu C, Belavy DL, Armbrecht G, Bansmann M, Felsenberg D, Zheng G. Fully Automatic Localization and Segmentation of 3D Vertebral Bodies from CT/MR Images via a Learning-Based Method. PLOS ONE 10(11):e0143327, 2015, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0143327 (Guoyan Zheng, Bern, senior author). Alternative: Kelm BM, Wels M, Zhou SK, Seifert S, Suehling M, Zheng Y, Comaniciu D. Spine detection in CT and MR using iterated marginal space learning. Medical Image Analysis 17(8):1283-1292, 2013, DOI 10.1016/j.media.2012.09.007 (Yefeng Zheng, Siemens). A third Zheng, Kang Zheng (PAII), co-authors Wang 2021 CVPR and Tao 2021 MICCAI, both already in the SN record (TSV rows 8, 58, 60) and neither a forest-plus-HMM method.
- What it does: Chu/Zheng 2015 trains one random-forest regressor per predefined vertebral body, aggregates patch votes into a response volume, regularises the chain of centres as an HMM (Gaussian offsets between adjacent centres as transitions, response volumes as observations) solved by dynamic programming, then segments by a forest voxel classifier; 23 T2 MR scans (T11 to L5) and 10 SpineWeb CTs (L1 to L5), leave-one-out, localisation 1.6 and 1.9 mm. Kelm/Zheng 2013 detects vertebrae and discs by iterated marginal space learning with a global probabilistic prior model of the column, on 42 MR and 30 CT volumes.
- Where the transcript errs: The strategy the transcript describes (forest votes refined by a chain model with dynamic programming) is Glocker's 2012 contribution; the Chu/Zheng paper adopts it three years later at fixed, pre-named lumbar levels, with no identification and no arbitrary field of view, and would be cited as Chu et al. Calling 'Zheng et al.' a co-pioneer is not supported by any record found.
- What bears on the plan: Nothing beyond Glocker's chain prior; if the plan wants a second forest-plus-HMM instance it is Chu 2015 at fixed levels, and if it wants a global column prior over local detections it is Kelm 2013. Neither carries a landmark shape manifold or interpretable measurements.
- Searched: WebSearch standard 'Zheng vertebra localization identification CT random forest Markov random field 2012 2013 arbitrary field of view'; WebSearch extended '"Zheng" first author vertebra localization "random forest" "hidden Markov" ... MICCAI'; WebSearch for Kelm/Zheng 2013 iterated marginal space learning; the Chu 2015 PMC full text (PMC4658120); SN prior-art-records.tsv rows naming Zheng.

### Hanaoka et al. 2017, numerical anomalies by competing count hypotheses

- Claim: Hanaoka et al. 2017 targeted detecting numerical spinal anomalies (4 or 6 lumbar vertebrae) by evaluating competing structural hypotheses, with a multi-stage Landmark Point Distribution Model (L-PDM), five hand-crafted discrete hypotheses (13-thoracic, 4-lumbar, ...) checked by posterior, on full neck-to-pelvis CT.
- Status: real
- Record: Hanaoka S, Nakano Y, Nemoto M, Nomura Y, Takenaga T, Miki S, Yoshikawa T, Hayashi N, Masutani Y, Shimizu A. Automatic detection of vertebral number abnormalities in body CT images. International Journal of Computer Assisted Radiology and Surgery 12(5):719-732, May 2017 (online 6 January 2017). DOI 10.1007/s11548-016-1516-y; PMID 28063076. Not in the SN record.
- What it does: From the abstract (Europe PMC, PubMed): the group's previously reported landmark detection system is reused; its L-PDM is 'a statistical probabilistic model of all landmark positions in the human body, including five landmarks for each vertebra'. Given a new volume the algorithm applies five hypotheses (normal, 11 or 13 thoracic vertebrae, 4 or 6 lumbar vertebrae), attempts to detect all landmarks under each, and selects 'the most plausible hypothesis with the largest posterior likelihood'. On 300 neck-to-pelvis CT datasets: normal subjects 211 of 217 (97.2 percent) determined normal; subjects with 23 or 25 vertebrae and no transitional vertebra 9 of 10 (90 percent); subjects with a transitional vertebra 71 of 73 (97.3 percent) 'judged as partially successfully determined'. The full text is paywalled (Springer login), so how the L-PDM is parametrised and what 'partially' means could not be read.
- Where the transcript errs: Substantially accurate: a 2017 paper exists, five discrete count hypotheses, selection by posterior, neck-to-pelvis CT. Small slips: 'multi-stage L-PDM' conflates the one statistical model with the multi-stage detection framework it sits in (independent detectors, then combinatorial optimisation with the L-PDM as prior, Medical Image Analysis 35, 2017, listed below); the transcript's author list mixes in Ohtomo, who is on the MIA paper but not this one; and it leaves out that 73 of 300 subjects (24 percent) had a transitional vertebra and were only 'partially' resolved, which is the hardest stratum.
- What bears on the plan: The clearest precedent for the transcript's own proposal: a population distribution over landmark coordinates (the latent-shape family) scored under competing count hypotheses, which is what schwingcheck's exact chain does over named Panjabi measurements instead of coordinates. The evaluation it reports is per-subject count accuracy by stratum (normal, 23 or 25 without TV, with TV); the plan should report the same strata alongside level naming, and its 'partial' transitional outcome marks where latent coordinates and explainable measurements are most likely to differ.
- Searched: WebSearch extended 'Hanaoka "point distribution model" vertebrae numerical anomaly ...'; PubMed 28063076 (abstract); Springer article page (redirected to login); springermedicine.com page (abstract only); Europe PMC REST record (full abstract); Crossref record; Semantic Scholar record (no abstract); Hanaoka's TUAT thesis PDF (503, unreachable).

### Bayat et al. 2022, standing spine posture from two radiographs, called a statistical shape model

- Claim: 'Anatomy-Aware Inference of the 3D Standing Spine Posture from 2D Radiographs' (Feb 2022), described as a statistical shape model of the spine by region.
- Status: real but misdescribed
- Record: Bayat A, Pace DF, Sekuboyina A, Payer C, Stern D, Urschler M, Kirschke JS, Menze BH. Anatomy-Aware Inference of the 3D Standing Spine Posture from 2D Radiographs. Tomography 8(1):479-496, 11 February 2022. DOI 10.3390/tomography8010039; PMID 35202204; PMC8879677. Not in the SN record.
- What it does: TransVert+ takes orthogonal sagittal and coronal radiographs (64x64 patches around each vertebra centroid with a 1 mm disc marking the vertebra) plus the 3D centroid coordinates; two encoders with squeeze-and-excitation fusion feed an affine decoder (per-vertebra scale and rotations, fused across the batch, which is assumed to be one spine in order) and a fully convolutional deformable decoder (a per-voxel vector field); the summed fields warp an atlas vertebra template: 'Instead of regressing the shapes directly, our model deforms an atlas to the desired vertebral shape', and 'We also define the spine curvature by atlas vertebral centroids, for enforcing the shape of the spine'. Trained with an L1 and smoothness loss on DRRs from 800 LIDC-IDRI lung CTs and 154 in-house spine CTs (about 12,000 vertebrae), five-fold: DRR Dice 0.951, Hausdorff 5.70 mm; on clinical standing radiographs 30 patients qualitatively and 2 patients with prone CT (32 vertebrae) quantitatively by a rotation-invariant distance (nWESD 0.13 versus 0.15, not significant). Outputs are per-vertebra voxel shapes stacked at their centroids.
- Where the transcript errs: The date is right. 'A statistical shape model of the spine by region' is wrong on both counts: no PCA or statistical shape model is built (PCA appears only in the related work); the prior is an atlas template deformed by an affine plus a free-form field, and the spine prior is the atlas centroid curve; nothing is per region, the paper only noting weaker lumbar results from scarce lumbar training data. It produces shapes, not landmarks or coefficients.
- What bears on the plan: A template-deformation prior gives no low-dimensional coefficient to read measurements from, so it is not a precedent for the L-PDM or hybrid arms and should not be counted as one; its DRR-to-radiograph training with a prone-CT check is the pattern the plan's view=2d block (FluXray synthetics paired with CT) will need, and its two-patient quantitative validation shows how thin real standing truth is.
- Searched: WebSearch of the exact title; Crossref record for DOI 10.3390/tomography8010039; PMC8879677 full text read for the method, data and results.

### Lessmann et al. 2019, iterative fully convolutional vertebra segmentation and identification

- Claim: 'Iterative fully convolutional neural networks for automatic vertebra segmentation and identification' (arXiv, Feb 2019).
- Status: real
- Record: Lessmann N, van Ginneken B, de Jong PA, Isgum I. Iterative fully convolutional neural networks for automatic vertebra segmentation and identification. Medical Image Analysis 53:142-155, April 2019. DOI 10.1016/j.media.2019.02.005; arXiv:1804.04383 (v1 12 April 2018, v2 22 November 2018, v3 11 February 2019). Already resolved and its printed figures checked in SN prior-art.md (CT pre-processing entry 4 and vertebra labelling entry 11; prior-art-records.tsv rows 5 and 57).
- What it does: One FCN segments and labels one vertebra at a time in a 128^3 patch at 1 mm, with an instance memory of already segmented vertebrae as a second channel and a completeness output that excludes partially visible vertebrae; the patch walks along the column; each vertebra receives a regressed label and the best consecutive labelling of the detected sequence is kept by maximum likelihood, so labels have no gaps or duplicates whatever the field of view. Five CT and MR datasets; 94.9 percent Dice, 93 percent of scans correctly labelled.
- Where the transcript errs: 'arXiv, Feb 2019' matches only the third arXiv version; the work was first posted April 2018 and is a Medical Image Analysis journal paper. It carries no shape prior and no point distribution model; listing it beside shape-prior references is a category slip.
- What bears on the plan: A counting baseline: naming by maximum likelihood over an offset of consecutive labels, with no latent shape and no variant states (the SN record notes the VerSe finding that an L6 shifts its whole scan). It belongs in the plan only as the no-prior comparator for level naming.
- Searched: grep of SN prior-art.md and prior-art-records.tsv (resolved there); Crossref record for DOI 10.1016/j.media.2019.02.005; arXiv abstract page 1804.04383 for the submission history.

### Chu et al. 2015, localization and segmentation of vertebral bodies with a learned column prior

- Claim: 'Fully Automatic Localization and Segmentation of 3D Vertebral Bodies from CT/MR Images via a Learning-Based Method' (PLOS ONE, Nov 2015).
- Status: real
- Record: Chu C, Belavy DL, Armbrecht G, Bansmann M, Felsenberg D, Zheng G. Fully Automatic Localization and Segmentation of 3D Vertebral Bodies from CT/MR Images via a Learning-Based Method. PLOS ONE 10(11):e0143327, 23 November 2015. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0143327; PMC4658120. Not in the SN record.
- What it does: A random-forest regressor per vertebral body aggregates patch votes into a response volume, coarse to fine; the chain of body centres is regularised as a hidden Markov model whose transitions are Gaussian models of the offsets between adjacent centres learnt from training data and whose observations are the response volumes, solved by dynamic programming; segmentation then uses a random-forest voxel classifier with a Parzen spatial prior thresholded at 0.5. Data: 23 T2-weighted MR scans (T11 to L5, 161 bodies) and 10 public SpineWeb CTs (L1 to L5, 50 bodies), leave-one-out: localisation 1.6 +/- 0.9 mm (MR) and 1.9 +/- 1.5 mm (CT), Dice 88.7 and 91.0 percent. Each regressor is trained for a named level, so the paper does not identify vertebrae or handle an arbitrary field of view, and its CT arm is 10 scans.
- Where the transcript errs: Title, venue and month are right. It is not an identification method and its CT evidence is 10 lumbar scans; if this is the transcript's 'Zheng et al.', it is a later use of Glocker's chain at fixed levels, not a co-pioneer.
- What bears on the plan: A chain prior over centroids with hand-set Gaussian offsets and nothing on shape or measurements; too small and too fixed in level to serve as a baseline for the plan.
- Searched: WebSearch of the exact title; Crossref record for DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0143327; PMC4658120 full text read for the method, data and results.

### Hanaoka et al. 2017 (Medical Image Analysis), the landmark point-distribution model the anomaly paper reuses

- Claim: Extra: the L-PDM framework paper that the Hanaoka 2017 anomaly paper reuses (not cited by the transcript, but its 'multi-stage' description belongs here).
- Status: real
- Record: Hanaoka S, Shimizu A, Nemoto M, Nomura Y, Miki S, Yoshikawa T, Hayashi N, Ohtomo K, Masutani Y. Automatic detection of over 100 anatomical landmarks in medical CT images: A framework with independent detectors and combinatorial optimization. Medical Image Analysis 35:192-214, January 2017. DOI 10.1016/j.media.2016.04.001. Not in the SN record.
- What it does: From the abstract (Europe PMC): detects 197 anatomically defined landmarks in CT; each landmark detector proposes candidates, and the best combination is searched by combinatorial optimisation using a landmark point distribution model (L-PDM) as prior knowledge, by simulated annealing and iterative Gibbs sampling; before each Gibbs cycle a second sampling estimates each landmark's spatial distribution so landmarks with no correct candidate, or outside the imaging range, are still placed. On 104 CT volumes of various ranges: average error 6.6 mm; 83.8, 93.2 and 96.5 percent within 10, 15 and 20 mm; vertebral centroid identification 70 percent on public datasets including severely diseased spines. The full text is paywalled (ScienceDirect 403), so whether the L-PDM is PCA-reduced could not be read.
- Where the transcript errs: The transcript's 'multi-stage L-PDM' credited to the anomaly paper describes this framework: the stages are detection, then combinatorial optimisation under the L-PDM; the anomaly paper adds the five hypotheses on top.
- What bears on the plan: This L-PDM is a statistical prior over landmark positions used inside a sampler over detector candidates, not a network predicting latent coefficients with a Mahalanobis penalty, so the transcript's arm (2) is a different use of the same kind of model; its 70 percent vertebra identification on public data is the figure a PDM-based namer has reached, below the chain-prior labellers in the SN record.
- Searched: WebSearch for the title; Crossref bibliographic query and DOI record; Europe PMC REST record (abstract); Semantic Scholar (no abstract); ScienceDirect article page (403).

## Anatomical constraints as losses, augmentation and heatmap regularisation

### Yang et al. 2024, anatomical priors as a loss for vertebral landmark detection (the transcript's Li et al.)

- Claim: A 2024 paper the transcript calls "Anatomical Prior-Based Vertebral Landmark Detection", attributed to "Li et al. in Artificial Intelligence in Medicine", said to put distance-based and spatial-order constraints into the training loss of a soft-argmax landmark network as one-sided margin penalties (L_dist = max(0, d_min - D)^2 + max(0, D - d_max)^2 and an order term max(0, z_i - z_{i+1} + eps)).
- Status: real but misdescribed
- Record: Yukang Yang, Yu Wang, Tianyu Liu, Miao Wang, Ming Sun, Shiji Song, Wenhui Fan, Gao Huang. Anatomical prior-based vertebral landmark detection for spinal disorder diagnosis. Artificial Intelligence in Medicine 159:103011; issue January 2025, online 12 November 2024. DOI 10.1016/j.artmed.2024.103011; PMID 39612522; DBLP journals/artmed/YangWLWSSFH25; Semantic Scholar CorpusId 274015343. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0933365724002537
- What it does: From the abstract (Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC and PubMed records): most existing machine-learning methods for automatic vertebral landmark detection suffer from overlapping landmarks or abnormally long distances between nearby landmarks, so the authors formulate anatomical priors on the distances among vertebrae and their spatial order within the spine and integrate these geometric constraints in three places: an anatomy-constraint training loss, an anatomy-aided sequential inference procedure (sequential rather than parallel prediction), and new anatomy-related plausibility metrics for evaluation. On 1,410 anterior-posterior radiographs it improves landmark localization accuracy and gives Cobb angle estimates comparable to the baselines, with ablations showing lower localization error and more anatomically plausible predictions. It also transfers to sagittal CT slices and improves vertebra-level compression fracture classification. The full text is paywalled (ScienceDirect and the OUCI mirror answered 403 through the proxy; Semantic Scholar lists no open-access PDF), so the exact form of the loss and the readout could not be read.
- Where the transcript errs: The authors are Yang et al. (Tsinghua), not Li et al.; the journal is right. 2024 is the online date, the issue is January 2025. The one-sided margin formulas (d_min, d_max, the order hinge with eps) and the soft-argmax readout are not in the abstract and could not be verified from the paywalled text; treat them as the transcript's reconstruction, not the paper's. The data are 2-D AP radiographs (plus sagittal CT slices as a transfer test), not 3-D CT volumes. The transcript omits that the priors also enter inference (sequential prediction) and the evaluation metrics, which is the larger part of the paper.
- What bears on the plan: This is the nearest published cousin of the explainable-rule arm: hand-written distance and order constraints used as loss, as inference and as plausibility metrics, on 2-D. Its anatomy-related plausibility metrics are exactly the human-understandable features the plan wants beside the latent prior, so the plan should name such metrics for 3-D (inter-level distance within a population range, monotone z order) and report them for every arm. The hinge formulas cannot be cited from this paper without the full text; the owner's library access could fetch it.
- Searched: WebSearch: exact title with Li and AIM 2024 (no match); "Artificial Intelligence in Medicine" vertebra landmark anatomical prior loss 2024; spine landmark loss inter-vertebral distance ordering penalty hinge 2023-2024; extended search Li et al. vertebral landmark anatomical prior distance order soft-argmax (found the Yang et al. record); Yukang Yang Gao Huang anatomy-constraint loss (no open copy); extended search for the loss formula (none). WebFetch: ScienceDirect abs page (403), ouci.dntb.gov.ua (403), api.semanticscholar.org record (metadata, tldr, citing papers), Europe PMC REST record (abstract), pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov search (record). The prior-art note (SN/docs/research/autoanatomist-search/prior-art.md, prior-art-records.tsv) does not hold this paper; its nearest entries are Wang 2021 CVPR (spine rectification, soft distance and hard order constraints at inference, CT pre-processing entry 7 and chain prior entry 12).

### Wu et al. 2021, level-wise articulated augmentation (the transcript's VerSe augmentation scheme)

- Claim: "A vertebral level-wise data augmentation scheme" and work from the VerSe challenge that applies intervertebral translations and rotations within clinical ranges of motion to augment data.
- Status: real but misdescribed

### DeepSSM (Bhalodia, Elhabian, Kavan, Whitaker et al.), 2018 and 2024

- Claim: DeepSSM (University of Utah; Bhalodia, Elhabian, Kavan, Whitaker et al.): an image-to-PDM latent space that regularizes the network by forcing outputs to live within the PCA Gaussian; the 2018 ShapeMI workshop paper and the 2024 Medical Image Analysis blueprint; and a spine application.
- Status: real but misdescribed
- Record: Bhalodia R, Elhabian SY, Kavan L, Whitaker RT. DeepSSM: A Deep Learning Framework for Statistical Shape Modeling from Raw Images. - What it does: 2018 (text read at ar5iv 1810.00111): a CNN of five convolution and two fully connected layers takes a 3D image and outputs an ordered set of PCA loadings of a ShapeWorks point distribution model (1024 correspondence points, 10 to 15 modes); the loss is the L2 norm between the true PCA loadings and the output; the PDM is used to turn 40 to 50 scans into 4000 to 5000 training images by sampling N(mu, Sigma) in PCA space, rejecting far samples and thin-plate-spline warping the nearest original image; the objective holds no Gaussian or Mahalanobis term on the loadings, Mahalanobis distance appearing only afterwards as a severity score for metopic craniosynostosis; the datasets are cranial CT, femur CT (cam FAI) and left atrium MRI, and the paper states that DeepSSM is not an approach to discriminate normal from pathological morphology, the cam lesion being reconstructed poorly because the normative PDM has no such mode. 2024 (ar5iv 2110.07152; Crossref gives MedIA 91:103034): Base-DeepSSM predicts PCA scores and a final linear layer whose weight is the PCA basis and bias the mean shape, kept fixed during training, reconstructs the correspondence points; the loss is L2 on the points, the PCA-score loss of the earlier version being dropped because it under-weights subtle modes; TL-DeepSSM replaces the PCA latent by an autoencoder code; augmentation is a kernel density estimate over PCA scores (5,000 samples per dataset); the only regularisation is weight decay 5e-5 and early stopping; downstream tasks are a Mahalanobis severity score for metopic skulls (AUC 0.946 base, 0.98 TL, 0.932 ShapeWorks) and atrial fibrillation recurrence from the latent (59.5% base, 71.4% TL, 61.9% ShapeWorks); inference 0.56 s against 30 to 65 min for ShapeWorks.
- Where the transcript errs: Forcing outputs to live within that Gaussian is not what DeepSSM does: the PCA prior enters as a fixed linear decoder, which confines the output to the PCA subspace with no penalty on the coefficients, and as the sampler for augmentation; neither paper's loss carries an N(0, I) or Mahalanobis term on the loadings, and the loss itself moved from loadings (2018) to points (2024) because the loadings loss was found worse. - What bears on the plan: The transcript's hybrid (network predicts b, a fixed linear map gives the landmarks, loss on the landmarks) is Base-DeepSSM 2024 minus its augmentation, so the plan can name it as the baseline form and note that DeepSSM itself found a loss on points better than a loss on loadings. A Mahalanobis penalty on b is a separate choice whose only precedents found are VIB-DeepSSM's KL to a unit Gaussian (extra entry) and Ansart 2022's L2-regularised spine SSM fit (claim 3), so it belongs in an ablation arm, not in the baseline. DeepSSM's downstream pattern, the latent fed to a Mahalanobis score or a classifier and compared with the classical PDM's latent, is the pattern the plan needs, and its femur failure (a lesion the normative PDM never saw is reconstructed badly) is the warning for L6 and sacralization.

### Uncertain-DeepSSM 2020 and VIB-DeepSSM 2022, the DeepSSM variants with a Gaussian on the latent

- Claim: Extra entry: the DeepSSM variants that do put a Gaussian on the latent, Uncertain-DeepSSM 2020 and VIB-DeepSSM 2022, which is what the transcript's regularize-by-the-Gaussian sentence most resembles.
- Status: real
- Record: Adams J, Bhalodia R, Elhabian S. Uncertain-DeepSSM: From Images to Probabilistic Shape Models. ShapeMI 2020 (MICCAI workshop); arXiv:2007.06516. Adams J, Elhabian S. From Images to Probabilistic Anatomical Shapes: A Deep Variational Bottleneck Approach. MICCAI 2022; arXiv:2205.06862.
- What it does: Uncertain-DeepSSM (arXiv abstract) keeps DeepSSM's PCA-supervised latent and fixed PCA decoder but makes the encoder stochastic, trained by a negative log-likelihood on the PCA scores, with Monte Carlo dropout for epistemic uncertainty. VIB-DeepSSM (text read at ar5iv 2205.06862) drops the PCA supervision and the fixed linear decoder: a CNN encodes the image to the mean and variance of a Gaussian over a latent z whose dimension is set by the 95 percent PCA rule, the prior is a fixed spherical unit Gaussian, a KL term with weight beta = 0.01 pulls each encoded distribution toward it, an MLP decodes z to the correspondence points, and the predictive distribution over points gives aleatoric uncertainty; on 1,001 left-atrium LGE MRI scans (739 train, 92 validation, 92 inlier and 78 outlier test) it reports better accuracy and better-calibrated uncertainty than PCA-DeepSSM and PPCA-DeepSSM, especially with limited training data.
- Where the transcript errs: The transcript attributes the Gaussian-latent regulariser to DeepSSM; it belongs to this later line, where the latent is learnt, not PCA, and the penalty is a KL on the encoder's distribution, not a Mahalanobis penalty on predicted PCA coefficients.
- What bears on the plan: This is the published form of the transcript's prior b ~ N(0, I) and the one place where it was measured against the fixed-PCA form: the KL-regularised learnt latent beat PCA-DeepSSM on points error, which is evidence against the transcript's assumption that the PCA basis should be fixed and penalised. The plan should test both b spaces (PCA with a Mahalanobis penalty, and a learnt latent with a KL), and VIB-DeepSSM's per-point aleatoric variance is the counterpart of the auto-anatomist's delta-method spread, so calibration can be compared on the same footing.
