Thread "Extract morphometrics from gold tier 3 CT" of the claude.ai project symphony_4, 29 October 2026, from the owner's message of 02:12 UTC that day: start a survey of the morphometrics that can be taken programmatically from the gold tier 3 data, using patented validated approaches and those of the clinical reread (Eisenstein's posterior limit of the canal among them), implement and test them on the masked CTs, and look for test cases with ground-truth measurements, open datasets and code to compare with and reproduce. Confidential, like everything in bordercall: nothing here goes to a public place. No paper's text is quoted; the papers' methods are described in our own words and their values come from the paper tables (series.csv, below). No mask, CT or per-study row is in git: the rows stay in the project's files, and only aggregates are written here.

## What was built

The package [morphometry/](../../morphometry/) measures a labelled CT: every vertebra, every disc, the column and the pelvis, one row per measure with its value or the reason it has none. It builds on [scanimport/](../../scanimport/) (the label schemes, the frames and the canal's slabs) and adds nothing outside Python's standard library, NumPy, SciPy and nibabel.

```
python -m morphometry measure --labels 0004.nii.gz --scheme enhance --study 0004 --out rows.csv
python -m morphometry run --masks DIR --scheme enhance --out DIR --workers 3 --max-slice 1.25 [--lines]
python -m morphometry compare --rows DIR/rows.csv --series series.csv --out DIR/compare.csv --max-slice 1.25
python -m morphometry phantom
python -m morphometry thicken --labels 0004.nii.gz --scheme enhance --study 0004 --to 2.5 5 --out thick.csv
python -m morphometry bench --slice 2.5
python -m maskedge measure --ct CT.nii.gz --labels 0004.nii.gz --scheme enhance --study 0004 --out edge.csv
```

`run` measures every mask in a folder whose coarsest spacing is at most `--max-slice`, one CSV per study, so a run cut short resumes; each CSV carries a stamp of the measuring code, and a study is measured again when the code has changed since. With `--lines`, `run` also writes, beside each study's rows, the lines [morphoview/lines.py](../../morphoview/lines.py) draws and each measured vertebra's frame (lines/ID.csv and frames/ID.csv, joined as lines.csv and frames.csv), built on the vertebrae the rows were read from and checked against the values written. A vertebra's lines are those of the 25 keys morphoview.lines.VERTEBRA_KEYS names, Panjabi's seven chords (scanimport.SEVEN) among them, and a disc's those of its three heights (DISC_KEYS); the other keys, 56 of the 81 a lumbar vertebra's rows hold, have no line and leave no line row. A line row gives the line's rule and panel, the value its construction gives beside the row's, its drawn length, whether it is drawn, withheld or not measured, and its two ends in the world's millimetres of the mask's affine, each named (a_end and b_end, from morphoview.lines.ENDS: left or right, posterior or anterior, inferior or superior, medial or lateral, the canal or the tip) as it lies in the frame of the vertebra the line is drawn in, a disc's line in that of the vertebra below the disc. A frame row gives the vertebra's origin, the centroid of its label's main piece, and its three axes, x to the patient's right, y from the canal to the body and z up; which axis its z lies along (axis: the end-plates' bisector, one end-plate's normal, or the column's line kept); and the column's line z was turned from (cx, cy, cz). Ends, values and frames are written whole, so a reader can hold a length to its ends and a frame to its orthonormality to the bit; measured is the row's value, to the rows' four places. This is the export the latent shape prior's evaluation reads (PR 45, stage 1), less two frames its stage 1 compares, the oracle's and the one found without the mask, which need the CT and a step of their own. A run with the lines has a stamp of its own, over morphoview/lines.py too: a study whose rows carry another stamp, or whose lines or frames file is missing, is measured again, and the joins take only the studies whose rows carry the run's stamp, each join written whole or not at all. A drawing that fails is flagged (flag:lines, naming its builder) and loses only its own lines. On three phantom columns on one worker the drawing adds 9 per cent to the measuring of L1 to L5, 13 to that of C2 to C7 and 13 to that of a column from C2 to L5. `compare` sets our values beside every row of the paper tables. `phantom`, `thicken` and `bench` are the checks below.

Each vertebra is read in its own frame: z starts on the column's line through the centroids of its whole neighbours and is turned once onto the vertebra's own axis, the bisector of its two end-plates' planes, each plate the body's face toward the neighbouring vertebra (the section on the axis, below); y points to the body, and the canal is the hole the slabs across the vertebra find. The body is the bone in front of the canal's anterior wall, cut along that wall's line as it leans through the canal's full rings, and in the neck clipped at the transverse foramina. Every module's docstring gives its definitions and the failures that shaped them; this note does not repeat them.

## The measures

Per vertebra, 81 keys:

- Body (body.py): anterior, middle and posterior heights read from corner to corner 2.5 mm inside each end-plate's own corners, and Panjabi's posterior height at the wall itself (h_ant, h_mid, h_post_inset, h_post); Genant's ratios (body_index_ant_post, body_index_mid_post); Panjabi's end-plate widths and depths (endplate, endplate_depth, endplate_lower, endplate_lower_depth); the waist's width and depth (body_w_mid, body_d_mid, Eisenstein's mid-waist width and body depth); each end-plate's area, concavity and inclination to the plane square to the posterior wall; the wedge angle; the body's and the vertebra's volumes.
- Canal (canal.py): the least midline depth and where it lies (canal_ap_min, canal_ap_min_share), the depth at the pedicles (canal_ap), the lateral depth (canal_ap_lateral), Eisenstein's lateral-film construction at L1 to L4 (canal_ap_lateral_film), the interpedicular distance at its least and its greatest (interpedicular, interpedicular_max), the canal's width at the pedicles (canal_w), its least and middle areas (canal_area_min, canal_area), and the Torg, Jones and trefoil indices.
- Pedicles (pedicle.py): width, height and area at the isthmus, the transverse and sagittal angles, and the chord a screw would take through bone (Zindrick's), each side and their mean.
- Posterior elements (posterior.py): transverse processes' span, length and height (Panjabi's TPW; Castellvi's 19 mm), the spinous process's length and angle, the laminae's thickness and angle, the facets' axial angles, tropism and the interfacet widths, and the articular processes' apex heights.
- The vertebra's axis: how far its own axis lies from the column's line through its neighbours (axis_offset), how far the axis read again in the turned frame lies from that frame's z (axis_residual), and how far the bisector of the end-plates as the frame's cells read them lies from z (plate_offset).

Per disc, 12 keys (column.py): the disc heights in front, in the middle and behind and their mean; the segmental angles sagittal, coronal and axial between the two end-plates drawn from rim to rim; the slip by Meyerding's corners and as a share of the end-plate's depth; the foramen's height on each side.

Per pelvis, 13 keys (pelvis.py): pelvic incidence, sacral slope and pelvic tilt as the patient lay, the hip axis's width, the acetabula's radii, the intercristal line's level, the transverse processes' gaps to the sacrum and the ilium, and which lumbar vertebra is lowest. Per column, 6 keys: lordosis L1 to S1, kyphosis T4 to T12, the cervical curve C2 to C7, and the coronal Cobb angle with its end vertebrae.

## How accurate it is where the truth is known

The phantoms of [morphometry/tests/phantom.py](../../morphometry/tests/phantom.py) draw lumbar vertebrae whose every measure is fixed by their parameters, on 0.8 by 0.8 by 1 mm voxels with the column tilted 18 degrees and the grid turned 7: a straight column, the same with one part changed at a time (a wedge, concave end-plates, a waist, angled pedicles, a steeper facet and spinous process), pedicles pitched down (PitchedDown), a superior articular process overhanging the pedicle's top (Overhang), pedicles with a lateral mass and its foramen beside them (LateralMass), a column whose joints open 8 degrees, tilt 4, turn 5 and slip 4 mm, a pelvis, thick slices, a posterior wall leaned 5 degrees on a grid 2 degrees off the vertebra's frame, clean and with specks of bone behind it (LeanedWall), a speck that leaves the label's parting on the solver's tolerance (SpeckOnTheMargin), a basivertebral notch below mid-height (BasivertebralNotch), a notch too deep to read past (DeepNotch), a wall the lattice leaves free on thick slices (FreeWall), a C2 with its dens on cervical-sized vertebrae (DensAtC2), and two masks a segmenter gets wrong (two labels swapped, a column cropped). The 39 tests require linear measures within 0.75 mm, angles within 1.5 degrees (3 for the spinous and facet angles), areas within 4 per cent, the slip within 1.5 mm and the posterior wall's slope within 0.5 degrees; all 39 pass (`python -m unittest morphometry.tests.test_morphometry`, 428 s on 30 October 2026 on four CPUs).

## What real masks broke, and what was changed

- The canal's wall line leaned 69 degrees at study 0004's T6, where holes in the body 20 mm in front of the canal counted as canal: the line is now fitted through the canal's full rings only and bounded at 30 degrees.
- Thoracic pedicles read 20 to 30 mm wide: the cut across them took in the rib's head. The pedicle is now the island beside the canal in coronal slabs from the wall back, its axis following the medial wall; 0004's widths now read 6.5 (T3), 6.0 (T7), 8.9 (T11), 10.0 (L1), 9.2 (L3) and 12.4 mm (L4).
- The pedicle's sagittal angle read 3 degrees on a level pedicle and 8 on one pitched 6, the raster's steps: it was the slope of one column's middle a row, and a layer of 1.24 mm over the isthmus rows' 7.5 mm is 9 degrees. Each of the pedicle's top and bottom surfaces is now parted from the scan's own voxel centres under the columns across the isthmus rows, bone from air, by the surface of widest margin (a linear program), and the climb is the mean of the two; over six phantoms on six grids on 1 mm slices every reading whose columns span 5.5 mm or more lies within 1.8 degrees of the truth, but for one of the overhang phantom's at 2.1 and those on a grid within a few degrees of the vertebra's frame, where the posterior wall's slope, as it was then read, stood 1.8 to 4.2 degrees off and the angle 2.5 to 3.4 high (the next item). ## The vertebra's own axis

On 29 October 2026 at 13:45 UTC the owner saw that the axial cut of study 0004's C5 did not run through the vertebra's principal axis, and asked that every measure that needs the axis take it into account. The frame's z was the column's line through the neighbours' centroids, which is the axis of the column at the vertebra and not of the vertebra: over the 228 studies of the run below, the bisector of the end-plates lay a median of 16.7 degrees from that line at C3, 13.3 at C4, 8.9 at C5, 5.4 at C6, 7.4 at C7, 2.4 to 4.6 from T2 to T12 and 18.1 at L5 (the key axis_offset of that run), so in the neck and at L5 the frame cut the body obliquely. Everything read along or across the frame's z depends on it: the heights and the end-plates' widths and depths, the plates' inclinations and the wedge, the waist, the canal's slabs and so its depths, widths and areas, the pedicles' isthmus and angles, the posterior elements' angles, the discs (read along the lower vertebra's z), and the renders' cuts; the volumes alone do not.

Five readings of the vertebra's own axis taken inside the frame were tried on 0004 and set aside, since each moved with the frame it was read in: with the body's voxels fixed once and the frame turned 10 and 20 degrees about x and about y, the bisector of the plates as the frame's cells read them moved by 4 to 19 degrees, a line through the body's layers' centroids by 17 to 47, one through the layers' posterior tenth by 21 to 78, the canal's axis through the slabs' holes stood on fewer than four full rings at many thoracic levels, and the body's least principal direction, which needs no frame, lay 9 to 87 degrees off the column's line and is no axis of a body as wide as it is tall. A reading that needs no frame is in anatomy.plate_face: each end-plate is the body's face toward the neighbouring vertebra, the body's voxels on the label's surface that lie within 15 mm of the neighbour's label and whose outward normal, the smoothed label's gradient, points within 45 degrees of the neighbour's nearest voxel; its plane is the direction of the face's least spread, refitted without the points more than two slices off it; the axis is the bisector of the two planes, or one plate's normal where only one faces a neighbour (flagged), and the column's line is kept, flagged, where no plate faces a neighbour, where the axis lies more than 25 degrees off the line or where the turned frame loses the canal. The frame is turned onto the axis once (anatomy.own_axis), the vertebra read again there, and the axis read again in the turned frame written as axis_residual.

On study 0004 the turn is 21.7 degrees at C3, 14.4 at C4, 17.0 at C5, 11.3 at C6, 5.9 at C7, 5.6 at T1, 0 to 4 through the thorax, 4 to 7 at L1 to L4 and 16.7 at L5, and the residual after the turn is at most 2.2 degrees at every level (0.0 to 0.6 from T1 to L1), so the reading holds still where every in-frame reading moved; the sagittal and axial cuts of C3, C5 and L5 then run square to the body. ## Carrying the lines onto FleXray's X-rays

The lines export gives each measure's two ends and each vertebra's frame in the world's millimetres of the mask's affine. `python -m morphoview project` carries them onto a synthetic X-ray FleXray renders from the same CT, through FleXray's own camera (morphoview/projection.py), and writes lines2d.csv (each end's pixel, its depth along the beam, whether the render sampled it, whether it lies in the slices the render was made of, its magnification and its place on FleXray's canvas, and the line's length on the render), frames2d.csv (each origin and the tips of its three axes drawn 10 mm long), column2d.csv (the centripetal spline through the origins in the levels' order) and VIEW.json (the view, its crop, the study, where each was read, the CT's grid, the FleXray that made the render, the edits of it where they are given, and the sha256 of the lines, the mask and the code). With the mask the lines were measured on, it measures how far each end and each midpoint lies from its level's shadow on the render, a disc's from its two vertebrae's.

```
python -m morphoview project --lines lines.csv --frames frames.csv --manifest manifest.json --file images/<stem>_v00.png --labels <mask> --scheme enhance --ct <ct> --out DIR
python -m morphoview project --lines lines.csv --frames frames.csv --realism RENDER --sample <sample_id> --ct <ct> --edits EDIT --filter FILTER --labels <mask> --scheme enhance --out DIR
python -m morphoview project --lines lines.csv --view view.json --out DIR
```

The first form reads the view from SchwingNet's render manifest (tools/flexray_render.py: the row's pose, isocentre and crop, the blocks' detector and ray samples). The second reads a render of SchwingNet's realism programme (tools/realism.py, render_one), whose records name no camera: the sample's row of the render folder's manifest.jsonl gives its pose, alpha, y and z, as rot_deg (alpha, 0, 0) and xyz_mm (0, y, z), the folder's recipe.json the detector, 1250 mm, 0.6 mm and 1024 pixels, and the CT given with `--ct` the isocentre, its volume centre, which the recipe must name, with no crop; a render the records do not bear out refuses. The third reads a file of the view's fields (morphoview.projection.View), which may carry the crop as `"crop": {"z_start", "z_stop", "pad_before", "pad_after"}`.

With `--edits`, the realism edit run's folder (tools/realism.py, run_edit: edits.jsonl and edited/<sample>__a<arm>.npy), VIEW.json lists each fluoroscopic edit of the render: its file, its arm and seed, whether it holds the render's pixels, as an edit at the render's size does, and how far it moved the render's picture, along a row and down the image (shift_px) and as a length on FleXray's canvas (shift_canvas_px). The shift is the peak of the two images' phase correlation, as the realism programme reads a pair's shift (tools/realism.py, paired_metrics), taken by its magnitude, since an edit may turn the contrast over, and refined between pixels by Foroosh, Zerubia and Berthod's ratio (IEEE Transactions on Image Processing 11:188, 2002), which reads a known shift, whole or fractional, near or far, contrast turned over or not, to 0.01 px; a picture of one grey has none. A line's pixel on the render is its pixel on the edit, moved by that shift. With `--filter`, the filter's folder (run_filter or run_keep: filter_kept.csv and filter_dropped.csv), it says whether FluXray's paired filter kept each edit. The summary says how many edits hold the render's pixels, the most one moved on FleXray's canvas and how many the filter kept.

A render shows one subject, so one study's lines go onto it: the one `--study` names, else the lines' only study, else the subject the manifest's row names; lines of several studies with none of these refuse, and VIEW.json says which rule chose.

SchwingNet's renders are made of a crop of the CT, 256 of its slices along the volume's last voxel axis, padded with air where the CT is shorter (fxr/datasets/ct_crops.py, CropSpec and crop_z; tools/render_blocks.py, cut), and the manifest's row records the crop. An end outside the crop is in the beam but on no bone of the render, so every end, origin and column point carries in_volume, whether its nearest voxel lies in the crop's slices: placed on the CT's grid, read from the header of the CT given with `--ct`, else from the labels', which must share the CT's grid; empty where neither is given, when the summary says it counted the lines on the render as far as the beam goes. The shadows are cut to the crop as FleXray cut the CT.

FleXray 1.0.2 renders through nanoDRR 0.1.5, and its camera is three pieces of arithmetic, which projection.py copies with the files they come from: the pose (the ZXY Euler rotation, the source at the rotated offset from the isocentre, a fixed turn for the orientation), the intrinsics (sdd over the pixel spacing, the principal point offset from the centre) and one ray a pixel from the source to the pixel's centre on the detector, row by row. Those releases are morphoview.projection.CHECKED; VIEW.json names the FleXray that made the render where its record says, and a render of another release is projected all the same, with a warning to run test_projection where that release is installed before trusting a pixel. The world is the CT's nibabel affine, which a crop keeps, so a mask read with its own affine from the same scan, as morphometry reads it, shares it. What holds the copy to FleXray, in tests/test_projection.py against fixtures/flexray.json, which tests/flexray_record.py recorded from FleXray itself on the straight L2 to L4 phantom at 0.8 by 0.8 by 1 mm in three views (a lateral cone beam, an oblique cone beam with its principal point off the centre and pixels taller than wide, and a parallel beam from behind) and a fourth, of a crop of the phantom's slices:

- FleXray's camera matrices and the rays it casts through 41 pixels, the corners among them, agree with ours to 5e-13 mm under all three of nanoDRR's orientations, and come in FleXray's order of pixels.
- 95 beads of 2.5 mm radius on the ends of the phantom's lines, rendered by FleXray at 2000 samples a ray: each bead's centroid on the render lies within 0.21 px of its projected centre (medians 0.05 to 0.08 px), where a camera slipped by half a pixel misses by 0.5. Fresh renders where FleXray is installed, from behind on a square detector of 256 pixels as SchwingNet's are, put all 95 within 0.11 px (median 0.04) through nanoDRR's AP turn half round, as SchwingNet poses its posterior views, and within 0.17 px (median 0.05) through its PA turn, which SchwingNet does not use.
- FleXray's labels come at the two thresholds a request names. At 0.0, at which its training profile renders them (fxr/models/camera/config.py, the threshold of seg_cfg), a label is the vertebra's support, where its channel is above zero, and the shadow of each vertebra, sampled here as nanoDRR samples a ray (500 points from the source to the detector, each the label of its nearest voxel), equals it pixel for pixel in all nine views and levels, and on the crop's render, where L4 lies wholly outside the crop, L3 across its edge and L2 within it. At 0.5, a bare request's default, at which SchwingNet's realism programme renders its truth masks (tools/realism.py, render_one), a label is the channel above 0.5, which a ray reaches only through 0.5 over the bone's attenuation, about 8.7 mm at 1000 HU, less a step: no pixel of those masks lies where our path length falls short of that, and in every view they drop more than a hundred pixels of the shadows, where the bone is thinner along the ray.
- Over a volume of one attenuation FleXray's channel for each vertebra is the attenuation times the step times the samples whose nearest voxel the vertebra holds, a whole number of steps to 1e-5, and the path length here over the step is that number at every pixel of all nine views and levels. nanoDRR reads the attenuation between voxels (grid_sample's bilinear mode) and the label at the nearest voxel, so where bone borders air FleXray's channel falls short of the attenuation times the path length near the bone's edge, by up to three steps at a pixel of the phantom, while its support is the same.
- Every drawn line's ends and midpoint land within one pixel of their level's shadow in all three views, with two exceptions. The inferior apex Eisenstein's lateral construction places at the midline between the facets, the second end of the line joining the facets' apexes, lands on the shadow in the lateral view, where a lateral film shows it on the facets, and lies 3 to 7 px off it in the other two; the construction's other places land on it in all three. Each disc height's midpoint lies 3 to 4 px into the disc space on the lateral render and within a pixel of the vertebrae's shadow in the other two. On the crop's render every end in the crop lands within one pixel of its level's shadow.
- Shapes drawn inside the phantom's vertebral bodies, from the frames the phantom placed the bodies in (an ellipse, an arc of 300 degrees, a closed spline, a patch's outline, a dome over a plane and a spherical cap through points), land wholly on FleXray's support of their body in all three views, every sample of a curve and every pixel of a surface's shadow, and each body's outline ellipse lies within a pixel of it.

The shadow is every voxel of the level in the mask the lines were measured on, as the scheme reads it, a SPINEPS vertebra with its end-plates, so it is FleXray's support of the level where the render's labels are the same. SchwingNet's renders of SPINEPS masks label each vertebra without its end-plates, 200 + X, which lie outside the VerSe alphabet its relabelling keeps (tools/render_blocks.py, label_spineps), so on those a level's shadow can reach past the render's support of its label by the end-plates. Since a mask at 0.5 drops bone thinner than about 9 mm along the ray, an end on a thin process can lie up to 20 px outside it while on its shadow; the landing is measured on the shadow, never the mask. FleXray's segmentation reads a render on the canvas its model's preprocessing.json names, at FleXray's defaults a square of 256 pixels, zero-padded to a square at its centre and resized, which `--canvas` changes; the mapping here matches its content box in eight image sizes and carries a spot's centre where FleXray's resize carries it, to 0.002 px.

The fluoroscopic look is SchwingNet's realism edit (the FluXray recipe, schwingnet.realism): it renders at 1024 pixels and edits at the same size, keeping the renderer's masks beside each image, so a line's pixel on the render is its pixel on the edited X-ray as far as the edit leaves the picture in place, and FluXray's stored copies at 256 pixels are FleXray's canvas, a quarter of the 1024 with no padding. SchwingNet's geometry bar (docs/research/flux-realism/PREREG.md, GEOMETRY_SHIFT_PX in src/schwingnet/realism.py, a median shift of at most 1 px at 256) holds its raw renders against FluXray's images, not an edit against its render; the edit's own shift is what `--edits` measures, file by file. How far an edit moves an edge it redraws inside the picture, which the shift of the whole picture does not say, is not measured; the edited image's FleXray segmentation against the render's shadow would measure it.

The shapes beside the straight lines are morphoview/shapes.py, each in the world's millimetres and each projected by the same camera: curves, open or closed, which project exactly, since a straight segment of the world is straight on the render, and are cut where they pass behind the source; arcs, circles, ellipses and centripetal splines sampled to 0.05 mm of the true curve; planes, fitted to points by least squares with their residual, with the angle between two, where a line meets one and the angle the render sees one at, and their patches, rectangles of the half sides asked for; flat polygons; surfaces, triangles of the world, through a grid of points, as a height above a plane over a patch's rectangle with the grid refined until the height lies within 0.05 mm of the triangles, through scattered points by Delaunay's rule on their plane, or as the least-squares quadric through points, a height of degree two over their plane, which a concave end-plate's dip below its rim is, each with its area, its rims, the edges only one triangle has, and its sag, the furthest of its points from its rim's plane; and volumes, whose projection is the length each ray runs through them, by a fine sampling that is the chord through their voxels or by FleXray's own, and their silhouettes. A flat triangle of the world is a triangle of the render, so a surface's shadow is the pixel centres inside its triangles' projections, and a surface reaching behind the source, which has no one picture on a cone beam's render, is refused. tests/test_shapes.py holds each to its geometry: the curves to their tolerance, a spline to the centripetal spans written again in Hermite form, a resampled curve to its corners and its step, a ring the source's plane cuts three times to its three pieces, a square's length and area on the render, a circle seen through parallel rays to the ellipse's area to 1e-4, a fitted plane to its points and an exact fit to its residual, a plane off the beam's axis to the angle of its own ray, a ball's path lengths to its chords, on rays 18 degrees off the axis too, and their sum to its volume to 0.005, both samplings to the same points either way along a ray, and FleXray's sampling to every point of each ray counted again; on the render, an arc, an ellipse, a spline and a face-on circle within their tolerance times the most the render enlarges them, a face-on circle, arc and ellipse to the magnification sdd over their depth, a spline through its points' places, a patch's area to its face-on, edge-on and tilted projections; and the surfaces, a grid's triangles turning about its rows crossed with its columns, a surface over a plane within its tolerance and a flat one equal to its patch, a quadric fitted to points on one being that quadric, a concave end-plate of the phantom sagging by its concavity to 1e-9, a spherical cap's area, rim and sag, and a cap's shadow, bulging toward the source or away, tilted, under a cone beam and parallel rays, being what its rim encloses on the render. test_projection.py holds the sampling's count to FleXray's own.

bordercall holds a second copy of the same camera, autoanatomist/project.py (pull request 40), which its docstring says is read from nanoDRR 0.1.7, where projection.py copies 0.1.5. Checked on 2026-10-30: the two wheels' camera code is the same (no file under nanodrr/camera/ differs; 0.1.7 changes how a ray is sampled, n_samples inside the volume and a step of the length over n where 0.1.5 takes it over n - 1, and adds fused backends, which moves a render's values and can move a support's edge by a sample, not where a ray runs). ## The survey

The 43 papers of the clinical reread (bordercall PR 21, one note per paper) gave the definitions: Panjabi's for the body, end-plates, pedicles, canal, processes and facets; Eisenstein's for the least canal depth, the greatest interpedicular distance, the mid-waist and his lateral-film construction, which puts the canal's posterior limit on the line from the superior articular process's apex to the inferior's inverted apex, opposite the upper half of the body; Genant's and Teijin's corner heights and ratios; Zindrick's pedicle chord; Meyerding's slip; Castellvi's 19 mm. Their means, 4,476 rows from 39 papers, are the paper tables' series.csv (bordercall branch claude/project-thread-adplcz at f067734), which `compare` reads.

Patented approaches that measure what a mask can show, none of which publishes per-case truth: Teijin US 5,224,035 (central and rim heights and their ratios, the wedge, biconcave and crush types); Hologic US 5,850,836 (posterior, middle and anterior heights and the wedge indices); Siemens US 11,074,688 (six sagittal points per vertebra and height loss against neighbours, AI-Rad Companion, FDA K222361) and US 10,588,588 (holder not confirmed); the Penn family US 2022/0237779 and US 12,450,731 (anterior, middle and posterior heights from landmarks); Hitachi US 9,594,975 (canal asymmetry and area ratios); Theseus AI US 11,488,717 (canal and foraminal areas); 25SEGMENTS US 12,616,522 (the minimum transverse pedicle width plane and screw trajectory); Holo Surgical EP 3470006 (segmentation of the spinous process and laminae). The implementation here follows the clinical definitions, which these patents also follow; the patents add no measure the definitions lack.

Open data with measured truth, and what each could test:

- VerSe 19 and 20 with the readers' Genant grades (Riedel 2026, OSF node 4skx2): 3,548 thoracic and lumbar vertebrae of 331 patients, graded 0 to 3 by an attending neuroradiologist and a resident independently, 116 of them (3.3 per cent) settled by consensus. The masks are VerSe's public 473 files ending _seg.nii.gz, 1,428,261,472 bytes, already listed and checked in the thread "Read the rest of VerSe's masks" (/mnt/project-files/gold3-ct/verse-read-2026-10-29/LISTING.json); the reference table, fracture_data/Genant_Fractures_thoracolumbar_reference.csv, is 11,823 bytes (osf.io/download/zy68u/). This is the one test of our heights against human reads on masks we did not make; it needs a container with full network access, since this one is refused OSF.
- CTLungCa-500 with six points per vertebra (anchor-free-genant, 3,565 vertebrae of 100 studies, CC BY-NC 4.0) and Pisov 2020's heights: per-vertebra heights, but on CTs with no released masks, so a test there needs new segmentations, which the owner's rule rules out.
- xVertSeg Data1 (Genant grade and type per lumbar level, with masks, 15 CTs; its licence asks for written permission beyond the challenge), the Ashish 2023 dry-bone caliper table (54,754 bytes, CC BY 4.0, figshare 21307917: lamina, facet, pars and interpedicular values per vertebra) and the SVAD subadult canal diameters (Zenodo 6342097) test distributions, not cases.
- No open CT set gives per-case canal, pedicle or facet dimensions measured by people.

Open code that claims these measures: Swin-X2S's morphometry (MIT, its morphometry folder and licence 99,924 bytes at 3f8b8ed: spinous length and angle, anterior and posterior heights, end-plate lengths, canal length) and naamii's xrayto3D-morphometry, which it copies; TPTBox's spinestats (Apache-2.0, needs SPINEPS's subregions); TotalSegmentator's spine report (heights at 15, 50 and 85 per cent of the depth); Spine-Morphology-Analyzer (no licence shown; synthetic spines with a measurements.csv); pedicle-screw-simulator (MIT, the isthmus as the least coronal section, as here); Comp2Comp and HealthiVert-GAN for heights; the Spinal Cord Toolbox for canal sections on MRI. None comes with measured truth; each is a second implementation to check ours against on the same mask.

What masks cannot give: cortical thickness and the inner pedicle width, density and anything read from intensities, the disc's own height where the vertebra labels take in the end-plate's cartilage, ligaments, and the posterior limit of the canal where the ligamentum flavum, not bone, bounds it.
