# Open 3D vertebra models and landmark sets, for exact-label phantoms (bordercall #54)

Source: the thread "Train on randomized spine phantoms", 2026-10-30, step 3 of #51, for greg's ask of 23:35 UTC to depend on online 3D meshes or spines with classified landmarks where they exist, and to consider them even where their landmarks differ from ours. A web survey made that evening, read-only, by a research helper of the thread; every claim below carries its link and a mark, [V] for a page read and [U] for a search snippet or an inference. Nothing was downloaded.

## What it decides

No open source publishes Panjabi's 3D landmarks (end-plate corners, pedicle, canal, transverse and spinous points) per person across C3 to L5. Every corner-point landmark set confirmed is 2D radiographs (rows 15 and 16 below). So the landmarks of step 4 (#55) are made, not downloaded: our own measurement code places them, and a template only carries them.

The template for #55, in order:

1. Our own gold tier masks: TotalSegmentator v2.0.1 is CC BY 4.0 (row 3), commercial use allowed, and its admitted subjects are already on the grid with the morphometry engine's rows. Per level, the subject nearest the population mean under the fitted prior gives the template, meshed from its mask, its landmarks the engine's own constructions. Nothing leaves the grid, as the corpora rule asks.
2. The UPF thoracolumbar repository (row 5, CC BY 4.0, 87.3 MB for the 42 patients' STL): one hexahedral template with the same node ids in every model, T1 to L5, so correspondence across people and levels comes free. Its posterior elements are the template's, not the person's (EOS reconstructions). A fetch on the grid under the size rule.
3. Meng et al. 2020 (row 1): the only open model with correspondence over C1 to L5 and one shape space across levels, but for non-commercial use only. SchwingNet's licence classes (data/licences.json) are loosened only by the owner, so it waits for greg's word and is never mixed into a commercial recipe.

VerSe is CC BY-SA 4.0 (row 2): a template derived from it carries the share-alike, so it is a second choice to TotalSegmentator for the same use. LumASe (row 6, CC BY 4.0) gives seven region labels on 663 lumbar vertebrae, from which the pedicle and process points can be checked by rule; BodyParts3D (row 8) is one hand-refined man, a template and not a population.

Still to do for #54: the z-score of each gold tier member's engine measures against Panjabi's mean and spread at each level, from the rows the grid already holds.

## The survey

### Bottom line

No open source found publishes Panjabi-style 3D landmark coordinates (end-plate corners, pedicle, canal, transverse
and spinous points) per person across C3 to L5. The closest options are (a) a point-correspondent statistical shape
model of C1-L5 trained on VerSe (Meng et al. 2020, non-commercial), on whose template the Panjabi points are defined
once by vertex index, and (b) building your own template from open CT masks (VerSe, TotalSegmentator) with the
region labels of LumASe to place pedicle and spinous points. Every landmark dataset with corner points that we
confirmed is 2D radiographs only.

Checked and found no public release:
- Rasoulian et al. 2013 multi-vertebrae shape+pose model: the author pages https://people.ece.ubc.ca/abtinr and https://people.ece.ubc.ca/abtinr/CTSegmentation.html mention no download [V].
- Campbell and Petrella 2016 lumbar SSM: no SimTK or other release found by search [U]; the related Denver thesis (52 CTs, L1-L5, ~3,500 nodes per vertebra) shows modes only as figures [V, https://core.ac.uk/download/217242581.pdf].
- Meakin et al. lumbar SSM: MR-based, no release found [U].
- Population-based 3D atlas of the pathological lumbar segment (24 CTs, L1-L5): "available on request", not public [V, https://mdpi.com/2306-5354/9/8/408].
- OSU digital spine geometry database (60 CTs, T12-S1, 16 measures per body including interpedicular and transverse process width): the paper gives no database URL [V, https://spine.osu.edu/media/document/2024-04-23/digital-spine-geometry-database-inform-computational-modeling.pdf].
- Sketchfab and Thingiverse: pages not readable; search snippets show artist and CT-derived single vertebrae of mixed and mostly unstated licences [U]. Not recommended for a labelled template.

### Ranked recommendation for a Panjabi-driven parametric template, C3 to L5, with correspondence

1. Meng et al. 2020 full spine SSM (row 1). The only open model found that covers C1-L5 with vertex correspondence across individuals and a joint shape space across levels. Define the Panjabi points once as vertex indices on each level's template and every sample carries them. Limits: non-commercial; repository contents and file format not verified from here (gitlab.inria.fr blocked); trained on only 80 partial VerSe CTs.
2. VerSe (row 2) with LumASe region labels (row 6) as the build-your-own route. VerSe gives C1-L5 masks from 355 people under CC BY-SA 4.0; LumASe gives pedicle, spinous and transverse regions on 663 lumbar vertebrae under CC BY 4.0, from which pedicle, spinous-tip and canal points can be placed by rule and checked. Needs your own correspondence step (template registration).
3. TotalSegmentator v2.0.1 (row 3), or its STL copies in MedShapeNet (row 4). The largest commercial-friendly source (CC BY 4.0, 1228 CTs, C1-S1): the population for fitting and validating template deformations against Panjabi ranges. No landmarks, no correspondence.
4. UPF thoracolumbar FE repository (row 5). One shared hexahedral template T1-L5 with identical node IDs in 42 patient and 16,807 virtual models, CC BY 4.0: correspondence across levels and individuals for free. Limits: EOS biplanar reconstructions, so pedicle, lamina and process shape come from the template rather than the person; no cervical spine; 35 GB for all STL (87 MB for the 42 patients).
5. Clouthier morphable lumbar model (row 7). MIT-licensed SSM with code that moves shape along measured axes, the nearest thing to "move landmarks to match morphometry"; lumbar only (L2-L5 per the README read), modality and landmark set not verified.

Honourable mention: BodyParts3D (row 8) as a clean single-person base mesh with every vertebra separate under CC BY-SA 2.1 JP, but it is one MRI-derived, hand-refined male, so it is a template, not a population.
