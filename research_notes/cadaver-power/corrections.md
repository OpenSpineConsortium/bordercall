## Corrections after review (2026-10-28, 02:40 UTC)

Sixteen reviewers checked this note: its arithmetic, its reading of Panjabi's tables and its simulation. The
answer holds and gets stronger: no number of cadavers matches the CTs. Six things below are wrong or
incomplete, and the rest of the note is being rewritten to match. Each figure here comes from the reviewers'
scripts, which will be kept beside the note.

1. **"The 42 series" are 42 papers.** They hold 39 distinct series, about 15 of them cadaveric or skeletal and
   the rest imaging of living people, and their counts are recorded (6 to 1,481). Only 29 of the 704 level and
   measure cells hold any series beside Panjabi's 12 spines. The series' own means differ by about 0.9 of a
   person's SD (median over the 28 cells with three or more series), at which they are worth about 0.7 to 1.4
   scans per level mean. "Their counts are not known here" is wrong.
2. **The spread of 0.39 is biased low.** It is a median of noisy per-cell figures. Pooled, it is about 0.7. Over
   the five measures the rule reads it is 0.50, at which an unlimited meta-analysis is worth 4.7 scans per level
   mean, not 7.7. Leaving out every row the papers themselves call a different definition takes the median to
   0.28, about 15 scans. So the range is about 2 to 15 scans, not "about 8".
3. **"Near r = 0 ... keeps the promise" rested on 4 lucky tables.** On 8 fresh tables the r = 0 table loses about
   4.5 points at 3 bodies, and its 0.99 answers are right 98.9% of the time, short of 99%. With the shift pooled
   the way the level-by-level part is, it loses about 7 points (97.6%). If the earlier series held more than 12
   specimens, it loses 6 to 13 points whatever r is. The published series name about as well as 50 of the
   population's own people only in the most favourable reading.
4. **"About 60 CTs make the same table" holds for the CT error only.** The 50 simulated people are measured
   exactly and have no spread of body size. With body size left in, 58 CTs lose about 6 points at 3 bodies,
   against about 4 for the 50; the estimator that takes size out (item 4) is not built.
5. **The 2,000 CTs are assumed to come from the population the rule names.** Pooled from other populations, they
   carry the same population term the published series do, and gain only by measuring as the rule reads and by
   giving the correlations.
6. **Smaller:** the 1,700 cadavers of item 1 rest on assumed CT errors (1,184 to 1,917 with them doubled or
   halved), and are a best case, not a reason; section A's figures are medians over cells; part C's factor is
   1.17, and matching the SDs as well takes about 68 CTs.
