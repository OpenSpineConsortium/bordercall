## 1. Six-point and four-point vertebral morphometry: point placement, heights, widths, reproducibility

### Takeaway
Quantitative morphometry places six points per vertebra on a lateral film (the four body corners plus the mid-points of the superior and inferior end-plates) and takes anterior, middle and posterior heights as the distances between the paired upper and lower points; Genant's semiquantitative grades are defined on percentage reductions of those three heights. Reproducibility is reported mostly as CVs or ICCs rather than mm: SpineAnalyzer heights had ICC > 0.95 and RMS CV 2.5-4.4 percent; a recent six-landmark network reached a vertebral-height MAE of 0.61 mm against a three-radiologist mean.

### Cited Findings
- Genant semiquantitative grades (scored on T4 to L4): grade 1 (mild) 20-25 percent reduction in one or more of the anterior, middle or posterior heights and 10-20 percent reduction in body area; grade 2 (moderate) about 25-40 percent height reduction and 20-24 percent area reduction; grade 3 (severe) at least 40 percent reduction in height and area. Citation given: Genant HK, Wu CY, van Kuijk C, Nevitt M. Vertebral fracture assessment using a semi-quantitative technique. J Bone Miner Res 1993;8:1137-1148. (The secondary page's ranges overlap at the boundaries.) -- [La Rhumatologie, Index de Genant](https://www.larhumatologie.fr/outils/index-de-genant/)
- SpineAnalyzer (Optasia Medical) places six morphometry points per vertebra, "the four corners of the vertebral body, as well as the mid-point of the superior and inferior endplate"; analysts click 13 centre points (T4 to L4), the software outlines the six points per vertebra, analysts may adjust them, and pixel coordinates are saved; a "95 points morphometry" option "has not been studied and validated comprehensively yet". Radiographs in that study used a focus-to-detector distance of 120 cm, and distortion from "oblique projection of the conical X-ray beams" (citing Hurxthal) is listed as a limitation. Citation: Oei L et al. Quant Imaging Med Surg 2013;3(5):249-255, doi:10.3978/j.issn.2223-4292.2013.09.03. -- [Oei et al. 2013, QIMS](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/2910/html)
- SpineAnalyzer's method is described as shape-based statistical modelling; on CT lateral scout views (96 subjects, 1,246 vertebrae) intra- and inter-reader ICCs for vertebral heights were both > 0.95 for all levels combined; RMS CV for height was 2.5-3.9 percent intra-reader and 3.3-4.4 percent inter-reader; kappa for fracture identification 0.59-0.69; 5.4 +/- 1.7 min per subject. Citation: Kim YM, Demissie S, Eisenberg R, Samelson EJ, Kiel DP, Bouxsein ML. Osteoporos Int 2011, doi:10.1007/s00198-011-1530-4. -- [Kim et al. 2011, SpringerMedicine abstract](https://www.springermedicine.com/intra-and-inter-reader-reliability-of-semi-automated-quantitativ/20634906)
- Six-landmark automatic morphometry on lateral lumbar films (1,318 standing lateral radiographs, 291 test images, T12 to L5): two anterior, two middle and two posterior points per vertebra; vertebral body height is the distance between the paired top and bottom landmarks; against the mean of three radiologists, VBH MAE 0.61 mm, SD 0.95, ICC 0.98 (0.97-0.98), Bland-Altman mean difference 0.11 mm; landmark PCK at 3 mm 98 percent overall (lowest S1, 94 percent); inter-observer agreement within 3 mm 92-94 percent and intra-observer 96 percent. The paper does not say how pixels were converted to mm or whether magnification was corrected. Citation: Yao H et al. Quant Imaging Med Surg 2024;14(8):5877-5890, doi:10.21037/qims-23-1859. -- [Yao et al. 2024, QIMS](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/127000/html)
- Four-point AP morphometry (L1 to L5): four margin points per vertebra (right and left, superior and inferior); VBH is the distance between the superior and inferior margin points on one side, left and right averaged; ICC 0.91-0.96 for L1 to L4 but 0.53 for L5 (MAE 2.53, units not stated); no pixel-to-mm or magnification handling described; end-plate widths were not reported. Citation: Chen Z et al. Quant Imaging Med Surg 2024;14(8):5385-5395, doi:10.21037/qims-22-540. -- [Chen et al. 2024, QIMS](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/110366/html)
- A fully automatic morphometry algorithm on 50 patients' lateral films: four corners (P1, P2, A1, A2) placed on an active-shape-model contour "by maximizing the area formed by the corresponding quadrilateral", with the mid-points M1, M2 constructed equidistant from the corners; localisation error against one radiologist (mean +/- SD, mm): P1 0.82 +/- 1.07, P2 1.72 +/- 2.10, M1 2.81 +/- 1.79, M2 2.57 +/- 0.70, A1 2.15 +/- 2.01, A2 2.04 +/- 2.31; wedge ratio Ha/Hp relative error 5.17 +/- 1.81 percent, biconcavity Hm/Hp 6.19 +/- 1.60 percent; sensitivity 86.5 percent (35 of 260 vertebrae missed); pixel spacing not reported. Citation: Franchini R et al. 3rd IMEKO TC13 Symposium, Lecce, 2014, pp. 28-31. -- [Franchini et al. 2014, IMEKO](https://imeko.org/publications/tc13-2014/IMEKO-TC13-2014-06.pdf)
- An automated quantitative morphometry pipeline (JBMR Plus 2025) used Genant et al.'s landmarking; manual quantitative morphometry "can take over 10 min per radiograph"; its authors state "ambiguities in landmarking using lateral radiographs alone were unavoidable"; errors are expressed only as percentages of each body's width or height (landmark error 2.1 percent in x and 2.3 percent in y on the test set; external evaluators 2.9-3.4 percent x and 2.9-4.0 percent y), with no mm values, pixel spacing or magnification reported. Citation: Arakawa S et al. JBMR Plus 2025;9(4):ziaf017, doi:10.1093/jbmrpl/ziaf017. -- [Arakawa et al. 2025, JBMR Plus](https://academic.oup.com/jbmrplus/article/9/4/ziaf017/7979261)

### Inferences
- With six points (two per column), the three heights are Euclidean distances between the anterior, middle and posterior point pairs; the end-plate length (sagittal depth) on a lateral film is the distance between the posterior and anterior corner of the same end-plate, and on an AP film the end-plate width is the distance between the left and right corners of one end-plate. None of the readable sources reports end-plate depth or width reproducibility in mm; the published reproducibility is for heights and ratios.
- Reproducibility in percent converts roughly to mm on a lumbar body of about 30 mm height (Yao's mean VBH 30.22 mm): an RMS CV of 2.5-4.4 percent is about 0.75-1.3 mm, consistent with Yao's 0.61 mm MAE and with Franchini's 0.8-2.8 mm point errors (arithmetic on the cited figures, not a reported result).
- The middle points are the weakest in two independent pipelines (Franchini's M1, M2 errors are the largest), which matters because middle height drives the biconcavity grade.

### Gaps
- Black DM et al. (1991), Eastell R et al. (1991) and McCloskey EV et al. (1993) were not read: their exact deformity definitions (ratio thresholds in SD units, McCloskey's predicted posterior height) and their mm precision are not reported here.
- MorphoXpress (Guglielmi et al., Eur J Radiol 2009) and its reproducibility were not read; no readable source described it.
- Manual six-point reproducibility on plain radiographs in mm (the classic precision studies) was not found in a readable source.

## 2. Corner datasets in deep learning (AASCE 2019, BUU-LSPINE, CSXA) and how end-plate lines or Cobb angles are computed from corners

### Takeaway
AASCE stores 68 landmarks per AP film, four corners for each of 17 vertebrae; the widely used reference code computes each vertebra's direction from the mid-points of its left and right sides and takes the Cobb angle as the largest angle between any two such vertebra lines. BUU-LSPINE carries per-vertebra corners on AP and lateral lumbar films with reported automatic corner errors of 4.63 mm (AP) and 4.91 mm (lateral); CSXA's paper could not be read.

### Cited Findings
- AASCE 2019: training data on SpineWeb (Dataset 16), test data as a separate download; the challenge page names "Cobb angle accuracy" as the metric and moved to Grand Challenge; the page itself does not state the image count or the landmark count. -- [AASCE 2019 challenge page](https://aasce19.github.io/)
- The held copy is 609 labelled AP whole-spine films (481 BoostNet training plus 128 BoostNet test) and 98 challenge-test films whose landmarks the release never published. -- [SchwingNet dataset record data/sources/aasce.json (internal)](file:///home/claude/SchwingNet/data/sources/aasce.json)
- Reference Cobb-from-corners code (Yi et al.'s Vertebra-Landmark-Detection): the landmark array is 68 x 2 per film, read in groups of four per vertebra; for each vertebra it takes pt1 = mean of points i and i+2 and pt2 = mean of points i+1 and i+3 (the left-side and right-side mid-points, given AASCE's top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right order), forms one line per vertebra (17 lines), computes all pairwise angles by arccos of normalised dot products, takes the largest as the main Cobb angle, and uses an S-curve test to pick the two others. -- [yijingru/Vertebra-Landmark-Detection, cobb_evaluate.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yijingru/Vertebra-Landmark-Detection/master/cobb_evaluate.py)
- BUU-LSPINE (Applied Sciences, July 2023): plain films from 3,600 patients, AP and lateral views, with vertebral positions, spondylolisthesis diagnosis and lumbosacral transitional vertebra truth; automatic vertebral corner extraction had a lowest average error of 4.63 mm on AP (ResNet152V2) and 4.91 mm on lateral (DenseNet201); YOLOv5 vertebra detection precision 81.93 percent (AP) and 83.45 percent (lateral); SVM spondylolisthesis accuracy 95.14 percent (AP) and 92.26 percent (lateral). The abstract does not say how corners were placed or how measurements are derived. -- [BUU-LSPINE, Applied Sciences 2023 (DOAJ record)](https://doaj.org/article/07d0825c5b2c444aa717d93b1396e6fb)
- A separate Mendeley release, "Spondylolisthesis Vertebral Landmark", is a landmark set over lumbar films. -- [Mendeley Data 5jdfdgp762](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/5jdfdgp762)
- CSXA: "A high-quality dataset featuring classified and annotated cervical spine X-ray atlas", Scientific Data, June 2024 (PMC11176335). -- [CSXA, PMC record](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11176335/); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/b5d75f43ff994b9db54ecb4a3ac665c9)
- The held CSXA copy is 4,963 lateral cervical radiographs with corner JSON. -- [SchwingNet dataset record data/sources/csxa.json (internal)](file:///home/claude/SchwingNet/data/sources/csxa.json)
- Six-landmark disc angle in a lateral lumbar pipeline: the intervertebral disc angle is the arccos of the normalised dot product of the top-edge vector of vertebra i and the bottom-edge vector of vertebra i-1; disc height is the distance between the bottom landmarks of vertebra i-1 and the top landmarks of vertebra i. -- [Yao et al. 2024, QIMS](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/127000/html)

### Inferences
- In AASCE-style corners, the upper end-plate line is the segment between corners 1 and 2 of a vertebra and the lower between corners 3 and 4; the end-plate's projected width on an AP film is the length of that segment in pixels, and both heights (left and right) are the distances 1-3 and 2-4. The reference Cobb code uses neither end-plate directly; it uses the line between the two side mid-points, an average of the two end-plates, so it measures vertebral tilt rather than the classical end-plate-to-end-plate Cobb.
- BUU-LSPINE's reported corner errors (about 4.6-4.9 mm) are several times Yao's height MAE (0.61 mm), so a pipeline that derives widths from network corners on BUU-like data should expect mm-level, not sub-mm, error.

### Gaps
- The CSXA paper (point count per vertebra, JSON fields, pixel spacing, morphometric parameters and reliability) could not be read: PMC returned a CAPTCHA.
- The BUU-LSPINE full text (corner convention, mm conversion, pixel spacing) and its authors, volume and DOI were not read; only the abstract record.
- AASCE's original paper (Wu H et al., BoostNet, MICCAI 2017) was not read; the 68-landmark order is inferred from the reference code.

## 3. Mask-to-corner conversion in published pipelines and its accuracy against manual corners

### Takeaway
Published pipelines turn a predicted vertebra mask into corners in three ways: a minimum-area bounding rectangle (cv2.minAreaRect plus boxPoints), a polygon approximation to four vertices (approxPolyDP or approxPolyN) with a rectangle fallback, or an edge band at the top or bottom of the contour; peer-reviewed accuracy for mask-derived landmarks is thin (median 1.7-4.1 mm in one sagittal pipeline), and the strongest morphometry results come from pipelines that use the mask only to locate and crop a vertebra and then regress the six points directly.

### Cited Findings
- Sagittal whole-spine pipeline: the GUI takes the predicted masks of vertebrae, sacrum and femur and "approximated the shape of each generated mask and subsequently determined the two upper corner points" with OpenCV; the method and its parameters are not named; landmark error median 1.7-4.1 mm; MAE against a specialist C2-7 SVA 2.112 mm and C7 SVA 5.219 mm, and angle MAEs from 3.577 (PT) to 8.789 (LL); ICC(2,1) from 0.62 (SS) to 0.98 (C2-7 SVA). Citation: Song S-Y et al. Bioengineering 2023;10(10):1229, doi:10.3390/bioengineering10101229. -- [Song et al. 2023, MDPI Bioengineering](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5354/10/10/1229)
- Minimum-area-rectangle route in public code: each vertebra contour gets cv.minAreaRect, the rectangles are sorted by centre y, cv.boxPoints gives four corners, the longer side decides which edges are the upper and lower end-plates, and their slopes feed the Cobb angle. -- [LijunRio/Spine-cobb-angle-measurement, coronal_plane_cobb.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LijunRio/Spine-cobb-angle-measurement/master/coronal_plane_cobb.py)
- Polygon route in public code ("robust 4-corner extraction"): try polygon approximation to four vertices (cv2.approxPolyN if available, else an epsilon sweep of approxPolyDP), check the quadrilateral is valid (minimum pair distance, area, edge length), merge near-duplicate points (6 px), else fall back to minAreaRect on the convex hull; corners are ordered TL, TR, BR, BL using the previous vertebra's bottom points, refined to sub-pixel with cv2.cornerSubPix on a blurred mask, and written as labelme JSON; bottom-edge endpoints can instead be taken as the leftmost and rightmost contour points in a band of 12 percent of the body height at its lower edge. -- [JaroslavRadimsky/vertebra_segmentation_and_keypoint_extraction, extraction.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/JaroslavRadimsky/vertebra_segmentation_and_keypoint_extraction/main/Src/Atlas/inference/extraction.py)
- Contour-to-corner by maximal quadrilateral area on an active shape model contour gave corner errors of 0.82-2.15 mm and mid-point errors of 2.57-2.81 mm against one radiologist (numbers in section 1). -- [Franchini et al. 2014, IMEKO](https://imeko.org/publications/tc13-2014/IMEKO-TC13-2014-06.pdf)
- Mask as locator only: a Mask R-CNN mask per body gives a centre of gravity, RANSAC fits a polynomial up to fourth degree through the centres to reject outliers, a 224 x 224 crop is cut, and an EfficientNetB2 head regresses the six landmarks directly; the stage-1 masks are not used for the points. Test-set Bland-Altman for C/A 0.006 (LOA -0.104 to 0.117), C/P 0.016 (-0.070 to 0.101), A/P 0.012 (-0.082 to 0.106); agreement worsened with collapse, within-LOA C/A falling from 98.6 percent (< 20 percent collapse) to 0.0 percent (> 40 percent, n = 6). -- [Arakawa et al. 2025, JBMR Plus](https://academic.oup.com/jbmrplus/article/9/4/ziaf017/7979261)
- FleXray's project page reports a Cobb-angle error of 5.13 degrees on AASCE with no failures, computed from each vertebra's mask, against 31.8 for FluoroSAM; the FleXray code carries no Cobb-angle code, so how its masks became end-plate lines is not public. -- [symphony-template docs/research/flexray-audit-plan-2026-10-25.md (internal, citing the project page flexray.csail.mit.edu)](file:///home/claude/symphony-template/docs/research/flexray-audit-plan-2026-10-25.md)

### Inferences
- A minimum-area rectangle forces the upper and lower end-plates parallel and equal in length, so it cannot express wedging (Ha differs from Hp) or biconcavity (Hm lower than Ha and Hp): it gives one height (the short side) and one end-plate length (the long side) per vertebra. It is adequate for a width or depth on a normal body, and wrong exactly where morphometry matters.
- A four-vertex polygon fit keeps wedging but still loses the middle height; the middle points need the contour itself (the upper and lower contour points at the mid-line between the anterior and posterior corners), which is how SpineAnalyzer's six points are defined.
- The end-plate band approach (leftmost and rightmost points in the lower 12 percent of the contour) is sensitive to osteophytes and to the end-plate's oblique projection (section 4), which a predicted mask includes or excludes inconsistently.
- Mask-derived landmarks in the only readable peer-reviewed sagittal pipeline (median 1.7-4.1 mm) are roughly three to seven times the error of direct six-point regression (0.61 mm MAE in heights), which suggests that a mask-only route to heights should be validated against manual corners before its mm are trusted.

### Gaps
- Suri A et al., "Vertebral Deformity Measurements at MRI, CT, and Radiography Using Deep Learning", Radiol Artif Intell 2021 (doi:10.1148/ryai.2021210015), which derives heights from segmentations across modalities, could not be read (the RSNA site refused the fetch); its algorithm and radiograph accuracy are not reported here.
- Kim et al., "Automated Vertebral Segmentation and Measurement of Vertebral Compression Ratio Based on Deep Learning in X-Ray Images", J Digit Imaging 2021 (doi:10.1007/s10278-021-00471-0), could not be read (Springer client error).
- No readable source compared minAreaRect, polygon and contour-band corners on the same masks against manual corners; the relative accuracy of these routes is not measured in anything read here.
- Cobb-from-mask papers on AASCE (arXiv 2211.14122 and others) could not be read: arXiv is blocked.

## 4. Magnification and projection: distances, factors, calibration and DICOM, rotation and bending

### Takeaway
Measured magnification of vertebral bodies on lateral digital films is larger and more variable than a nominal 1.1-1.2: 1.21 (range 1.06-1.57) at the cervical spine and 1.31 (range 1.09-1.63) at the lumbar spine against CT or MRI, rising with BMI. DICOM's Estimated Radiographic Magnification Factor is SID over a source distance measured to the table or bucky, not to the spine; no readable source quantified how axial rotation or lateral bending change apparent end-plate width or depth, so that part rests on geometry.

### Cited Findings
- Lateral spinal digital radiographs against CT/MRI vertebral body dimensions (250 patients, C2, C5, L1, L4): magnification cervical mean 1.21 +/- 0.01, range 1.06-1.57 (n = 177); lumbar mean 1.31 +/- 0.01, range 1.09-1.63 (n = 284); positive correlation with BMI (cervical p = 0.0019, lumbar p < 0.0001); lumbar non-obese 1.28 +/- 0.01 (n = 207) against obese 1.38 +/- 0.01 (n = 71); the authors state clinical decisions on linear radiographic measures that ignore this error are invalid and that the error can be corrected by comparison with CT/MRI morphometry. SID not reported in the abstract. Citation: Ravi B, Rampersaud YR. Orthop Procs 2009;91-B(SUPP_II):251-252. -- [Ravi and Rampersaud 2009, Bone and Joint](https://boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/0301-620X.91BSUPP_II.0910251d)
- Serial vertebral radiographs (EPOS): a statistical method reduced the variance of magnification differences in vertebral heights by 14 percent, "considerably more" than correction from the spine-film distance; vertebrae with a height reduction of 15 percent or more judged free of incident deformity fell from 100 to 46, and at 20 percent from 15 to 9. Citation: Lunt M et al. Osteoporos Int 2001;12(11):909-913, doi:10.1007/s001980170018. -- [Lunt et al. 2001, Springer abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s001980170018)
- DICOM DX Positioning Module (C.8.11.5): Distance Source to Detector (0018,1110), type 3, "Distance in mm from source to detector center"; Distance Source to Patient (0018,1111), type 3, distance in mm "from source to the table, support or bucky side that is closest to the Imaging Subject", which "does not take into account any air gap" and is "traditionally referred to as Source Object Distance (SOD)"; Estimated Radiographic Magnification Factor (0018,1114), type 3, "Ratio of Source Image Receptor Distance (SID) over Source Object Distance (SOD)". -- [DICOM PS3.3 2024c, C.8.11.5](https://dicom.nema.org/medical/dicom/2024c/output/chtml/part03/sect_C.8.11.5.html)
- A morphometry study's lateral films used a 120 cm focus-to-detector distance and named oblique projection of the conical beam as a distortion source. -- [Oei et al. 2013, QIMS](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/2910/html)
- Long-leg standing radiographs: SID 300 cm for CR and DR, 130 cm for EOS with the subject centred; functional leg length differed 8.7 percent between CR and EOS ("significant projectional magnification with CR"); femoral length differences up to 54 mm (13.6 percent of the mean); EOS images the vertical plane "true to size by means of the physical process alone" while horizontal fan-beam distortion is "corrected by the internal image processing algorithm"; the authors call for "a calibration device in the immediate neighborhood" for precise planning on long-leg films. Citation: Birkenmaier C et al. Skeletal Radiol 2024;53:1517-1528, doi:10.1007/s00256-024-04592-9. -- [Birkenmaier et al. 2024, LMU ePub](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/123378/1/s00256-024-04592-9.pdf)
- The recent deep-learning morphometry papers read here report heights in mm (Yao 2024) or only in percentages of body size (Arakawa 2025) without describing pixel spacing, calibration or magnification correction. -- [Yao et al. 2024](https://qims.amegroups.org/article/view/127000/html); [Arakawa et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/jbmrplus/article/9/4/ziaf017/7979261)
- FleXray's training renders (MOOSE profile) use a source-to-detector distance of 1150-1250 mm, a source 500-900 mm from the isocentre and a detector pixel of 1.25-2.25 mm, framing 128-451 mm at the isocentre (magnification 1.28-2.5) for the cone-beam renders; a quarter of renders are rescaled 0.8-1.2 in plane. -- [symphony-template docs/research/flexray-audit-plan-2026-10-25.md (internal, read from FleXray's src/fxr/configs/training/base.yml and docs/camera.md)](file:///home/claude/symphony-template/docs/research/flexray-audit-plan-2026-10-25.md)

### Inferences
- Point-source geometry gives magnification M = SID / (SID - OID), with OID the object-to-detector distance; for example SID 100 cm and OID 20 cm give 1.25, SID 120 cm and OID 15 cm give 1.14, SID 180 cm and OID 20 cm give 1.125 (arithmetic). Ravi and Rampersaud's measured lumbar 1.31 (up to 1.63) implies larger OIDs than such textbook figures, consistent with its BMI dependence (a larger body puts the spine further from the detector on a lateral film).
- Because DICOM's SOD is measured to the table or bucky side nearest the patient, the stored magnification factor describes the patient's surface on that side, not the spine. On a lateral film the spine's mid-sagittal plane is half a body width from that surface, so true spine magnification exceeds the stored factor; the stored factor alone under-corrects. The tags are type 3 (optional), so many films will not carry them.
- Options for mm without a calibration object, in order of the evidence above: normalise to a known in-image size (a vertebral dimension from the same patient's CT/MRI, as Ravi and Rampersaud propose, or a ratio such as height over depth, which cancels uniform magnification); report ratios (Genant grades and the Arakawa ratios are magnification-free); or take SID and SOD from DICOM and add an estimated table-to-spine distance, with its uncertainty stated.
- Projection geometry, not from a source: an end-plate seen at a craniocaudal angle theta to the beam projects as an ellipse whose vertical extent is about W sin(theta) for an end-plate width W; with W = 45 mm and theta = 5 degrees that band is about 3.9 mm, which inflates apparent height or doubles the end-plate contour. Off-axis vertebrae see such angles from beam divergence alone: a vertebra 15 cm from the central ray at an 80 cm source distance is viewed about 10.6 degrees obliquely.
- Axial rotation phi on a lateral film changes apparent depth by an amount that depends on the body's cross-section: for an ellipse of depth 35 mm and width 45 mm, phi = 5 degrees lengthens the projected depth by about 0.25 percent (35.09 mm), while for a rectangle of the same size it lengthens it by about 10.8 percent (38.8 mm), with the posterior wall doubling. Real bodies lie between, so rotation errors in depth should be measured on the project's own renders rather than assumed. On an AP film the same rotation shortens the projected end-plate width roughly by cos(phi) for an elliptical section.
- FleXray's renders span magnification 1.28-2.5 and in-plane rescale 0.8-1.2, so its masks carry no fixed physical scale; any mm from FleXray masks needs the input film's own pixel spacing and a magnification estimate, as for manual corners.

### Gaps
- No readable source quantified the effect of axial rotation or lateral bending on apparent vertebral end-plate width, depth or height on radiographs (Frobin W, Brinckmann P et al., Clin Biomech 1997, "Precision measurement of disc height, vertebral height and sagittal plane displacement from lateral radiographic views of the lumbar spine", which corrects for magnification by normalising to vertebral depth and sets rotation tolerances, could not be read: ScienceDirect blocked; Harvey and Hukins' simulation of out-of-plane movement could not be fetched).
- Published magnification factors for AP lumbar and thoracic views, and for standing full-spine films at 180 cm, were not found in a readable source; only lateral cervical and lumbar figures (Ravi and Rampersaud) and long-leg figures (Birkenmaier).
- Reported accuracy of calibration-marker correction for vertebral dimensions was not found; Lunt 2001 reports variance reduction, not mm accuracy.
- Imager Pixel Spacing (0018,1164) lies in the DX Detector Module, which was not read.

## 5. FleXray: repository, label ids, file format, resolution, coordinate convention, licence

### Takeaway
FleXray (pip package flexray, import fxr; upstream github.com/VictorButoi/FleXray, fork OpenSpineConsortium/FleXray) writes, per input image, three channel-first NumPy arrays of shape 61 x 256 x 256 (uint8 thresholded masks, float32 sigmoid probabilities, float32 logits) on the zero-padded square canvas, plus one label_names.json; it is a multilabel model with one channel per vertebra from C1 to L5 plus the sacrum (channels 30 to 55), ribs 1 to 12, and background at channel 0, with no L6 or T13 channel. Code is MIT; weights are CC BY-NC 4.0.

### Cited Findings
- Identity and paper: "FleXray: Universal Clinical X-ray Segmentation", Butoi VI, Gopalakrishnan V, Guttag JV, Dalca AV, Dey N, arXiv 2609.26756 (2026); project page flexray.csail.mit.edu; pip package flexray, import name fxr; weights on Hugging Face VictorButoi/flexray. -- [FleXray README (upstream)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorButoi/FleXray/main/README.md); [fork README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenSpineConsortium/FleXray/main/README.md)
- Inputs: PNG, JPEG, TIFF, BMP and DICOM (DICOM needs pip install flexray[dicom]); a directory is processed non-recursively in sorted order. DICOM pixels are rescaled by RescaleSlope and RescaleIntercept, MONOCHROME1 is inverted, and the image is min-max scaled to [0, 1] over its own range before padding and resize. -- [FleXray docs/inference.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorButoi/FleXray/main/docs/inference.md)
- Outputs per image named image.png: image_masks.npy (uint8, thresholded, one channel per anatomical label), image_probabilities.npy (float32), image_logits.npy (float32); "All three arrays are channel-first with shape CxHxW, where C is the number of model output labels and H/W come from the bundle's preprocessing size"; "The CLI writes .npy arrays and does not write PNG overlays"; one label_names.json per output directory names the C channels in order (omitted when the bundle declares no label schema); --binary LABEL restricts output to one channel (C = 1); masks use --threshold 0.5 by default. -- [FleXray README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorButoi/FleXray/main/README.md); [docs/inference.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorButoi/FleXray/main/docs/inference.md)

- Probability mode: bundle metadata probability_mode in preprocessing.json, "multilabel for the public model", i.e. a sigmoid on every channel; channels are therefore independent and can overlap. Python API: FleXraySegmenter.from_pretrained(...).predict(...) returns logits, probabilities and masks as B x C x H x W tensors, masks uint8. -- [docs/inference.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorButoi/FleXray/main/docs/inference.md)
- Resolution and geometry: every input is zero-padded to a square and resized with an antialiased bilinear resize to the bundle's image_size, default 256 x 256 ("a 512x512 tensor produces 256x256 predictions when the bundle declares the default image_size"); EXIF orientation is honoured; grayscale keeps its bit depth; with zero_one scaling, integers up to 16 bits use their dtype's bounds. -- [docs/inference.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorButoi/FleXray/main/docs/inference.md)

### Inferences
- Coordinate convention: the arrays are NumPy image arrays, so index [c, row, col] with row increasing downward and col to the right, on the 256 x 256 padded square, not on the input grid. To map a mask pixel (r, c) back to the input image of height H and width W: side = max(H, W), x_in = c x side / 256 - (side - W) // 2 and y_in = r x side / 256 - (side - H) // 2 (inverse of the code's centred pad and resize; half-pixel conventions of the antialiased resize shift this by up to half a canvas pixel). The tool does no such mapping itself.
- FleXray's masks are not a label map: a pixel can be on in several vertebra channels or none. Converting to one instance per level means taking each vertebra channel's largest connected component, or an argmax over the vertebra channels restricted to pixels above threshold.
- Physical resolution at 256: a film whose long side is 43 cm maps to about 1.68 mm per canvas pixel at the detector, so a 30 mm lumbar body is about 18 canvas pixels tall (before magnification correction), and a whole-spine film is coarser still. Corner extraction at this scale has a quantisation floor near 1-2 mm at the detector; upsampling the probabilities to the input grid before thresholding, or windowed inference at higher resolution, would be needed for sub-mm work (the network is fully convolutional, but the public tools resize to 256).
- Because the labels are anatomical classes per level and L6 and T13 were trained as background, a corner or width pipeline over FleXray masks inherits its level naming, including a missed sixth lumbar body on transitional spines.
