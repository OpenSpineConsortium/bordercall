# Classical (non-deep) location of vertebral bodies, corners and end-plates on single plain radiographs and DXA lateral images

Source of these notes: a research subagent's web reading on 2026-10-27 (PMC, PubMed, publisher landing pages, the NLM Lister Hill publication PDFs, Google Patents, author pages). Numbers are copied as the pages gave them. Several full texts could not be read (listed under each Gaps section). Where a fetch tool summarised a PDF, the figures are those the summary quoted from the paper's tables; garbled tables are flagged.

## 1. Active shape and appearance models (ASM, AAM) for vertebrae: what training they need and what accuracy they reach

### Takeaway
Every ASM/AAM result found needs a trained statistical model (77 to 670 annotated images) and, in the classic form, a few operator seeds (3 points per DXA spine, or 1 approximate centre per vertebra, or 2 clicks per cervical column); with that, corner/contour accuracy is about 0.6 to 0.96 mm on normal or mildly fractured vertebrae and degrades to about 2 mm on grade 3 fractures. Full automation of the seed step (Roberts 2012) still uses a trained random forest and works in 91% of 320 DXA images.

### Cited Findings

Smyth, Taylor, Adams (Manchester): ASM on lateral DXA
- Smyth PP, Taylor CJ, Adams JE, "Vertebral shape: automatic measurement with active shape models", Radiology 1999;211(2):571-578. The abstract says the authors statistically modelled the shape and appearance of the spine on lateral DXA, matched the model to unseen scans, and that the method was faster than manual analysis and "as accurate as human observers in the measurement of vertebral shape"; the abstract page gives no image count or millimetre figures. -- [University of Manchester research record](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/vertebral-shape-automatic-measurement-with-active-shape-models/)
- The Radiology full text (and its abstract page on pubs.rsna.org) could not be fetched (client error). -- [RSNA page, not readable](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/abs/10.1148/radiology.211.2.r99ma40571)
- The group's BMVC 1996 conference paper (precursor of the 1999 paper, same group and method) gives the numbers: 78 lateral spine DXA images of women over 45, Hologic QDR2000plus, ten vertebrae T7 to L4 per image; leave-one-out (train on 77, test on 1); the operator marks three points ("the top of vertebra T7, one at the top of T12, and one at the bottom of L4"); each experiment repeated 20 times with randomly perturbed start points; multiresolution ASM (Gaussian pyramid) searching all vertebrae at once, about 30 s per scan. -- [Smyth et al., BMVC 1996](https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/1996/smyth_1.pdf)
- BMVC 1996 accuracy, in pixels (not mm): rms point-to-line error of the ASM from 0.98 (T8) to 1.61 (L4); manual operators 1.11 to 1.66 (40 random images marked by 4 operators, 6 points per vertebra); precision (repeatability) 0.11 to 0.28 pixels; failure rates 0.8% (T7) to 6.0% (L3), a failure being an error above the mean of the successful cases plus 3 SD. -- [Smyth et al., BMVC 1996](https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/1996/smyth_1.pdf)
- Smyth, Taylor, Adams, "Automatic measurement of vertebral shape using active shape models", Image and Vision Computing (online 19 May 1998): lateral DXA has low spatial resolution and substantial random and structural noise; all vertebrae are searched simultaneously so neighbours constrain each other; "minimal user interaction"; the fit to the grey-level model is thresholded to reject poorly located vertebrae, which improves accuracy. -- [Image and Vision Computing record](https://www.datalearner.com/academic/journal-papers/0262-8856/volumes-and-issues/96/paper-detail/86898)

Roberts, Cootes, Adams (Manchester): AAMs on DXA and lumbar radiographs
- Roberts MG, Cootes TF, Adams JE, "Vertebral morphometry: semiautomatic determination of detailed shape from dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry images using active appearance models", Investigative Radiology 2006;41(12):849-859: 250 lateral DXA scans; spine modelled as a sequence of AAMs of overlapping vertebral triplets; each vertebra needs only an approximate initial centre; accuracy "comparable to manual precision" for normal vertebrae, decreasing with fracture severity; the abstract gives no mm figure or failure rate. -- [PubMed 17099422](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17099422/)
- The group's project page gives the DXA numbers: normal vertebrae mean accuracy c. 0.7 mm with 95% of points within 2 mm; fractured vertebrae mean 0.96 mm with about 90% within the 2 mm manual precision limit (the same page's overview says "around 85%", an internal inconsistency); lumbar radiographs mean 0.64 mm on over 500 digitised radiographs. The page also says a single global spine model is likely over-constrained for fractures and scoliosis, which is why overlapping triplet sub-models linked by constraints, dynamic fitting order and multiple initialisations are used. -- [Cootes, Automated Vertebral Fracture Assessment project page](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/timothy.f.cootes/Projects/VertFrac/vert_frac.html)
- A Medline record (PMID 16686025) whose abstract matches the MICCAI 2005 dynamically sequenced AAM paper: overlapping triplet AAMs, a new dynamic ordering algorithm that fits the best-fitting sub-model candidate first, a dataset with "a significant number of pathologies"; no numeric errors in the abstract. -- [BVS record mdl-16686025](https://busqueda.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-16686025)
- Roberts, Cootes, Adams, "Automatic segmentation of lumbar vertebrae on digitised radiographs using linked active appearance models", MIUA 2006: 250 digitised lumbar radiographs, chain of triplet AAMs; "0.64mm mean point-to-line error"; mild fractures 0.84 mm; moderate and severe fractures too few, so the models were undertrained for them; initialisation not described in the abstract. -- [MIUA 2006 abstract page](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/Timothy.F.Cootes/Papers/MIUA06/roberts_miua06.html)
- Roberts MG, Oh T, Pacheco EM, Mohankumar R, Cootes TF, Adams JE, "Semi-automatic determination of detailed vertebral shape from lumbar radiographs using active appearance models", Osteoporos Int 2012;23(2):655-664: 670 lumbar radiographs annotated by expert radiologists; "miss-40-out" train/test cross-validation; accuracy 0.82 mm for normal vertebrae, 1.19 mm for fracture grades 1 and 2, 2.12 mm for grade 3 (the abstract does not name the metric); the abstract does not state the number of initialisation points; conclusion "Vertebral body shape annotation can be substantially automated on lumbar radiographs." -- [PubMed 21431411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21431411/)
- Roberts, Cootes, Adams, "Automatic location of vertebrae on DXA images using random forest regression", MICCAI 2012, Vol. III pp. 361-368: separate detectors for upper and lower end-plates by random forest regression voting on Haar-like features, each tree voting for an end-plate centre; modes of the smoothed vote image are candidates; geometric constraints between neighbours resolve ambiguity, but the start of the sequence can be unclear (for example whether the lowest vertebra is L4 or L5); end-plate centres initialise an AAM; 320 DXA images; "Accuracy is comparable to manually initialised AAM segmentation in 91% of images"; multiple grade 3 fractures cause edge confusion in severe osteoporosis. -- [Springer Professional abstract](https://www.springerprofessional.de/automatic-location-of-vertebrae-on-dxa-images-using-random-fores/3978140)

Benjelloun, Mahmoudi, Lecron (Mons): ASM on cervical radiographs (NHANES II)
- Benjelloun M, Mahmoudi S, Lecron F, "A framework of vertebra segmentation using the active shape model-based approach", International Journal of Biomedical Imaging 2011: cervical C3 to C7 from NHANES II; ASM with 20 landmarks per vertebra (corners included), Generalized Procrustes alignment, 7-point grey profiles spaced at 5% of vertebra size matched by Mahalanobis distance; training sizes 25, 50, 75 and 100 compared, 75 chosen; test set 100 images separate from the 75 training images, no cross-validation; 50 to 250 iterations, about 15 s per 50 iterations. -- [PMC3149802](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3149802)
- Benjelloun 2011 accuracy: mean segmentation error "about 0.6 mm" (Euclidean landmark-to-reference distance, specialist annotation); success rates with the single-vertebra model C3 92.2%, C4 98%, C5 96.1%, C6 96.1%, C7 98%; with the whole-column model C3 54.9%, C4 80.4%, C5 82.4%, C6 76.5%, C7 56.9%; "success" is not precisely defined. -- [PMC3149802](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3149802)
- Benjelloun 2011 compares with other groups (different data, so not directly comparable): Roberts 0.88 mm on DXA and 0.64 mm on lumbar radiographs; de Bruijne and Nielsen 1.4 mm; Klinder 1.12 mm on CT. -- [PMC3149802](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3149802)

Other trained shape-model work found
- de Bruijne M, Nielsen M, "Shape particle filtering for image segmentation", MICCAI 2004 pp. 168-175: maximum-likelihood shape inference from pixel classification combined with a global shape model, optimised by stochastic sampling to remove the need for accurate initialisation; tested on vertebra segmentation in spine radiographs (and lung fields, myocardium); the record gives no numbers. -- [University of Copenhagen record](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/da/publications/shape-particle-filtering-for-image-segmentation/)
- Mysling P, Petersen PK, Nielsen M, Lillholm M, "Automatic segmentation of vertebrae from radiographs: a sample-driven active shape model approach", MLMI 2011: fully automatic, "does not require operator interaction"; a shape-model sampling scheme gives coarse spine alignment and vertebra positions, which initialise an ASM with a nonlinear appearance model constrained by a conditional shape model; lumbar radiographs; numbers not on the record. -- [University of Copenhagen record](https://test01.ku.dk/:obvius/pureproxy/3974268/en/publications/automatic-segmentation-of-vertebrae-from-radiographs-a-sample-dri)
- A 2014 review, "Statistical Shape and Appearance Models in Osteoporosis", lists the vertebral SSM line: Smyth 1997 (IVC), Roberts 2003/2005/2006/2009/2012, Iglesias and de Bruijne 2007 (conditional shape model, lateral X-rays), Mysling 2012 (Mach Vis Appl, called the first multi-object/multi-resolution SSM in osteoporosis), de Bruijne 2006/2007 (neighbour-conditional shape models); only the abstract and reference list were readable. -- [Springer review landing page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11914-014-0206-3)

### Inferences
- ASM/AAM search is deterministic given a fixed model and a fixed initialisation (a gradient-descent style fit), but its result depends on the start: Smyth's 20 perturbed starts give a repeatability of 0.11 to 0.28 pixels, and Benjelloun and the Cootes page both name initialisation as the main weakness. Shape particle filtering (de Bruijne) uses stochastic sampling and so is deterministic only with a fixed random seed.
- The training burden is large: 77 DXA images (Smyth), 250 DXA scans or radiographs (Roberts 2006), 670 radiographs (Roberts 2012), 75 cervical images (Benjelloun). None of these runs with no trained model.
- The best "no-seed" ASM/AAM pipeline found (Roberts 2012 MICCAI) replaces the operator's seeds with a second trained model (random forests on Haar features) and still fails to match manual initialisation in 9% of 320 DXA images.

### Gaps
- The Radiology 1999 paper's own image count, training size, seed count and mm accuracy could not be read (pubs.rsna.org returned an error; doi.org is blocked here). The BMVC 1996 figures above are from the precursor conference paper, not the 1999 journal paper.
- The Investigative Radiology 2006 full text (point-to-line mm, failure rate, training split) was not readable; only the abstract and the project page's summary numbers were.
- The MICCAI 2012 random forest paper's mm errors, training size and the definition of "comparable" were behind a paywall.
- de Bruijne and Nielsen 2004 and Mysling 2011 numbers were not on the readable records; the 1.4 mm figure for de Bruijne comes only from Benjelloun's comparison.

## 2. Generalized Hough transform and hierarchical methods on NHANES II films (Texas Tech and the US National Library of Medicine)

### Takeaway
The NLM/Texas Tech NHANES II line combined a rigid generalized Hough transform (a mean template built from 50 annotated shapes) for pose, then an ASM with edge blending, then a dynamic-programming deformable model; it reached a 3 mm success bin in 75% of 100 cervical films but only 49% of 100 lumbar films, and the GHT can lock onto the wrong vertebra (shifted matches along the spine). NLM's own training-free landmarking found the spine axis and edges but not the vertebrae.

### Cited Findings

Tezmol, Sari-Sarraf, Mitra, Long, Gururajan 2002: customised GHT (cervical)
- Citation: Tezmol A, Sari-Sarraf H, Mitra S, Long R, Gururajan A, "Customized Hough transform for robust segmentation of cervical vertebrae from X-ray images", SSIAI 2002:224-228. -- [NLM LHC publication page](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/pubs/CustomizedHoughTransformforRobustSegmentationofCervicalVertebraefromXRayImages.html)
- Algorithm: unsharp masking (image minus its Gaussian blur), averaging filter, binarisation, Sobel (gives thin double edges); gradient direction from Ando's 3x3 "optimum" operator; template of 80 radiologist landmarks; the mean template averaged from 50 NHANES II training shapes; R-table of (r, alpha) per edge point; a 4-D accumulator (x, y, scale, rotation) treated as j x k 2-D accumulators, each Gaussian-smoothed; a new vote criterion that, per scale/rotation, combines the cumulative value of the highest peak and its two neighbours along the estimated spine direction with the peak's decay rate (sharpness) in a weighted sum, instead of taking the single largest bin. Filter sizes, threshold and weights are not legible in the PDF. -- [Tezmol et al. 2002 PDF](https://www.lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2002014.pdf)
- Results on 50 NHANES II images (mean landmarks of 80 falling inside a bounding box around the found template; orientation error): each image's own template 78 and <1 deg (an oracle); mean template with traditional voting 62.48 and 7.21 deg; mean template with the proposed criterion 72.06 and 4.16 deg. No pixel or mm error is given; whether the 50 template images and the 50 test images are disjoint is not stated. -- [Tezmol et al. 2002 PDF](https://www.lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2002014.pdf)

Zamora, Sari-Sarraf, Long 2003: hierarchical GHT + ASM + deformable model
- Data: NHANES II collection at NLM (more than 17,000 films); 100 cervical and 100 lumbar images chosen at random; cervical 1463x1755 pixels, lumbar 2048x2487 pixels, digitised at 146 dpi; ground truth morphometric points from three expert radiologists. -- [Zamora et al. 2003 PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf)
- Step 1, GHT: global pose (location, orientation, scale) with three templates per region (the mean shape and two along the first two PCA modes); cervical template C2 inferior end-plate plus C3 to C6, lumbar L1 to L5; because the spine repeats, two shifted matches of the mean template are also output, so five candidate poses per image; the rigid template cannot segment on its own. -- [Zamora et al. 2003 PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf)
- Step 2, ASM initialised from the GHT pose: 80 landmarks (cervical) and 200 (lumbar); the plain grey-level profile model was judged invalid on these films, so edges (unsharp masking and thresholding at five Gaussian size/sigma pairs, combined by XOR) were averaged into the grey image ("edge blending"). Step 3, deformable model at key points (mainly anterior corners) with an energy of grey-profile match, proximity to a reference template and smoothness, solved by dynamic programming. -- [Zamora et al. 2003 PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf)
- Results (success = mean landmark error within 3 mm): cervical GHT 65%, GHT+ASM 75%, GHT+ASM+DM 75% (example errors 0.86 mm and 2.1 mm); lumbar GHT 40%, GHT+ASM 47%, GHT+ASM+DM 49% (example errors 5.18 mm and 2.27 mm); the abstract states "6.4mm in 50% of the lumbar images", which does not match the 49% in the results. The deformable model moved no additional cervical case into the 3 mm bin. -- [Zamora et al. 2003 PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf); also listed at [NLM LHC page](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/pubs/HierarchicalSegmentationofVertebraefromXrayImages.html)
- Stated limitations: the ASM needs a good initialisation; the GHT template is rigid; GHT shifted matches mean the algorithm cannot tell from the template alone which match is the correct vertebra; lumbar is much worse than cervical; manual ground truth is subjective. -- [Zamora et al. 2003 PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf)

Howe, Gururajan, Sari-Sarraf, Long 2004
- Howe BM, Gururajan A, Sari-Sarraf H, Long LR, "Hierarchical segmentation of cervical and lumbar vertebrae using a customized generalized Hough transform and extensions to active appearance models", 6th IEEE SSIAI 2004, doi 10.1109/IAI.2004.1300970. Only the citation was readable. -- [Applied Vision Lab publication record](https://appliedvisionlab.owlstown.net/publications/47001-hierarchical-segmentation-of-cervical-and-lumbar-vertebrae-using-a-customized-generalized-hough-transform-and-extensions-to-active-appearance-models)

Long and Thoma (NLM Lister Hill): training-free landmarking of the spine region
- Long LR, Thoma GR, "Landmarking and feature localization in spine x-rays", Journal of Electronic Imaging 10(4):939-956 (the NLM page dates it 2002 Oct; volume 10 suggests 2001, a discrepancy in the record): the goal was indexing 17,000 NHANES II spine films for osteoarthritis and vertebral morphometry; the paper reports landmark acquisition and spine-region localisation only. -- [NLM LHC page](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/pubs/LandmarkingandFeatureLocalizationinSpineXRays.html)
- Algorithm (cervical, 8-bit): Gaussian blur (100x100, sigma 100) and subsampling to a 6x7 matrix; region growing for the two brightest blobs (skull and shoulder) with tolerance 0.05 of dynamic range, background below 0.1; their centroids are landmarks; the line joining skull and shoulder is the coarse spine axis; 20 transverse lines normal to it; on each line the first large grey rise from background sets a search grid; dynamic programming (lambda = 0.05 between gradient and length terms) finds four curves: posterior spine edge, spine ridge, anterior edge, anterior valley. Thresholds were set "experimentally" in "relatively few trials"; no trained model in these stages. -- [Long and Thoma PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/system/files/pub2001001.pdf)
- Results: landmark labelling on 48 images, preferred method (d) skull 46 accepted / 2 rejected, shoulder 48/0, background 48/0; curves computed on 46 of 48 images; spine orientation from the curves within 15 deg of the expert-derived slope for all images but one; no pixel or mm localisation error. -- [Long and Thoma PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/system/files/pub2001001.pdf)
- The same paper reports that grey level alone cannot separate body from disc: an interior C4 region (mu = 197.4, sigma = 2.2) closely matches the adjacent disc space (mu = 198.1, sigma = 1.6); earlier NLM ASM work used boundary data from 15 to 20 images and saw non-convergence even with templates started near ground truth; expert points (up to 9 per vertebra) exist for 550 images. -- [Long and Thoma PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/system/files/pub2001001.pdf)

Dinesh Kumar and Thomas 2005 (NHANES II, training-free spine pose)
- Dinesh Kumar VP, Thomas T, "Automatic estimation of orientation and position of spine in digitized X-rays using mathematical morphology", J Digit Imaging 2005;18(3):234-241: coarse segmentation, top-hat transform to enhance ridge points on the posterior spine boundary, H-maxima transform to remove external objects (ornaments), Radon transform to find the line through ridge-point clusters; no template and no training described; 100 cervical NHANES II images; orientation within the accepted 15 deg tolerance in all cases, average error 4.6 deg; a posterior-boundary point located within +/-5.2 mm. -- [Springer abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10278-005-5150-4)

### Inferences
- The NHANES II work shows a ladder: training-free steps (Long and Thoma, Dinesh Kumar and Thomas) reliably find the spine's position and orientation (within 15 deg; about 4.6 deg average; about 5 mm for a boundary point), a mean-template GHT (built from 50 annotated shapes, so lightly "trained") finds a rough vertebra pose, and only a trained ASM gets near millimetre corners, and then in 75% of cervical and under half of lumbar films.
- The GHT, the ASM fit and the DP deformable model are all deterministic for fixed parameters.
- A rigid template matched along a repeating structure produces off-by-one-vertebra matches (Zamora); level naming therefore needs an external anchor (C2, the sacrum, the skull/shoulder landmarks) even when bodies are found.

### Gaps
- Howe et al. 2004 results (and the Howe thesis at Texas Tech) were not readable.
- The number of ASM training images in Zamora 2003 was not stated in what was read.
- No NHANES II corner-level result in pixels was found; all are mm or landmark counts.

## 3. Training-free or nearly training-free approaches: edges plus Hough lines, polar or template matching, profiles along a centreline, dynamic programming, Cobb tools

### Takeaway
Fully automatic, training-free pipelines for end-plates exist in the patent record (Siemens: curvature ridge maps, line or parabola projections across the spine, and dynamic programming with anatomical ratio constraints; Lunar: profile minima and 30% thresholds on lateral DXA), but none of them publishes an accuracy figure. The training-free methods that do publish numbers either locate only the spine (orientation within 15 deg) or need seeds (Cobb by fuzzy Hough with the user selecting the top and bottom vertebrae: ICC > 0.95, mean absolute difference < 5 deg on 76 radiographs). No source found reports corner or end-plate point accuracy in mm for a method that uses neither a trained model nor seeds.

### Cited Findings

Siemens Corporate Research patents (Wei, Qian; fully automatic, no training)
- US 6,608,916 B1, "Automatic detection of spine axis and spine boundary in digital radiography" (Wei GQ, Qian J; Siemens Corporate Research; application 09/638,120 filed 2000; expired 2015): downsize and smooth; intensity ridge maps from the curvature of the intensity profile scanned in at least two directions, fused; equally spaced horizontal cutting lines; dynamic programming picks one point per line for a piecewise-linear axis using segment orientation, inter-segment angle and ridge strength; gradient map enhanced along the axis direction; a "dual" dynamic programme picks left and right boundary points per line jointly; the axis is refined as the mid-locus of the boundaries; no manually placed points, no trained model; results only as figures, no accuracy numbers. -- [Google Patents US6608916](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6608916)
- US 6,608,917 B1, "Detection of vertebra endplates in digital radiography" (Wei GQ, Qian J; Siemens Corporate Research; filed 14 Aug 2000, granted 19 Aug 2003; expired 2015): ROI from the spine boundaries; ridge/valley curvature map from second-order intensity derivatives (horizontal, vertical, diagonal) with an orientation map, since end-plates appear as bright ridges; keep ridges nearly orthogonal to the spine axis; at each axis point fit a line segment spanning left to right boundary over a range of angles, score = sum of curvature along it with outliers excluded; the maximum projection marks end-plate candidates and the minimum projection marks discs; the sign of the summed directional gradient between peaks tells an upper end-plate (bright to dark) from a lower one (dark to bright); knowledge-based reasoning with vertebral height ratios, disc-to-vertebra thickness ratio and a maximum disc thickness picks the best end-plate pairs; output positions and orientations for Cobb, wedge and tilt angles; described as fully automatic with no training; no accuracy reported. -- [Google Patents US6608917](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6608917)
- US 7,010,151 B2, "Endplate detection in digital radiography by dynamic programming using both local and global constraints" (Wei GQ, Qian J, Schramm H; Siemens; provisional 60/326,358 filed 1 Oct 2001; expired 2018): curvature map in the spine ROI; at each axis point fit parabolas y = kx^2 + b through the left and right boundary points; maximum curvature sum = end-plate candidate, minimum = disc candidate; an objective E summing end-plate and disc projections; dynamic programming over states (end-plate position, disc thickness) with local constraints (vertebral height within [hmin, hmax], neighbour height ratio, disc/height ratio, angle between neighbouring end-plates at most theta_max) and a global height model h_v = k*a^v + b (a <= 1) fitted iteratively during back-tracing, pruning candidates whose residual exceeds delta_h; the first and last end-plate ranges are assumed known; parameters predefined, no learned model; no accuracy numbers. -- [Google Patents US7010151](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7010151)

Lunar DXA morphometry patent (Mazess; training-free profile analysis)
- US 5,228,068, "Device and method for automated determination and analysis of bone density and vertebral morphology" (Mazess RB; Lunar Corporation; filed 14 Sep 1992, granted 13 Jul 1993): on a lateral scan's "pseudo bone mineral" matrix, a superior-inferior profile along a spine-centred line has local minima at the intervertebral spaces, with vertebral centres midway; an anterior-posterior profile has minima at the borders; orientation by rotating a rectangle in small steps to maximise the enclosed sum (or by the peak histogram derivative over canted axes); bilinear rebinning to the vertebra-aligned grid; in each zone the superior border is the first row on the rising edge above 30% of the histogram peak and the inferior border the first row below 30% on the falling edge (a fixed threshold is the alternative), or a local-comparison rule (a border element has similar neighbours anteriorly, posteriorly and inferiorly but a much lower value superiorly); superior, central and inferior bands give anterior, middle and posterior columns whose intersections are the six morphometry points; no operator input or training described for boundary finding; no validation or error figures. -- [Justia US5228068](https://patents.justia.com/patent/5228068)

Hough- and template-based end-plate or vertebra finding with published numbers
- Zhang J, Lou E, Le LH, Hill DL, Raso JV, Wang Y, "Automatic Cobb measurement of scoliosis based on fuzzy Hough Transform with vertebral shape prior", J Digit Imaging 2009;22(5):463-472: the user adjusts brightness/contrast and selects the top and bottom of the vertebrae; Canny edges; a fuzzy Hough transform detects lines; peaks are selected under vertebral shape constraints to fit end-plate lines; Cobb from line directions; 76 PA radiographs, one experienced surgeon manually and two examiners twice with the method; automatic versus manual ICC > 0.95 and mean absolute difference < 5 deg; inter-observer ICC > 0.95, MAD < 5 deg; intra-observer ICC 0.985 and 0.978, MAD < 3 deg. The abstract does not say how the "shape prior" is obtained. -- [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s10278-008-9127-y&resultType=core&format=json)
- Anitha H, Prabhu GK, "Automatic quantification of spinal curvature in scoliotic radiograph using image processing", J Med Syst 2012;36(3):1943-1951: vertebral inclination slopes from active contour models and morphological operators; the radiologist selects the end vertebrae; the abstract claims inter/intra-observer variation "completely eliminated" and gives observer error only in tables not readable here. -- [Manipal research record](https://researcher.manipal.edu/en/publications/automatic-quantification-of-spinal-curvature-in-scoliotic-radiogr/)

The text also says "all the vertebrae were detected perfectly", which contradicts the table. -- [IntechOpen chapter 38131](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/38131)

Edge-plus-learning corner detectors (for contrast: classical features, but trained)
- Ebrahimi S et al. (CMBBE: Imaging and Visualization; author copy dated 2018): seven operator clicks (superior sacral end-plate, T12 inferior and C7 superior end-plates, odontoid tip) place a 2D statistical spine model; Wiener and median filters, CLAHE, Canny, Douglas-Peucker simplification of edge segments gives corner candidates; four random forests per region (500 trees) classify corners with HOG, Haar-like and contextual features; regression fills missing corners; 109 EOS sagittal radiographs (60 train, 49 test), C3 to C7 and L1 to L5; corner RMSE cervical 0.9 +/- 0.7 mm, lumbar 1.7 +/- 1.2 mm; before regression, within a 1.4 mm radius, sensitivity 81% cervical / 67% lumbar and precision 88% / 60%. -- [Ebrahimi et al. author copy](https://sam.ensam.eu/bitstream/10985/15786/3/IBHGC_CMBBEIV_2018_Ebrahimi.pdf)

### Inferences

- The training-free algorithms all rest on the same three intensity cues: end-plates are bright ridges or steep gradients roughly perpendicular to the spine axis, discs are profile minima between them, and anatomical ratios (vertebral height, disc thickness, smooth change of height along the column) prune candidates; these map directly onto a 1D profile or line-projection analysis along a centreline followed by dynamic programming, which is deterministic.
- On the evidence found, a fully mask-free and training-free radiograph mode is plausible for the spine axis, the column boundaries and approximate end-plate lines (the Siemens and Lunar designs), but no publication validates it at corner precision; every published millimetre-level corner result uses either a trained shape/appearance model or seeds plus a trained model. A seeded, training-free mode (the user marking the end vertebrae or the column ends) is the closest validated option, and it is validated for Cobb angles (degrees), not for corner positions (mm).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed accuracy study of the Siemens or Lunar training-free end-plate algorithms was found; whether Siemens products shipped them, and how they performed, is unknown.
- The source of Zhang 2009's "vertebral shape prior" (hand-set or learned from images) is not in the abstract; PMC full text (PMC3043716) returned a CAPTCHA.
- Benjelloun and Mahmoudi, "Spine localization in X-ray images using interest point detection", J Digit Imaging (doi 10.1007/s10278-007-9099-3), could not be read (Springer client error; PMC CAPTCHA).
- No GitHub repository implementing a classical (non-deep) vertebra or end-plate detector on radiographs was found in the searches made.
- Allen et al. 2008 (ASM for Cobb angle) and other classical scoliosis tools were not reached.

## 4. Semi-automatic methods that need only a few clicks, and their accuracy

### Takeaway
A handful of clicks is the norm in the validated classical literature: 3 points per DXA spine (Smyth), an approximate centre per vertebra (Roberts 2006, MorphoXpress), 2 clicks for a cervical column (Benjelloun 2011), 2 points for an ROI (Larhmam GHT), the top and bottom vertebrae for Cobb (Zhang 2009), or one click per vertebral centre (Al Arif). Seeds lift detection markedly (GHT 64.5% to 89.0%), but the corner-level accuracy still comes from a trained model.

### Cited Findings
- Smyth (BMVC 1996): three operator points (top of T7, top of T12, bottom of L4) initialise an ASM over T7 to L4 on lateral DXA; rms point-to-line error 0.98 to 1.61 pixels against manual 1.11 to 1.66; repeatability 0.11 to 0.28 pixels under perturbed starts; failure rates 0.8% to 6.0% by level. -- [Smyth et al., BMVC 1996](https://www.bmva-archive.org.uk/bmvc/1996/smyth_1.pdf)
- Roberts 2006: each vertebra needs only "an approximate initial centre location"; 250 DXA scans; accuracy comparable to manual precision for normal vertebrae. -- [PubMed 17099422](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17099422/)
- Benjelloun 2011, semi-automatic mode: two clicks (superior-left corner of the first vertebra, inferior-left corner of the last); Canny and Harris corners; a neighbour filter (in one example 231 of 427 Harris corners removed); an angle filter keeping corners whose neighbour-line angle is between 10 and 160 deg (removes about 75%); a recursive search for the 2N corners forming the shortest path between the two clicks under vertebra height alpha and disc space beta with alpha about 4*beta and total length d about N*alpha + (N-1)*beta; these corners then initialise the trained ASM. The paper is inconsistent on the manual mode's click count (abstract: two points; section 2.3: left superior and inferior corners of each vertebra). -- [PMC3149802](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3149802)
- Benjelloun, Mahmoudi, Larhmam GHT: two user points defining an ROI raise global detection from 64.5% (automatic) to 89.0% on 40 NHANES II films, C7 from 32.5% to 60.0%. -- [IntechOpen chapter 38131](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/38131)
- Zhang 2009: the user selects the top and bottom vertebrae (after adjusting brightness/contrast); Cobb versus manual ICC > 0.95, MAD < 5 deg; intra-observer ICC 0.985 and 0.978, MAD < 3 deg, on 76 radiographs. -- [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1007/s10278-008-9127-y&resultType=core&format=json)
- MorphoXpress (Image Metrics plc and Procter and Gamble), as used by Montala et al., J Rheumatol 2011;38(5): the operator "manually targeted the centers of the upper and lower vertebrae (T7 to L5) to initialize the analysis" and "the software automatically finds the landmarks for a standard 6-point morphometry measurement"; 176 lateral thoracolumbar radiographs analysed; the paper gives no agreement statistics of its own and does not say whether a trained shape model is used; it cites a multicentre reproducibility study (Guglielmi et al., Eur J Radiol 2009;69:173-8). -- [Montala et al. 2011 PDF](https://www.jrheum.org/content/jrheum/early/2011/02/24/jrheum.100851.full.pdf)
- Al Arif et al. IPTA 2015 (trained Hough forest, for contrast): the user clicks each vertebral centre; orientation and size come from the centres; 90 lateral cervical radiographs (Royal Devon and Exeter Hospital, 2014, ages 17 to 96, Philips, Agfa, Kodak, GE), 10-fold cross-validation; corner error mean 3.03 mm, average median 2.08 mm, SD 3.08 mm with mixed features. -- [Al Arif et al. IPTA 2015](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/g.slabaugh/publications/CervicalCornersIPTA2015.pdf)
- Ebrahimi et al.: seven clicks place a statistical spine model; trained forests then give corner RMSE 0.9 +/- 0.7 mm cervical and 1.7 +/- 1.2 mm lumbar. -- [Ebrahimi et al. author copy](https://sam.ensam.eu/bitstream/10985/15786/3/IBHGC_CMBBEIV_2018_Ebrahimi.pdf)
- The Guglielmi et al. 2008 review of vertebral morphometry (Eur Radiol) lists the semi-automatic six-point morphometry software studies (Guglielmi 2007, 2008, Eur J Radiol) and the ASM/AAM papers among its references; only its abstract and reference list were readable. -- [Guglielmi et al. 2008 review landing page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00330-008-0899-8)

### Inferences
- The honest classical baseline for corners is "seeds plus a trained model": two to seven clicks, or one per vertebra, then a statistical model fitted locally, giving roughly 0.6 to 1.7 mm on good films.
- A seeded but training-free path exists only for end-plate lines and angles (Zhang 2009's fuzzy Hough, Anitha's active contours, the polar-signature and Harris methods of the Mons group); its validated outputs are Cobb angles or detection rates, not point errors.
- In the seeded methods, the seeds also fix the level names (the user says which vertebrae are the ends), which a training-free automatic method would otherwise have to infer.

### Gaps
- No study was found that reports corner or end-plate point error in mm for a seeded method with no trained model.
- The MorphoXpress algorithm (whether it uses an AAM) and its published precision figures were not reached; SpineAnalyzer was not reached.

## 5. Typical failure modes on films and DXA

### Takeaway
The sources name the same failure classes: the wrong vertebra (off-by-one template matches, L4/L5 start ambiguity), severe fractures (edge confusion, errors near 2 mm), low contrast at the column ends (C7 detection 32.5% automatic, column-model success 56.9%; the upper cervical spine near the jaw), grey-level overlap between body and disc, superimposed bone in scoliosis, degenerative change and osteophytes, film artefacts (border light leakage), and the geometry of projection radiography (10 to 15% variable magnification, beam divergence at the field edges). Ribs, bowel gas and the end-plate "ellipse" (double contour) were not described explicitly in the sources read.

### Cited Findings
- Off-by-one matches: because the spine repeats, the GHT gives shifted matches along the spine and the template alone cannot tell which is the correct vertebra. -- [Zamora et al. 2003 PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf)
- Start-of-sequence ambiguity: geometric constraints between neighbours resolve most ambiguity but it can be unclear whether the lowest vertebra is L4 or L5. -- [Roberts et al. MICCAI 2012 abstract](https://www.springerprofessional.de/automatic-location-of-vertebrae-on-dxa-images-using-random-fores/3978140)
- Fractures: multiple grade 3 fractures cause edge confusion (MICCAI 2012); lumbar radiograph accuracy falls from 0.82 mm (normal) to 1.19 mm (grades 1-2) and 2.12 mm (grade 3) (Osteoporos Int 2012); a single global model is over-constrained for fractures and scoliosis, and fits can snap to the adjacent vertebra's edge, which multiple initialisations reduce (project page). -- [MICCAI 2012 abstract](https://www.springerprofessional.de/automatic-location-of-vertebrae-on-dxa-images-using-random-fores/3978140); [PubMed 21431411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21431411/); [Cootes project page](https://personalpages.manchester.ac.uk/staff/timothy.f.cootes/Projects/VertFrac/vert_frac.html)
- Lumbar films are harder than cervical: GHT+ASM+DM success 75% cervical versus 49% lumbar at 3 mm. -- [Zamora et al. 2003 PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf)
- Cervicothoracic junction (C7): GHT detection 32.5% automatic and 60.0% semi-automatic against 65 to 97.5% for C3 to C6; ASM whole-column model success C7 56.9% and C3 54.9%. -- [IntechOpen chapter 38131](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/38131); [PMC3149802](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3149802)
- Upper cervical spine: near the jaw the anterior slope is less distinct and C3 and C4 often fall close to C2. -- [Long and Thoma PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/system/files/pub2001001.pdf)
- Intensity overlap: an interior C4 region and the adjacent disc space have nearly the same grey statistics (mu 197.4, sigma 2.2 versus mu 198.1, sigma 1.6), so grey-level segmentation alone is not robust; overlapping edges and concealed boundaries complicate measurement. -- [Long and Thoma PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/system/files/pub2001001.pdf)
- Grey-level profile models fail on digitised films: Zamora's plain grey-level ASM model was judged invalid and had to be blended with multi-scale edges. -- [Zamora et al. 2003 PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf)
- Film artefacts: two NHANES II images had bright light leakage along the bottom edge, which put a skull landmark on the border. -- [Long and Thoma PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/system/files/pub2001001.pdf)
- DXA image quality: lateral DXA has low spatial resolution and substantial random and structural noise; poorly located vertebrae are detected by thresholding the grey-level model fit. -- [Smyth et al. IVC record](https://www.datalearner.com/academic/journal-papers/0262-8856/volumes-and-issues/96/paper-detail/86898)
- Cervical radiographs: low contrast or absent edges (example C6), unusually long vertebrae or rare bone structure (C5, C6), variation in neck bending, degenerative change and injury, high noise. -- [Al Arif et al. IPTA 2015](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/g.slabaugh/publications/CervicalCornersIPTA2015.pdf)
- Sagittal full-spine radiographs: noise, occlusion and poor contrast at corners degrade the edge map; bone superimposition in severe lumbar scoliosis; osteophytes and cervical deformities in older subjects; visual ambiguity in corner shape giving up to 4 outliers per vertebra. -- [Ebrahimi et al. author copy](https://sam.ensam.eu/bitstream/10985/15786/3/IBHGC_CMBBEIV_2018_Ebrahimi.pdf)
- Projection geometry: analog radiographs are magnified 10 to 15% with variable magnification; edges perpendicular to the film blur; cone-beam angulation distorts disc spacing at the field edges; the Lunar patent therefore scans a narrow fan beam along the spine and uses the low-angulation centre of the fan. Vertebral tilt and curvature are handled by its rotation step, posterior elements by an analysis rectangle, and crushed bone, hypertrophic end-plates or nodes are flagged to the operator. -- [Justia US5228068](https://patents.justia.com/patent/5228068)
- Initialisation sensitivity is named by nearly every model-based paper: ASM results depend heavily on initialisation (Benjelloun 2011), deformable-model success depends on the template start (Zamora 2003), and NLM's earlier ASM failed to converge even near ground truth (Long and Thoma). -- [PMC3149802](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3149802); [Zamora et al. 2003 PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2003011.pdf); [Long and Thoma PDF](https://lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/system/files/pub2001001.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a training-free intensity method, the critical failures are the ones it cannot resolve from local intensity: which vertebra a body is (needs an anchor), whether a weak ridge is an end-plate or an overlapping rib/organ edge (needs ratio constraints like the Siemens height model), and where the column ends lose contrast (C7, upper cervical, and by analogy the thoracolumbar region under the diaphragm). The dynamic-programming designs with global height models (US7010151) address the first two in principle, but without published validation.
- The end-plate double contour (the two rims of a tilted end-plate projected as an ellipse) is a known radiographic effect in morphometry practice, but none of the sources read here discussed it or measured its effect on automatic end-plate finding; treat it as unquantified.

### Gaps
- No source read here named ribs or bowel gas explicitly as a failure mode, nor the elliptical projection of end-plates ("bean can" or double contour); a targeted search returned only patents and figure pages that were not read.
- No failure-rate breakdown by cause (fracture, overlap, contrast) was found for any training-free method.
