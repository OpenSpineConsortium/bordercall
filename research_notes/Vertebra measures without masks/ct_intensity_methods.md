## 1. Bone thresholds in HU, the half-maximum (FWHM) edge rule, and adaptive/local thresholding (incl. Kang, Engelke, Kalender 2003); osteoporotic bone

### Takeaway
No single HU value defines "bone": published heuristic pipelines use roughly 100 to 130 HU as a permissive "any bone" floor, 200 to 400 HU for dense/cortical bone, and -50 to +50 HU for canal contents, while osteoporotic trabecular bone is routinely below 110 to 160 HU, so a fixed global threshold either leaks into soft tissue and contrast or loses the osteoporotic body. Edge position should not come from a fixed HU but from a relative (about 50 percent, half-maximum) rule between the two adjacent tissue levels, which is only accurate when the structure is thicker than the scanner blur (about 3 mm in clinical CT); thinner cortex needs model fitting (section 2).

### Cited Findings

Fixed HU values used by training-free pipelines

### Inferences
- A workable deterministic rule set from these sources: body = above -500 HU or so (entropy or air cut at -1000 HU per Westerhoff); candidate bone = above about 100 to 130 HU (Radnostics BIF, Westerhoff); dense cortex/end-plate rims = above 200 to 400 HU (Radnostics disc weight); canal = -50 to 50 HU (Radnostics). Then place every measured edge with a relative rule (about 49 to 50 percent of the local step between the two adjacent tissue levels, per Hangartner) rather than a fixed HU, and switch to a blurred-model fit where the bone is thinner than about 3 mm (Treece).
- Because osteoporotic trabecular bone sits at about 100 to 150 HU (Pickhardt; Burns) and contrast-filled vessels can be brighter, the body interior should be found by connectivity from the cortical shell and canal geometry (flood fill inside a closed rim), not by thresholding the trabecular core. Vertebral end-plate and canal chords are measured on the cortical rim, which stays bright (hundreds of HU) even in osteoporosis, so the chords are less threshold-sensitive than volumes.
- Kang 2003's "local adaptive threshold" is most likely a local relative (half-maximum-like) rule, but that was not confirmed: the defining formula and neighbourhood size are only in the full text.

### Gaps
- Kang 2003's exact local threshold definition, neighbourhood size, closing kernel and hole-filling parameters: full text not reachable (IEEE).
- Prevrhal 1999's minimum accurate width relative to the PSF FWHM (often quoted as about twice the FWHM): abstract not read, so no number is reported here.
- No source read gives separately validated HU thresholds for "cortical" vs "trabecular" vertebral bone in clinical CT; the cited 150/200/250/300 HU values in the brief could not each be traced to a primary source in this thread beyond those listed above.
- Effect of kVp and IV contrast on Pickhardt's thresholds: not in the summary read.

## 2. Sub-voxel bone edges by fitting a blurred step or plateau (Treece et al. cortical bone mapping and successors)

### Takeaway
Cortical bone mapping models each 1D intensity profile normal to the bone surface as piecewise-constant tissue levels (soft tissue, cortex, trabecular bone) convolved with a Gaussian point spread function, and fits it by least squares; because blur and cortical density trade off for thin cortex, the problem is stabilised by fixing cortical density (CBM v1, per hip), correcting it with locally estimated blur (CBM v2, 2015), or tying it to thickness through a prior look-up table (Humbert's patent). Reported accuracy against high-resolution references is about 0.1 mm mean error with about 0.4 to 0.6 mm SD over 0.3 to 4 mm cortices, against 0.25 to 0.90 mm mean error for threshold and half-maximum methods.

### Cited Findings
- Treece GM, Gee AH, Mayhew PM, Poole KES. High resolution cortical bone thickness measurement from clinical CT data. Med Image Anal 2010. PMID 20163980 (from the PMC link in search results). Volume 14(3), pages 276-290 and doi:10.1016/j.media.2010.01.003 are [memory]. Full text not read (PMC bot check). — [PMC link](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/20163980)
- The 2009 technical report of that work (Treece, Gee, Poole, CUED/F-INFENG/TR634) [abstract]: fits "a mathematical model of the anatomy and the imaging system" at many sites over the proximal femur, about 17,000 thickness estimates per specimen; sub-1 mm cortices lie within the scanner PSF; 16 cadaveric femurs; thickness error over cortices 0.3 to 4 mm = -0.01 +/- 0.58 mm (model) versus 0.25 +/- 0.69 mm (simple threshold) and 0.90 +/- 0.92 mm (50 percent relative threshold variant); about 15 minutes per femur on a modest laptop. — [TR634 abstract](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/reports/abstracts/treece_tr634.html)
- Treece, Poole, Gee 2011/2012 (CUED/F-INFENG/TR665, 29 Apr 2011; published as "Imaging the femoral cortex: thickness, density and mass from clinical CT", Med Image Anal 2012, PMC3417239; volume 16(5):952-965 [memory]) [abstract]: the earlier method was ill-posed and stabilised by assuming a fixed cortical density; this version estimates that constant density more rigorously and adds cortical mass (mineral per cm^2); 16 cadaveric femurs vs micro-CT; peak cortical density and cortical mass are measured reliably; thickness errors arise mainly where cortex is thin and are limited by how far local density departs from the peak; for 0.5 mm cortex the average thickness error is about 20 percent. — [TR665 abstract](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/reports/abstracts/treece_tr665.html)
- Treece GM, Gee AH. Independent measurement of femoral cortical thickness and cortical bone density using clinical CT. Med Image Anal 2015;20:249-264. doi:10.1016/j.media.2014.11.012 [abstract]: uses local estimates of imaging blur to correct the global density estimate, giving local cortical density, more accurate thickness, cortical mass surface density and adjacent trabecular density; ex vivo clinical QCT of proximal femurs vs HR-pQCT of the same bones; thickness "accuracy 0.12 +/- 0.39 mm" for cortices 1 to 3 mm; local density estimation feasible only above 800 mg/cm^3. — [Cambridge repository item](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/6d6be37d-98f8-479a-8450-30df363c10f7); [TR691 abstract](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/reports/abstracts/treece_tr691.html). Pages 249-264 confirmed by the citation in Turmezei 2016.
- Attribution conflict: Turmezei et al. 2016 attribute "0.12+/-0.39 mm" to Treece, Poole and Gee 2012 (their ref. 18), whereas the TR691 abstract gives the same figure for the 2015 method. — [Turmezei 2016 PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/75866/1/Published_Version.pdf) vs [TR691](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/reports/abstracts/treece_tr691.html)
- Explicit model formula (from a successor patent, US 10,360,681 B2, Humbert L; Galgo Medical, now 3D-Shaper Medical; priority 2015-05-28) [full]: y_mod(x) = y0 + (y1 - y0)/2 * (1 + erf((x - (x1 - t/2)) / (sigma*sqrt(2)))) + (y2 - y1)/2 * (1 + erf((x - (x1 + t/2)) / (sigma*sqrt(2)))), where y0, y1, y2 are densities of surrounding tissue, cortex and trabecular bone, t the cortical thickness, x1 the centre of the cortex and sigma the SD of the Gaussian blur. Fitted by Levenberg-Marquardt with free parameters y0, y1, y2, t, x1 (claims say blur is also searched), and at each iteration y1 is set from t through a thickness-density look-up table. — [Google Patents US10360681B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10360681B2/en)
- That look-up table is a learned prior (not a network): built from 23 cadaveric proximal femurs (8 female, 15 male) micro-CT scanned at 0.082 mm isotropic; per specimen a threshold, 3D closing and hole filling make a mesh, 100 points are sampled along each node normal, the model is fitted with y1 fixed at "the maximum value observed in the density profile", and node results are averaged over specimens. Leave-one-out on simulated low-resolution volumes: 0.33 x 0.33 x 1.0 mm voxels, cortex 0.3 to 6.0 mm, thickness 0.08 +/- 0.21 mm and density -12 +/- 93 mg/cm^3; 1.0 x 1.0 x 3.0 mm voxels, thickness 0.10 +/- 0.25 mm and density -11 +/- 121 mg/cm^3; thin cortex 0.3 to 1.0 mm at 0.33 mm voxels 0.12 +/- 0.16 mm; thick 3.0 to 6.0 mm at 0.33 mm voxels -0.04 +/- 0.32 mm. Compared in its Table 1 with Treece's "CBM v3" on different samples. — [Google Patents US10360681B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10360681B2/en)
- Stradview (Treece's free software) implementation notes [documentation]: the measurement line runs along the outer-surface normal into the bone; line length should be "approximately three times the thickness of the thickest cortex"; line width zero (no orthogonal averaging) for clinical CT, larger for noisy cone-beam or micro-CT; for CBM v1 cortical density is held constant once "Estimate cortical density" is pressed, and for CBM v2 the in-plane and out-of-plane FWHM are fixed at that point too; options include "Use thresholding" (instead of model-based deconvolution, suggested for very high-resolution data), "Use local peak as density" (FWHM), "Post correct density for blur" (CBM v2), "metal", and an endocortical model; outputs per vertex thickness, density, mass and an estimated SD per measurement, invalid measurements set to 0 with error 1e10; warns that measurement fails or inner surfaces overlap on highly curved surfaces with thick cortex, and that the best results need a prior estimate of unblurred cortical HU. — [Stradview: Cortical Thickness Mapping](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/~gmt11/stradview/thickness.htm)
- Stradview procedure [documentation]: start from a smooth approximate outer surface within "a couple of mm" of the true surface (ideally outside the cortex), regularised marching tetrahedra for even vertex spacing; estimate cortical density and blur globally first; a cortical density "much less than 1000 HU" likely means thickness is overestimated; for clinical CT start with "CBM v2"; reject outliers by angle and offset thresholds, then smooth. — [Stradview: How-to CBM](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/~gmt11/stradview/howto_cbm.htm)
- Applied use (Turmezei TD, Treece GM, Gee AH, Fotiadou AF, Poole KES. Eur Radiol 2016;26:2047-2054, doi:10.1007/s00330-015-4048-x) [full]: semi-automatic segmentation and mesh generation in Stradwin (most of about 15 minutes per map), thickness at each of about 6,000 vertices along the surface normal, the algorithm "selects the innermost of two layers as the true cortex", registration to a canonical femur by B-spline free-form deformation in wxRegSurf, 10 mm FWHM smoothing for SPM; reported precision 5 percent of the mean at single points and 1 percent over a region; validated for clinical CT "against high resolution quantitative CT data acquired at 82 microns per pixel" and applied at "a slice separation of 3 mm". — [Turmezei 2016 PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/75866/1/Published_Version.pdf)

### Inferences
- For vertebral chords the same fit can be run along 1D profiles crossing the end plates (craniocaudal), the anterior/posterior/lateral body walls and the canal walls; the fitted cortex outer edge x1 - t/2 (or x1 + t/2, whichever faces outwards) gives the sub-voxel surface position, which is what a caliper chord between opposite surfaces needs. This is deterministic given fixed initial values and a deterministic optimiser.
- Vertebral end plates and canal walls are often under 1 mm thick, inside the regime where thickness is poorly determined; but the outer surface position (what the chord needs) is better conditioned than thickness, because the outer edge is the large soft-tissue-to-bone step. This was not tested in any source read and should be checked on phantoms.
- With thick slices (3 mm), sigma differs in-plane and out-of-plane, so craniocaudal profiles (heights) need the out-of-plane blur; CBM v2 handles in-plane and out-of-plane FWHM separately (Stradview), which a reimplementation should copy.
- Humbert's prior table is a trained component (derived from 23 femurs), so it does not meet a strict "no training" rule; Treece CBM v1/v2 estimate density from the scan itself and do.

### Gaps
- Treece 2010 and 2012 full texts (exact profile length, sampling step, initial values, optimiser, the CBM v1 density estimator, the CBM v2 blur estimator as a function of profile angle to the slice plane) were not read; PMC and Europe PMC were blocked and the TR PDF links were not resolvable.
- No source read applies CBM to vertebrae with validation; all accuracy numbers above are proximal femur.
- The "wxRegSurf" tool was only mentioned as the registration step; no documentation was read.

## 3. Spinal canal detection and tracking without a trained model, and the curved centreline

### Takeaway
Training-free canal finders all exploit the canal as a dark, roughly round, bone-enclosed hole posterior to the body: Yao et al. 2006 threshold the spine and then extract the canal with a watershed plus directed graph search; the Radnostics patent finds it per slice with a -50 to 50 HU map, flood fill and shape tests seeded from the spinous-process tips; Banik/Rangayyan detect it with a Hough circle transform on an edge map plus opening-by-reconstruction. Centrelines are then polylines of per-slice canal centroids, cleaned by regression and extended, or polynomial spine curves fitted by optimisation (Vrtovec).

### Inferences
- A deterministic canal tracker can combine the Radnostics seed (posterior spinous tip, then search anteriorly 10 to 80 mm for a -50 to 50 HU enclosed region) with per-slice flood fill bounded by the bony ring; the canal's ring closure fails at the foramina and between laminae, so per-slice flood fill needs a maximum area or a convex-hull/closing guard, and slices where it leaks should be bridged by interpolation along the centreline.
- The canal centreline (not the body centreline) is the most robust curved axis in CT because the canal is a low-HU hole in a high-HU ring at every level from C1 to the sacrum, and its centroid is insensitive to osteoporosis; the body centreline can be derived from it by an anterior offset along the local sagittal direction.
- Canal width (left-right) and AP depth chords can then be measured in planes orthogonal to that centreline, with edges placed by the half-maximum rule or the blurred-step fit (sections 1 and 2).

- Yao 2006's thresholds, watershed parameters and graph-search cost were not read (closed IEEE paper).
- Archip 2002 and Rangayyan/Deglint 2006 method parameters (Hough radius range, fuzzy connectivity thresholds) were not read.
- No source read reports automated canal width or AP depth on CT compared with manual measurement without a trained model; the automated canal measurement papers found by search were MRI.

## 4. Partitioning the column into vertebrae: disc-space minima along a curved axis, curved planar reformation, deformable fences; which are training-free

### Takeaway
The training-free partitioning methods all find intervertebral discs as intensity valleys between bony peaks along a curved axis: Yao 2006 on curved sagittal/coronal reformations, Radnostics on a 1D "bone weight" profile with moving-window rules, Kim and Kim 2009 with valley-emphasised Gaussian images and deformable fences, and Stern 2010 from spine geometry. Klinder 2009, Korez 2015 and every method in the 2016 multi-centre challenge rely on trained shape or appearance models and so are contrasts, not candidates.

### Cited Findings
- Yao 2006: curved sagittal and coronal reformations along the cord, aggregated intensity profiles, 2 partition errors in 69 extracted columns (71 scans). — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FISBI.2006.1624935) [abstract]
- Radnostics disc localisation (US8891848B2) [full]: a rectangular parametric curve along the expected vertebral-body centres from typical dimensions; per cross-section weight = Frequency(200 <= HU < 400) + 4 x Frequency(HU >= 400); discs are dips between spikes; moving average, moving maximum and moving minimum over a window of about 40 mm ("the height of a vertebra plus adjacent discs"); classes in order: extreme spike > ~60 percent of moving max, spike > ~33 percent of moving max, high > ~2x moving average, min at moving min (threshold ~1), depression < ~5 above moving min, then above/below average; ordered rules (listed in claim 4) mark "a depression between two spikes" as a disc; after each rule a gap analysis flags gaps > 150 percent of the local average gap (75 percent from the border for end discs), and the process repeats until no new discs; two discs closer than ~40 percent of the window, the wider is dropped. Each disc is then bounded by a cuboid limited by noise-resilient level-set "flexible surfaces" and flood-filled with 1-NN voting at threshold min[100 HU, mean + 2 SD]. — [Google Patents US8891848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8891848B2/en)

### Inferences
- The Radnostics 1D profile rules are the most directly reimplementable training-free partitioner found; Yao 2006 and Kim and Kim 2009 describe the same idea (disc = valley between bony peaks along the column axis) with less published detail.
- Sampling the profile along the canal-derived curved axis (section 3) rather than a straight z line copes with lordosis/kyphosis and scoliosis; with 5 mm slices (Yao 2006) a disc of 5 to 10 mm can fall within one or two samples, so valley detection should use the 3D profile through the body centre, not single slices.
- The disc mid-plane found this way gives the cranial and caudal limits of each vertebra; end-plate chords are then measured on planes parallel to and just inside those limits.

### Gaps
- Klinder 2009's identification rate and segmentation error, Kim and Kim 2009's success rates and fence parameters, and Vrtovec 2005 "Automated curved planar reformation of 3D spine images" (Phys Med Biol) could not be read.
- Training-free partition accuracy against manual disc levels, in mm, exists only as Stern 2010's 2.8 +/- 1.9 mm (vertebral body and disc centres combined).

## 5. Parametric models fitted to intensities: Stern, Likar, Pernus, Vrtovec 2011 superquadric vertebral bodies

### Takeaway
Stern 2011 fits a 3D superquadric, started as an elliptical cylinder and progressively deformed, with 25 clinically meaningful shape parameters plus 6 rigid pose parameters, to vertebral bodies in CT and MR (75 + 75 thoracolumbar vertebrae); its 2013 follow-up used the fitted parameters directly as morphometry (height, end-plate inclination and concavity) to classify 682 bodies. The abstracts give no dimension errors in mm against manual measurement.

### Cited Findings
- Stern D, Likar B, Pernus F, Vrtovec T. Parametric modelling and segmentation of vertebral bodies in 3D CT and MR spine images. Phys Med Biol 2011;56(23):7505-7522. doi:10.1088/0031-9155/56/23/011. PMID 22080628 [abstract]: superquadric initialised as an elliptical cylinder and gradually deformed; shape described by 25 clinically meaningful parameters and pose by 6 rigid-body parameters; aligned to the observed body in the 3D image; 75 vertebrae from CT and 75 from T2-weighted MR, thoracolumbar, normal and pathological; the abstract reports no numeric accuracy, only that the method "can be used for 3D segmentation of vertebral bodies in CT and MR images", and suggests it may initialise whole-vertebra segmentation or measure body deformations. — [PubMed 22080628](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22080628/); [Semantic Scholar record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1088/0031-9155/56/23/011?fields=title,abstract,venue,year,authors,externalIds,journal)
- Stern D, Njagulj V, Likar B, Pernus F, Vrtovec T. Quantitative vertebral morphometry based on parametric modeling of vertebral bodies in 3D. Osteoporos Int 2013 (issue 1 April 2013), doi:10.1007/s00198-012-2089-4 [abstract]: the parametric 3D model is automatically aligned to vertebral bodies in CT; its parameters are compared with statistical reference values, and statistical classification sets thresholds; 454 normal and 228 fractured bodies; reported "sensitivity 92.5% at 7.5% specificity" (as rendered by the page reader; probably 7.5 percent false-positive rate, not confirmed), accuracy 92.5 percent, precision 86.1 percent; best-separating parameters were vertebral body height and the inclination and concavity of both end plates; classifies wedge, biconcave and crush types and grades 1 to 3. Volume 24(4):1357-1368 [memory]. — [Novi Sad repository record](https://open.uns.ac.rs/handle/123456789/9029)
- A related SPIE 2012 paper, "Automatic measurement of vertebral body deformations in CT images based on a 3D parametric model" (Proc SPIE 8315, 831510, doi:10.1117/12.911107), exists but its page returned a bot check. — [SPIE page](https://photonicsforenergy.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/8315/831510/Automatic-measurement-of-vertebral-body-deformations-in-CT-images-based/10.1117/12.911107.full) [not read]

### Inferences
- Because the superquadric's size parameters are the body's half-axes (an elliptical cylinder's radii and height before deformation), end-plate width and depth and body height can be read off the fitted parameters without any mask, which is exactly what the 2013 study did for height. Whether the 2011 fit's objective is intensity- or gradient-based, and whether it needs a manually given body centre, could not be confirmed; Stern 2010's automated body centres (section 3) are the natural training-free initialiser from the same group.
- The 2013 classification thresholds are learned from labelled data, but the morphometric parameters themselves come from a model fit, so the measurement part is training-free as far as the abstracts say.

### Gaps
- The 2011 superquadric equation, the meaning of each of the 25 parameters, the fitting criterion and optimiser, the initialisation, and any mm error of dimensions versus manual measurement: full text not reachable (IOP PDF robots-disallowed; PubMed abstract gives no numbers).
- The 2013 paper's agreement of automated heights with manual heights: not in the abstract read.

## 6. Automated heights, widths, depths and canal dimensions from CT in opportunistic screening and morphometry, and agreement with manual measurement

### Takeaway
The automated CT morphometry systems found report fracture detection and grading, not mm agreement of chords with manual or caliper values: the NIH (Summers) system's "height compass" divides each body into zones matching Genant's scheme (Burns 2017: 95.7 percent detection sensitivity, 0.68 grade accuracy), Stern 2013 classifies from superquadric parameters, and Radnostics checks heights only as pass/fail. No study read reports automated end-plate width/depth, posterior body height or canal width/depth versus manual in mm without a trained model.

### Cited Findings
- Burns JE, Yao J, Summers RM. Vertebral body compression fractures and bone density: automated detection and classification on CT images. Radiology 2017;284(3) (online 16 Mar 2017), doi:10.1148/radiol.2017162100 [abstract]: 150 patients (75 cases, 75 age- and sex-matched controls), 210 marked thoracic/lumbar fractures; vertebral bodies divided into two lateral zones (R, L) and three central coronal zones (A, C, P) to match Genant; the "height compass" is a central sector with two concentric ring bands of eight sectors; detection sensitivity 95.7 percent (201 of 210; 95 percent CI 87.0, 98.9) with 0.29 false positives per patient; case-level sensitivity 98.7 percent, specificity 77.3 percent; Genant type accuracy 0.95 (107/113), weighted kappa 0.90; Genant grade accuracy 0.68 (77/113), weighted kappa 0.59; a support vector machine curve is shown (so the classifier is trained), and the system is called an "automated machine learning computer system". Pages 788-797 [memory]. — [Radiology](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/10.1148/radiol.2017162100)
- Stern 2013 (section 5): fracture classification from parametric-model height and end-plate inclination/concavity, 682 bodies. — [Novi Sad repository record](https://open.uns.ac.rs/handle/123456789/9029) [abstract]
- Radnostics (US8891848B2) computes no numeric heights: an observer grades whether the mid-sagittal segmentation gives adequate anterior, central and posterior heights for fracture assessment (83.3 percent adequate on 7,777 vertebrae; 85.4 percent using the best of 3 sagittal planes in a 219-study, 1,641-vertebra subset, per the text). — [Google Patents US8891848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8891848B2/en) [full]
- The 2016 challenge reports that vertebral-body Dice (0.936 average) was better than other substructures (0.852 average), i.e. the body is the most reliably delineated part for chord measurement, while process tips were incompletely segmented. — [Yao 2016, eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5dj0636d/qt5dj0636d.pdf) [full]

### Inferences
- A deterministic pipeline from these sources: (1) body crop and canal seed (Radnostics); (2) canal centreline (Radnostics/Yao); (3) disc valleys along the curved axis (Radnostics/Yao/Kim); (4) per-vertebra local frame from the canal tangent and the left-right symmetry plane (Vrtovec's mirror-half registration); (5) in that frame, profiles in the mid-sagittal plane give posterior body height (posterior wall, between the two end-plate surfaces) and end-plate depth (AP), profiles in the mid-coronal/axial plane just inside each end plate give end-plate width; (6) in the axial plane through the pedicles, canal width and AP depth; (7) each profile edge placed by half-maximum where the structure is thick and by the blurred-step fit (Treece; Humbert formula) where it is thin. Every step is deterministic. Agreement with manual Panjabi chords would have to be measured, since no published equivalent was found.
- Superquadric fitting (Stern 2011) is an alternative to steps 5 and 7 for the body, giving width, depth and height as model parameters; it does not give the canal.

### Gaps
- Panjabi's exact landmark definitions for EPWu, EPDu, EPWl, EPDl, VBHp, SCW and SCD (Spine 1991 and 1992) were not retrieved in this thread; the one morphometry page fetched (Shangloo et al. 2021, J Res Appl Basic Med Sci 7(4):195-199, vernier calipers on 100 dry lumbar vertebrae) did not define them. — [Shangloo 2021 record](https://ijrabms.umsu.ac.ir/article-171.xml)
- No source read gives automated-versus-manual agreement (bias, limits of agreement, ICC) for CT vertebral chords or canal dimensions from a training-free method.
- The SPIE height-compass paper (Yao J, Burns JE, et al. 2012, Proc SPIE 8315) could not be read (oa.mg server error).

## 7. Typical failure modes: fused or bridged vertebrae (DISH), metal, osteoporosis, low-dose noise, contrast, partial volume with thick slices, sacrum and C1/C2

### Takeaway
The documented failures are osteoporosis and fractures (Dice falling from 0.87 to 0.95 healthy to 0.47 to 0.90 osteoporotic), the upper thoracic spine at the costovertebral junctions, contrast approaching osteoporotic bone density, thin cortex blurred below about 3 mm (FWHM and thresholds fail, model fits lose accuracy below 1 mm), the T1-T2 and lowest levels lost to cropping, and abutting calcification or vessels; DISH/fusion, metal, low-dose noise, the sacrum and C1/C2 are not quantified in any source read.

### Cited Findings
- Osteoporosis/fractures: all five challenge methods were worse on the osteoporotic set (Dice 0.472 to 0.897 vs 0.868 to 0.947 healthy; maximum surface distance up to 44 mm vs 22 mm); one method leaked into the disc space on fractured vertebrae; one, whose bone prior was trained on healthy vertebrae, failed to segment 34 of 85 vertebrae. — [Yao 2016, eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5dj0636d/qt5dj0636d.pdf) [full]
- Upper thoracic/ribs: mean Dice T1-T6 was 0.867 (healthy) and 0.652 (osteoporotic), versus lumbar 0.933 / 0.854; interfaces are "more complex at the upper thoracic level", particularly the costovertebral junctions; one method "had trouble locating the endplates in the thoracic spine". — [Yao 2016, eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5dj0636d/qt5dj0636d.pdf)
- Contrast: contrast "may cause problems in osteoporotic cases" because bone density can approach contrast density; some contrast-enhanced vessels were included in two methods; no aortic leakage seen. — [Yao 2016, eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5dj0636d/qt5dj0636d.pdf)
- Slice thickness/partial volume: the challenge scans were 0.7 to 2.0 mm; accuracy fell slightly with thicker slices (one method 96.3 percent at 0.7 mm vs 95.7 percent at 1 mm healthy; another 90.6 percent at 1 mm vs 89.3 percent at 2 mm diseased); partial volume was discussed only as a source of gradient variation. — [Yao 2016, eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5dj0636d/qt5dj0636d.pdf). Cortex under about 3 mm cannot be measured by threshold or FWHM in clinical CT. — [TR691](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/reports/abstracts/treece_tr691.html). Local density estimation is infeasible below 800 mg/cm^3. — [TR691](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/reports/abstracts/treece_tr691.html)
- Partitioning at the column ends: Yao 2006's one partition error included a missed T1-T2 level. — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109%2FISBI.2006.1624935). Radnostics' inferior crop "may" remove L5, end discs use a separate gap rule (75 percent from the border), and spinous tips from ribs must be removed. — [Google Patents US8891848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8891848B2/en)
- Abutting structures: Radnostics removes structures abutting the body, "e.g., aortic calcification", with a ray-based connectivity test toward the estimated body centre, then 1-NN voting flood fill; scoliosis is handled by tolerances in tip alignment and centreline regression. — [Google Patents US8891848B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8891848B2/en)
- Noise: in Kang 2003, noise increased by factors of two and five changed cortical thickness by one voxel side length. — [PubMed 12846428](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12846428/) [abstract]
- Metal: Stradview has a "metal" option that "accounts for metal in CT data" (no method described). — [Stradview thickness page](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/~gmt11/stradview/thickness.htm)
- Curvature: thickness fitting may fail or give overlapping inner surfaces where the surface is highly curved and the cortex thick relative to the curvature. — [Stradview thickness page](https://mi.eng.cam.ac.uk/~gmt11/stradview/thickness.htm)
- Fractures can violate size-based bone criteria in threshold pipelines. — [Google Patents US10706538B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10706538B2/en)

### Inferences
- DISH and ankylosis: bridging osteophytes fill the disc valley with bone along the anterior/right-lateral column, so a 1D bone-weight profile through the body centre loses its dip; sampling the profile through the posterior third of the body or the canal-adjacent posterior wall (away from anterior bridging) and requiring the vertebral-height periodicity (the Radnostics 40 mm window and gap rules) should still place the level, but true fusion leaves no disc to find and needs an expected-height prior.
- Metal (pedicle screws, cages) produces streaks well above 1000 HU and dark bands; any threshold-and-flood-fill step should mask voxels above about 2500 to 3000 HU and their streak neighbourhood before profiles are taken (not tested in any source read).
- Sacrum and C1/C2 break the body-plus-disc periodicity (no disc below S1 within the fused sacrum, no body at C1, the dens at C2); a training-free partitioner needs special-case rules there; none was found.
- Low-dose noise raises the SD of the canal (-50 to 50 HU) and disc (min[100 HU, mean + 2 SD]) maps; the Radnostics R = 1 mean filter and the >50 percent-of-voxels vote along z are its noise guards; a profile fit (Treece) averages noise over the profile but its thickness SD grows.

### Gaps
- No source read quantified failure for DISH, ankylosing spondylitis, metal implants, low-dose CT, the sacrum or C1/C2 for any training-free method.
- The Radnostics specification beyond character 100,000 (possibly further failure analysis) was not read.
