# Prior art: the spinal canal and the foramina (canal_w, canal_ap, canal_area, canal_index, interpedicular, lateral_recess, foramen_h, foramen_w, foramen_area)

Source: the thread "Plan clinical reread next steps", 2026-10-29, web reading (WebSearch and WebFetch from a cloud container; the publisher pages of Springer, Nature, MDPI and RSNA refused the fetch, so each such paper was read from PMC, Europe PMC, arXiv, Zenodo, GitHub or a challenge page, and the tag on each claim says what was read). The family is the nine keys above from bordercall's vocabulary.csv: Panjabi's canal width (the transverse diameter at the pedicles), canal depth (the midline anteroposterior diameter) and canal area, and six keys Panjabi did not measure: a canal ratio (Torg-Pavlov or a shape index), the interpedicular distance, the lateral recess, and the foraminal height, width and area. Automatic stenosis grading is swept too, since most of the deep-learning literature on the canal and the foramina grades rather than measures.

Reading tags, as the existing notes use them: [full] the paper read whole from an open copy; [abstract] only the abstract or the PubMed record; [page] a dataset, challenge or code page; [code] a repository read. Nothing is copied from any source beyond a number, a name and a link; no PDF, image or dataset was put into any repository.

Reviewed on 2026-10-30 (thread "Finish the clinical reread", project symphony_5): a second reader checked 290 claims at their sources and found 12 wrong, unsupported or imprecise; a refuter held all 12, of which 11 are corrected here and one, the README's count of public test sets, in the README. Sources read in part before were read in full (SPIDER and the LSS-MRI-AISSLab paper from PMC, Phadke 2026, SpineNetV2 and Lu 2018 on ar5iv), and the licences of four code bases were read from their LICENSE files. A second sweep added eight rows (Gaonkar 2019, Nixon 2025, Huang 2025 in the European Spine Journal, Payne 2024, TotalSpineSeg, SPINEPS, Kareem 2026 and the external-testing archive "Same Patients, Different Verdicts"), each read again at its source by a verifier, who returned a ninth, Bharadwaj 2022, as a fact, since its method is tabled through Bharadwaj 2023. The record is [review/](review/README.md).

## 1. What the existing notes already say

- SchwingNet docs/research/morphometry-beyond-the-42-papers.md, the table "Dry, boiled bone measures like CT or fluoroscopy": on CTSpinoPelvic1K's own labels, canal width and canal depth were within a millimetre of Panjabi 1992 at L1 and L3, while posterior body height ran high; so the two Panjabi canal chords are the measures the current estimator already reproduces best. The same note's rows for Shin 2024 (J Am Acad Orthop Surg 32(22):e1186; 1,000 patients aged 18 to 35 on CT, L1 to L5 interpedicular distance, pedicle length, canal AP diameter and canal area, by sex, race and ethnicity, measured by hand, no file), Griffith 2016 (CT of 1,080 Hong Kong patients, the lumbar canal from L1 to L5, a seed-growing program corrected by hand), Pierro 2017 (MRI of 604, lumbar canal depth), Yukawa 2012 (radiographs of 1,230 Japanese adults, cervical canal diameter from C2 to C7 by sex and decade) and Nell 2019 (SHIP, 2,453, cervical canal and body depth) are the large normative series for the canal; Shin 2025 (Brain Spine 2025;5:104162; CT of 700 young patients, disc height and foraminal size from L1-L2 to L5-S1) is the one large foraminal series among the 42. All are manual measurements but Griffith 2016's, a seed-growing program corrected by hand ("a semi-automated computerized method", PMC5219964) [full]; none publishes its images, and none is a method with a public test set.
- The same note's table under "The assumptions", row "Healthy middle-aged spines stand for everyone": lumbar canal size differs by sex, race and ethnicity in Shin 2024's 1,000 people aged 18 to 35, so a level named from a canal chord would need covariates the owner has set to zero.
- bordercall's "Vertebra measures without masks/ct_intensity_methods.md", section 3: training-free canal finders (Yao 2006, the Radnostics patent US 8,891,848, Banik and Rangayyan 2010) exploit the canal as a dark hole in a bright ring; the canal centreline is the most robust curved axis in CT; canal width and AP depth can then be measured in planes orthogonal to it. Its finding that matters here, section 3 and section 6: "No source read reports automated canal width or AP depth on CT compared with manual measurement without a trained model; the automated canal measurement papers found by search were MRI", and "no source read gives automated-versus-manual agreement (bias, limits of agreement, ICC) for CT vertebral chords or canal dimensions from a training-free method". This sweep confirms both for trained models too, with one exception (Zhou 2024, section 3 below).
- bordercall's masks_corners_magnification.md, section 2: AASCE 2019 stores four corners per vertebra on AP films, BUU-LSPINE per-vertebra corners on AP and lateral films; neither carries canal or foraminal truth. radiograph_classical_methods.md: NHANES II's truth is radiologists' morphometric points (corners and mid-points) on cervical and lumbar films; no canal measure.
- benchmarks-to-beat.md and public-spine-and-pelvis-models-to-benchmark.md say nothing of the canal or the foramina (grep for canal, foramen, interpedicular, stenosis found no line), so those families have no benchmark on the project's list yet.

## 2. The datasets that carry truth for this family

- SPIDER (van der Graaf et al., Scientific Data 2024;11:264; arXiv 2306.12217) [full, from the arXiv v3 PDF and PMC10908819]: 447 sagittal T1 and T2 series from 218 patients with low back pain at four Dutch hospitals; reference masks of the vertebrae, the discs and the spinal canal (447 canal segmentations); CC BY 4.0 on Zenodo (doi 10.5281/zenodo.10159290; the record 10159224 holds only overview.csv and radiological_gradings.csv) [page]. The vertebrae are "labelled from the bottom up with the most caudal lumbar vertebra labelled as 1", and the paper says this "should not be interpreted as an anatomical label", since a lumbosacral transitional vertebra or a sixth lumbar vertebra could not be told apart on the sagittal field of view; the baseline's anatomical-label output "was not used in our experiments since no accurate anatomical labels regarding lumbosacral transitional vertebrae were present in our dataset". The canal mask is of the whole canal as seen on sagittal MRI (the paper names it spinal canal, not dural sac). Benchmark on a sequestered test set of 39 studies (97 series) at spider.grand-challenge.org: Dice 0.93 T1 and 0.92 T2 for the vertebrae, 0.85 and 0.84 for the discs, 0.93 and 0.92 for the spinal canal (0.93, 0.84 and 0.92 over both; canal ASD 0.29 mm T1, 0.46 mm T2) for the iterative baseline (Table 4); nnU-Net 0.92, 0.86 and 0.92 over both, canal ASD 0.37 mm (Table 5) [full, PMC10908819]; code at github.com/DIAGNijmegen/SPIDER-Baseline-IIS, Apache-2.0 by its LICENSE file, its README naming no training set and no weights [code], and the nnU-Net baseline. Radiological gradings per disc (Modic, endplate change, spondylolisthesis, herniation, narrowing, bulging, Pfirrmann) but no stenosis grade and no canal or foraminal measurement in mm. What it lacks for naming levels: no anatomical level labels at all, by design; MRI only; lumbar only; no measurement truth.
- RSNA 2024 Lumbar Spine Degenerative Classification, the LumbarDISC dataset (Richards, Flanders et al., arXiv 2506.09162) [abstract]; the RSNA wiki and the AWS Open Data registry [page]: 2,697 patients, 8,593 series (sagittal T1, sagittal T2/STIR, axial T2) from 8 institutions (the AWS page says twelve), about 60 volunteer radiologists by the AWS page; at each disc from L1/L2 to L5/S1 a grade normal_mild, moderate or severe for spinal canal stenosis, left and right neural foraminal narrowing, and left and right subarticular (lateral recess) stenosis, with the x,y pixel centre of each graded site per series and instance; non-commercial, no redistribution (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 on the arXiv page), on Kaggle and RSNA MIRA. 1,874 teams competed. A 2026 Zenodo archive, "Same Patients, Different Verdicts" (23233755, anonymous authors, article under review, code MIT) [page], trained foraminal and lateral recess graders on LumbarDISC, tested them on AISSLab's sagittal and axial sets with a blinded Lee-criteria re-read of the external foramina, and removed the LumbarDISC reference labels in accordance with the RSNA 2024 challenge terms, consistent with the no-redistribution term above. It is the largest public truth for this family, but it is ordinal grades and one point per site, not a chord or an area in mm; no inter-reader agreement is published; lumbar only; the disc levels are the radiologists' clinical names, so a transitional vertebra is numbered as the reader chose, which the dataset does not record.
- LSS-MRI-AISSLab (github.com/AISSLab2025/LSS-MRI-AISSLab-Dataset [code page]; Abdulmahmod et al., Sci Data 2026;13:809, s41597-026-07138-x, PMC13222877, PMID 41957051, article CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 [full, from Europe PMC]): 500 patients aged 23 to 91, one examination each, 8,500 sagittal T2 lumbar slices (Philips Ingenia 1.5 T or 3 T, GE Signa HDxt 1.5 T, GE Signa Excite 1.5 T); masks of vertebral bodies, discs, sacrum and two posterior-element classes (Posterior A and B) on 500 mid-sagittal slices, seeded by a SegResNet trained on 515 mid-sagittal slices of the public Sudirman set and validated and refined by three expert neurosurgeons; 2,979 foraminal boxes (1,396 right, 1,583 left; 469 patients with at least one, 31 with none) drawn in LabelImg by three Firat University neurosurgeons with 3, 11 and 26 years of experience (Figure 1's caption calls them expert neurologists) at L1-L2 to L5-S1, left and right, each with a four-grade perineural-fat score (normal 2,009, mild 508, moderate 254, severe 208 from the GitHub level table; 67.45, 17.06, 8.53 and 6.99% in the article); the GitHub About line's 3,885 boxes appear in neither the article nor the Mendeley page; no numbering rule, no transitional mention and no inter-annotator agreement; the authors' pipeline reached 86% slice selection, 90% ROI localisation and 65% severity accuracy. Data on Mendeley (10.17632/rgb77xm3jf.4, version 4 of 10 February 2026), licensed CC BY 4.0 while its own description says the collection is for a scientific, noncommercial purpose, a conflict on the page [page]; the code's licence is not stated; no mm foraminal dimension. Phadke et al. (J Imaging 2026;12(8):388; PMC13514485) [full] report on 468 of its 469 expert-annotated patients (2,978 foramina; test 94 patients, 580 foramina) an ImageNet ResNet-18 fused through an ordinal head with ten morphometric descriptors (disc height and its ratio to vertebral height, upper and lower body heights, listhesis offset and its ratio to vertebral depth, segmental angulation, disc AP width, two disc-centroid distances to the posterior elements), reaching QWK 0.813 and 75.3% four-class accuracy against the expert grade, prior pipelines about 65% on another partition, with the experts' foraminal boxes given, so localisation is not measured; code at github.com/rohanpa27/LSS_foraminal_stenosis_imaging, MIT [code], with the patient-level split. Its truth is one expert reader's four grades, and it does not mention transitional vertebrae.
- The Lumbar Spine MRI Dataset (Sudirman, Al Kafri et al., Mendeley 10.17632/k57fr854j2.2 with label images 10.17632/zbf6b4pttk.2) [page]: 515 symptomatic patients, axial T1 slices of the lowest three discs, label images of the disc, the posterior element, the thecal sac and the "area between anterior and posterior elements", drawn by five labellers under a radiologist; CC BY 4.0; no mm truth, no level labels beyond "the last three discs". It is the training set of Saravi 2023, Ghobrial 2025 and Wu 2024 below.
- Kareem, Adnan and Shabat 2026 (University of Sumer; Zenodo 23298064, 814 kB, CC BY 4.0, deposited 11 October 2026) [page]: central canal stenosis grades at L3-L4, L4-L5 and L5-S1 for the Sudirman set's 515 patients, derived from its expert dural-sac masks, read from its radiology notes by rules and a language model, and blind re-labelled with adjudication by a board-certified radiologist; no images. The record lists as its sources the dataset (10.17632/k57fr854j2.2), its radiologists' notes (10.17632/s6bgczr8s2.2), its label images (10.17632/zbf6b4pttk.2) and its MATLAB ground-truth code (10.17632/8cp2cp7km8.2). The companion code (Zenodo 23298032, MIT, 17 Colab notebooks) trains an ordinal conformal grader on RSNA 2024 and tests it on Irbid and on an Iranian set, LSpineSMRI, that was not located. It makes the Sudirman set a public central canal stenosis test set with per-disc grades beside RSNA's non-commercial one; grades, not mm; no paper linked.
- VerSe 2019 and 2020, CTSpine1K and TotalSegmentator carry whole-vertebra CT masks and no canal or foraminal label: TotalSegmentator's class list has spinal_cord and no spinal_canal, and it ships no canal measurement tool (its spine report is for osteoporotic fractures) [code page]; CTSpine1K's labels are the vertebrae alone (arXiv 2105.14711) [abstract]. The canal is the hole inside a whole-vertebra mask, so these sets support a canal chord computed from the mask, as CTSpinoPelvic1K's estimator does (section 1), but give no independent canal truth. VerSe's level labels do include transitional vertebrae (its masks label L6 and T13 where present), which is what makes the canal-from-mask route the only one with transitional truth.
- MRSpineSeg (SpineParseNet, github.com/pangshumao/SpineParseNet; data at spinesegmentation-challenge.com) [code page]: 215 cases listed in its data tree; the page names no canal or foraminal class, and the data page was not read.
- TotalSpineSeg (neuropoly; github.com/neuropoly/totalspineseg, PyPI totalspineseg 20260807) [code]: an MRI segmenter trained on public data (whole-spine OpenNeuro ds005616, SPIDER's manual labels, Spine Generic; sacrum truth from GoldAtlas, SynthRAD2023 and MRSpineSeg, else TotalSegmentator) that labels the spinal canal (label 2) apart from the cord (label 1), the vertebrae C1 to L5, the sacrum and the discs C2-C3 to L5-S; it outputs masks and canal centreline points at each disc but no diameter or area, prints no accuracy in its README, and has no S1, L6, T13 or transitional label. LGPL-3.0 by its LICENSE file (the README and PyPI name none). After SPIDER's, it is the second public code base a canal measurer could be built on.
- SPINEPS (Moller; github.com/Hendrik-code/spineps, PyPI spineps 2.1.3) [code, from its PyPI description]: the segmenter behind the lab's census masks, for sagittal T2w and T1w MRI and, in a recent release, CT; its subregion mask labels Spinal_Canal (61) apart from Spinal_Cord (60), with masks, centroids and a snapshot as outputs and no diameter or area; Apache-2.0 by its licence field (one PyPI classifier says Other/Proprietary), weights on the release page. Its optional VERIDAH labeller has labels for T13 (28) and L6 (25) and a flag to force 12 thoracic vertebrae, so it is the one public tool found that models a transitional count, and a canal chord from the lab's own SPINEPS masks may already be possible.
- Nixon 2025 (Zenodo 15613730, CC BY 4.0) [page]: a single-author deposit of an annotated mid-sagittal MRI set (18.2 MB), a manual-versus-automated AP canal diameter sheet, 361.2 MB of YOLO-OBB and Attention U-Net models and the pipeline code (as a .docx); source, patient count, levels and annotators are not stated, no number is printed on the page and no paper is linked. It is the one public deposit found pairing images, code and a manual AP canal diameter.
- BUU-LSPINE (Applied Sciences 2023;13(15):8646) [abstract and project page]: AP and lateral lumbar films with vertebral corner coordinates, spondylolisthesis diagnoses and, by the abstract, lumbosacral transitional vertebra annotations on 3,600 patients' films (the project page offers a 400-pair set by direct download, BUU-LSPINE(3600) by a Google Form request with the full version sent by email after review, and a 2,000-pair flexion, extension and bending set by direct download, all under an end-user licence agreement; it lists lumbar edge-line coordinates and four listhesis diagnoses and does not mention transitional vertebrae) [page]; no canal or foraminal truth. Its LSTV annotation is the one radiograph truth found for a transitional vertebra, but it labels the vertebra, not a canal measure.
- NHANES II, AASCE, SpineWeb: corner and landmark truth only, as the existing notes say (section 1); no canal or foraminal measure was found on any of their pages.

## 3. The methods, by measure

### canal_ap, canal_w and canal_area

- Zhou et al., Medicine 2024;103(18):e37943 (Wuxi; PMC11062721) [full]: the one CT method found that reports a canal chord against manual measurement. Faster R-CNN localisation, a shallow U-Net, PCA tilt correction of the canal cross-section, then the AP and left-right diameters; Dice 96.4% and IoU 88% (Table 2 prints Jaccard 0.85, a conflict) on 20 test images; AP diameter error 0.57 mm, left-right 1.58 mm against clinicians' manual values. Patient count, source, levels and readers are not reported; the paper names cervical and lumbar but shows lumbar; no code; data on request. Lacks: no public test set, no level labels, no per-level result, 20 images.
- Saravi et al., Bioengineering 2023;10(9):1072 (PMC10525778) [full] and Ghobrial and Roth, Frontiers in Radiology 2025;5:1503625 (PMC11975661) [full]: dural sac cross-sectional area on axial T1 MRI. Saravi trained U-Net, Attention U-Net and MultiResUNet on the Lumbar Spine MRI Dataset (515 patients, 3 to 8 slices each) and validated on 50 private Freiburg stenosis patients; MultiResUNet MAE 17.9 mm2 internal and 20.7 mm2 external, Pearson 0.998 and 0.997; code at github.com/Freiburg-AI-Research/Automated_DSCA_measurement, a single notebook in 3 commits with no README and no licence [code page]. Ghobrial added SPIDER's axial T1 (683 scans in all, 50 held out), one radiologist's masks, MultiResUNet Pearson 0.9917 and MAE 23.7 mm2 in its abstract, while its Table 2 and external test print Saravi 2023's 17.9487 and 20.7329 mm2 to four decimals, so the two are not shown independent; no code. Both are area only, lowest three discs only, no level labels, no inter-reader agreement, T1 only.
- Jeon et al., Skeletal Radiology 2024;54(5):947 (SNUBH with Coreline Soft; PMC11953181) [full]: semi-supervised U-Net on lumbar and abdominal CT, dural sac area; 100 development patients (one disc each) and 9 temporal test patients (90 levels, 990 slices), three radiologists' masks; Dice 0.85, ICC 0.82 for area, 83.9% accuracy for stenosis at 100 mm2 (sensitivity 71.9%); no external set; code not public. The vertebra localiser is Coreline's. Lacks: private, tiny test set, area not chords, CT soft tissue rather than bone.
- Fan et al., J Appl Clin Med Phys 2024;25(7):e14378 (PMC11244674) [full]: 3D U-Net on 518 private CT myelograms, dura segmentation, 210 labelled cases (150 train, 30 test, 30 external); Dice 0.933 test, 0.928 external; on 446 stenotic levels 59% of masks needed revision, automatic versus revised CSA 77.5 versus 74.7 mm2, Pearson 0.805, by level L1/L2 to L5/S1; code at github.com/Huatsing-Lau/CTM-DuraSeg, MIT, one inference script with no weight file, training code or data listed [code page]. Lacks: myelography only, area only, culprit levels from reports.
- Li et al. (MANet), Neural Computing and Applications 2021;33:11589 (PubMed 33723476) [abstract]: segments vertebral body, lamina and dural sac on lumbar MRI from one Chinese hospital, Dice 0.9252, defines canal AP diameter and dural sac area as its indicators but the record prints no agreement numbers; no code.
- Shastry et al., arXiv 2503.11281 (2025) [abstract]: nnU-Net on a proprietary MRI set across cervical, thoracic and lumbar, Dice 0.94, 0.91, 0.90, outputs disc height and canal AP diameter with no mm agreement on the page; no code.
- Netherton et al., Medical Physics 2020;47:5592 (MD Anderson; PMC7756475) [full]: a CNN segments the spinal canal on 2,296 radiotherapy CTs (centroid error 0.399 mm per slice in the abstract, 0.339 in the results) and X-Net labels levels S2 to C1 from canal-centred projections: identification 94.2%, localisation 2.2 mm (X-Net ensemble) on 94 private test patients, 74 normative and 20 outliers; 91.3% and 3.3 mm on MICCAI 2014 CSI's 60 public CTs with transfer learning (86.8% and 3.8 mm without). The authors write that the model "did not address the distinct classification of transitional lumbosacral vertebrae" in training, so the L5-S1 junction "should be verified by a clinician", since 35.6% of people may have one; on the 20 outliers (6 implants, 6 abnormal anatomy, setups, pacemakers and others) identification fell to 82.6%. It uses the canal to name levels by counting, not by any canal measure; no canal chord is reported; code not stated. It is the closest published cousin of the bordercall idea: the canal as the axis along which levels are counted.
- The ACR Define-AI use case "Automated Measurements of Lumbar Spinal Canal on CT" [page]: asks for central canal area in mm2 at every disc from L5/S1 upward on 0.625 mm CT, after the software "verifies the lumbar level numbering"; names normative data for canal and foraminal areas as a research possibility, with no reference standard described. No product was found that fills it with a public test set.
- Gaonkar et al., AJNR 2019;40(9):1586-1591 (PMC7048444, PMID 31467240) [full]: the one machine-made normative canal series found. A network (DLib, Keras on TensorFlow) segments the thecal sac, not the bony canal, on axial T2 MRI, trained on 100 images drawn from 39,295 accessions at one unnamed institution and validated on 109 axial MRIs masked by two student-physician pairs: Dice 0.84 and 0.83 against the two raters, who agree with each other at 0.90 (median surface distance 0.10 to 0.14 mm); levels are named by matching axial slices to sagittal disc masks. Over a normative cohort of 1,755 people (797 men, 958 women, aged 19 to 81; 8,775 level observations, L1 to L5) the area rises with height (estimate 8.0, t 12.1) and not with age (P 0.54) or sex; no per-level mean or SD is printed, transitional vertebrae are not mentioned, and there is no data or code statement. So it gives bordercall no canal truth by level either.

### canal_index (Torg-Pavlov)

- Wu et al., BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders 2025;26:405 (PMC12016334) [full]: HRViT predicts 18 landmarks on 1,623 private lateral cervical radiographs (1,466 train, 157 test; 0.15 mm pixels resampled to 512), then the Torg-Pavlov ratio at C2 to C7; against one senior surgeon MAE 0.01 (per level 0.02 to below 0.01), ICC 0.77 to 0.90 by level, 84.1% within 0.05; the two junior readers reached ICC 0.56 to 0.58. Mean ratios C2 to C7 over the 1,623 radiographs of normal adults 1.31, 0.98, 0.96, 0.99, 0.98, 0.93 (Table 3); the abstract's 1.26, 0.92, 0.90, 0.93, 0.92, 0.89 are the surgeon's reference values in Table 4, from the test set. No landmark error in mm, no code, no external set. The ratio cancels magnification, which is why a radiograph can carry it; it is cervical and lateral only.
- Wang et al., Medical Physics 2025;52(3):1536 (PubMed 39715317) [abstract]: YOLOX then HRNet with deconvolution, 38 keypoints, Pavlov ratio on 874 radiographs from Changhai (787 train, 87 test) with external sets of 112 and 108 from two other Shanghai hospitals; MAE 0.05 to 0.08, SMAPE 4.4 to 6.4% versus juniors' 8.7 to 26.2%; annotators, code and data access not in the record.
- Li Y, Zhang E, Ouyang H and 11 others (corresponding Lang N and Yuan H), Chinese Journal of Academic Radiology 2024;7(1):50-57 (doi 10.1007/s42058-024-00136-1, online 16 February 2024) [search, Crossref and OpenAlex records]: a VB-Net CT study measuring cervical sagittal canal and body diameters and Pavlov's ratio, by its title and a search snippet; closed access, and neither Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar nor Europe PMC carries its abstract, so what it measures and against what remain unread; listed under Gaps.

### interpedicular

- Zhang et al., J Bone Oncol 2024;47:100627 (Zhejiang Provincial People's Hospital; PMC11345936) [full]: a ResNet34 U-Net predicts pedicle landmarks on 1,410 axial CT slices (L1 to L5, one per level) of 282 private screw-fixation patients, 200 test slices; against the mean of two radiologists and a surgeon, interpedicular distance ICC 0.91, MAE 1.254 mm, RMSE 1.431 mm; pedicle width ICC 0.97 to 0.98, MAE 0.33 to 0.37 mm; screw path MAE 1.8 to 2.2 mm. Readers agreed within 3 mm on 95.8 to 98.5% of landmarks; no code. Lacks: private, one slice per level chosen by hand, patients with deformity, fracture or prior surgery excluded, no transitional cases, no public test set.
- No radiograph method measuring the interpedicular distance was found; the Lenke-modifier system (PMC13252961) segments L1 to L5 pedicles on AP films (Dice 0.901) but measures only each pedicle's offset from the central sacral line [full], and Shin 2024's CT series is manual (section 1).

### lateral_recess

- No method measuring lateral recess depth or width in mm was found. Hallinan et al., Radiology 2021;300:130 (PubMed 33973835) [abstract] grade it: a two-stage CNN on axial T2 and sagittal T1 of 446 private patients (50 internal test, 100 external) grades central canal, lateral recess and foraminal stenosis in four classes; dichotomised Gwet kappa against a 31-year radiologist 0.96 central, 0.92 lateral recess, 0.89 foramen (the two subspecialists 0.98/0.92-0.95/0.94-0.95), external 0.95 to 0.96; code not stated. The RSNA 2024 dataset grades the subarticular recess at every lumbar disc (section 2), so a public grade exists without any public measurement.

### foramen_h, foramen_w, foramen_area

- Chen et al., Orthopaedic Surgery 2022;14:2256 (PMC9483078) [full]: 3D U-Net on 0.8 mm isotropic T2-SPACE MRI of 100 prospective participants, L4-5 only, segments the two vertebrae and the disc, from which the foramen is reconstructed; Dice 0.925 bone, 0.904 disc; foraminal area 133.3 versus 133.4 mm2 manual (r 0.978), height 19.7 versus 19.8 mm (r 0.924), width 6.2 versus 6.3 mm (r 0.778); ICCs 0.98 to 0.99; private data, code not stated. It is the only method found that reports all three foraminal keys in mm and mm2 against manual truth, and it does so at one level in 100 people.
- Wu et al., Bioengineering 2024;11(10):981 (PMC11504142) [full]: YOLOv4 then ResNet50-U-Net on the public Lumbar Spine MRI Dataset (canal, axial, 515 images) and on about 40 normal subjects' sagittal images from two Radiopaedia cases (80 images plus 80 augmented) for the foramen; IoU 77.4% canal (Table 3) and 80.9% foramen after morphological processing (Table 5; 79.1% before, Table 4); the abstract and the Discussion print 79.11% for the canal and 80.89% for the foramen, and the Discussion's closing paragraph gives 80.89% to the canal, so the paper's own figures disagree; pixel counts converted to mm2 from the field of view but never compared with a reference area; annotators unnamed; no code. Radiopaedia was read here only as the paper's stated source, not fetched.
- Huang et al., Diagnostics 2025;15(17):2162 (PMC12427634) [full]: YOLOv8s on 200 private cervical radiographs (160/20/20), vertebra localisation 99.5%, foramen detection mAP50 97.7%; a vertebra-to-foramen distance in pixels only, Pearson 0.904 against two neurosurgeons on five cases; no calibration, no mm, no code. It shows how thin the radiograph side is: the foraminal distance is uncalibrated and validated on five films.
- Huang et al. 2025, European Spine Journal 35(2):583 (PMID 40848156; doi 10.1007/s00586-025-09281-2) [abstract]: the one CT method for the foramina found, and it grades rather than measures: a Faster R-CNN finds the foramen on sagittal CT, then EfficientNet-B0 or MobileNetV3-Large grades it by Lee's criteria; 177 private patients scanned 2016 to 2025, 868 sagittal images, 125/31/21 patients for training, validation and test, two spine surgeons' grades as truth; accuracy 82.35% and 80.39%, kappa 0.815 (0.723 to 0.907) and 0.799 against the senior surgeon, who scored 83.33% (the junior 62.75%); no AUC, no external set, levels not in the record, code not stated.
- Bharadwaj et al., European Radiology 2023;33:3435 (UCSF; PMC10566647) [full]: V-Net segments the dural sac (Dice 0.93) and the disc (0.94) on axial T2 of 200 private patients (987 slices, L1/L2 to L5/S1; 30 test), geometric rules place 36 x 36 mm facet boxes from the top of the predicted dural sac and, by "a similar approach" with no size printed, foraminal boxes (IoU 0.72 foramen, 0.83 facet), then BiT ResNet-50 grades: foraminal stenosis binary AUROC 0.92; central canal four-grade kappa 0.80 by a decision tree on the dural-sac-to-disc area ratio (threshold 0.31 for normal), 0.54 by the CNN; inter-reader kappa 0.74 to 0.86; code not stated. The area-ratio tree is a canal index of a kind the vocabulary does not list; it automates Bharadwaj et al., AJNR 2022;43(10):1530-1538 (PMC9575539) [full], where a trained researcher and a radiology trainee drew the dural sac and the disc by hand on MD.ai in 260 patients at one institution (130 to set the thresholds, 130 to test) against one neuroradiologist's Schizas grade (inter-reader weighted kappa 0.65 to 0.79 on 32 patients): binary AUROC 98.6 for the area ratio and 98.0 for the AP diameter ratio against 96.5 for dural sac area, multigrade kappa 0.80 and 0.75 against 0.62; patients with transitional anatomy were excluded.
- Lu et al., DeepSPINE, MLHC 2018 (arXiv 1807.10215, PMLR v85) [full]: 22,796 disc levels from 4,075 MGH patients, grades text-mined from reports in four classes for central canal and foraminal stenosis, U-Net plus spine-curve fitting names the six discs T12-L1 to L5-S1 with a separate S1 detector (Dice 0.93); binary AUC 0.983 canal, 0.961 foramen; accuracy by level 94.5 to 99.1% canal, 89.0 to 94.0% foramen; its Table 3 records Jamaludin et al. 2017 (SpineNet, sagittal) at 94.7, 85.9 and 93.7% for canal stenosis at L3-4, L4-5 and L5-S1, which confirms SpineNet grades central canal stenosis. Segmentation was trained on 1,000 cases (60:20:20) and succeeded on 188 of 200 test cases, a success requiring one true centroid in each detected area, a detected vertebra count equal to the truth and no overlap of S1 with a lumbar vertebra; the whole text (read on ar5iv) never uses transitional, sacralisation, lumbarisation or L6, so how a six-lumbar spine would fare is not stated. Its stated limitations are the middle sagittal slice only, severe scoliosis and fused bodies, motion and metal artefact, and single-report labels. Private data (7,108 examinations signed by 57 radiologists at the start), no code or data statement.
- SpineNetV2 (Windsor, Jamaludin, Kadir, Zisserman, arXiv 2205.01683; github.com/rwindsor1/SpineNet) [full, read on ar5iv; code page]: detects and labels vertebrae on sagittal T1, T2 and STIR across lumbar, cervical and whole-spine fields of view, and grades T2 lumbar discs T12/L1 to L5/S1 for Pfirrmann (5 grades), disc narrowing (4), central canal stenosis (4, and binary), endplate defects and marrow change above and below, left and right foraminal stenosis, spondylolisthesis and herniation (binary). Levels are not counted from an anchor, which the authors reject since it needs the anchor in view: a penalised beam search over an appearance and a context network assigns labels from S1 up to C3, with penalties where a transitional vertebra or a numerical variation occurs, and double S1 or C3 detections are relabelled afterwards; L6, the lumbosacral transitional vertebra and sacralisation are not named. Data: OWS (710 whole-spine scans of 196 patients, S1 to C2 marked by a non-specialist), Genodisc (2,279 patients, 2,819 lumbar scans from 6 centres, T2 graded by an expert radiologist) and the public Zukic SpineWeb set (17 scans), with splits printed as 80:20:20%; detection and labelling IDR 96.5% and LE 2.4 mm on OWS's 37 test scans, 98.4% and 1.6 mm on Genodisc's 421, 90.9% and 2.0 mm on Zukic; balanced accuracy for central canal stenosis 93.2% binary (V1 95.8%) and 64.9% four-class, foraminal 84.8% left and 82.4% right; no kappa; about 2 minutes on CPU for detection and labelling. The code's terms are the Oxford Visual Geometry Group's own (LICENCE.md): strictly non-commercial research, not a medical device, outputs not for a commercial product; the README offers a more permissive licence on request. It is the one public code base that both names lumbar levels and grades the canal.
- Feng et al., QIMS 2025;15(9):8457 [full]: DETR then Swin Transformer grades cervical canal stenosis (Kang 0 to 3) at C2/3 to C6/7 on sagittal T2 of 795 private patients from three Shanghai hospitals (206 internal test, 95 external); AUC 0.936 internal, 0.931 external at grade 2 or more; kappa against the reference 0.736; no mm, no code. Cervical grading only.
- Payne et al., AJNR 2024;45(4):432 (PMID 38360785, PMC11288556) [full]: a vision transformer plus rules grades cervical stenosis on axial gradient-echo MRI of 142 private Stony Brook patients (29 test examinations), slice grades by three senior residents confirmed by neuroradiology faculty; slice accuracy 82%, patient-level cord compression 93% (AUC 0.92) when one severe slice makes a patient positive; grades by percentage loss of subarachnoid space, no mm, canal measurement named as future work.

## 5. What the family's prior art settles and does not

Settled. The canal is the best-segmented structure in the spine: SPIDER's public benchmark puts it at Dice 0.92 with sub-millimetre surface error on sagittal MRI, radiotherapy CT gives its centroid to 0.4 mm, and the dural sac area on axial T1 MRI is recovered within about 18 to 21 mm2 (around 10 to 15% of a normal 150 to 200 mm2 sac) by Saravi 2023 (MultiResUNet MAE 17.9 mm2 on the public Lumbar Spine MRI Dataset, 20.7 mm2 on 50 private Freiburg patients) [full]; Ghobrial 2025 prints 23.7 mm2 in its abstract but Saravi's 17.9487 and 20.7329 mm2 in its Table 2 and external test, so the two are not shown independent [full]; Wu 2024 converts pixels to mm2 and compares no area with a reference [full]. The Torg-Pavlov ratio is the one measure of this family that a radiograph carries without magnification correction, and two 2025 groups land it within 0.01 to 0.08 of a surgeon on lateral cervical films. On CT, the interpedicular distance lands within 1.25 mm (ICC 0.91) and pedicle width within 0.35 mm from learnt landmarks, and the one CT canal-diameter method reports 0.57 mm AP error. Netherton 2020 settles that the canal is a usable axis for counting levels: 94% identification on private CT and 91% on MICCAI 2014, with the canal doing the localisation and projections of the bone doing the naming.

Not settled. No method found measures Panjabi's canal width and depth per vertebra on CT against manual truth with a public test set; the one CT chord paper has 20 private test images and no levels, and the automated canal literature is otherwise area on axial MRI, mostly the dural sac rather than the bony canal that Panjabi and CT measure. No automatic method measures the lateral recess at all; the field grades it. Foraminal height, width and area in mm exist from one 3D-MRI study at L4-5 in 100 people; on radiographs the foraminal distance is uncalibrated pixels on five cases. No public dataset carries a canal or foraminal chord in mm as truth: SPIDER gives masks and refuses anatomical level names, RSNA gives grades and a point, LSS-MRI-AISSLab gives grades and boxes, Kareem 2026 gives the Sudirman set central canal grades at three discs, and the one Zenodo deposit with a manual AP diameter (Nixon 2025) states no source, count or reader. Every stenosis grader names levels by counting from S1, by a beam search that only penalises a transitional vertebra (SpineNetV2) or by the clinical report, and SPIDER's authors say outright that sagittal lumbar MRI cannot resolve a transitional vertebra; Bharadwaj 2022 excluded transitional anatomy and Netherton 2020 did not address it. Of the public tools, only SPINEPS's VERIDAH labeller has labels for L6 and T13, and it measures nothing. Normative canal values by level and sex exist only from manual or hand-corrected series (Shin 2024, Griffith 2016, Yukawa 2012, Nell 2019, section 1), none with released images; the one machine-made normative series, Gaonkar 2019's thecal sac areas in 1,755 people, prints no per-level values. So for bordercall's question, naming a level from a canal or foraminal measure, the prior art offers the segmentation (SPIDER's code and masks, TotalSpineSeg's and SPINEPS's canal labels on MRI, TotalSegmentator's whole vertebrae from which the canal hole follows), a counting axis (Netherton), and the measurement precision to expect (0.5 to 1.5 mm for canal chords and the interpedicular distance on CT), but no benchmark where a canal measure names a level, and no transitional truth anywhere but VerSe's and BUU-LSPINE's vertebra labels.

## 6. Gaps: what could not be read

- Springer refused the European Spine Journal paper of Huang 2025, whose abstract was read from PubMed, so its levels and foramina counts are unknown, and the VB-Net cervical CT paper (Li 2024), whose abstract no index carries, so what it measures is unread; the European Radiology full text was read from PMC instead.
- SpineNetV2 was read on ar5iv, since its arXiv PDF exceeded the fetch limit and spinenet.org answered 503; it reports grading only on the private Genodisc set.
- Kaggle's competition pages return only their title to the fetcher; the RSNA wiki prints no series count, metric or annotator count, and the figure of about 60 radiologists comes from the AWS Open Data page.
- LSS-MRI-AISSLab's code licence is not stated, and its Mendeley data carry CC BY 4.0 beside a description that says noncommercial; which governs was not settled.
- Nixon 2025's deposit states no source, patient count, levels or annotators and links no paper; its validation sheet was not opened. The Iranian LSpineSMRI set named by Kareem 2026's code was not located, and the AISSLab axial set named by the external-testing archive is not listed in section 2.
- SPINEPS was read from its PyPI description only, not its code, so whether its canal label (61) supports a chord on CT as on MRI is not known.
- No Grand Challenge other than SPIDER and the RSNA Kaggle challenge carries canal or foraminal truth among the challenges seen (MICCAI 2014 CSI is centroids and masks of vertebrae).
