# Prior art: the spinal canal and the foramina (canal_w, canal_ap, canal_area, canal_index, interpedicular, lateral_recess, foramen_h, foramen_w, foramen_area)

Source: the thread "Plan clinical reread next steps", 2026-10-29, web reading (WebSearch and WebFetch from a cloud container; the publisher pages of Springer, Nature, MDPI and RSNA refused the fetch, so each such paper was read from PMC, Europe PMC, arXiv, Zenodo, GitHub or a challenge page, and the tag on each claim says what was read). The family is the nine keys above from bordercall's vocabulary.csv: Panjabi's canal width (the transverse diameter at the pedicles), canal depth (the midline anteroposterior diameter) and canal area, and six keys Panjabi did not measure: a canal ratio (Torg-Pavlov or a shape index), the interpedicular distance, the lateral recess, and the foraminal height, width and area. Automatic stenosis grading is swept too, since most of the deep-learning literature on the canal and the foramina grades rather than measures.

Reading tags, as the existing notes use them: [full] the paper read whole from an open copy; [abstract] only the abstract or the PubMed record; [page] a dataset, challenge or code page; [code] a repository read. Nothing is copied from any source beyond a number, a name and a link; no PDF, image or dataset was put into any repository.

## 1. What the existing notes already say

- SchwingNet docs/research/morphometry-beyond-the-42-papers.md, the table "Dry, boiled bone measures like CT or fluoroscopy": on CTSpinoPelvic1K's own labels, canal width and canal depth were within a millimetre of Panjabi 1992 at L1 and L3, while posterior body height ran high; so the two Panjabi canal chords are the measures the current estimator already reproduces best. The same note's rows for Shin 2024 (J Am Acad Orthop Surg 32(22):e1186; 1,000 patients aged 18 to 35 on CT, L1 to L5 interpedicular distance, pedicle length, canal AP diameter and canal area, by sex, race and ethnicity, measured by hand, no file), Griffith 2016 (CT of 1,080 Hong Kong patients, the lumbar canal from L1 to L5, a seed-growing program corrected by hand), Pierro 2017 (MRI of 604, lumbar canal depth), Yukawa 2012 (radiographs of 1,230 Japanese adults, cervical canal diameter from C2 to C7 by sex and decade) and Nell 2019 (SHIP, 2,453, cervical canal and body depth) are the large normative series for the canal; Shin 2025 (Brain Spine 2025;5:104162; CT of 700 young patients, disc height and foraminal size from L1-L2 to L5-S1) is the one large foraminal series among the 42. All are manual measurements; none publishes its images, and none is a method with a public test set.
- The same note, "Unsettled: the covariates": lumbar canal size differs by sex, race and ethnicity in Shin 2024's 1,000 people, so a level named from a canal chord would need covariates the owner has set to zero.
- bordercall's "Vertebra measures without masks/ct_intensity_methods.md", section 3: training-free canal finders (Yao 2006, the Radnostics patent US 8,891,848, Banik and Rangayyan 2010) exploit the canal as a dark hole in a bright ring; the canal centreline is the most robust curved axis in CT; canal width and AP depth can then be measured in planes orthogonal to it. Its finding that matters here, section 3 and section 6: "No source read reports automated canal width or AP depth on CT compared with manual measurement without a trained model; the automated canal measurement papers found by search were MRI", and "no source read gives automated-versus-manual agreement (bias, limits of agreement, ICC) for CT vertebral chords or canal dimensions from a training-free method". This sweep confirms both for trained models too, with one exception (Zhou 2024, section 3 below).
- bordercall's masks_corners_magnification.md, section 2: AASCE 2019 stores four corners per vertebra on AP films, BUU-LSPINE per-vertebra corners on AP and lateral films; neither carries canal or foraminal truth. radiograph_classical_methods.md: NHANES II's truth is radiologists' morphometric points (corners and mid-points) on cervical and lumbar films; no canal measure.
- benchmarks-to-beat.md and public-spine-and-pelvis-models-to-benchmark.md say nothing of the canal or the foramina (grep for canal, foramen, interpedicular, stenosis found no line), so those families have no benchmark on the project's list yet.

## 2. The datasets that carry truth for this family

- SPIDER (van der Graaf et al., Scientific Data 2024;11:264; arXiv 2306.12217) [full, from the arXiv v3 PDF]: 447 sagittal T1 and T2 series from 218 patients with low back pain at four Dutch hospitals; reference masks of the vertebrae, the discs and the spinal canal (447 canal segmentations); CC BY 4.0 on Zenodo (doi 10.5281/zenodo.10159290; the record 10159224 holds only overview.csv and radiological_gradings.csv) [page]. The vertebrae are "labelled from the bottom up with the most caudal lumbar vertebra labelled as 1", and the paper says this "should not be interpreted as an anatomical label", since a lumbosacral transitional vertebra or a sixth lumbar vertebra could not be told apart on the sagittal field of view; the baseline's anatomical-label output "was not used in our experiments since no accurate anatomical labels regarding lumbosacral transitional vertebrae were present in our dataset". The canal mask is of the whole canal as seen on sagittal MRI (the paper names it spinal canal, not dural sac). Benchmark on a sequestered test set of 39 studies (97 series) at spider.grand-challenge.org: Dice 0.93 vertebrae, 0.85 discs, 0.92 spinal canal (ASD 0.29 mm T1, 0.46 mm T2) for the iterative baseline, nnU-Net comparable; code at github.com/DIAGNijmegen/SPIDER-Baseline-IIS and the nnU-Net baseline (licence not read). Radiological gradings per disc (Modic, endplate change, spondylolisthesis, herniation, narrowing, bulging, Pfirrmann) but no stenosis grade and no canal or foraminal measurement in mm. What it lacks for naming levels: no anatomical level labels at all, by design; MRI only; lumbar only; no measurement truth.
- RSNA 2024 Lumbar Spine Degenerative Classification, the LumbarDISC dataset (Richards, Flanders et al., arXiv 2506.09162) [abstract]; the RSNA wiki and the AWS Open Data registry [page]: 2,697 patients, 8,593 series (sagittal T1, sagittal T2/STIR, axial T2) from 8 institutions (the AWS page says twelve), about 60 volunteer radiologists; at each disc from L1/L2 to L5/S1 a grade normal_mild, moderate or severe for spinal canal stenosis, left and right neural foraminal narrowing, and left and right subarticular (lateral recess) stenosis, with the x,y pixel centre of each graded site per series and instance; non-commercial, no redistribution (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 on the arXiv page), on Kaggle and RSNA MIRA. 1,874 teams competed. It is the largest public truth for this family, but it is ordinal grades and one point per site, not a chord or an area in mm; no inter-reader agreement is published; lumbar only; the disc levels are the radiologists' clinical names, so a transitional vertebra is numbered as the reader chose, which the dataset does not record.
- LSS-MRI-AISSLab (github.com/AISSLab2025/LSS-MRI-AISSLab-Dataset; Scientific Data 2026, s41597-026-07138-x, which could not be fetched) [code page]: 500 patients, about 8,500 sagittal T2 lumbar slices, masks of vertebral bodies, discs, sacrum and two posterior-element classes made in a human-in-the-loop workflow and checked by neurosurgeons; 2,979 foraminal boxes (the About line says 3,885, a conflict on the page) at L1-L2 to L5-S1, left and right, each with a four-grade perineural-fat score (normal 2,009, mild 508, moderate 254, severe 208 summed from its level table); data on Mendeley (rgb77xm3jf/4); licences of code and data not stated on the page; no mm foraminal dimension. Phadke et al. (J Imaging 2026;12(8):388) [abstract, from a citing index] report on 469 of its patients a fused ResNet-18 plus ten morphometric descriptors reaching QWK 0.813 and 75.3% four-class accuracy against the expert grade, prior pipelines about 65%, with the foraminal boxes supplied by the experts, so localisation is not measured, and the ten descriptors not named in the abstract.
- The Lumbar Spine MRI Dataset (Sudirman, Al Kafri et al., Mendeley 10.17632/k57fr854j2.2 with label images 10.17632/zbf6b4pttk.2) [page]: 515 symptomatic patients, axial T1 slices of the lowest three discs, label images of the disc, the posterior element, the thecal sac and the "area between anterior and posterior elements", drawn by five labellers under a radiologist; CC BY 4.0; no mm truth, no level labels beyond "the last three discs". It is the training set of Saravi 2023, Ghobrial 2025 and Wu 2024 below.
- VerSe 2019 and 2020, CTSpine1K and TotalSegmentator carry whole-vertebra CT masks and no canal or foraminal label: TotalSegmentator's class list has spinal_cord and no spinal_canal, and it ships no canal measurement tool (its spine report is for osteoporotic fractures) [code page]; CTSpine1K's labels are the vertebrae alone (arXiv 2105.14711) [abstract]. The canal is the hole inside a whole-vertebra mask, so these sets support a canal chord computed from the mask, as CTSpinoPelvic1K's estimator does (section 1), but give no independent canal truth. VerSe's level labels do include transitional vertebrae (its masks label L6 and T13 where present), which is what makes the canal-from-mask route the only one with transitional truth.
- MRSpineSeg (SpineParseNet, github.com/pangshumao/SpineParseNet; data at spinesegmentation-challenge.com) [code page]: 215 cases listed in its data tree; the page names no canal or foraminal class, and the data page was not read.
- BUU-LSPINE (Applied Sciences 2023;13(15):8646) [abstract and project page]: AP and lateral lumbar films with vertebral corner coordinates, spondylolisthesis diagnoses and, by the abstract, lumbosacral transitional vertebra annotations on 3,600 patients' films (the project page describes 400 pairs; which is the release was not settled); request-form access; no canal or foraminal truth. Its LSTV annotation is the one radiograph truth found for a transitional vertebra, but it labels the vertebra, not a canal measure.
- NHANES II, AASCE, SpineWeb: corner and landmark truth only, as the existing notes say (section 1); no canal or foraminal measure was found on any of their pages.

## 3. The methods, by measure

### canal_ap, canal_w and canal_area

- Zhou et al., Medicine 2024;103(18):e37943 (Wuxi; PMC11062721) [full]: the one CT method found that reports a canal chord against manual measurement. Faster R-CNN localisation, a shallow U-Net, PCA tilt correction of the canal cross-section, then the AP and left-right diameters; Dice 96.4% and IoU 88% (Table 2 prints Jaccard 0.85, a conflict) on 20 test images; AP diameter error 0.57 mm, left-right 1.58 mm against clinicians' manual values. Patient count, source, levels and readers are not reported; the paper names cervical and lumbar but shows lumbar; no code; data on request. Lacks: no public test set, no level labels, no per-level result, 20 images.
- Saravi et al., Bioengineering 2023;10(9):1072 (PMC10525778) [full] and Ghobrial and Roth, Frontiers in Radiology 2025;5:1503625 (PMC11975661) [full]: dural sac cross-sectional area on axial T1 MRI. Saravi trained U-Net, Attention U-Net and MultiResUNet on the Lumbar Spine MRI Dataset (515 patients, 3 to 8 slices each) and validated on 50 private Freiburg stenosis patients; MultiResUNet MAE 17.9 mm2 internal and 20.7 mm2 external, Pearson 0.998 and 0.997; code at github.com/Freiburg-AI-Research (no licence named). Ghobrial added SPIDER's axial T1 (683 scans in all, 50 held out), one radiologist's masks, MultiResUNet Pearson 0.9917 and MAE 17.9 or 23.7 mm2 (the table and the text disagree); no code. Both are area only, lowest three discs only, no level labels, no inter-reader agreement, T1 only.
- Jeon et al., Skeletal Radiology 2024;54(5):947 (SNUBH with Coreline Soft; PMC11953181) [full]: semi-supervised U-Net on lumbar and abdominal CT, dural sac area; 100 development patients (one disc each) and 9 temporal test patients (90 levels, 990 slices), three radiologists' masks; Dice 0.85, ICC 0.82 for area, 83.9% accuracy for stenosis at 100 mm2 (sensitivity 71.9%); no external set; code not public. The vertebra localiser is Coreline's. Lacks: private, tiny test set, area not chords, CT soft tissue rather than bone.
- Fan et al., J Appl Clin Med Phys 2024;25(7):e14378 (PMC11244674) [full]: 3D U-Net on 518 private CT myelograms, dura segmentation, 210 labelled cases (150 train, 30 test, 30 external); Dice 0.933 test, 0.928 external; on 446 stenotic levels 59% of masks needed revision, automatic versus revised CSA 77.5 versus 74.7 mm2, Pearson 0.805, by level L1/L2 to L5/S1; code at github.com/Huatsing-Lau/CTM-DuraSeg [code page not read for licence]. Lacks: myelography only, area only, culprit levels from reports.
- Li et al. (MANet), Neural Computing and Applications 2021;33:11589 (PubMed 33723476) [abstract]: segments vertebral body, lamina and dural sac on lumbar MRI from one Chinese hospital, Dice 0.9252, defines canal AP diameter and dural sac area as its indicators but the record prints no agreement numbers; no code.
- Shastry et al., arXiv 2503.11281 (2025) [abstract]: nnU-Net on a proprietary MRI set across cervical, thoracic and lumbar, Dice 0.94, 0.91, 0.90, outputs disc height and canal AP diameter with no mm agreement on the page; no code.
- Netherton et al., Medical Physics 2020;47:5592 (MD Anderson; PMC7756475) [full]: a CNN segments the spinal canal on 2,296 radiotherapy CTs (centroid error 0.399 mm per slice in the abstract, 0.339 in the results) and X-Net labels levels S2 to C1 from canal-centred projections: identification 94.2%, localisation 2.2 mm on 94 private test scans; 91.3% and 3.3 mm on MICCAI 2014 CSI's 60 public CTs. The model "does not classify transitional lumbosacral vertebrae", and atypical anatomy fell to 82.6%. It uses the canal to name levels by counting, not by any canal measure; no canal chord is reported; code not stated. It is the closest published cousin of the bordercall idea: the canal as the axis along which levels are counted.
- The ACR Define-AI use case "Automated Measurements of Lumbar Spinal Canal on CT" [page]: asks for central canal area in mm2 at every disc from L5/S1 upward on 0.625 mm CT, after the software "verifies the lumbar level numbering"; names normative data for canal and foraminal areas as a research possibility, with no reference standard described. No product was found that fills it with a public test set.
- Normative canal values by machine: an AJNR paper segmenting lumbar canal areas L1 to L5 in a normative cohort (area correlated with height, not age or sex) appeared only as a search snippet and could not be fetched before the search budget ran out; it is listed under Gaps.

### canal_index (Torg-Pavlov)

- Wu et al., BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders 2025;26:405 (PMC12016334) [full]: HRViT predicts 18 landmarks on 1,623 private lateral cervical radiographs (1,466 train, 157 test; 0.15 mm pixels resampled to 512), then the Torg-Pavlov ratio at C2 to C7; against one senior surgeon MAE 0.01 (per level 0.02 to below 0.01), ICC 0.77 to 0.90 by level, 84.1% within 0.05; the two junior readers reached ICC 0.56 to 0.58. Normative ratios C2 to C7 1.26, 0.92, 0.90, 0.93, 0.92, 0.89 in asymptomatic Chinese adults. No landmark error in mm, no code, no external set. The ratio cancels magnification, which is why a radiograph can carry it; it is cervical and lateral only.
- Wang et al., Medical Physics 2025;52(3):1536 (PubMed 39715317) [abstract]: YOLOX then HRNet with deconvolution, 38 keypoints, Pavlov ratio on 874 radiographs from Changhai (787 train, 87 test) with external sets of 112 and 108 from two other Shanghai hospitals; MAE 0.05 to 0.08, SMAPE 4.4 to 6.4% versus juniors' 8.7 to 26.2%; annotators, code and data access not in the record.
- A VB-Net CT study measuring cervical sagittal canal and body diameters and Pavlov's ratio (Chinese Journal of Academic Radiology 2024, doi 10.1007/s42058-024-00136-1) was refused by Springer and not read; listed under Gaps.

### interpedicular

- Zhang et al., J Bone Oncol 2024;47:100627 (Zhejiang Provincial People's Hospital; PMC11345936) [full]: a ResNet34 U-Net predicts pedicle landmarks on 1,410 axial CT slices (L1 to L5, one per level) of 282 private screw-fixation patients, 200 test slices; against the mean of two radiologists and a surgeon, interpedicular distance ICC 0.91, MAE 1.254 mm, RMSE 1.431 mm; pedicle width ICC 0.97 to 0.98, MAE 0.33 to 0.37 mm; screw path MAE 1.8 to 2.2 mm. Readers agreed within 3 mm on 95.8 to 98.5% of landmarks; no code. Lacks: private, one slice per level chosen by hand, patients with deformity, fracture or prior surgery excluded, no transitional cases, no public test set.
- No radiograph method measuring the interpedicular distance was found; the Lenke-modifier system (PMC13252961) segments L1 to L5 pedicles on AP films (Dice 0.901) but measures only each pedicle's offset from the central sacral line [full], and Shin 2024's CT series is manual (section 1).

### lateral_recess

- No method measuring lateral recess depth or width in mm was found. Hallinan et al., Radiology 2021;300:130 (PubMed 33973835) [abstract] grade it: a two-stage CNN on axial T2 and sagittal T1 of 446 private patients (50 internal test, 100 external) grades central canal, lateral recess and foraminal stenosis in four classes; dichotomised Gwet kappa against a 31-year radiologist 0.96 central, 0.92 lateral recess, 0.89 foramen (the two subspecialists 0.98/0.92-0.95/0.94-0.95), external 0.95 to 0.96; code not stated. The RSNA 2024 dataset grades the subarticular recess at every lumbar disc (section 2), so a public grade exists without any public measurement.

### foramen_h, foramen_w, foramen_area

- Chen et al., Orthopaedic Surgery 2022;14:2256 (PMC9483078) [full]: 3D U-Net on 0.8 mm isotropic T2-SPACE MRI of 100 prospective participants, L4-5 only, segments the two vertebrae and the disc, from which the foramen is reconstructed; Dice 0.925 bone, 0.904 disc; foraminal area 133.3 versus 133.4 mm2 manual (r 0.978), height 19.7 versus 19.8 mm (r 0.924), width 6.2 versus 6.3 mm (r 0.778); ICCs 0.98 to 0.99; private data, code not stated. It is the only method found that reports all three foraminal keys in mm and mm2 against manual truth, and it does so at one level in 100 people.
- Wu et al., Bioengineering 2024;11(10):981 (PMC11504142) [full]: YOLOv4 then ResNet50-U-Net on the public Lumbar Spine MRI Dataset (canal, axial, 515 images) and on about 40 normal subjects' sagittal images from two Radiopaedia cases (80 images plus 80 augmented) for the foramen; IoU 77.4% canal, 80.9% foramen (the abstract swaps them); pixel counts converted to mm2 from the field of view but never compared with a reference area; annotators unnamed; no code. Radiopaedia was read here only as the paper's stated source, not fetched.
- Huang et al., Diagnostics 2025;15(17):2162 (PMC12427634) [full]: YOLOv8s on 200 private cervical radiographs (160/20/20), vertebra localisation 99.5%, foramen detection mAP50 97.7%; a vertebra-to-foramen distance in pixels only, Pearson 0.904 against two neurosurgeons on five cases; no calibration, no mm, no code. It shows how thin the radiograph side is: the foraminal distance is uncalibrated and validated on five films.
- Bharadwaj et al., European Radiology 2023;33:3435 (UCSF; PMC10566647) [full]: V-Net segments the dural sac (Dice 0.93) and the disc (0.94) on axial T2 of 200 private patients (987 slices, L1/L2 to L5/S1; 30 test), geometric rules place 36 x 36 mm foraminal boxes (IoU 0.72), then BiT ResNet-50 grades: foraminal stenosis binary AUROC 0.92; central canal four-grade kappa 0.80 by a decision tree on the dural-sac-to-disc area ratio (threshold 0.31 for normal), 0.54 by the CNN; inter-reader kappa 0.74 to 0.86; code not stated. The area-ratio tree is a canal index of a kind the vocabulary does not list.
- Lu et al., DeepSPINE, MLHC 2018 (arXiv 1807.10215, PMLR v85) [full]: 22,796 disc levels from 4,075 MGH patients, grades text-mined from reports in four classes for central canal and foraminal stenosis, U-Net plus spine-curve fitting names the six discs T12-L1 to L5-S1 with a separate S1 detector (Dice 0.93); binary AUC 0.983 canal, 0.961 foramen; accuracy by level 94.5 to 99.1% canal, 89.0 to 94.0% foramen; its Table 3 records Jamaludin et al. 2017 (SpineNet, sagittal) at 94.7, 85.9 and 93.7% for canal stenosis at L3-4, L4-5 and L5-S1, which confirms SpineNet grades central canal stenosis. Private data, no code, levels counted from S1 with no transitional handling stated.
- SpineNetV2 (Windsor, Jamaludin, Kadir, Zisserman, arXiv 2205.01683; github.com/rwindsor1/SpineNet) [page]: detects and labels vertebrae on sagittal T1, T2 and STIR across lumbar, cervical and whole-spine fields of view and grades T2 lumbar discs T12/L1 to L5/S1 for "a range of common degenerative changes"; non-commercial terms of access; the arXiv PDF exceeded the fetch limit, so its grading list, level-naming rule and test sets were not read. It is the one public code base that both names lumbar levels and grades the canal.
- Feng et al., QIMS 2025;15(9):8457 [full]: DETR then Swin Transformer grades cervical canal stenosis (Kang 0 to 3) at C2/3 to C6/7 on sagittal T2 of 795 private patients from three Shanghai hospitals (206 internal test, 95 external); AUC 0.936 internal, 0.931 external at grade 2 or more; kappa against the reference 0.736; no mm, no code. Cervical grading only.

## 5. What the family's prior art settles and does not

Settled. The canal is the best-segmented structure in the spine: SPIDER's public benchmark puts it at Dice 0.92 with sub-millimetre surface error on sagittal MRI, radiotherapy CT gives its centroid to 0.4 mm, and the dural sac area on axial MRI is recovered within about 20 mm2 (around 10 to 15% of a normal 150 to 200 mm2 sac) by three independent groups on public data. The Torg-Pavlov ratio is the one measure of this family that a radiograph carries without magnification correction, and two 2025 groups land it within 0.01 to 0.08 of a surgeon on lateral cervical films. On CT, the interpedicular distance lands within 1.25 mm (ICC 0.91) and pedicle width within 0.35 mm from learnt landmarks, and the one CT canal-diameter method reports 0.57 mm AP error. Netherton 2020 settles that the canal is a usable axis for counting levels: 94% identification on private CT and 91% on MICCAI 2014, with the canal doing the localisation and projections of the bone doing the naming.

Not settled. No method found measures Panjabi's canal width and depth per vertebra on CT against manual truth with a public test set; the one CT chord paper has 20 private test images and no levels, and the automated canal literature is otherwise area on axial MRI, mostly the dural sac rather than the bony canal that Panjabi and CT measure. No automatic method measures the lateral recess at all; the field grades it. Foraminal height, width and area in mm exist from one 3D-MRI study at L4-5 in 100 people; on radiographs the foraminal distance is uncalibrated pixels on five cases. No public dataset carries a canal or foraminal chord in mm as truth: SPIDER gives masks and refuses anatomical level names, RSNA gives grades and a point, LSS-MRI-AISSLab gives grades and boxes. Every stenosis grader names levels by counting from S1 or by the clinical report, and SPIDER's authors say outright that sagittal lumbar MRI cannot resolve a transitional vertebra; none of the methods above claims to. Normative canal values by level and sex exist only from manual series (Shin 2024, Griffith 2016, Yukawa 2012, Nell 2019, section 1), none with released images. So for bordercall's question, naming a level from a canal or foraminal measure, the prior art offers the segmentation (SPIDER's code and masks, TotalSegmentator's whole vertebrae from which the canal hole follows), a counting axis (Netherton), and the measurement precision to expect (0.5 to 1.5 mm for canal chords and the interpedicular distance on CT), but no benchmark where a canal measure names a level, and no transitional truth anywhere but VerSe's and BUU-LSPINE's vertebra labels.
