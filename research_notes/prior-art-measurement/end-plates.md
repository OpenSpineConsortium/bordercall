# Prior art for the end-plate family: width, depth, area, inclination, wedge angle, concavity

Source: the thread "Plan clinical reread next steps", 2026-10-29, web reading (WebSearch and WebFetch; arXiv, Europe PMC's REST API, MICCAI's open PDF, GitHub raw files and Zenodo's API, since PMC answered with a reCAPTCHA page, Springer with a sign-in redirect, and MDPI, DOAJ, JoVE and Frontiers' search pages with 403). The family is the ten keys of bordercall's vocabulary.csv: endplate (EPWu), endplate_depth (EPDu), endplate_lower (EPWl), endplate_lower_depth (EPDl), endplate_area (EPAu), endplate_lower_area (EPAl), endplate_incl (EPItu), endplate_lower_incl (EPItl), endplate_wedge (EPIt, the sum of the two inclinations) and endplate_concavity (depth, no Panjabi code). Reading-status tags follow the existing notes: [full] the paper or page read whole, [abstract] abstract or record only, [page] a web page or search summary, [code] a repository read.

## 1. What the existing notes already settle (read first, not re-derived)

- SchwingNet docs/research/morphometry-beyond-the-42-papers.md, row "Panjabi's printed tables are what was measured": the lumbar paper prints its upper and lower end-plate inclinations at L1 to L5 as the same 20 numbers the cervical paper prints at C3 to C7, an open finding of the record check; and row "Dry, boiled bone measures like CT or fluoroscopy": on CTSpinoPelvic1K's labels end-plate width, canal width and canal depth were within a millimetre of Panjabi at L1 and L3 while posterior body height ran 2.3 to 7.5 mm high. Its bullet on the pilot says the vendored bordercall measure step writes upper end-plate width only, no depth and no lower end plate.
- bordercall research_notes "Vertebra measures without masks/ct_intensity_methods.md", section 5 and its table: Stern 2011 fits a superquadric with 25 shape parameters to CT and MR bodies, and Stern 2013 classifies 682 bodies from height, end-plate inclination and concavity read off the fitted parameters, with no millimetre error against manual measurement; no study read there reports automated end-plate width or depth versus manual in mm without a trained model.
- The same folder's masks_corners_magnification.md: six-point morphometry gives heights, not end-plate widths, and no readable source reports end-plate depth or width reproducibility in mm (section 1); AASCE corners give the upper end-plate as the segment between corners 1 and 2 and the reference Cobb code uses the mid-point line, so it measures tilt, not end-plate-to-end-plate Cobb (section 2); a minimum-area rectangle forces the two end-plates parallel and equal, so it cannot express wedging (section 2); the projected end-plate on a tilted view is an ellipse about W sin(theta) tall, and rotation's effect on apparent width and depth is unquantified in the literature (section 4). Its magnification section: lateral films magnify 1.21 cervical and 1.31 lumbar against CT or MRI.
- radiograph_classical_methods.md: the Siemens patents US 6,608,917 and US 7,010,151 find end-plate lines on radiographs by curvature ridges and dynamic programming with no published accuracy; Zhang 2009's fuzzy Hough gives end-plate lines and Cobb (ICC above 0.95, 76 films) with the user marking the end vertebrae; no published millimetre corner result is training-free.
- SchwingNet docs/research/benchmarks-to-beat.md and public-spine-and-pelvis-models-to-benchmark.md carry nothing on end-plate measures beyond FleXray's Cobb angle on AASCE (5.13 degrees, computed from masks by code that is not public, cited in masks_corners_magnification.md section 2).

## 2. The sweep

### 2.1 By measure

End-plate width and depth (EPWu, EPDu, EPWl, EPDl).

- Kramer, Rittel, Blomenkamp, Bauer, Paulus (University of Koblenz), "3D vertebrae measurements: assessing vertebral dimensions in human spine models using automated landmark detection", arXiv 2402.01462, later IEEE ISBI 2025 [full, arXiv HTML]. Training-free geometry on a vertebra mesh: a cubic spline through the vertebrae's centres of mass gives the local up-vector, the bounding-box axis nearest global right gives the right-vector, end-plates are the vertex sets whose normals lie within 45 degrees of the up-axis, and the landmarks are the extreme intersections of each end-plate with the local sagittal and frontal planes. It measures upper and lower end-plate width and depth and five heights, no angle. Test: 6 of VerSe's 141 patients (L2, L3, T4, T5) against three raters in 3D Slicer, and 50 artificial lumbar spines. MAE in mm (VerSe lumbar / VerSe thoracic / artificial): upper width 0.75 / 3.73 / 0.89, lower width 0.88 / 0.26 / 0.86, upper depth 0.49 / 1.22 / 0.73, lower depth 0.54 / 1.45 / 0.80; raters' ICC 0.84 lumbar and 0.96 thoracic; manual widths had an SD of 3.41 mm. The paper's own averages (1.09 mm abstract, 1.32 mm text) do not match its table. Code: github.com/VisSim-UniKO/Spine-Morphology-Analyzer [code]: no licence file, 16 commits, a SlicerPlugins folder and a SpineLib submodule (Caetox/SpineLib); the README is two lines. Lacks: six patients, no cervical, assumes LPS orientation, no level labels of its own (it takes a labelled mesh), no transitional handling.
- Cappetti, Di Angelo, Fontana, Marzola, "A computer-based method for the automatic identification of the dimensional features of human cervical vertebrae", Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine Update 7:100175, 2025 [page, from search summaries; DOAJ and the Salerno record refused, Europe PMC has no record]: a local coordinate system and rules measure body, foramen and spinous process on high-density point clouds from CT or 3D scans, compared with manual measurement on nine cervical tracts; the manual method showed poor repeatability. Which end-plate dimensions are among the features, and the errors, could not be read.
- Li, Lv, Tang, Jia, Gao, "Morphological characteristics of lumbar vertebral bodies and regional distribution patterns of bone mineral density: a CT study", Front Physiol 17:1820186, 2026 [abstract]: 100 healthy volunteers aged 20 to 70, L1 to L5, superior and inferior end-plate widths, three heights and anterior and posterior third cortical thickness of the superior end-plate; widths rise from L1 to L5. Manual or automatic not stated, no reliability, no depth, area or concavity. A population table, not a method.
- Kishimoto, Akeda, Sudo, Espinoza Orias, Inoue, "In vivo measurement of vertebral endplate surface area along the whole-spine", J Orthop Res 34(8):1418-1430, 2016 (PMC6521950) [abstract; the full text answered 500 and 503]: 49 patients, myelogram CT, 3D mesh models of superior and inferior bony end-plates from C3 to S1; total mesh area, projected area, anteroposterior and transverse diameters, each end-plate placed by its cumulative distance from C2; area correlates with position along the column (r 0.938 men, 0.911 women); local reductions at T4 and L5-S1. This is the one whole-column area table found, EPAu and EPAl at every level in one person, and the one source that relates an end-plate measure to the position along the column, which is bordercall's question. The segmentation steps and whether they were manual could not be read; no reliability in the abstract; data not public.
- Yao Q et al., "Differences of the morphology of subaxial cervical spine endplates between Chinese and White men and women", Biomed Res Int 2018 (PMC5838464) [page, title only from the Europe PMC listing]: a cervical end-plate morphology table, not read.

End-plate area (EPAu, EPAl).

- Kishimoto 2016 above is the only in vivo whole-spine area source found; the implant-sizing cervical CT studies the search returned (Frontiers Bioeng 2022, AOTT 2016, PMC6145136, PMC7886465 [page, search summaries]) measure anteroposterior and mediolateral diameters by hand on CT and report mismatch with implant footprints; none is automatic and none was read whole.

End-plate inclination and wedge angle (EPItu, EPItl, EPIt).

- Doerr, De Silva, Vijayan, Han, Uneri, Ketcha, ... Siewerdsen (Johns Hopkins), "Automatic analysis of global spinal alignment from simple annotation of vertebral bodies", J Med Imaging 7(3):035001, 2020 (PMC7218103) [abstract; the SPIE page, PMC and Europe PMC render all refused]: two methods take vertebral labels in spine CT, EndSeg segments the end-plates from the labels as seeds and SpNorm fits a 2D curve through the labels; five clinicians' manual measurements of proximal and main thoracic kyphosis and lumbar lordosis are the truth; 93.8 percent of automatic end-plate angle estimates fell inside the inter-reader 95 percent interval. The dataset size and the degree errors are in the full text, which could not be read. This is the one CT method found that validates per-end-plate angles against readers.
- Shi, Meng, Zhuang, ... Zhang (Hong Kong), "Accurate Cobb angle estimation via SVD-based curve detection and vertebral wedging quantification", arXiv 2509.24898, 2025 [full, arXiv HTML]: an HRNet-18 with a heatmap and vector head predicts the superior and inferior end-plate angle and midpoint of each of 18 vertebrae, C7 to L5, on 630 standing PA EOS radiographs of adolescents aged 10 to 18 from two Hong Kong hospitals (441/63/126 split), two surgeons' annotations with Cobb inter-rater MAD 3.1 +/- 1.8 degrees; end-plate angle MAE 2.10 +/- 0.08 degrees, maximum Cobb MAE 2.55 degrees. Its Vertebral Wedging Index is the mean over a curve of the absolute difference between a vertebra's upper and lower end-plate angles (the README instead calls it an anterior to posterior height ratio; the two disagree). Code github.com/SCBoy1007/AIS-Cobb-SVD-Analysis, MIT [code]; data not released. This is the closest published analogue of EPIt measured directly as an angle, on the coronal plane. Lacks: coronal only, adolescents, no mm scale, no lumbosacral end, no level labels beyond the fixed 18-vertebra count.
- Germann, Meyer, Staib, Sutter, Fritz, "Performance of a deep convolutional neural network for MRI-based vertebral body measurements and insufficiency fracture detection", Eur Radiol 2023 (PMC10121505) [full, Europe PMC XML]: a U-Net segments bodies on the mid-sagittal T2 slice and four corners per body give anterior and posterior height, superior and inferior end-plate concavity (maximum depression) and a vertebral angle (the angle between the two end-plates, EPIt's sagittal analogue); 200 patients, 1,000 lumbar bodies, mean age 75, two radiologists as truth; ICC against readers: vertebral angle 0.944 and 0.941 (bias 0, limits +/- 6.9 degrees), superior concavity 0.846 and 0.834 (bias 0, limits +/- 2.7 mm), inferior concavity 0.792 and 0.788 (bias -0.2, limits +/- 2.0 mm), against inter-reader ICC 0.932 and 0.891 for concavity. No code, no data. Lacks: one slice, L1 to L5 only, elderly, Schmorl nodes and degenerative end-plates degrade concavity, no width.
- The lumbar MR deformity framework (Mask R-CNN, 200 subjects, median height errors 0.78 to 0.86 mm, wedge median error 3.3 percent as a height ratio) [page, search summary; ScienceDirect not fetched] measures wedging as Ha/Hp, not as an angle.
- Hipp, Grieco, Newman, Reitman, "Definition of normal vertebral morphometry using NHANES-II radiographs", JBMR Plus 6(10):e10677, 2022 (PMC9549721) [full, Europe PMC XML, first 100,000 characters; the rest is the reference list]: Spine Camp (Medical Metrics) places four corners per body by neural networks trained on over 50,000 analyst-landmarked lateral films (QMA software, "previously validated"), withholding low-confidence points; NHANES-II lateral films, 42,980 lumbar vertebrae (L1 to S1, 7,364 films) and 54,093 cervical (C2 to C7, 9,662 films), ages 25 to 74, obtained through public access. Six metrics per vertebra, two of them in this family: the superior over inferior end-plate width ratio (EPWR) and the angle between the superior and inferior end-plates (EPA, which is EPIt on a lateral film), beside the anterior/posterior height ratio, the diagonal ratio, the height/width ratio and the posterior-wall to superior end-plate angle. Vertebrae beyond 2 SD on any metric were trimmed; per-vertebra means and SDs are in Appendix S1; the derived data are "upon reasonable request"; raw landmark coordinates are not released; no mm or degree accuracy of the landmarking is given in the body (appendices S2 and S3 hold reproducibility and projection analyses, not read). Findings that matter here: EPA and the height ratio are almost perfectly correlated; the forward/backward diagonal ratio separates S1 from L1 to L5 with AUC 0.995; level explained more variance than age, sex or size; the authors say labels could be wrong without whole-spine films and the prevalence of transitional vertebrae in the set is unknown. Lacks: ratios and angles only, no mm (uncalibrated films), no thoracic, no mid-points, one view, labels assumed.
- Hipp's later automated metrics, QIMS 2025 (PMC11994510) and Bioengineering 2026 (PMC13024340) [abstract]: lumbar and C2 to C7 instability from flexion-extension films with the same FDA-cleared pipeline; rotation, translation and disc height, not this family's measures.
- Cina, Bassani, Panico, Luca, Masharawi, Brayda-Bruno, Galbusera, "2-step deep learning model for landmarks localization in spine radiographs", Sci Rep 11:9482, 2021 (PMC8096829) [full, Europe PMC XML]: four corners per body on 10,193 uncalibrated lateral lumbar films (Galeazzi, not public), one annotator, 30 images by four observers (ICC 0.99 x, 0.96 y); errors as percent of body width and height (medians 1.98 and 1.68 percent), angles from end-plate lines: L1-L5 lordosis median 1.84 degrees, L1-S1 2.43, sacral slope 1.98, maxima 34 to 50 degrees from missed vertebrae. The discussion says segmental wedging is obtainable from the corners but it was not evaluated. No code. Lacks: no mm, no per-vertebra inclination evaluated, lumbar only, detection failures propagate.
- Yao et al. (Hangzhou Jianpei), "Automatic measurement of anatomical parameters of the lumbar vertebral body and the intervertebral disc on radiographs by deep learning", Quant Imaging Med Surg 2024 (PMC11320507) [full, PMC HTML]: HRNet with distribution-aware decoding, six landmarks per body (anterior, middle and posterior points of each end-plate, placed to ignore osteophytes), 1,318 lateral lumbar films from one hospital (not public), three radiologists' mean as truth; VBH MAE 0.61 mm (ICC 0.98), disc height 0.63 mm, intervertebral disc angle from adjacent end-plate vectors 1.15 degrees (ICC 0.93); landmarks within 3 mm 98 percent, within 1 mm 69 percent (S1 44 percent). No code; a website is promised. The disc angle is the inter-vertebral analogue of the inclinations; no body wedge angle, width or depth. Lacks: T12 to S1 only, single centre, implants and severe deformity excluded.
- Suri et al., "Vertebral deformity measurements at MRI, CT, and radiography using deep learning", Radiol Artif Intell 4(1):e210015, 2022 (PMC8823454) [abstract]: six landmarks per body, 1,744 patients (1,123 MRI, 137 CT, 484 radiographs), heights with 1.5 to 1.9 percent error and lumbar lordosis MAE 2.26 to 3.60 degrees; no end-plate measure of this family, no code stated.
- Alukaev, Kiselev, Mustafaev, Ainur, Ibragimov, Vrtovec, "A deep learning framework for vertebral morphometry and Cobb angle measurement with external validation", Eur Spine J 31(8):2115-2124, 2022 [abstract]: U-Net ensemble on 3D CT, 160 training and 15 external CTs, body height MAE 1.17 mm, disc height 0.54 mm, Cobb 3.42 degrees; no end-plate dimension or inclination named.

End-plate concavity (endplate_concavity).

- Germann 2023 above: the only automatic concavity measurement with reader agreement found (ICC 0.79 to 0.85, limits +/- 2.0 to 2.7 mm), MRI, lumbar.
- Stern 2013 (Osteoporos Int, from the existing ct_intensity_methods.md note) [abstract]: concavity read from the superquadric's parameters, validated as fracture classification only.
- Chen, Zhong, Tan, Wu, Jiang, "Sagittal geometry of the middle and lower cervical endplates", Eur Spine J 22(7):1570-1575, 2013 [abstract; Springer redirected to sign-in]: 97 people, reformatted CT, C3 to C7, manual midsagittal measures; concavity depth 0.9 to 1.2 mm superior and 2.1 to 2.7 mm inferior, apex in the posterior half (56 to 67 percent superior, 52 to 57 inferior), slope 4.5 to 9.0 degrees superior and 4.5 to 7.5 inferior, largest at C5 and smallest at C7; age and sex had no effect. Reference values and a level trend for EPItu, EPItl and concavity, by hand.
- Cui, Wang, Sun, Zhang, Yin, Cui, Jiao, "Three-dimensional computed tomography morphometric analysis of lumbar endplates in Chinese young adults", Front Surg, April 2026 (PMC13095621) [full]: 33 adults aged 18 to 45, 165 end-plates (L4 and L5 both, S1 superior), Mimics, Geomagic and 3-Matic, manual segmentation with region growing, end-plates separated by curvature; sagittal and coronal diameter, concavity depth and partial lengths; two surgeons on 50 end-plates, ICC above 0.75; sagittal depth means about 1.3 to 2.1 mm at L4 and L5 and 0.4 to 0.6 at S1, coronal depth about 3.2 to 3.4 at the inferior end-plates; four shape classes (uniform concave, asymmetric, flat-bottomed, flat; S1 flat in 87.9 percent). Not automatic, no area, data "without undue reservation" but no link.
- A CT and Mimics lumbar study (PubMed 30583653) [page, search summary]: concavity depth 1.5 to 2.0 mm rising to 2.2 to 3.9 mm from L1 to L5; not read.
- Feng et al., "Precision measurements and parametric models of vertebral endplates", J Vis Exp 151, 2019 (PMID 31609326) [abstract; the JoVE page was empty]: a high-speed scanner's point cloud of the end-plate, three sagittal and three frontal curves with 11 points each, parametric equations fitted; specimen count and reliability not in the abstract. The one explicit end-plate surface model found; it is a protocol on specimens, not an image method.
- Korez, Likar, Pernus, Vrtovec, "Parametric modeling of the intervertebral disc space in 3D: application to CT images of the lumbar spine", Comput Med Imaging Graph 38(7):596-605, 2014 [abstract]: a truncated elliptical cone deformed to the disc space, initialised from the adjacent bodies' models; 20 lumbar CTs, 100 disc spaces, 93 modelled, mean radial error 1.06 +/- 0.98 mm against manual points. Its two end faces are the adjacent end-plates, so heights, widths and inclinations are available from the parameters, but none is reported as a measure.
- Reinhold et al., "An analysis by synthesis approach for automatic vertebral shape identification in clinical QCT", GCPR 2018, arXiv 1812.00693 [abstract]: a statistical bone model fitted by as-rigid-as-possible deformation to find the cortical surface, validated on the European Spine Phantom and one micro-CT cadaver vertebra; no end-plate measure, no numbers in the abstract.

### 2.2 By dataset

- VerSe 2019 and 2020 [page, grand-challenge and PMC8553749 search summaries; existing notes]: per-vertebra masks and centroids, some Genant labels, no end-plate landmark or measure. Kramer 2024 adds three raters' measurements on 6 of its patients (not released as a table; its artificial set is). Zakharov et al., "Interpretable vertebral fracture quantification via anchor-free landmarks localization", arXiv 2204.06818, 2022 [full, ar5iv]: six coplanar points per vertebra, heights and the Genant index, trained on 100 LungCancer-500 CTs (3,565 vertebra annotations, released CC BY-NC 4.0 at github.com/neuro-ml/anchor-free-genant [code]) plus a private set, tested on VerSe 2020's public validation and test subsets; the six points are not defined in the text and no end-plate dimension is computed.
- TotalSegmentator [page]: masks only. The one tool found that measures from its output is mohamed32145/vertebral-heights [code]: an intrinsic frame from RANSAC plane fits of the two end-plates (normals only), a mirror plane by Dice, heights Ha, Hc, Hp, a wedge index and a "wedge angle" (undefined in the README), validated on synthetic phantoms alone (+/- 0.03 mm at 1 mm slices, "wedge angle unusable" at 3 mm), one commit, no licence, no reader comparison. TPTBox (Hendrik-code/TPTBox, Apache-2.0) [code, licence file and docs page]: points of interest on vertebrae and a spinestats module (distances, angles, disc heights per its description); its documentation page names no end-plate function, and the spinestats source was not read.
- CTSpine1K [page]: masks only, but Endplate3D-QCT is built on 119 of its volumes (below). CTSpinoPelvic1K's own per-level table of end-plate width and wedge ratio is the project's, covered in the existing notes.
- Endplate3D-QCT, Yin, Zou, Zhao, Zhang, Li, Wu, Yan, Wang (Peking University), MICCAI 2025, LNCS 10.1007/978-3-032-05141-7_15 [full, the open PDF at papers.miccai.org]: the first public pixel-level end-plate dataset. 170 lumbar CT volumes: 119 from CTSpine1K at 0.8 mm isotropic (CTSpine-Refined, 1,309 surfaces) plus 51 preoperative degenerative cases at 0.4 x 0.4 x 0.75 mm (25 LDD-Mild, 26 LDD-Severe, 10 with scoliosis), mean age 66; superior and inferior end-plates L1 to L5 and superior S1, 11 per scan, 1,870 instances, cortical end-plate segmented slice by slice in the sagittal plane by four spine surgeons under a manual and checked by a fifth; no inter-reader statistic. Benchmarks trained with nnU-Net: on CTSpine-Refined, VNet and 3D U-Net Dice 0.901, instance Dice 0.905, HD95 about 1.0 mm, ASD 0.20 mm, detection F1 0.947; on LDD-Severe Dice 0.885 (VNet) and detection recall 0.55 (EfficientUNet) to 0.89 (3D U-Net); S1 is missed for sacral curvature. Zenodo record 15784487 [page, Zenodo API]: access right open, licence CC BY 4.0, one 2.67 GB zip, published 2025-09-19; the GitHub README links the record through an access token and names the evaluation code end_plate_postproc; the code itself is not in the repository's README. No geometric measure is computed from the masks: the paper's motivation is end-plate BMD. For this family it is the one public truth from which EPWu, EPDu, EPWl, EPDl, EPAu, EPAl, both inclinations and the concavity could be measured and compared to a reader's surface, lumbar only.
- SPIDER (Zenodo 10159224) [page, Zenodo record]: 218 patients, 447 sagittal T1 and T2 series, segmentations of vertebrae, discs and canal, CC BY 4.0; end-plate changes and Schmorl nodes are binary gradings per level, no geometric end-plate measure.
- MRSpineSeg [page]: nothing on end-plates found in the one search that reached it (an arXiv secondary source), not read.
- BUU-LSPINE (services.informatics.buu.ac.th/spine) [page]: 400 AP and lateral pairs, upper and lower edge lines of each lumbar vertebra as point coordinates in CSV, listhesis labels, an end-user licence by request, a 2,000-pair set with bending labels; the number of points per line is not stated. Jang, Kim, Ku, Lee, "Reliability analysis of vertebral landmark labelling on lumbar spine X-ray images", Diagnostics 13(8):1411, 2023 (PMC10137367) [abstract]: 1,000 AP and lateral pairs, 12 raters under an SOP, landmark points and end-plate lines, ICC 0.934 to 0.991, means and SDs of error offered as a reference for automatic detectors; whether these are the BUU images and whether the labels are released could not be read.
- NHANES II [full, Hipp 2022 above]: the end-plate width ratio and the angle between end-plates are tabulated per level for 97,073 vertebrae, cervical and lumbar, by a commercial network; the images are public, the landmarks are not.
- AASCE 2019 [page, existing note and search summaries]: 609 training AP films with 68 corners (17 vertebrae T1 to L5), 98 test; corners give coronal end-plate lines and so a coronal wedge angle per vertebra, but the metric is Cobb SMAPE and no paper found reports per-vertebra end-plate angle error on it. Shi 2025 used its own 630 EOS films instead.
- SpineWeb [page, secondary]: only CSI 2014 and xVertSeg among CT sets per one arXiv source; no end-plate truth found.

### 2.3 By code and challenge

Public code found that touches an end-plate measure: VisSim-UniKO/Spine-Morphology-Analyzer (width and depth, no licence), mohamed32145/vertebral-heights (end-plate plane normals, wedge angle, no licence, phantoms only), SCBoy1007/AIS-Cobb-SVD-Analysis (coronal end-plate angles, MIT), neuro-ml/anchor-free-genant (six-point annotations, CC BY-NC 4.0), Hendrik-code/TPTBox (landmarks and spine statistics, Apache-2.0), yin876705249/Endplate3D-QCT (evaluation code named, dataset CC BY 4.0). No PyPI or Zenodo code release for end-plate dimensions was found. No MICCAI or Grand Challenge task scores an end-plate dimension or angle; Endplate3D-QCT is a segmentation benchmark and AASCE scores Cobb.

## 4. What the family's prior art settles and does not

Settled. End-plate width and depth can be read off a labelled CT mesh without training to within about 0.5 to 0.9 mm of three raters in the lumbar spine (Kramer 2024), which is below the millimetre error bordercall assumes for every length, but on six patients and with 3.7 mm on one thoracic width; the raters themselves disagree by an SD of 3.41 mm on widths. A public lumbar end-plate surface truth exists since September 2025 (Endplate3D-QCT, CC BY 4.0, 1,870 surfaces on CTSpine1K and degenerative cases) against which any end-plate measure, area and concavity included, can be scored, though nobody has done so. Per-vertebra inclinations can be learned on radiographs to about 2 degrees (Shi 2025 coronal, 2.10 degrees MAE; Yao 2024's disc angle 1.15 degrees), and the sagittal inter-end-plate angle agrees with radiologists at ICC 0.94 on MRI (Germann 2023), so a wedge angle of a few degrees, Panjabi's 2 to 5 degree inclinations summed, sits at the edge of what these methods resolve. Concavity depth is the weakest end-plate measure: ICC 0.79 to 0.85 against readers with limits of agreement of 2 to 2.7 mm on a depth of 1 to 3 mm (Germann 2023; Chen 2013 and Cui 2026 give the reference range by hand), and degenerative end-plates are where every method loses (Germann's false positives, Endplate3D-QCT's recall of 0.55 on severe degeneration). The level trends bordercall needs exist as tables but not as code: widths rise from L1 to L5 (Li 2026), area follows position along the column with r above 0.9 and dips at T4 and L5-S1 (Kishimoto 2016), cervical slopes peak at C5 (Chen 2013), and NHANES II's 97,073 vertebrae give the end-plate width ratio and the inter-end-plate angle per level, with the finding that the angle and the height ratio carry the same information (Hipp 2022). The only method that tried to name a level from such a measure is Hipp 2022's diagonal ratio separating S1 from L1 to L5 (AUC 0.995); nothing found names a level from an end-plate width, depth, area or angle, and no method handles transitional vertebrae.

Not settled. No public test set carries expert end-plate width, depth, area or inclination values per vertebra over the whole column: Endplate3D-QCT is lumbar masks, VerSe and TotalSegmentator are masks, NHANES II's landmarks are commercial, Kramer's three-rater table covers six patients and is not released. No method reports end-plate area automatically against a reader. No published code computes EPWu, EPDu, EPWl, EPDl, the inclinations and the concavity together from a mask; the two repositories that come closest have no licence, and TPTBox's end-plate functions, if any, are undocumented. No radiograph method gives end-plate width or depth in mm, since every published one works on uncalibrated films in ratios or percent of body size, and the existing note's magnification and oblique-projection findings stand unaddressed by any source found. Thoracic end-plates are the least measured: Kramer's thoracic errors are the largest, Hipp has no thoracic films, the lumbar MRI and radiograph methods stop at T12 or T9.
