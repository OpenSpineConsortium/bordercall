# Prior art for the end-plate family: width, depth, area, inclination, wedge angle, concavity

Source: the thread "Plan clinical reread next steps", 2026-10-29, web reading (WebSearch and WebFetch; arXiv, Europe PMC's REST API, MICCAI's open PDF, GitHub raw files and Zenodo's API, since PMC answered with a reCAPTCHA page, Springer with a sign-in redirect, and MDPI, DOAJ, JoVE and Frontiers' search pages with 403). The family is the ten keys of bordercall's vocabulary.csv: endplate (EPWu), endplate_depth (EPDu), endplate_lower (EPWl), endplate_lower_depth (EPDl), endplate_area (EPAu), endplate_lower_area (EPAl), endplate_incl (EPItu), endplate_lower_incl (EPItl), endplate_wedge (EPIt, the sum of the two inclinations) and endplate_concavity (depth, no Panjabi code). Reading-status tags follow the existing notes: [full] the paper or page read whole, [abstract] abstract or record only, [page] a web page or search summary, [code] a repository read.

Reviewed on 2026-10-30 (thread "Finish the clinical reread", project symphony_5): a second reader checked 262 claims at their sources and found 14 wrong, unsupported or imprecise; a refuter held 13, which are corrected here, and refuted one (Shi 2025 releases no data: the README's clone line is the code repository). Most of the corrections come from reading in full what the sweep had only as abstracts (Doerr 2020, Kishimoto 2016, Chen 2013, Jang 2023, Li 2026, Yao 2018). A second sweep added six rows (Yao 2018, Liu 2018, Jang 2023, Medical Metrics, Vrtovec, the six-point morphometry software) and read the code of Spine-Morphology-Analyzer, TPTBox, vertebral-heights and Endplate3D-QCT, each checked again at its source by a verifier. The record is [review/](review/README.md).

## 1. What the existing notes already settle (read first, not re-derived)

- SchwingNet docs/research/morphometry-beyond-the-42-papers.md, row "Panjabi's printed tables are what was measured": the lumbar paper prints its upper and lower end-plate inclinations at L1 to L5 as the same 20 numbers the cervical paper prints at C3 to C7, an open finding of the record check; and row "Dry, boiled bone measures like CT or fluoroscopy": on CTSpinoPelvic1K's labels end-plate width, canal width and canal depth were within a millimetre of Panjabi at L1 and L3 while posterior body height ran 2.3 to 7.5 mm high. Its bullet on the pilot says the vendored bordercall measure step writes upper end-plate width only, no depth and no lower end plate.
- bordercall research_notes "Vertebra measures without masks/ct_intensity_methods.md", section 5 and its table: Stern 2011 fits a superquadric with 25 shape parameters to CT and MR bodies, and Stern 2013 classifies 682 bodies from height, end-plate inclination and concavity read off the fitted parameters, with no millimetre error against manual measurement; no study read there reports automated end-plate width or depth versus manual in mm without a trained model.
- The same folder's masks_corners_magnification.md: six-point morphometry gives heights, not end-plate widths, and no readable source reports end-plate depth or width reproducibility in mm (section 1); AASCE corners give the upper end-plate as the segment between corners 1 and 2 and the reference Cobb code uses the mid-point line, so it measures tilt, not end-plate-to-end-plate Cobb (section 2); a minimum-area rectangle forces the two end-plates parallel and equal, so it cannot express wedging (section 2); the projected end-plate on a tilted view is an ellipse about W sin(theta) tall, and rotation's effect on apparent width and depth is unquantified in the literature (section 4). Its magnification section: lateral films magnify 1.21 cervical and 1.31 lumbar against CT or MRI.
- radiograph_classical_methods.md: the Siemens patents US 6,608,917 and US 7,010,151 find end-plate lines on radiographs by curvature ridges and dynamic programming with no published accuracy; Zhang 2009's fuzzy Hough gives end-plate lines and Cobb (ICC above 0.95, 76 films) with the user marking the end vertebrae; no published millimetre corner result is training-free.
- SchwingNet docs/research/benchmarks-to-beat.md and public-spine-and-pelvis-models-to-benchmark.md carry nothing on end-plate measures beyond FleXray's Cobb angle on AASCE (5.13 degrees, computed from masks by code that is not public, cited in masks_corners_magnification.md section 2).

## 2. The sweep

### 2.1 By measure

End-plate width and depth (EPWu, EPDu, EPWl, EPDl).

- Kramer, Rittel, Blomenkamp, Bauer, Paulus (University of Koblenz), "3D Vertebrae Measurements: Assessing Vertebral Dimensions in Human Spine Mesh Models Using Local Anatomical Vertebral Axes", arXiv 2402.01462 (one version, 2 Feb 2024), published as "3D Vertebrae Measurements: Assessing Vertebral Dimensions in Human Spine Models Using Automated Landmark Detection", IEEE ISBI 2025, pp 1-5, doi 10.1109/ISBI60581.2025.10981279 [full, arXiv HTML; the ISBI version from its OpenAlex record, abstract only, which gives 1.09 mm and 0.69 mm where the arXiv abstract gives 1.09 mm and 0.7 mm]. Training-free geometry on a vertebra mesh: a cubic spline through the vertebrae's centres of mass gives the local up-vector, the bounding-box axis nearest global right gives the right-vector, end-plates are the vertex sets whose normals lie within 45 degrees of the up-axis, and the landmarks are the extreme intersections of each end-plate with the local sagittal and frontal planes. It measures upper and lower end-plate width and depth and five heights, no angle. Test: 6 of VerSe's 141 patients (L2, L3, T4, T5) against three raters in 3D Slicer, and 50 artificial lumbar spines. MAE in mm (VerSe lumbar / VerSe thoracic / artificial): upper width 0.75 / 3.73 / 0.89, lower width 0.88 / 0.26 / 0.86, upper depth 0.49 / 1.22 / 0.73, lower depth 0.54 / 1.45 / 0.80; raters' ICC 0.84 lumbar and 0.96 thoracic; manual widths had an SD of 3.41 mm. The paper's own averages (1.09 mm abstract, 1.32 mm text) do not match its table. Code: github.com/VisSim-UniKO/Spine-Morphology-Analyzer [code]: no licence file, 16 commits; a 3D Slicer module (SlicerPlugins/Dimensions) whose result table gives upper and lower end-plate width and depth and four heights per vertebra and whose save button writes inter-vertebral angles, the signed angle between adjacent upper end-plate regression lines; the SpineLib submodule (Caetox/SpineLib at 773b590, no licence either) keeps the faces within 45 degrees of the superior or inferior direction and cuts a sagittal and a frontal curve through each end-plate. No inclination per end-plate and no area. Lacks: six patients, no cervical, assumes LPS orientation, no level labels of its own (it takes a labelled mesh), no transitional handling.
- Cappetti, Di Angelo, Fontana, Marzola, "A computer-based method for the automatic identification of the dimensional features of human cervical vertebrae", Computer Methods and Programs in Biomedicine Update 7:100175, online 18 December 2024, gold open access CC BY-NC-ND [abstract, OpenAlex record; the publisher's full text not reached and the L'Aquila repository record holds no file]: a local coordinate system and rules measure the vertebral body, foramen and spinous process in the sagittal and coronal planes of high-density point clouds from CT or 3D scans, compared with manual measurement on nine complete cervical tracts; the manual method showed poor repeatability and reproducibility, the automatic one consistent and repeatable results. The abstract names no end-plate dimension and gives no error.
- Li, Lv, Tang, Jia, Gao, "Morphological characteristics of lumbar vertebral bodies and regional distribution patterns of bone mineral density: a CT study", Front Physiol 17:1820186, 2026 [full, Europe PMC XML, PMC13215853]: 100 healthy volunteers aged 20 to 70, L1 to L5, superior and inferior end-plate widths, three heights and anterior and posterior third cortical thickness of the superior end-plate; widths rise from L1 to L5. Manual, on the mid-sagittal and mid-coronal planes, by one trained observer blinded to subject characteristics, each parameter measured three times and averaged; intra-observer ICC above 0.90 for every parameter on 20 vertebrae re-measured after 2 weeks; inter-observer reliability not evaluated, as the authors say; no depth, area or concavity. A population table, not a method.
- Kishimoto, Akeda, Sudo, Espinoza Orias, Inoue, "In vivo measurement of vertebral endplate surface area along the whole-spine", J Orthop Res 34(8):1418-1430, 2016 (PMC6521950) [full, PMC HTML]: 49 patients, myelogram CT, 3D mesh models of superior and inferior bony end-plates of C2 through S1, 2,352 end-plate models from 1,176 vertebral bodies, tabulated from the inferior end-plate of C2 to the superior end-plate of S1; total mesh area, projected area, anteroposterior and transverse diameters, each end-plate placed by its cumulative distance from C2; area correlates with position along the column (polygon area r 0.938 men, 0.911 women; projected area 0.943 and 0.922); the anteroposterior or transverse diameters dip at T4 and L5-S1, and the area falls from L4 to L5 (inferior end-plates) and from L5 to S1 (superior). This is the one whole-column area table found, EPAu and EPAl at every level in one person, and the one source that relates an end-plate measure to the position along the column, which is bordercall's question. Mimics 16.0 at a 226 HU threshold with Wrap smoothing at a smallest detail of 1.0 mm, superior and inferior end-plates segmented from each vertebral model with manifest osteophytes left out, polygon, projected and elliptical areas and diameters along the end-plate's eigenvectors by the authors' own Visual C++ routine; no manual editing is described; no reliability statistic anywhere in the paper; no data statement; the 49 patients (25 men, 24 women, aged 22 to 88, mean 61.8) had suspected stenosis or cord disorders on 1.0 mm myelogram CT.
- Yao Q et al., "Differences of the morphology of subaxial cervical spine endplates between Chinese and White men and women", Biomed Res Int 2018:2854175 (PMC5838464) [full, Europe PMC XML]: 41 Chinese and 24 White healthy subjects, 0.625 mm CT, the upper and lower bony end-plates C3 to C7 (650 end-plates) reconstructed in Rhinoceros, then an automatic algorithm fits each end-plate's plane, projects it for the area and measures width and depth along left-right and anteroposterior axes set by the body centre and the spinous process tip, as EPWu, EPDu, EPWl, EPDl, EPAu and EPAl, the vocabulary's own codes; all linear parameters rise from C3 to C7 except EPDl; Chinese EPWu 14.6 +/- 1.8 mm at C3 to 19.1 +/- 2.5 at C7 and EPAu 169.9 +/- 39.2 to 263.1 +/- 64.8 mm2 (Tables 2 and 3); no reliability statistic and no data statement. The one in vivo source measuring upper and lower widths, depths and areas under the vocabulary's codes, cervical only.
- Medical Metrics, J Imaging Inform Med 39(3):2427, online 2025-09-29 [full]: scales lateral films by an assumed anterior-posterior width of the superior end-plate per level (C2 12.02 to C7 16.52 mm; L1 31.74 to L4 33.05 and S1 32.25 mm), taken from a proprietary set of 3,000 cervical and 870 lumbar films; scale error against a calibration ring 9.74 to 9.91 percent cervical and 7.51 to 7.69 lumbar, against 19.57 to 35.69 percent for the DICOM scale factor. The width is the lateral-view depth, bordercall's EPDu, and the level picks the width, the reverse of naming a level from it; the labelling and landmarking modules are trade secrets.

End-plate area (EPAu, EPAl).

- Kishimoto 2016 above is the only in vivo whole-spine area source found; the implant-sizing cervical CT studies the search returned (Frontiers Bioeng 2022, AOTT 2016, PMC6145136, PMC7886465 [page, search summaries]) measure anteroposterior and mediolateral diameters by hand on CT and report mismatch with implant footprints; none is automatic and none was read whole.

End-plate inclination and wedge angle (EPItu, EPItl, EPIt).

- Doerr, De Silva, Vijayan, Han, Uneri, Ketcha, ... Siewerdsen (Johns Hopkins), "Automatic analysis of global spinal alignment from simple annotation of vertebral bodies", J Med Imaging 7(3):035001, 2020 (PMC7218103) [full, PMC HTML]: two methods take one point label per vertebra, placed by an expert radiologist near the body centroid, in spine CT, EndSeg segments the end-plates from the labels as seeds and SpNorm fits a 2D curve through the labels; seven patient CTs from SpineWeb Dataset 14, C7 to S1, with normal or abnormal kyphosis and lordosis; five clinicians (two radiologists, three spinal neurosurgeons) each marked single end-plates on 128 digitally reconstructed radiographs (49 lateral and 49 AP, plus 15 repeats of each); the automatic end-plate angles at T2, T3, T4, T8, L1, L3 and S1 differ from the readers' by 1.9 degrees for EndSeg and 2.7 for SpNorm (mean absolute difference over the seven levels, Table 5; per level -4.0 to 1.9 and -5.3 to 5.6 degrees), against an inter-reader CI95 of 5.8 degrees in end-plate angle, 93.8 percent of estimates inside it; no Dice or mm for the end-plate segmentation, no code or data statement. This is the one CT method found that validates per-end-plate angles against readers.
- Shi, Meng, Zhuang, ... Zhang (Hong Kong), "Accurate Cobb angle estimation via SVD-based curve detection and vertebral wedging quantification", arXiv 2509.24898, 2025 [full, arXiv HTML]: an HRNet-18 with a heatmap and vector head predicts the superior and inferior end-plate angle and midpoint of each of 18 vertebrae, C7 to L5, on 630 standing PA EOS radiographs of adolescents aged 10 to 18 from two Hong Kong hospitals (441/63/126 split), two surgeons' annotations with Cobb inter-rater MAD 3.1 +/- 1.8 degrees; end-plate angle MAE 2.10 +/- 0.08 degrees, maximum Cobb MAE 2.55 degrees. Its Vertebral Wedging Index is the mean over a curve of the absolute difference between a vertebra's upper and lower end-plate angles (the README instead calls it an anterior to posterior height ratio; the two disagree). Code github.com/SCBoy1007/AIS-Cobb-SVD-Analysis, MIT [code]; data not released. This is the closest published analogue of EPIt measured directly as an angle, on the coronal plane. Lacks: coronal only, adolescents, no mm scale, no lumbosacral end, no level labels beyond the fixed 18-vertebra count.
- Germann, Meyer, Staib, Sutter, Fritz, "Performance of a deep convolutional neural network for MRI-based vertebral body measurements and insufficiency fracture detection", Eur Radiol 2023 (PMC10121505) [full, Europe PMC XML]: a U-Net segments bodies on the mid-sagittal T2 slice and four corners per body give anterior and posterior height, superior and inferior end-plate concavity (maximum depression) and a vertebral angle (the angle between the two end-plates, EPIt's sagittal analogue); 200 patients, 1,000 lumbar bodies, mean age 75, two radiologists as truth; ICC against readers: vertebral angle 0.944 and 0.941 (bias 0, limits +/- 6.9 degrees), superior concavity 0.846 and 0.834 (bias 0, limits +/- 2.7 mm), inferior concavity 0.792 and 0.788 (bias -0.2, limits +/- 2.0 mm), against inter-reader ICC 0.932 and 0.891 for concavity. No code, no data. Lacks: one slice, L1 to L5 only, elderly, Schmorl nodes and degenerative end-plates degrade concavity, no width.
- The lumbar MR deformity framework, Al-Haidri, Akhatov, Usmanova, Salimov, Al-Habeeb, Il'yasov, Brui, "Deep learning-assisted framework for automation of lumbar vertebral body segmentation, measurement, and deformity detection in MR images", Biomed Signal Process Control 106:107770, 2025 [page, Crossref and OpenAlex records, neither with an abstract] (Mask R-CNN, 200 subjects, median height errors 0.78 to 0.86 mm, wedge median error 3.3 percent as a height ratio, all from a search summary) measures wedging as Ha/Hp, not as an angle.
- Hipp, Grieco, Newman, Reitman, "Definition of normal vertebral morphometry using NHANES-II radiographs", JBMR Plus 6(10):e10677, 2022 (PMC9549721) [full, Europe PMC XML, first 100,000 characters; the rest is the reference list]: Spine Camp (Medical Metrics) places four corners per body by neural networks trained on over 50,000 analyst-landmarked lateral films (QMA software, "previously validated"; the validation NASSJ 2026 cites is Zhao 2005, J Biomech 38(9):1943, six cadaveric lumbar spines with electromagnetic sensors, rotation error 0.47 +/- 0.24 degrees against 2.16 +/- 0.78 for three surgeons, whose abstract names only "new, FDA-approved, image-analysis software"; Pearson 2011 gives QMA's observer ICCs on 30 flexion-extension sets; neither abstract gives a landmark error in mm [abstract, both]), withholding low-confidence points; NHANES-II lateral films, 42,980 lumbar vertebrae (L1 to S1, 7,364 films) and 54,093 cervical (C2 to C7, 9,662 films), ages 25 to 74, obtained through public access. Six metrics per vertebra, two of them in this family: the superior over inferior end-plate width ratio (EPWR) and the angle between the superior and inferior end-plates (EPA, which is EPIt on a lateral film), beside the anterior/posterior height ratio, the diagonal ratio, the height/width ratio and the posterior-wall to superior end-plate angle. Vertebrae beyond 2 SD on any metric were trimmed; per-vertebra means and SDs are in Appendix S1; the derived data are "upon reasonable request"; raw landmark coordinates are not released; no mm or degree accuracy of the landmarking is given in the body (appendices S2 and S3 hold reproducibility and projection analyses, not read). Findings that matter here: EPA and the height ratio are almost perfectly correlated; the forward/backward diagonal ratio separates S1 from L1 to L5 with AUC 0.995; level explained more variance than age, sex or size; the authors say labels could be wrong without whole-spine films and the prevalence of transitional vertebrae in the set is unknown. Lacks: ratios and angles only, no mm (uncalibrated films), no thoracic, no mid-points, one view, labels assumed. Its fully automatic successor, SpineCAMP, matches the mean of five QMA analysts on 120 flexion-extension films from 49 sites (rotation MAE 0.16 degrees cervical and 0.18 lumbar, translation 0.09 and 0.14 mm; 4 lumbar images excluded for inconsistent numbering), with landmark agreement only as r2 0.9996 to 0.9999 and no corner error in mm (North Am Spine Soc J 28:100936, 2026, PMC13579288 [full]; Medical Metrics staff among the authors).
- Hipp's later automated metrics, QIMS 2025 (PMC11994510) and Bioengineering 2026 (PMC13024340) [abstract]: lumbar and C2 to C7 instability from flexion-extension films with the same FDA-cleared pipeline; rotation, translation and disc height, not this family's measures.
- Cina, Bassani, Panico, Luca, Masharawi, Brayda-Bruno, Galbusera, "2-step deep learning model for landmarks localization in spine radiographs", Sci Rep 11:9482, 2021 (PMC8096829) [full, Europe PMC XML]: four corners per body on 10,193 uncalibrated lateral lumbar films (Galeazzi, not public), one annotator, 30 images by four observers (ICC 0.99 x, 0.96 y); errors as percent of body width and height (medians 1.98 and 1.68 percent), angles from end-plate lines: L1-L5 lordosis median 1.84 degrees, L1-S1 2.43, sacral slope 1.98, maxima 34 to 50 degrees from missed vertebrae. The discussion says segmental wedging is obtainable from the corners but it was not evaluated. No code. Lacks: no mm, no per-vertebra inclination evaluated, lumbar only, detection failures propagate.
- Vrtovec 2011, 2013 and 2014 (Spine 36(13):E875; Clin Radiol 68(8):807; Comput Med Imaging Graph 38(7):620) [abstract]: vertebral inclination on sagittal CT, coronal MRI and sagittal MRI, 28 vertebrae each, three observers, six manual line methods against a computerised symmetry-plane method; on sagittal MRI the superior end-plate tangent varies by 2.6 degrees within and 3.7 between observers (SD) and the inferior by 3.2 and 4.5, against 1.4 and 1.9 for mid-wall lines; on CT the mid-wall lines reach 1.0 and 1.4, the computerised method 0.9 and 1.3. The authors conclude inclination should follow the body walls; tangent SDs are printed only for sagittal MRI.
- Yao, Zhang, Cheng, Chen, He, Wang, Zhou, Wang (Department of Radiology, Gansu Provincial Hospital, Lanzhou, with Gansu University of Chinese Medicine, Gansu Provincial Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine and two authors of Hangzhou Jianpei Technology), "Automatic measurement of anatomical parameters of the lumbar vertebral body and the intervertebral disc on radiographs by deep learning", Quant Imaging Med Surg 2024 (PMC11320507) [full, PMC HTML]: HRNet with distribution-aware decoding, six landmarks per body (anterior, middle and posterior points of each end-plate, placed to ignore osteophytes), 1,318 lateral lumbar films from one hospital (not public), three radiologists' mean as truth; VBH MAE 0.61 mm (ICC 0.98), disc height 0.63 mm, intervertebral disc angle from adjacent end-plate vectors 1.15 degrees (ICC 0.93); landmarks within 3 mm 98 percent, within 1 mm 69 percent (S1 44 percent). No code; a website is promised. The disc angle is the inter-vertebral analogue of the inclinations; no body wedge angle, width or depth. Lacks: T12 to S1 only, single centre, implants and severe deformity excluded.
- Suri et al., "Vertebral deformity measurements at MRI, CT, and radiography using deep learning", Radiol Artif Intell 4(1):e210015, 2022 (PMC8823454) [abstract]: six landmarks per body, 1,744 patients (1,123 MRI, 137 CT, 484 radiographs), heights with 1.5 to 1.9 percent error and lumbar lordosis MAE 2.26 to 3.60 degrees; no end-plate measure of this family, no code stated.
- Alukaev, Kiselev, Mustafaev, Ainur, Ibragimov, Vrtovec, "A deep learning framework for vertebral morphometry and Cobb angle measurement with external validation", Eur Spine J 31(8):2115-2124, 2022 [abstract]: U-Net ensemble on 3D CT, 160 training and 15 external CTs, body height MAE 1.17 mm, disc height 0.54 mm, Cobb 3.42 degrees; no end-plate dimension or inclination named.

End-plate concavity (endplate_concavity).

- Germann 2023 above: the only automatic concavity measurement with reader agreement found (ICC 0.79 to 0.85, limits +/- 2.0 to 2.7 mm), MRI, lumbar.
- Stern 2013 (Osteoporos Int, from the existing ct_intensity_methods.md note) [abstract]: concavity read from the superquadric's parameters, validated as fracture classification only.
- Chen, Zhong, Tan, Wu, Jiang, "Sagittal geometry of the middle and lower cervical endplates", Eur Spine J 22(7):1570-1575, 2013 (PMC3698350) [full, PMC HTML]: 97 people aged 25 to 51 (mean 41) with cervical CT for head and neck symptoms and no spinal complaint, 0.625 mm slices reformatted to the midsagittal plane, C3 to C7, 970 end-plates; landmarks marked by hand with the PACS's measurement tools by one trained user, no intra- or inter-observer statistic, precision 0.3 mm and 1.5 degrees; the slope is 90 degrees minus the angle between the rim line and the anterior wall; concavity depth 0.9 to 1.2 mm superior and 2.1 to 2.7 mm inferior, apex in the posterior half (56 to 67 percent superior, 52 to 57 inferior), slope 4.5 to 9.0 degrees superior and 4.5 to 7.5 inferior, largest at C5 and smallest at C7; age and sex had no effect. Reference values and a level trend for EPItu, EPItl and concavity, by hand.
- Cui, Wang, Sun, Zhang, Yin, Cui, Jiao, "Three-dimensional computed tomography morphometric analysis of lumbar endplates in Chinese young adults", Front Surg, April 2026 (PMC13095621) [full]: 33 adults aged 18 to 45, 165 end-plates (L4 and L5 both, S1 superior), Mimics, Geomagic and 3-Matic, manual segmentation with region growing, end-plates separated by curvature; sagittal and coronal diameter, concavity depth and partial lengths; two surgeons on 50 end-plates, ICC above 0.75; sagittal depth means about 1.3 to 2.1 mm at L4 and L5 and 0.4 to 0.6 at S1, coronal depth about 3.2 to 3.4 at the inferior end-plates; four shape classes (uniform concave, asymmetric, flat-bottomed, flat; S1 flat in 87.9 percent). Not automatic, no area, data "without undue reservation" but no link.
- Liu, Han, Gao, He, Cai, Niu, Gu, Li, Liang, He, "CT assisted morphological study of lumbar endplate", Zhongguo Gu Shang 31(12):1129-1135, 2018 (PubMed 30583653) [abstract, Europe PMC]: 60 healthy adults, men and women equal, lumbar CT into Mimics 16.0 for 3D reconstruction and measurement; midsagittal and maximum coronal diameters rise from L1 to L5; concavity depth 1.5 to 2.0 mm for the upper and 2.2 to 3.9 mm for the lower end-plates, changing little from L1 to L5, no sex difference, the lower deeper than the upper in men only; sagittal and coronal concavity angles change slightly with level; the deepest point of the caudal end-plates lies dorsally. How the points were placed and any reliability are not in the abstract.
- Feng et al., "Precision measurements and parametric models of vertebral endplates", J Vis Exp 151, 2019 (PMID 31609326) [abstract; the JoVE page was empty]: a high-speed scanner's point cloud of the end-plate, three sagittal and three frontal curves with 11 points each, parametric equations fitted; specimen count and reliability not in the abstract. The one explicit end-plate surface model found; it is a protocol on specimens, not an image method.
- Korez, Likar, Pernus, Vrtovec, "Parametric modeling of the intervertebral disc space in 3D: application to CT images of the lumbar spine", Comput Med Imaging Graph 38(7):596-605, 2014 [abstract]: a truncated elliptical cone deformed to the disc space, initialised from the adjacent bodies' models; 20 lumbar CTs, 100 disc spaces, 93 modelled, mean radial error 1.06 +/- 0.98 mm against manual points. Its two end faces are the adjacent end-plates, so heights, widths and inclinations are available from the parameters, but none is reported as a measure.
- Reinhold et al., "An analysis by synthesis approach for automatic vertebral shape identification in clinical QCT", GCPR 2018, arXiv 1812.00693 [abstract]: a statistical bone model fitted by as-rigid-as-possible deformation to find the cortical surface, validated on the European Spine Phantom and one micro-CT cadaver vertebra; no end-plate measure, no numbers in the abstract.

### 2.2 By dataset

- VerSe 2019 and 2020 [page, grand-challenge and PMC8553749 search summaries; existing notes]: per-vertebra masks and centroids, some Genant labels, no end-plate landmark or measure. Kramer 2024 adds three raters' measurements on 6 of its patients (not released as a table; its artificial set is). Zakharov et al., "Interpretable vertebral fracture quantification via anchor-free landmarks localization", arXiv 2204.06818, 2022 [full, ar5iv]: six coplanar points per vertebra, heights and the Genant index, trained on 100 LungCancer-500 CTs (3,565 vertebra annotations, released CC BY-NC 4.0 at github.com/neuro-ml/anchor-free-genant [code]) plus a private set, tested on VerSe 2020's public validation and test subsets; the six points are not defined in the text and no end-plate dimension is computed.
- TotalSegmentator [page]: masks only. The one tool found that measures from its output is mohamed32145/vertebral-heights [code]: an intrinsic frame from RANSAC plane fits of the two end-plates (normals only), a mirror plane by Dice, heights Ha, Hc, Hp, a wedge index and a "wedge angle" (undefined in the README), validated on synthetic phantoms alone (+/- 0.03 mm at 1 mm slices, "wedge angle unusable" at 3 mm), one commit, no licence, no reader comparison. TPTBox (Hendrik-code/TPTBox, Apache-2.0) [code, licence file and docs page]: points of interest on vertebrae and a spinestats module (distances, angles, disc heights per its description); its spinestats source (read in the review) gives a signed segmental end-plate angle between adjacent vertebrae (the code takes each vertebra's inferior direction, while the docstring speaks of the inferior end-plate of N and the superior of N+1), sagittal and coronal wedge angles per vertebra as atan of the height difference over a width, body widths, and the S1 superior end-plate from two points; no end-plate width, depth, area, per-end-plate inclination or concavity, and no accuracy against readers [code].
- CTSpine1K [page]: masks only, but Endplate3D-QCT is built on 119 of its volumes (below). CTSpinoPelvic1K's own per-level table of end-plate width and wedge ratio is the project's, covered in the existing notes.
- Endplate3D-QCT, Yin, Zou, Zhao, Zhang, Li, Wu, Yan, Wang (Peking University), MICCAI 2025, LNCS 10.1007/978-3-032-05141-7_15 [full, the open PDF at papers.miccai.org]: the first public pixel-level end-plate dataset. 170 lumbar CT volumes: 119 from CTSpine1K at 0.8 mm isotropic (CTSpine-Refined, 1,309 surfaces) plus 51 preoperative degenerative cases at 0.4 x 0.4 x 0.75 mm (25 LDD-Mild, 26 LDD-Severe, 10 with scoliosis), mean age 66; superior and inferior end-plates L1 to L5 and superior S1, 11 per scan, 1,870 instances, cortical end-plate segmented slice by slice in the sagittal plane by four spine surgeons under a manual and checked by a fifth; no inter-reader statistic. Benchmarks trained with nnU-Net: on CTSpine-Refined, VNet and 3D U-Net Dice 0.901, instance Dice 0.905, HD95 about 1.0 mm, ASD 0.20 mm, detection F1 0.947; on LDD-Severe Dice 0.885 (VNet) and detection recall 0.55 (EfficientUNet) to 0.89 (3D U-Net); S1 is missed for sacral curvature. Zenodo record 15784487 [page, Zenodo API]: access right open, licence CC BY 4.0, one 2.67 GB zip, published 2025-09-19; the GitHub README links the record through an access token and names the evaluation code end_plate_postproc; the evaluation code (end_plate_postproc: Dice, Jaccard, HD95 and ASD after resampling to 1 mm, detection at Dice above 0.8, mean HU inside each end-plate) is public with no licence file [code]. LNCS 15970, pp 147-156. No geometric measure is computed from the masks: the paper's motivation is end-plate BMD. For this family it is the one public truth from which EPWu, EPDu, EPWl, EPDl, EPAu, EPAl, both inclinations and the concavity could be measured and compared to a reader's surface, lumbar only.
- SPIDER (Zenodo 10159224) [page, Zenodo record]: 218 patients, 447 sagittal T1 and T2 series, segmentations of vertebrae, discs and canal, CC BY 4.0; end-plate changes and Schmorl nodes are binary gradings per level, no geometric end-plate measure.
- MRSpineSeg [page]: nothing on end-plates found in the one search that reached it (an arXiv secondary source), not read.
- BUU-LSPINE (services.informatics.buu.ac.th/spine) [page]: 400 AP and lateral pairs, upper and lower edge lines of each lumbar vertebra as point coordinates in CSV, listhesis labels, the page sets out an end-user licence agreement; a 400-pair sample is a direct Google Drive download, the full set (the button says BUU-LSPINE(3600)) is e-mailed after a Google Form request is reviewed, and a 2,000-pair set with side bending, rotation and flexion/extension labels is a direct Google Drive download; the number of points per line is not stated. Jang, Kim, Ku, Lee, "Reliability analysis of vertebral landmark labelling on lumbar spine X-ray images", Diagnostics 13(8):1411, 2023 (PMC10137367) [full, Europe PMC XML]: the images are 1,000 of 2,000 patients' lumbar films taken at Catholic Kwandong University International St. Mary's Hospital, Korea, February 2014 to May 2019, not BUU-LSPINE; four corner points per vertebra (p1 to p4) joined into upper and lower end-plate lines on AP and lateral views; on the 200 pairs all 12 raters labelled, mean point variation 1.185 to 3.430 mm and ICC 0.934 to 0.991; the data are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request, so the labels are not released. End-plate angle RMSE among the raters 0.906 to 1.486 degrees lateral and 0.577 to 0.979 AP.
- NHANES II [full, Hipp 2022 above]: the end-plate width ratio and the angle between end-plates are tabulated per level for 97,073 vertebrae, cervical and lumbar, by a commercial network; the images are public, the landmarks are not.
- AASCE 2019 [page, existing note and search summaries]: 609 training AP films with 68 corners (17 vertebrae T1 to L5), 98 test; corners give coronal end-plate lines and so a coronal wedge angle per vertebra, but the metric is Cobb SMAPE and no paper found reports per-vertebra end-plate angle error on it. Shi 2025 used its own 630 EOS films instead.
- SpineWeb [page, secondary]: only CSI 2014 and xVertSeg among CT sets per one arXiv source; no end-plate truth found.

### 2.3 By code and challenge

Public code found that touches an end-plate measure: VisSim-UniKO/Spine-Morphology-Analyzer (width and depth of both end-plates and an inter-vertebral angle, no licence), mohamed32145/vertebral-heights (end-plate plane normals, wedge angle, no licence, phantoms only), SCBoy1007/AIS-Cobb-SVD-Analysis (coronal end-plate angles, MIT), neuro-ml/anchor-free-genant (six-point annotations, CC BY-NC 4.0), Hendrik-code/TPTBox (landmarks, a segmental end-plate angle and a wedge angle per vertebra, Apache-2.0), yin876705249/Endplate3D-QCT (evaluation code with no licence, segmentation metrics and BMD only; dataset CC BY 4.0). Commercial six-point morphometry (SpineAnalyzer, Hologic MXA, GE Lunar DVA) reports heights and height ratios, never an end-plate width, depth, area or inclination [page]. No PyPI or Zenodo code release for end-plate dimensions was found. No MICCAI or Grand Challenge task scores an end-plate dimension or angle; Endplate3D-QCT is a segmentation benchmark and AASCE scores Cobb.

## 4. What the family's prior art settles and does not

Settled. End-plate width and depth can be read off a labelled CT mesh without training to within about 0.5 to 0.9 mm of three raters in the lumbar spine (Kramer 2024), which is below the millimetre error bordercall assumes for every length, but on six patients and with 3.7 mm on one thoracic width; the raters themselves disagree by an SD of 3.41 mm on widths. A public lumbar end-plate surface truth exists since September 2025 (Endplate3D-QCT, CC BY 4.0, 1,870 surfaces on CTSpine1K and degenerative cases) against which any end-plate measure, area and concavity included, can be scored, though nobody has done so. Per-vertebra inclinations can be learned on radiographs to about 2 degrees (Shi 2025 coronal, 2.10 degrees MAE; Yao 2024's disc angle 1.15 degrees), and the sagittal inter-end-plate angle agrees with radiologists at ICC 0.94 on MRI (Germann 2023), so a wedge angle of a few degrees, Panjabi's 2 to 5 degree inclinations summed, sits at the edge of what these methods resolve; readers themselves place a lumbar end-plate line on films to an angle RMSE of 0.6 to 1.5 degrees among twelve raters (Jang 2023), and an end-plate tangent on sagittal MRI varies by 2.6 to 4.5 degrees SD (Vrtovec 2014). Concavity depth is the weakest end-plate measure: ICC 0.79 to 0.85 against readers with limits of agreement of 2 to 2.7 mm on a depth of 1 to 3 mm (Germann 2023; Chen 2013 and Cui 2026 give the reference range by hand), and degenerative end-plates are where every method loses (Germann's false positives, Endplate3D-QCT's recall of 0.55 on severe degeneration). The level trends bordercall needs exist as tables but not as code: widths rise from L1 to L5 (Li 2026), area follows position along the column with r above 0.9 (polygon area 0.938 men, 0.911 women; projected area 0.943 and 0.922), while the anteroposterior or transverse diameters dip at T4 and L5-S1 and the end-plate area falls from L5 to S1 (Kishimoto 2016), cervical widths, depths and areas rise from C3 to C7 under the vocabulary's own codes (Yao 2018, computed from hand-built models with no accuracy reported), cervical slopes peak at C5 (Chen 2013), and NHANES II's 97,073 vertebrae give the end-plate width ratio and the inter-end-plate angle per level, with the finding that the angle and the height ratio carry the same information (Hipp 2022). The only method that tried to name a level from such a measure is Hipp 2022's diagonal ratio separating S1 from L1 to L5 (AUC 0.995); nothing found names a level from an end-plate width, depth, area or angle, and no method handles transitional vertebrae.

Not settled. No public test set carries expert end-plate width, depth, area or inclination values per vertebra over the whole column: Endplate3D-QCT is lumbar masks, VerSe and TotalSegmentator are masks, NHANES II's landmarks are commercial, Kramer's three-rater table covers six patients and is not released. No method reports end-plate area automatically against a reader. No published code computes EPWu, EPDu, EPWl, EPDl, the inclinations and the concavity together from a mask; the two repositories that come closest have no licence, and TPTBox gives a wedge angle per vertebra and a segmental end-plate angle but no end-plate width, depth, area or concavity; commercial six-point morphometry reports none of them either. No radiograph method gives end-plate width or depth in mm, since every published one works on uncalibrated films in ratios or percent of body size (Medical Metrics runs the other way, assuming a per-level superior end-plate depth in mm to scale a film whose levels it already has), and the existing note's magnification and oblique-projection findings stand unaddressed by any source found. Thoracic end-plates are the least measured: Kramer's thoracic errors are the largest, Hipp has no thoracic films, the lumbar MRI and radiograph methods stop at T12 or T9.

## 5. Gaps: what could not be read

- Cappetti 2025: the abstract is read (OpenAlex, DOAJ's API); the publisher's full text was not reached and the L'Aquila record holds no file, so which end-plate dimensions it measures, and its errors, stay unread.
- Hipp 2022 appendices S2 and S3 (landmark reproducibility and projection error): the XML's second half read was the reference list; the appendices are supporting files not fetched. QMA's published validations (Zhao 2005, Pearson 2011) were read as abstracts and give no landmark error in mm.
- Feng 2019 JoVE (specimens, reliability, the fitted equations): the page returned empty and there is no PMC copy; abstract only.
- Endplate3D-QCT's Zenodo zip's contents (not downloaded, by rule). The README's Zenodo link carries an access token; it is not copied here.
- The lumbar MR deformity framework (Al-Haidri 2025, BSPC 106:107770): identified by Crossref and OpenAlex, neither with an abstract; its figures stay a search summary. MRSpineSeg was not searched for end-plates again.
- Read at abstract level only: Vrtovec 2011, 2013 and 2014 (the end-plate tangent SDs are printed only for sagittal MRI), Zhao 2005, Pearson 2011 and the VFA reader study of Mostert 2021. Jang 2023's supplementary zip and the attribution of SpineWeb Dataset 14 were not read.
