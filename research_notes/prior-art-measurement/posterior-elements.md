# Prior art for the posterior elements other than pedicles and facets

Source: the thread "Plan clinical reread next steps", 2026-10-29, web reading (WebSearch and WebFetch) by one
sweeper for one family of bordercall's vocabulary. The family is the transverse processes, the spinous processes,
the laminae and the costal and uncovertebral articulations: the keys spinous_length (SPL), spinous_h,
spinous_angle, transverse_width (TPW), transverse_length, transverse_h, lamina_thickness, lamina_h, lamina_w,
lamina_angle, uncovertebral_area_upper and _lower (UJAru, UJArl), the four uncovertebral inclinations (UJIfu,
UJIsu, UJIfl, UJIsl), costovertebral_area_upper, _lower and total (CVAur, CVAlr, CVA), costotransverse_area (CTAr)
and rib_articulation_area (CVA + CTA), as /home/user/bordercall/research_notes/paper-tables/tools/vocabulary.csv
defines them. Of these, SPL, TPW and the eleven area and inclination keys (the two uncovertebral areas, the four
uncovertebral inclinations, the three costovertebral areas, the costotransverse area and the rib articulation
total) are Panjabi's, thirteen of the family's 21 [code: vocabulary.csv] (the cervical paper of 1991 for the
uncovertebral joints, the thoracic paper of 1991 for the rib articulations); the lamina keys, spinous_h,
spinous_angle, transverse_length and transverse_h come from the clinical reread and have no Panjabi column.

## What the existing notes already say about this family

- morphometry-beyond-the-42-papers.md (SchwingNet, "What the rule takes from the papers" and "What they cost"):
  the family's Panjabi keys come from the twelve cadaveric spines of 1990 or earlier; the archive's measure step
  reads whole-vertebra labels and writes, among seven measures, a "transverse-process span"; spineps_map.py takes
  two unregistered process extents, tp_span and spinous_length, and nothing reads a lamina, a joint area or an
  inclination. project-status-2026-10-28.md adds that the best seven measures on synthetic columns include spinous
  length and transverse width, which "no code measures" as registered measurers. So in bordercall's own record, the
  two Panjabi extents of this family are the ones most worth a measurer, and the mask recipes for them are only
  Inferences (clinical-reread/notes/panjabi1991c.md, SPL and TPW rows; the uncovertebral row says the joint surface
  "needs the joint surface" and no recipe beyond a patch of the body subregion facing the neighbour).
- The same note's dataset table: no public CT set it lists carries a substructure label; VerSe, TotalSegmentator,
  CTSpine1K and the RSNA cervical masks are whole vertebrae, VertebralBodiesCT-Labels is bodies only, and the
  NHANES II labels (Zenodo 10223910, Mo, Papiez and others, CC BY 4.0, one zip named for vertebral osteophytes) are
  described on the record as vertebra coordinates on 600 annotated films, with C2 and S1 reference coordinates
  added by two radiologists [page]; morphometry-beyond-the-42-papers.md reads its pickles as per-vertebra masks
  named by level on 543 films of 364 people; whether a mask outlines the body alone or the whole vertebra is not
  stated, and no posterior element is labelled. It records that bordercall measures only from existing SPINEPS
  masks and that SPINEPS "so far give[s] only the seven measures".
- "Vertebra measures without masks/ct_intensity_methods.md": the Radnostics patent (US 8,891,848 B2) [full] seeds its
  canal finder from the spinous-process tips, the most posterior high-density voxel per axial slice on the central
  sagittal planes, filtered for rib origins; the upper thoracic costovertebral junctions are where the intensity
  methods fail (Yao 2016). That is the only place the existing notes use a posterior element, and only as a seed.
- "Vertebra measures without masks/radiograph_classical_methods.md": Dinesh Kumar and Thomas 2005 [abstract] find the
  posterior spine boundary on 100 NHANES II cervical films by ridge points; no spinous or transverse measure.
- public-spine-and-pelvis-models-to-benchmark.md: FleXray is a 2D UNet with 60 structures plus background, one
  channel per vertebra C1 to the sacrum plus the hips, the femurs and the ribs; drr-domain-gap/README.md adds that
  its protocol all_structures_flexray_v4 merges left and right and keeps the ribs by number, dropping rib_left_13
  and rib_right_13 (flexray-audit-companion.md the same); whole vertebrae in projection, no process channels.
  benchmarks-to-beat.md does not name FleXray (its ribs at 0.54 Dice are FluoroSAM's, as one class). Nothing there
  measures a posterior element.

The existing notes therefore hold no prior art for this family beyond Panjabi; everything below is new.

## What the family's prior art settles and does not

Settled. Lamina, transverse and spinous processes can be labelled on CT by a 3D U-Net well enough to measure from: Da Mutten's external Dice is 0.98 to 0.99 for each of the seven subregions from 118 public VerSe 2020 training scans, on a public TotalSegmentator subset, and LumASe gives 663 public lumbar vertebrae with the same seven labels drawn by three physicians, the one public truth of this kind. SPINEPS, whose masks bordercall already holds, labels the spinous process, each costal process and the arch, so spinous_length and transverse_width have a mask recipe today, but the arch label lumps lamina and pedicle, so no lamina key does. Spinous processes segment well on lateral cervical films (Dice 0.93) and a tip-to-tip gap can be read with an RMSE of 0.68 (limits of agreement 0.11 to 1.36 mm) from DICOM pixel spacing, so a projected spinous measure is feasible, with the magnification caveat the DICOM note gives. Ribs can be labelled 1 to 24 on CT with 91.5% label accuracy on a public test set of 160 scans, and the T12 rib is already used as a cue for the thoracolumbar junction, so the family's rib keys have a public benchmark for presence and count, though none for area. On ultrasound the lamina and the transverse process are detected to find a plane or name an interspace (Pesteie 2015, SLIDE, Chen 2026), never measured, and one public lumbar set pairs ultrasound with CT and labels the processes' bone surface (Cavalcanti 2025).

Not settled. No one has measured any of this family's keys automatically and reported mm or degree error against a reader: every accuracy found is a Dice, a detection, recognition or label rate, or a mm or degree error for something other than a family key (the interspinous gap between tips, RMSE 0.68; SLD's ligament landmarks, MAE 0.7 mm; Lenga's rib centrelines, 0.787 mm; the ultrasound scoliosis angles, 2.7 and 3.3 deg); the one figure on a family key, Siemionow's spinous process height, is one surgeon's measurement on the network's segmented slice as 97.81 percent of the same on the original, with no mm. No automated work touches the uncovertebral or costal articulations at all; the only sources are Panjabi and a handful of manual CT series, and Beyer's facet study gives orientation but not area. Nothing measures a posterior element across the whole column from C2 to the sacrum, so nothing can say whether the family's measures carry level information in patients; the bordercall finding that spinous length and transverse width are among the best seven rests on synthetic columns. No method handles a thirteenth rib pair, an eleventh, or a transitional vertebra's transverse process (the Castellvi types): RibSeg v2 mislabels a missing floating rib, Elton's cue assumes a twelfth rib, and the LSTV detector gives no type. By hand the cue has been measured: Jankowski 2026 read lumbar transverse process lengths across 11, 12 and 13 rib-bearing patterns on CT (L3 the longest single process in 30.0, 76.7 and 69.2 percent; inter-reader ICC 0.88 for single-side lengths), and Forseen 2015 read the lowest rib and the facet transition on 244 CTs, so reader-measured truth exists to score an automatic transverse_length against. The one public substructure set is lumbar, cropped and without reader agreement; the one thoracolumbar method's labels are on request, though both its CT sets are public; no cervical substructure set exists, so the uncinate and the bifid cervical spinous process have no truth anywhere.
