# Prior art for the posterior elements other than pedicles and facets

Source: the thread "Plan clinical reread next steps", 2026-10-29, web reading (WebSearch and WebFetch) by one
sweeper for one family of bordercall's vocabulary. The family is the transverse processes, the spinous processes,
the laminae and the costal and uncovertebral articulations: the keys spinous_length (SPL), spinous_h, spinous_angle,
transverse_width (TPW), transverse_length, transverse_h, lamina_thickness, lamina_h, lamina_w, lamina_angle,
uncovertebral_area_upper and _lower (UJAru, UJArl), the four uncovertebral inclinations (UJIfu, UJIsu, UJIfl, UJIsl),
costovertebral_area_upper, _lower and total (CVAur, CVAlr, CVA), costotransverse_area (CTAr) and rib_articulation_area
(CVA + CTA), as /home/user/bordercall/research_notes/paper-tables/tools/vocabulary.csv defines them. Of these, SPL,
TPW and the twelve area and inclination keys are Panjabi's (the cervical paper of 1991 for the uncovertebral joints,
the thoracic paper of 1991 for the rib articulations); the lamina keys, spinous_h, spinous_angle, transverse_length
and transverse_h come from the clinical reread and have no Panjabi column.

Reading-status tags, as the existing notes use them: [full] the full text or the whole page was read; [abstract] an
abstract or an abstract-level record; [page] a dataset, repository or documentation page; [code] a code file or README;
[search] only a search engine's summary of the source, the source itself refused (the weakest tag; every such claim is
also listed under Gaps). The search budget of this session ran out after about thirty queries, so the last planned
searches (the Blomenkamp geometric method, the Di Angelo 2021 paper's record, cervical and lumbar rib detection, a

## What the existing notes already say about this family

- morphometry-beyond-the-42-papers.md (SchwingNet, "What the rule takes from the papers" and "What they cost"):
  the family's Panjabi keys come from the twelve cadaveric spines of 1990 or earlier; the archive's measure step
  reads whole-vertebra labels and writes, among seven measures, a "transverse-process span"; spineps_map.py takes
  two unregistered process extents, tp_span and spinous_length, and nothing reads a lamina, a joint area or an
  inclination. project-status-2026-10-28.md adds that the best seven measures on synthetic columns include spinous
  length and transverse width, which "no code measures" as registered measurers. So in bordercall's own record, the
  two Panjabi extents of this family are the ones most worth a measurer, and the mask recipes for them are only
  Inferences (clinical-reread/notes/panjabi1991c.md, SPL and TPW rows; the uncovertebral row says the joint surface
  "needs the joint surface" and no recipe beyond a patch of the body subregion facing the neighbour).
- The same note's dataset table: no public CT set it lists carries a substructure label; VerSe, TotalSegmentator,
  CTSpine1K and the RSNA cervical masks are whole vertebrae, VertebralBodiesCT-Labels is bodies only, and the NHANES
  II labels (Zenodo 10223910) are per-vertebra masks of the body on lateral films. It records that bordercall measures
  only from existing SPINEPS masks and that SPINEPS "so far give[s] only the seven measures".
- "Vertebra measures without masks/ct_intensity_methods.md": the Radnostics patent (US 8,891,848 B2) [full] seeds its
  canal finder from the spinous-process tips, the most posterior high-density voxel per axial slice on the central
  sagittal planes, filtered for rib origins; the upper thoracic costovertebral junctions are where the intensity
  methods fail (Yao 2016). That is the only place the existing notes use a posterior element, and only as a seed.
- "Vertebra measures without masks/radiograph_classical_methods.md": Dinesh Kumar and Thomas 2005 [abstract] find the
  posterior spine boundary on 100 NHANES II cervical films by ridge points; no spinous or transverse measure.
- benchmarks-to-beat.md and public-spine-and-pelvis-models-to-benchmark.md: FleXray's channels are one per vertebra
  C1 to the sacrum plus rib_1 to rib_12 (left and right in one channel) [code]; whole vertebrae in projection, no
  process channels. Nothing there measures a posterior element.

The existing notes therefore hold no prior art for this family beyond Panjabi; everything below is new.

## What the family's prior art settles and does not

Settled. Lamina, transverse and spinous processes can be labelled on CT by a 3D U-Net well enough to measure from: Da Mutten's external Dice is 0.98 to 0.99 for each of the seven subregions from 118 training scans, and LumASe gives 663 public lumbar vertebrae with the same seven labels drawn by three physicians, the one public truth of this kind. SPINEPS, whose masks bordercall already holds, labels the spinous process, each costal process and the arch, so spinous_length and transverse_width have a mask recipe today, but the arch label lumps lamina and pedicle, so no lamina key does. Spinous processes segment well on lateral cervical films (Dice 0.93) and a tip-to-tip gap can be read to 0.7 mm RMSE with DICOM pixel spacing, so a projected spinous measure is feasible, with the magnification caveat the DICOM note gives. Ribs can be labelled 1 to 24 on CT with 91.5% label accuracy on a public test set of 160 scans, and the T12 rib is already used as a cue for the thoracolumbar junction, so the family's rib keys have a public benchmark for presence and count, though none for area.

Not settled. No one has measured any of this family's keys automatically and reported mm or degree error against a reader: every accuracy found is a Dice or a recognition rate, and the one mm figure (interspinous motion) is a gap, not a length. No automated work touches the uncovertebral or costal articulations at all; the only sources are Panjabi and a handful of manual CT series, and Beyer's facet study gives orientation but not area. Nothing measures a posterior element across the whole column from C2 to the sacrum, so nothing can say whether the family's measures carry level information in patients; the bordercall finding that spinous length and transverse width are among the best seven rests on synthetic columns. No method handles a thirteenth rib pair, an eleventh, or a transitional vertebra's transverse process (the Castellvi types): RibSeg v2 mislabels a missing floating rib, Elton's cue assumes a twelfth rib, and the LSTV detector gives no type. The one public substructure set is lumbar, cropped and without reader agreement; the one thoracolumbar method's data appear private; no cervical substructure set exists, so the uncinate and the bifid cervical spinous process have no truth anywhere.

## Gaps: what could not be read

- Da Mutten 2026 full text (Springer refused with a sign-in redirect): data source, external set, annotators, code and data statements, level labelling. Only the PubMed abstract was read.
- The Blomenkamp geometric partition paper (the baseline Da Mutten names) was not identified; the search budget ran out before the query. SLD (arXiv 2601.16782) was read only as an abstract; its PDF fetched as unreadable binary.
- Di Angelo 2021: only a search summary and a restricted repository record; whether it ran on CSI 2014, and its exact rates, are unverified.
- The J Craniovertebral Junction Spine 2020 paper: DOAJ (403) and Ovid (402) refused; all figures are from the search summary.
- LumASe: no paper, format or inter-reader agreement found; the Zenodo page was read.
- SPINEPS: per-subregion Dice, the weights' licence and the CT model's training data were not on the pages read; the arXiv abstract and the GitHub README were read, the full paper was not.
- CL-Net (arXiv 2503.12698) names "costovertebral bones" as a structure group in a search summary; the abstract page read gives no such list, no per-structure Dice and no code statement. Not entered in the table.
- The 2015 J Orthop Surg Res lamina paper (Springer sign-in redirect), the 2019 Pain Physician uncinate area paper, the 2025 PLoS One micro-CT uncinate paper (DOAJ 403), the Korean and Turkish C2 lamina studies and the lumbar lamina study: search summaries only.
- The CSXA source of CervSpineNet: hosting and licence not found in the part of the article read (the article's reference list was cut).
- RibSeg v2's data licence: not on the GitHub page or the arXiv HTML read.
- Not searched at all (budget): cervical or lumbar rib detection, ultrasound lamina or transverse-process detection for anaesthesia, an automated Castellvi typing method, a "SPINEPS CT" paper, VerSe-based morphometry pipelines that output process measures, the Zhou 2023 or VERIDAH posterior-element handling.
