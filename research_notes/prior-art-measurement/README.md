## What the families say about naming levels

- No public dataset carries per-level expert measurements of a vertebral body, a facet or a posterior element
  against which a measuring method is scored; the public truth is masks (VerSe, TotalSegmentator, CTSpine1K,
  SPINEPS's labels), fracture grades (VerSe's Genant grades) or landmarks (NHANES II's proprietary six-point
  landmarks, with public C2 and S1 anchors on Zenodo 10223910). A method that names levels from a measure is
  therefore scored today against masks' level labels, which the segmentation sets themselves mislabel at
  transitional vertebrae, as Hipp 2022 and ImaLife's level misnumbering in 95 of 632 people record.
- Level-dependent prior art exists in the literature's own tables rather than in code: Masharawi 2004's facet
  angles by level and Palm 2025's height ratios by level are the kind of table the paper-tables record pools, and
  neither is wrapped in a public tool.
- What is public and runnable: SPINEPS (Apache-2.0) for subregion masks including the articular processes;
  TotalSegmentator for vertebra masks; HealthiVert-GAN's code without weights. Everything that measures a
  dimension in millimetres from those masks is either unpublished, a one-file script with no validation, or
  validated only in percent on cadaver CT.

## Gaps

Each note lists what could not be read (RSNA, Springer, Nature, Spine, Wiley and MDPI pages refused from the cloud
session; read through PMC, PubMed, arXiv and university records where those exist). The searches the first readers'
budgets did not reach (Comp2Comp and the body width, depth and area queries; Blomenkamp's geometric method and MRI
or synthetic-CT planning; Fujimori's cervical work, MRSpineSeg, Spinal-AI2024, BUU-LSPINE's own paper and the
commercial EOS and Keops validations; the counts family's MRSpineSeg, Castellvi and rib-detector searches) were run
in the review of 2026-10-30 and their rows added; what stayed closed is in each note's Gaps, chiefly MRSpineSeg's
host, which no longer serves the set, and the arXiv and Semantic Scholar APIs, which refused the counts searches.

Across the nine families, the one level-naming result scored against public truth is Hipp 2022's diagonal end-plate
ratio naming S1 on NHANES II (AUC 0.995), and the one labeller that models 11 to 13 thoracic and 4 to 6 lumbar
vertebrae is VERIDAH; every automatic measurement in millimetres or degrees against a reader is on private data, and
no automatic method beyond Hipp's S1 ratio reads a level from a measure, which is what the paper-tables comparison
asks whether the literature's own tables allow; the two attempts found that read one from a measure (Forseen 2015's
facet transition, rejected by its own authors, and Jankowski 2026's L3 transverse process) were made by hand.
