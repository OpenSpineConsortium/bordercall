# Prior art for the alignment family: Cobb angles, lordosis, kyphosis, pelvic incidence, pelvic tilt, sacral slope, SVA

Source: the thread "Plan clinical reread next steps", 2026-10-29, web reading (arXiv, Europe PMC's REST records and full-text XML, MDPI, Frontiers, PMC where its browser check let a page through, figshare's API, raw.githubusercontent.com). ## 1. The coronal Cobb angle: the AASCE challenge and what followed it

### What AASCE is
- The challenge "Accurate Automated Spinal Curvature Estimation" ran with CSI at MICCAI 2019; its page names the training data as SpineWeb Dataset 16, the test data as a separate archive, the metric as "Cobb angle accuracy", and the organisers as Shuo Li (Western University) and Liansheng Wang (Xiamen University); it gives no image count and no landmark count. [page] -- aasce19.github.io
- The Medical Image Analysis review of the challenge (Wang L et al., Med Image Anal 2021;72:102115, doi 10.1016/j.media.2021.102115) [full, the Erasmus repository copy on pure.eur.nl]: 707 AP films from London Health Sciences Center taken with the EOS system, 609 training and 98 test; landmarks by "two professional doctors" at LHSC, four corners on 17 thoracic and lumbar vertebrae, 68 points; the training angles computed from the landmarks by the organisers' Matlab code, the test set giving only the three Cobb angles as truth; no inter-reader figure for the truth; JPG, its Table 2 printing 72 DPI and a pixel dimension of 144 micron, image lengths 973 to 3,755 px (training) and 985 to 7,316 (test); Cobb 0 to 156.39 degrees in training and 0 to 60.93 in test, the test set milder (11 under 10 degrees, 58 mild, 26 moderate, 3 severe), the authors saying the two differ in distribution; 53 training and 4 test films with steel nails. It reviews eight top-ranked methods of 12 teams: SMAPE Tencent 21.71, XMU 22.18, iFLYTEK 22.17, ErasmusMC 22.96, XDU 24.8, PAT 25.48, NAAMII 25.7 and UC 45.99, Wilcoxon tests finding no method significantly better than all others; it links code for all eight (five in its Table 3, three in footnotes); no data licence or download term is stated.
- MMA-Net's paper gives the provider's split: 609 AP spine X-rays, 481 for training and 128 for testing; the shape-constrained cascaded CNN paper gives the official split as 481 training, 128 validation and 98 challenge-private test images "without annotations", and a second "consistent split" of 431 training and 50 test images drawn from the official training set that earlier papers used. [full, arXiv PDF; full, Europe PMC XML] -- arXiv 2309.13817; PMC11670676
- The truth is corners placed by "two professional radiologists at London Health Science Center", and the dataset "also contains the angle measurements of the proximal thoracic section, main thoracic section, and thoracolumbar section"; the landmark files hold some vertebrae in the wrong order, which Chen et al. excluded from their test set (their Appendix A lists the ids); the MedIA review says "two professional doctors". [full, arXiv PDF] -- arXiv 2211.14122
- The metric is SMAPE over the three angles, averaged over test images; MMA-Net also reports MAE, a circular MAE (CMAE), and the Euclidean, Manhattan and Chebyshev distances over the three angles, the last the mean of each image's largest angle error. [full, arXiv HTML] -- arXiv 2309.13817
- Each AASCE film carries exactly 17 vertebrae (12 thoracic, 5 lumbar) identified by their position in the ordered sequence; no paper read names them T1 to L5 anatomically, none has a transitional vertebra, and the cascaded-CNN paper initialises its 17 centres from a normalised average shape. [full] -- arXiv 2211.14122; PMC11670676
- SpineWeb's host (spineweb.digitalimaginggroup.ca) did not resolve from this session; the Grand Challenge page refused the fetch tool (403) but answers a plain request, and its winners list names Seg4Reg first (Tencent YouTu Lab); the download terms and the licence were not read there, and the MedIA review states none. [page]
- What followed as synthetic data: Spinal-AI2024 (Shao Z, Yuan Y, Ma L, Zhu X, CurvNet, Pattern Recognit 2025;172:112546; arXiv 2411.12604) [full; code] is 20,000 AP scoliosis films at 512 x 512 generated by a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion v1.5 from the private Spinal2023 set of 904 X-rays, with landmarks and three Cobb angles written by the authors' data engine, split 16,000 training and 4,000 test; no patient is imaged, no reader made the truth, and neither its repository (github.com/Ernestchenchen/Spinal-AI2024) nor its README names a licence or a download link. CurvNet (github.com/Ernestchenchen/CurvNet, a LICENSE file that is MMOCR's Apache 2.0, no weights linked) reaches SMAPE 5.05 percent on AASCE's 128-film split and 5.34 on Spinal-AI2024, and its own error analysis puts 29 of the 37 proximal thoracic errors over 10 degrees on AASCE's 128 films down to cervical vertebrae detected as proximal thoracic, a wrong level at the cervicothoracic end of the counted column. Kuo and Chang 2026 (Mach Learn Knowl Extr 8(9):286) [abstract] use 550 of its films for end-vertebra finding, 86.63 percent within one level.

### Results on AASCE, as its own papers report them
- MMA-Net (Qiu Z, Yang J, Wang J, arXiv 2309.13817, Sep 2023; ICRA 2024 pp 9139-9145 by OpenAlex): segmentation of the spine region, centreline and boundary feeding a regression head; on the 128-image test split SMAPE 7.28 percent, MAE 3.18 degrees, circular MAE 2.26 degrees; its Table II lists SCG-Net SMAPE 22.18, Seg4Reg 21.71 (circular MAE 4.85), KEF (Guo et al., EMBC 2021) 8.62, VF MAE 3.51 and SMAPE 7.84, which is its reference 6, Kim KC et al., IEEE Access 2020 (vertebral-tilt vectors), not Yi 2020's network, Seg4Reg+ MAE 3.73 and SMAPE 7.32; no code statement was found in the text. [full, arXiv PDF]
- LaNet (Yang J, Wang J, Meng MQH, arXiv 2405.19645, 2024) [full]: a feature enhancement module, a landmark-focused loss and bending segments located before the Cobb is computed; on the 128 validation films SMAPE 4.51 percent and CMAE 2.06 degrees, the lowest read, and on the 98 test films SMAPE 8.99 against landmarks the authors drew themselves, since the challenge withheld the test truth, so that figure does not rank against the 2019 leaderboard; landmark errors mostly 2 to 4 mm, larger at T4 and L1; no repository link. Its tables give on the validation films Seg4Reg+ 7.32, MMA-Net 7.28, TsNet (Liang 2022) 6.87, VLTENet 5.44, Yi 2020 10.48 and NdpaNet (Zhang 2021) 8.64.
- Seg4Reg (Lin et al., the challenge's first place, Tencent YouTu Lab): SMAPE 21.71 percent on the 98-image challenge test set, by ensembling models; Seg4Reg+ (arXiv 2208.12462) uses the 481/128 provider split, so its 7.32 percent is not comparable with the leaderboard's 21.71. [abstract] -- arXiv 2208.12462; the challenge summary above. Code: github.com/hust-linyi/Seg4Reg, whose README calls it the official first-place solution and cites the LNCS paper (CSI 2019 pp 69-74), with no LICENSE file and no weights named [code]. The method, from the MedIA review [full]: a modified PSPNet (ResNet-101) segments vertebrae and intervertebral spaces, then ResNet, DenseNet and EfficientNet ensembles regress the angles under gradient-reversal domain adaptation, first on all five metrics (SMAPE 21.71, CMAE 4.85).
- Vertebra-Focused Landmark Detection (Yi J, Wu P, Huang Q, Qu H, Metaxas DN, ISBI 2020, arXiv 2001.03187): locates each vertebra's centre and traces its four corners by learnt offsets; uses the challenge's 580 training images split 348/116/116; on its own 116-image test split SMAPE 10.81 percent (PT 6.26, MT 18.04, TL 23.42), landmark detection error 50.11 px at 1024 x 512 input, against a regression baseline at 23.43 and a segmentation baseline (minimum bounding rectangle of the mask) at 16.48; LaNet's table gives it SMAPE 10.48 percent and CMAE 4.48 degrees on the 128-film split, while the 7.84 and 3.51 in MMA-Net's table are Kim 2020's. [full, arXiv PDF; full, arXiv 2405.19645] Code: github.com/yijingru/Vertebra-Landmark-Detection, MIT licence, PyTorch, pretrained weights on Google Drive, the data expected as SpineWeb Dataset 16 .mat files. [code, raw README and LICENSE]
- Chen et al. (arXiv 2211.14122, Nov 2022): YOLACT instance segmentation, corners from the minimum bounding box of each mask, 609 SpineWeb images split 70/15/15, SMAPE 10.76 percent; 94.59 percent of predictions within 10 degrees; its Table 1 quotes revised U-Net 16.48, Fast RCNN 25.69, BoostNet 23.44, multi-view extrapolation 18.95; no code statement. [full, arXiv PDF]
- VLTENet (Zou L, Guo L, Zhang R et al., IEEE JBHI 2023, PMID 37030726) [abstract]: HRNet plus U-Net with vertebra localisation and tilt estimation as targets, on AASCE and an in-house set; the abstract gives no number, and LaNet's table gives it SMAPE 5.44 percent and CMAE 2.51 degrees on the 128-film split; closed access, no code found.
- W-Transformer (Yao Y, Yu W, Gao Y et al., Med Phys 2022;49(5)) [abstract]: a hybrid CNN-transformer landmark detector on AASCE's 609 films; SMAPE 8.26 percent overall (PT 5.27, MT 14.59, TL 20.97), errors 3.64, 3.84 and 3.80 degrees, sensitivity 0.9577 and specificity 0.8475 for a Cobb of 10 degrees or more, and a landmark figure of 50.89 whose metric the record leaves unclear; closed access, no code stated.
- KEF (Guo Y, Li Y, He W, Song H, "Heterogeneous consistency loss for Cobb angle estimation", EMBC 2021 pp 2588-2591) [abstract]: multi-task masks and keypoints tied by a consistency loss; the abstract gives no number, and MMA-Net's table gives it SMAPE 8.62 percent; closed access, no code stated.
- Centreline cascade (Dubost et al., arXiv 1911.01126, a challenge entry): the spine centreline from two cascaded CNNs, Cobb from its derivative, SMAPE 22.96 on the challenge test set; no code statement. [abstract]
- Diagnostics 2022 (Caesarendra et al., doi 10.3390/diagnostics12020396): AASCE plus local AP films from Brunei, 551 images, 481 training and 70 test; median Cobb difference 0 degrees (IQR -3 to 2) against a scoliosis neurosurgeon, ICC 0.995; no data or code statement found. [full, Europe PMC XML]
- Acta Neurochirurgica 2025 (keypoint R-CNN for thoracic Cobb, PMC12373668): trained on the 609 public films, validated on 83 institutional AIS patients with pre- and postoperative films, MAE 2.22 degrees (95 percent CI 1.06-3.39), ICC 0.98 against values calculated by board-certified radiologists; the institutional data may not be shared publicly, de-identified data on reasonable request under a data usage agreement; no code statement; the location of the apical vertebra or disc is named among parameters that could be included in the model. [full, Europe PMC XML]
- Shape-constrained cascaded CNNs (Fundamental Research 2024, PMC11670676): landmark normalised MSE 7.2e-4 against 3.2e-3 for Yi et al. on the consistent split; errors in px or mm and the SMAPE value fell outside the part read. [full, first 100,000 characters]
- The meta-analysis of deep-learning Cobb measurement (Zhu et al., Spine Deform 2025, PMC11729091): 50 studies, 55 models, 17 in the meta-analysis; pooled circular MAE 2.99 degrees (95 percent CI 2.61-3.38), 2.40 for segmentation-based and 3.31 for landmark-based methods, I-squared 94 percent; 21 of 55 models trained and tested on AASCE, 24 on private data alone; only 3 studies prospective and 1 multicentre; most studies, in the authors' words, "did not consider the accuracy and rationality of end vertebra selection"; nothing on per-vertebra labelling or transitional vertebrae [full, Europe PMC XML].

### What the coronal line settles
- The public test set with truth is one: AASCE's 609 films with corners and three angles (the 98 challenge-test films have no public truth). Every reported number is a Cobb angle in degrees or a SMAPE; corner error is given in px or normalised units, never in mm, since no paper read converts the films' pixels to mm (the MedIA review's Table 2 prints a nominal 144 micron at 72 DPI). The best published figures are MAE 3.2 to 3.7 degrees on the provider's 128-film split and 2.22 degrees on one institution's 83 patients against board-certified radiologists (Acta Neurochir 2025); the meta-analysis pools a circular MAE of 2.40 degrees over six segmentation-based models. LaNet reports SMAPE 4.51 percent (CMAE 2.06) on the 128 validation films, the lowest read, and 8.99 on the 98 test films against its own landmarks, so that figure does not rank against the 2019 leaderboard.
- Nothing in this line names a level. The 17 vertebrae are positions in a sequence that every film is assumed to show whole; a film with 18 or 16 would break the format, and no paper read checks whether the first vertebra is T1. CurvNet's own error analysis puts most large proximal thoracic errors on AASCE down to cervical vertebrae counted as thoracic.

## 2. Sagittal spinopelvic parameters on whole-spine or lumbar lateral radiographs

Every method below is deep learning on lateral radiographs; none has a public test set with reader truth for the spinopelvic set, and only Fujimori 2022's cervical tool publishes code under a licence (MIT); Kang 2026 is tested on the public CLX-34 films with its own readers' truth. The numbers are in degrees (mm for SVA) against one or several readers.

- Galbusera F et al., Eur Spine J 2019;28(5):951-960: 78 landmarks (end-plate centres, hip centres, S1 end-plate margins) on biplanar radiographs of 493 patients (AIS, adult deformity, stenosis), a fully convolutional network with a DSNT layer, truth from 3D reconstructions, 50 test patients; standard errors from 2.7 degrees (PT) to 11.5 degrees (L1-L5 lordosis) over T4-T12 kyphosis, L1-L5 lordosis, Cobb, PI, SS and PT; no data or code statement. [abstract, Springer Medicine page]
- Galbusera's group, Front Bioeng Biotechnol 2022 (doi 10.3389/fbioe.2022.863054): the tool extended from 2 to 10 landmarks per vertebra on a ResNet-34, training set from 443 to 810 EOS images, applied to 9,832 patients; validated against four observers on three sets of 30 patients, with 43 percent of predictions inside the observers' range (31 to 72 percent) and errors above 5 degrees in 0 percent (PT, adult deformity) to 20 percent (maximal coronal Cobb, AIS); data "available by the authors, without undue reservation", no code statement; nothing on how levels were numbered. [full, Frontiers page]
- Cina A et al., Sci Rep 2021;11:9482 (doi 10.1038/s41598-021-89102-w): a two-step model on 10,193 annotated sagittal thoracolumbar images, 195 test images; corner error median 1.98 percent (x) and 1.68 percent (y) of vertebral dimensions; absolute median errors L1-L5 1.84 degrees, L1-S1 2.43, SS 1.98. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- Korez R, Putzier M, Vrtovec T, Eur Spine J 2020;29(9):2295-2305: 97 upright sagittal films of 55 subjects; mean absolute difference SS 5.0 degrees, PT 2.7, spinal tilt 1.2, PI 5.5, SSA 5.0 against SurgiMap manual measurement; Pearson 0.71 to 0.95. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- Weng CH et al., J Clin Med 2019;8(11):1826 (PMC6912675): ResUNet keypoints for SVA on 990 standing lateral films; median absolute error 1.183 mm; 5 mm detection rate 91 percent for C7 and 87 percent for the sacrum; ICC 0.946-0.993 against physicians. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- Yeh YC et al., Sci Rep 2021;11:7618 (PMC8027006): whole-spine lateral DICOM films from Chang Gung Memorial Hospital, 2,210 of 2,900 kept (174 excluded for inadequate length, 294 for a column numbering more or fewer than 25 vertebrae, 222 for poor contrast), 400 test images, 45 landmarks (21 named points plus 24 vertebral centres C2 to L5), 18 parameters; test-set mean error PI 3.8 degrees, SS 3.5, PT 1.1, LL 5.1, SVA 1.9 mm, CL 6.6, T1S 5.3, cSVA 1.1 mm, global kyphosis 6.6, TPA 1.0, with medians about half of these; landmark medians 1.75-3.39 mm; truth by three stages (three junior and one senior orthopaedic resident, then a spine fellow, then a senior spine surgeon), with inter-observer ICCs between a junior resident, a spine fellow, a senior surgeon, the model and the truth on 90 test images shown as a heatmap, the model matching the observers' reliability for 15 of the 18 parameters; data "contain protected patient information", no code statement; the paper puts the long error tails mainly down to incorrect recognition of vertebral levels, where ribs, implants or cement occlude thoracolumbar landmarks and T7 resembles its neighbours, and says an algorithm that determines the number and identities of the vertebrae before localising landmarks may be needed for anomalous numbering such as lumbosacral transitional vertebrae. [full, Europe PMC XML]
- Weng CH et al., Eur Spine J 2022;31(8):2092-2103 (PMID 35366104): 1,800 whole-spine lateral films, 300 test, 25 landmarks, an individualised sagittal curve with inflection points and apices; landmark median error 1.7-4.1 mm; ICC above 0.85 against a surgeon and a radiologist. [abstract, PubMed]
- Song SY et al., Bioengineering 2023;10(10):1229: see the earlier note's section 3 (932 images, 13 parameters, MAE 3.577 PT to 8.789 LL). [full, in the earlier note]
- Cho BH et al., Global Spine J 2020 (PMC7359685): 780 lateral lumbar films, 629 learning and 151 test, lumbar lordosis only; L1 and S1 found on 84.1 percent of the test set and on 42 of the 50 films the surgeons measured; against the surgeons' average its Table 1 gives a median absolute difference of 8.055 degrees, a mean of 13.069 and an SD of 13.126 (against the gold standard: median 6.965, mean 13.441, SD 12.989), while the abstract calls 8.055 a mean and 6.965 a median; surgeons' mean differences 3.5 to 4.5 degrees from the gold standard; implants excluded; a film where L1 or S1 was not found counted as an algorithm failure and was left out of the comparison; segmentation failures (too few bodies, fused bodies) drove the errors. [full, Europe PMC XML]
- Schwartz JT et al., Spine 2021;46(12) (doi 10.1097/BRS.0000000000003830): 816 lateral lumbar films, 653 training and 163 test, a segmentation CNN plus computer vision; against the criterion standard on 40 films, L1-S1 Cobb 4.30 degrees (surgeons 4.99), PT 2.14 (1.58), PI 4.56 (3.74), SS 4.76 (4.75). [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- Orosz LD et al., J Neurosurg Spine 2022;37(6):893-901 (doi 10.3171/2022.5.SPINE22109): a commercial algorithm (unnamed in the abstract) on 200 lateral lumbar films of 100 fusion patients; all parameters returned in 98 percent preoperatively and 95 percent postoperatively; ICC 0.85-0.92 and 0.81-0.87; mean error PI -0.5 degrees, SS -2.2 preoperatively, LL 3.8 postoperatively. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- Zerouali M et al., Diagn Interv Imaging 2023;104(7-8):343-350: 100 patients (median age 14), whole-spine uniplanar standing films, ten coronal and sagittal parameters; coronal ICC at least 0.95 and MAE at most 2.9 degrees or 1.97 mm; sagittal ICC at least 0.85 and MAE at most 4.4 degrees or 2.7 mm except kyphosis (ICC 0.58, MAE 8.7 degrees); the AI was "reliable" in 72 of 100 patients. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- LanDet (MohammadiNasrabadi AA et al., J Orthop Surg Res 2024;19:199, PMC10962169): landmarks as detected objects with geometric constraints, two datasets of 1,470 images in all; MAE SS 3.7 degrees, PT 1.3, PI 4.2, LL 5.1, SVA 2.1 mm; ICC with three surgeons above 0.88. [abstract, Europe PMC record; the Springer page refused the fetch]
- The incidence-angle method (PMC10604057, Bioengineering 2023): 563 whole-spine standing lateral films (500 training, 63 validation), 11 landmarks, PI, PT, SS and the L1, T1 and C2 incidence angles measured, LL, TK and C2-C7 lordosis derived by the incidence equations; MAE about 1.8 to 2.0 degrees; about 80 percent within 3.5 degrees for most parameters, SS reaching 80 percent only at 7.5 degrees; two surgeons twice as truth; data not public, no code statement. [full, PMC]
- SpinePose (Harake ES et al., J Neurosurg Spine 2024;41(1):88-96; arXiv 2402.06185): 761 sagittal whole-spine films for training and validation, 40 test films labelled by four reviewers; median errors SVA 2.2 mm, PT 1.3 degrees, SS 1.7, PI 2.2, LL 2.6, T1PA 1.1, L1PA 1.4 against the most senior reviewer; ICC 0.91-1.0; no data or code statement. [abstract, arXiv and Europe PMC record]
- Yuan et al., Front Bioeng Biotechnol 2024 (doi 10.3389/fbioe.2024.1404058): 1,240 standing lateral lumbar films (992/124/124), LL, sacral horizontal angle, L4-L5 and L5-S1 disc angles and slip percentages; MAE LL 2.04 degrees (ICC 0.97), SHA 1.89, ISA L5-S1 2.47; truth the mean of three surgeons; data on request, no code statement; the authors say their model is currently unable to identify lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, since spine surgeons do not accurately identify and annotate them and the algorithm does not yet identify anatomical variations. [full, Frontiers page; Europe PMC XML]
- Bi-planar landmark detection (Spine Deform 2025, PMC11893627, Balgrist and ETH Zurich): 555 EOS radiographs, 455 training and 100 test, U-Nets per region, corners from rectangular masks; Cobb predicted in 61 percent of test cases with MAD 3.3 degrees; TK 5.6 degrees (69 percent reasonable), LL 4.7 (58 percent), SVA 2.8 mm (86 percent), SS 4.5 (85 percent), PT 1.8 (84 percent), PI 5.3 (84 percent); only 45 of 100 test films fully processed; data not public, code "from the corresponding author upon reasonable request"; levels not named, a reasonable segmentation needing at least 18 bodies from C7 to L5. [full, Europe PMC XML]
- Cureus 2026 systematic review (PMC13695552): 16 studies of AI versus manual sagittal parameters, 21 to 1,810 patients; ICC ranges SVA 0.99-1.00, PT 0.96-0.99, LL 0.91-0.97 preoperatively, PI 0.70-0.95, SS and TK 0.58-0.97; the one named commercial AI is Brainlab Elements; no meta-analysis; 11 of 16 at high risk of bias (QUADAS-3 in the methods, QUADAS-2 in the abstract); its CLAIM assessment finds confirmation of patient-level separation between partitions, formal external-site or cross-scanner validation and algorithm-version reporting "absent from all 16 studies" [full, Europe PMC XML].
- Fujimori et al., Sci Rep 2022;12:15732 (PMC9492662, Osaka University) [full]: DeepLabV3 with EfficientNet-B4 heat maps place four landmarks, the two ends of the C2 and C7 inferior end-plates, on 4,546 lateral cervical films (flexion, neutral, extension) of 1,674 patients, none excluded for deformity or instrumentation; truth by a surgeon of 18 years checked by one of 20; five-fold cross-validation by patient gives C2-C7 MAE 3.3 degrees (median 2.2), C2 slope 1.7 and C7 slope 2.7, and against two surgeons on 168 patients (415 films) the model's 3.1 +/- 3.4 degrees beside the surgeons' 3.9 and 3.8; no ICC. Code at github.com/ykszk/c2c7demo, MIT, with ONNX weights on Google Drive and a web demo [code]; images on request. It outputs no level label: it places the C2 and C7 points directly, uses C6 as reference where the shoulder hides C7, and names vertebral body misidentification as the cause of its 35 errors of 20 degrees or more (a hardly visible C7 12, severe deformity 8, instrumented fusion 7, fused vertebrae 6, infancy 2).
- Kang et al. 2026, npj Digit Med (doi 10.1038/s41746-026-02455-2, PMC13031614) [full]: a Keypoint R-CNN with 23 keypoints and an MLP that places the C7 region from the C3-C6 keypoints, then C2 and C7 specialist networks; trained on 5,604 films (CSXA's 4,963 and 641 from Samsung Medical Center), tested on 100 SMC films (39 percent with C7 obscured) and on 100 films of the public CLX-34 set chosen for at least partial C7 obscuration (82 percent), truth a consensus of two orthopaedic surgeons; external ICC and MAE C2-C7 lordosis 0.97 and 2.57 degrees, C2 slope over 0.99 and 0.83, C7 slope 0.93 and 2.31; the two surgeons agree with each other at ICC 0.67 for C7 slope, below the model's 0.81 and 0.84 against each. Data and code on request, no licence. It places C7 by position, never names it, and says nothing of transitional or fused vertebrae; extreme obscuration is excluded.

### Commercial EOS and Keops validations
- Rehm J et al., PLoS One 2017;12(2):e0171258 (PMC5289552) [full]: sterEOS 3D full-spine reconstructions of 73 scoliosis patients (mean age 17, Cobb 9.8 to 49.9 degrees) by two radiologists, who place the pelvic landmarks by hand and trace T1 to L5 with control points, 14.9 minutes a reconstruction; inter-reader ICC PI 0.97, SS 0.96, PT 0.98, T4/T12 kyphosis 0.95, L1/L5 lordosis 0.90 and L1/S1 0.85, with mean absolute inter-reader differences PI 3.1, SS 2.7, PT 1.0 and kyphosis and lordosis 3.0 to 4.1 degrees, the size of the deep-learning errors above; no comparison with manual 2D measurement. The reader names every vertebra, and the 4 patients with lumbosacral transitional vertebrae were excluded before the study began.

## 6. What the family's prior art settles and does not

- Settled: the only public benchmark with reader truth for an alignment angle is AASCE, coronal, 609 films, 17 vertebrae as positions, three Cobb angles; the state of the art there is MAE about 3.2 to 3.7 degrees on the provider's split and SMAPE 7.3 percent (LaNet reports 4.51 on the validation films, the lowest read), with code under a licence for two AASCE methods (Yi 2020, MIT; CurvNet, an Apache 2.0 LICENSE file inherited from MMOCR). Every sagittal spinopelvic tool, academic or commercial, is validated on private films against one to four readers, the cervical Kang 2026 alone also on public films (CLX-34), and the field's best figures cluster at PT 1.1 to 1.3 degrees, SVA 1.2 to 2.2 mm, PI 2.2 to 4.6 degrees, SS 1.7 to 5.0 degrees and LL 2.0 to 5.1 degrees, with kyphosis the weakest (ICC 0.58, MAE 8.7 in Zerouali; SE 11.5 for lordosis in Galbusera 2019). Those figures are of the same order as the readers' own disagreement, which Chai 2024 quantifies for pelvic tilt alone (95 percent clouds up to 17.9 mm, up to 16.55 degrees) and Rehm 2017 for the commercial sterEOS reconstruction (PI 3.1, SS 2.7 degrees between two readers).
- Settled: no public set carries sagittal truth for the whole family. The public pieces are pelvic tilt landmarks (Chai, 115 pelves), cervical keypoints with computed rather than measured parameters (CSXA, CLX-34), lumbar corners without angles (Mendeley, BUU-LSPINE), and NHANES II corners held by a company. Spinal-AI2024's 20,000 generated films carry machine-made Cobb truth and no reader. CT and MRI public sets (VerSe, CTSpine1K, TotalSegmentator, SPIDER, and MRSpineSeg, no longer obtainable) carry masks and no alignment truth, and no paper read computes an alignment angle on them.
- Settled for naming levels: the alignment literature does not name levels; it counts them. AASCE assumes 17, Yeh excludes every spine without exactly 25 vertebrae, the Zurich pipeline needs at least 18 bodies from C7 to L5, VertXNet counts from a visible C2 or S1, NHANES II's labels are machine-made and unchecked, SPIDER refuses to call its numbers anatomical, Yuan's authors say their tool is currently unable to identify a lumbosacral transitional vertebra, and sterEOS's reader names every vertebra (Rehm 2017 excluded transitional spines). Yeh puts its long error tails mainly down to wrong level recognition, as Fujimori 2022 does (vertebral body misidentification, a hidden C7 first) and CurvNet does (cervical vertebrae counted as proximal thoracic), and the Cobb meta-analysis says end-vertebra selection goes unassessed. So an alignment angle in this literature is a number over a column whose level names are assumed, not a measure from which a level is read; nothing read uses an angle, a slope or an incidence to decide which vertebra a body is.
- Not settled: whether per-level alignment quantities (a body's wedge, a disc angle, a slope at each level, which Cina, Yuan and CSXA compute) carry level information; nobody read tests it. Whether the sagittal tools generalise is unknown: the Cureus review finds external validation absent in all 16 studies, and the only external Cobb validation read (Acta Neurochir 2025) moved from AASCE to one institution; Kang 2026's cervical model, tested on 100 public CLX-34 films, is the one sagittal tool read with a public external test. The sagittal truth's own reproducibility at whole-spine scale is quantified only for the reader-driven sterEOS reconstruction (Rehm 2017: PI 3.1, SS 2.7 degrees between two readers) and, pooled, for EOS against manual (Kim 2018); for the cervical C7 slope two surgeons agree at ICC 0.67 (Kang 2026). Magnification is handled by none of the angle papers and by none of the SVA papers read (Yeh reports SVA in mm with no conversion described; see the earlier note's section 4).
