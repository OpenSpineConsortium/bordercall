# Prior art for the alignment family: Cobb angles, lordosis, kyphosis, pelvic incidence, pelvic tilt, sacral slope, SVA

Source: the thread "Plan clinical reread next steps", 2026-10-29, web reading (arXiv, Europe PMC's REST records and full-text XML, MDPI, Frontiers, PMC where its browser check let a page through, figshare's API, raw.githubusercontent.com). The family is the vocabulary rows `alignment` (a curvature or alignment angle over several levels: lordosis, kyphosis, Cobb, sacral slope) and `sacrum` (any sacral or pelvic measure) of bordercall's vocabulary.csv, read here as the clinical set Cobb angle, thoracic kyphosis, lumbar lordosis, pelvic incidence, pelvic tilt, sacral slope and the sagittal vertical axis, with the cervical set (C2-C7 Cobb, T1 slope, cSVA) where it was met. Reading status is tagged on every claim: [full] the full text or full-text XML was read; [abstract] an abstract-level record; [page] a web page, dataset record or API record; [code] a repository file. Where a publisher refused the fetch, the line says which copy was read instead. The web search budget of this session ran out after the first two rounds, so the last rounds were done by fetching Europe PMC's search API directly; what that left unread is in Gaps.

Already found, built on rather than repeated:

## 1. The coronal Cobb angle: the AASCE challenge and what followed it

### What AASCE is
- The challenge "Accurate Automated Spinal Curvature Estimation" ran with CSI at MICCAI 2019; its page names the training data as SpineWeb Dataset 16, the test data as a separate archive, the metric as "Cobb angle accuracy", and the organisers as Shuo Li (Western University) and Liansheng Wang (Xiamen University); it gives no image count and no landmark count. [page] -- aasce19.github.io
- The Medical Image Analysis review of the challenge (Wang L et al., Med Image Anal 2021;72:102115, doi 10.1016/j.media.2021.102115) reviews eight top-ranked methods of 12 teams; the best reached a SMAPE of 21.71 percent; the article is subscription-only and not in PMC, so its image counts were not read from it. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- MMA-Net's paper gives the provider's split: 609 AP spine X-rays, 481 for training and 128 for testing; the shape-constrained cascaded CNN paper gives the official split as 481 training, 128 validation and 98 challenge-private test images "without annotations", and a second "consistent split" of 431 training and 50 test images drawn from the official training set that earlier papers used. [full, arXiv PDF; full, Europe PMC XML] -- arXiv 2309.13817; PMC11670676
- The truth is corners placed by "two professional radiologists at London Health Science Center", and the dataset "also contains the angle measurements of the proximal thoracic section, main thoracic section, and thoracolumbar section"; the landmark files hold some vertebrae in the wrong order, which Chen et al. excluded from their test set (their Appendix A lists the ids). [full, arXiv PDF] -- arXiv 2211.14122
- The metric is SMAPE over the three angles, averaged over test images; MMA-Net also reports MAE, a circular MAE, a Euclidean distance over the three angles, and a maximum deviation. [full, arXiv PDF] -- arXiv 2309.13817
- Each AASCE film carries exactly 17 vertebrae (12 thoracic, 5 lumbar) identified by their position in the ordered sequence; no paper read names them T1 to L5 anatomically, none has a transitional vertebra, and the cascaded-CNN paper initialises its 17 centres from a normalised average shape. [full] -- arXiv 2211.14122; PMC11670676
- SpineWeb's host (spineweb.digitalimaginggroup.ca) did not resolve from this session, and the Grand Challenge page refused the fetch (403), so the leaderboard, the download terms and the licence were not read. [page: refused]

### Results on AASCE, as its own papers report them
- MMA-Net (Zhao et al., arXiv 2309.13817, Sep 2023): segmentation of the spine region, centreline and boundary feeding a regression head; on the 128-image test split SMAPE 7.28 percent, MAE 3.18 degrees, circular MAE 2.26 degrees; its Table II lists SCG-Net SMAPE 22.18, Seg4Reg 21.71 (circular MAE 4.85), KEF 8.62, Vertebra-Focused (VF) MAE 3.51 and SMAPE 7.84, Seg4Reg+ MAE 3.73 and SMAPE 7.32; no code statement was found in the text. [full, arXiv PDF]
- Seg4Reg (Lin et al., the challenge's first place, Tencent YouTu Lab): SMAPE 21.71 percent on the 98-image challenge test set, by ensembling models; Seg4Reg+ (arXiv 2208.12462) uses the 481/128 provider split, so its 7.32 percent is not comparable with the leaderboard's 21.71. [abstract] -- arXiv 2208.12462; the challenge summary above
- Vertebra-Focused Landmark Detection (Yi J, Wu P, Huang Q, Qu H, Metaxas DN, ISBI 2020, arXiv 2001.03187): locates each vertebra's centre and traces its four corners by learnt offsets; uses the challenge's 580 training images split 348/116/116; on its own 116-image test split SMAPE 10.81 percent (PT 6.26, MT 18.04, TL 23.42), landmark detection error 50.11 px at 1024 x 512 input, against a regression baseline at 23.43 and a segmentation baseline (minimum bounding rectangle of the mask) at 16.48. [full, arXiv PDF] Code: github.com/yijingru/Vertebra-Landmark-Detection, MIT licence, PyTorch, pretrained weights on Google Drive, the data expected as SpineWeb Dataset 16 .mat files. [code, raw README and LICENSE]
- Chen et al. (arXiv 2211.14122, Nov 2022): YOLACT instance segmentation, corners from the minimum bounding box of each mask, 609 SpineWeb images split 70/15/15, SMAPE 10.76 percent; 94.59 percent of predictions within 10 degrees; its Table 1 quotes revised U-Net 16.48, Fast RCNN 25.69, BoostNet 23.44, multi-view extrapolation 18.95; no code statement. [full, arXiv PDF]
- Centreline cascade (Dubost et al., arXiv 1911.01126, a challenge entry): the spine centreline from two cascaded CNNs, Cobb from its derivative, SMAPE 22.96 on the challenge test set; no code statement. [abstract]
- Diagnostics 2022 (Caesarendra et al., doi 10.3390/diagnostics12020396): AASCE plus local AP films from Brunei, 551 images, 481 training and 70 test; median Cobb difference 0 degrees (IQR -3 to 2) against a scoliosis neurosurgeon, ICC 0.995; no data or code statement found. [full, Europe PMC XML]
- Acta Neurochirurgica 2025 (keypoint R-CNN for thoracic Cobb, PMC12373668): trained on the 609 public films, validated on 83 institutional AIS patients, MAE 2.22 degrees (95 percent CI 1.06-3.39), ICC 0.98 against radiologists; institutional data not shareable, no code statement; the apical vertebra's location is "not yet included". [full, Europe PMC XML]
- Shape-constrained cascaded CNNs (Fundamental Research 2024, PMC11670676): landmark normalised MSE 7.2e-4 against 3.2e-3 for Yi et al. on the consistent split; errors in px or mm and the SMAPE value fell outside the part read. [full, first 100,000 characters]
- The meta-analysis of deep-learning Cobb measurement (Zhu et al., Spine Deform 2025, PMC11729091): 50 studies, 55 models, 17 in the meta-analysis; pooled circular MAE 2.99 degrees (95 percent CI 2.61-3.38), 2.40 for segmentation-based and 3.31 for landmark-based methods, I-squared 94 percent; 21 of 55 models trained and tested on AASCE, 24 on private data alone; only 3 studies prospective and 1 multicentre; "most studies did not assess whether end vertebrae were selected accurately"; nothing on per-vertebra labelling or transitional vertebrae. [full, first 100,000 characters]

### What the coronal line settles
- The public test set with truth is one: AASCE's 609 films with corners and three angles (the 98 challenge-test films have no public truth). Every reported number is a Cobb angle in degrees or a SMAPE; corner error is given in px or normalised units, never in mm, since the films carry no pixel spacing in anything read. The best published figures are MAE 3.2 to 3.7 degrees on the provider's 128-film split and 2.2 to 2.4 degrees on institutional films against radiologists.
- Nothing in this line names a level. The 17 vertebrae are positions in a sequence that every film is assumed to show whole; a film with 18 or 16 would break the format, and no paper read checks whether the first vertebra is T1.

## 2. Sagittal spinopelvic parameters on whole-spine or lumbar lateral radiographs

Every method below is deep learning on lateral radiographs; none has a public test set with truth, and none publishes code under a licence. The numbers are in degrees (mm for SVA) against one or several readers.

- Galbusera F et al., Eur Spine J 2019;28(5):951-960: 78 landmarks (end-plate centres, hip centres, S1 end-plate margins) on biplanar radiographs of 493 patients (AIS, adult deformity, stenosis), a fully convolutional network with a DSNT layer, truth from 3D reconstructions, 50 test patients; standard errors from 2.7 degrees (PT) to 11.5 degrees (L1-L5 lordosis) over T4-T12 kyphosis, L1-L5 lordosis, Cobb, PI, SS and PT; no data or code statement. [abstract, Springer Medicine page]
- Galbusera's group, Front Bioeng Biotechnol 2022 (doi 10.3389/fbioe.2022.863054): the tool extended from 2 to 10 landmarks per vertebra on a ResNet-34, training set from 443 to 810 EOS images, applied to 9,832 patients; validated against four observers on three sets of 30 patients, with 43 percent of predictions inside the observers' range (31 to 72 percent) and errors above 5 degrees in 0 percent (PT, adult deformity) to 20 percent (maximal coronal Cobb, AIS); data "available by the authors, without undue reservation", no code statement; nothing on how levels were numbered. [full, Frontiers page]
- Cina A et al., Sci Rep 2021;11:9482 (doi 10.1038/s41598-021-89102-w): a two-step model on 10,193 annotated sagittal thoracolumbar images, 195 test images; corner error median 1.98 percent (x) and 1.68 percent (y) of vertebral dimensions; absolute median errors L1-L5 1.84 degrees, L1-S1 2.43, SS 1.98. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- Korez R, Putzier M, Vrtovec T, Eur Spine J 2020;29(9):2295-2305: 97 upright sagittal films of 55 subjects; mean absolute difference SS 5.0 degrees, PT 2.7, spinal tilt 1.2, PI 5.5, SSA 5.0 against SurgiMap manual measurement; Pearson 0.71 to 0.95. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- Weng CH et al., J Clin Med 2019;8(11):1826 (PMC6912675): ResUNet keypoints for SVA on 990 standing lateral films; median absolute error 1.183 mm; 5 mm detection rate 91 percent for C7 and 87 percent for the sacrum; ICC 0.946-0.993 against physicians. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- Yeh YC et al., Sci Rep 2021;11:7618 (PMC8027006): whole-spine lateral DICOM films from Chang Gung Memorial Hospital, 2,210 of 2,900 kept (294 excluded for "not exactly 25 vertebrae"), 400 test images, 45 landmarks (21 named points plus 24 vertebral centres C2 to L5), 18 parameters; test-set mean error PI 3.8 degrees, SS 3.5, PT 1.1, LL 5.1, SVA 1.9 mm, CL 6.6, T1S 5.3, cSVA 1.1 mm, global kyphosis 6.6, TPA 1.0, with medians about half of these; landmark medians 1.75-3.39 mm; truth by three stages of residents, a fellow and a senior surgeon with no inter-annotator figure; data "contain protected patient information", no code statement; the paper names wrong level recognition in the thoracic spine (around T7) as the main source of large errors and says a future algorithm should identify "vertebral count and identity first". [full, PMC]
- Weng CH et al., Eur Spine J 2022;31(8):2092-2103 (PMID 35366104): 1,800 whole-spine lateral films, 300 test, 25 landmarks, an individualised sagittal curve with inflection points and apices; landmark median error 1.7-4.1 mm; ICC above 0.85 against a surgeon and a radiologist. [abstract, PubMed]
- Song SY et al., Bioengineering 2023;10(10):1229: see the earlier note's section 3 (932 images, 13 parameters, MAE 3.577 PT to 8.789 LL). [full, in the earlier note]
- Cho BH et al., Global Spine J 2020 (PMC7359685): 780 lateral lumbar films, 629 learning and 151 test, lumbar lordosis only; algorithm versus surgeon average mean absolute difference 8.055 degrees (SD 13.126) and the text and table disagree on whether 8.055 is a mean or a median; surgeons 3.5 to 4.5 degrees from the gold standard; implants excluded; films with L1 or S1 not identified excluded; segmentation failures (too few vertebrae, fused bodies) drove the errors. [full, PMC]
- Schwartz JT et al., Spine 2021;46(12) (doi 10.1097/BRS.0000000000003830): 816 lateral lumbar films, 653 training and 163 test, a segmentation CNN plus computer vision; against the criterion standard on 40 films, L1-S1 Cobb 4.30 degrees (surgeons 4.99), PT 2.14 (1.58), PI 4.56 (3.74), SS 4.76 (4.75). [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- Orosz LD et al., J Neurosurg Spine 2022;37(6):893-901 (doi 10.3171/2022.5.SPINE22109): a commercial algorithm (unnamed in the abstract) on 200 lateral lumbar films of 100 fusion patients; all parameters returned in 98 percent preoperatively and 95 percent postoperatively; ICC 0.85-0.92 and 0.81-0.87; mean error PI -0.5 degrees, SS -2.2 preoperatively, LL 3.8 postoperatively. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- Zerouali M et al., Diagn Interv Imaging 2023;104(7-8):343-350: 100 patients (median age 14), whole-spine uniplanar standing films, ten coronal and sagittal parameters; coronal ICC at least 0.95 and MAE at most 2.9 degrees or 1.97 mm; sagittal ICC at least 0.85 and MAE at most 4.4 degrees or 2.7 mm except kyphosis (ICC 0.58, MAE 8.7 degrees); the AI was "reliable" in 72 of 100 patients. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- LanDet (MohammadiNasrabadi AA et al., J Orthop Surg Res 2024;19:199, PMC10962169): landmarks as detected objects with geometric constraints, two datasets of 1,470 images in all; MAE SS 3.7 degrees, PT 1.3, PI 4.2, LL 5.1, SVA 2.1 mm; ICC with three surgeons above 0.88. [abstract, Europe PMC record; the Springer page refused the fetch]
- The incidence-angle method (PMC10604057, Bioengineering 2023): 563 whole-spine standing lateral films (500 training, 63 validation), 11 landmarks, PI, PT, SS and the L1, T1 and C2 incidence angles measured, LL, TK and C2-C7 lordosis derived by the incidence equations; MAE about 1.8 to 2.0 degrees; about 80 percent within 3.5 degrees for most parameters, SS reaching 80 percent only at 7.5 degrees; two surgeons twice as truth; data not public, no code statement. [full, PMC]
- SpinePose (Harake ES et al., J Neurosurg Spine 2024;41(1):88-96; arXiv 2402.06185): 761 sagittal whole-spine films for training and validation, 40 test films labelled by four reviewers; median errors SVA 2.2 mm, PT 1.3 degrees, SS 1.7, PI 2.2, LL 2.6, T1PA 1.1, L1PA 1.4 against the most senior reviewer; ICC 0.91-1.0; no data or code statement. [abstract, arXiv and Europe PMC record]
- Yuan et al., Front Bioeng Biotechnol 2024 (doi 10.3389/fbioe.2024.1404058): 1,240 standing lateral lumbar films (992/124/124), LL, sacral horizontal angle, L4-L5 and L5-S1 disc angles and slip percentages; MAE LL 2.04 degrees (ICC 0.97), SHA 1.89, ISA L5-S1 2.47; truth the mean of three surgeons; data on request, no code statement; the authors say it "cannot identify lumbosacral transitional vertebrae". [full, Frontiers page]
- Bi-planar landmark detection (Spine Deform 2025, PMC11893627, Balgrist and ETH Zurich): 555 EOS radiographs, 455 training and 100 test, U-Nets per region, corners from rectangular masks; Cobb predicted in 61 percent of test cases with MAD 3.3 degrees; TK 5.6 degrees (69 percent reasonable), LL 4.7 (58 percent), SVA 2.8 mm (86 percent), SS 4.5 (85 percent), PT 1.8 (84 percent), PI 5.3 (84 percent); only 45 of 100 test films fully processed; data not public, code "from the corresponding author upon reasonable request"; levels not named, a reasonable segmentation needing at least 18 bodies from C7 to L5. [full, Europe PMC XML]
- Cureus 2026 systematic review (PMC13695552): 16 studies of AI versus manual sagittal parameters, 21 to 1,810 patients; ICC ranges SVA 0.99-1.00, PT 0.96-0.99, LL 0.91-0.97 preoperatively, PI 0.70-0.95, SS and TK 0.58-0.97; the one named commercial AI is Brainlab Elements; no meta-analysis; 11 of 16 at high risk of bias; "patient-level data separation, external validation, and algorithm-version reporting were absent in all studies". [full, first 100,000 characters]

### Public truth for the sagittal family
- Chai W, Boudali AM, Maes V, Walter WL, Sci Data 2024 (doi 10.1038/s41597-024-04003-7, PMC11496730): 115 lateral pelvic EOS stereoradiographs of 93 hip-surgery patients, JPG with no pixel spacing (scaled by an in-image length), five annotators (a senior surgeon, two fellows, two engineers); landmarks for anatomical tilt (ASIS centre and pubic tubercle) and mechanical tilt (six femoral-head contour points, two sacral-plate ends); 95 percent point-cloud diameters femoral heads 6.05 mm, sacral plate 9.10 mm, ASIS 17.90 mm, giving maximum angular disagreement 9.51 degrees (mechanical) and 16.55 (anatomical); inter-rater ICC 0.982 mechanical and 0.920 anatomical. [full, PMC] Images: figshare 10.6084/m9.figshare.23820879, CC BY 4.0, one 287 MB zip; annotations, benchmark and MATLAB code: figshare 10.6084/m9.figshare.23831553, CC0. [page, figshare API] This is pelvic tilt alone; no sacral slope, PI, lordosis or vertebra appears, and the images stop at the pelvis.
- CSXA (Sci Data 2024, PMC11176335): 4,963 lateral cervical radiographs of 4,963 people (4,782 symptomatic), 23 keypoints each (three on C2, four corners on C3 to C7), labelme JSON, pixel equivalent per image from the ruler in an Excel file, hosted on Science Data Bank (doi 10.57760/sciencedb.15391) with Python code on GitHub; 77 parameters computed from the keypoints by the authors' code, among them C2-7 Cobb, C2-6 Cobb, C2-7 SVA, cervical lordosis, disc angles and slopes; no manual parameter truth, four surgeons annotating with a senior review, no agreement statistic, validation against PACS described only as "aligned consistently". [full, Europe PMC XML] The article is CC BY 4.0; the dataset's own licence is not stated in the text.
- Spondylolisthesis Vertebral Landmark (Mendeley Data 10.17632/5jdfdgp762.1, June 2025, CC BY 4.0): 716 lateral lumbar films (208 Honduran, 508 from BUU-LSPINE), bounding boxes and four corners per vertebra L3 to S1, Keypoint R-CNN JSON; no angle truth, no pixel spacing. [page]
- BUU-LSPINE's own paper was not found in Europe PMC; the Neurospine 2026 paper that uses 600 of its films annotated corners T11-L5 and S1 and seven categorical findings, lordosis among them as "straightened or preserved", never as an angle. [abstract, Europe PMC record] The earlier note records BUU-LSPINE's transitional-vertebra truth and its 4.6-4.9 mm corner errors.
- NHANES II (Hipp JA et al., JBMR Plus 2022, PMC9549721): 7,364 lumbar and 9,662 cervical lateral films, one per person, four corners per body placed by a proprietary pipeline (Spine Camp, Medical Metrics) trained on over 50,000 other films; six ratio and angle metrics per vertebra, the angle between end-plates (EPA) among them; no lordosis or whole-column angle; the derived metrics "available upon reasonable request"; levels labelled automatically with no whole-spine image to check them, the authors noting that transitional vertebrae (prevalence 4 to 36 percent in the literature) could mislabel. [full, Europe PMC XML] A 2024 Bioengineering paper gives reference data for spondylolisthesis and disc narrowing on the same films. [page, title only]

## 3. CT and MRI

- Doerr SA et al., J Med Imaging 2020;7(3):035001 (Johns Hopkins): two methods that take vertebral labels (point annotations) in CT as input, EndSeg segmenting end-plates from the labels as seeds and SpNorm fitting a 2D curve through them, compared with five clinicians on proximal thoracic kyphosis, main thoracic kyphosis and lumbar lordosis; 93.8 percent of end-plate angles and all three alignment measures within the readers' 95 percent interval; scan counts and the data statement fell in the full text, which Europe PMC's XML served with a 500 error three times. [abstract, Europe PMC record]
- SpineCurve-net (J Imaging Inform Med 2024, PMC11811352): 116 preoperative scoliosis CTs (89 training, 27 test), nnU-Net segmentation of the column without distinguishing vertebrae, a NURBS curve, a 3D Cobb from the tangent angle map; against surgeons' radiograph Cobb MAE 2.4 degrees (3D) and 4.1 (2D projection), r 0.983 and 0.934; data and code on request; supine CT against standing films. [full, PMC]
- Shao J et al., Sci Rep 2024;14:21431 (PMC11399230): CT pelvimetry, DRINet femoral-head segmentation (2,500 CTs from a hospital and DeepLesion) and a VGG16 slice classifier for the sacral end-plate; PI, PT and SS in 2D and 3D against one clinician and one surgeon in 15 patients; average deviation PI 2.5 degrees (3D) and 3.0 (2D), PT 1.9 and 2.3, SS 1.2 and 1.5; no ICC; data under licence, no code statement. [full, Europe PMC XML]
- Suri A et al., Radiol Artif Intell 2022 (doi 10.1148/ryai.2021210015, PMC8823454): six landmarks per body on 1,123 MR, 137 CT and 484 radiographs of 1,744 patients, test 225 MR, 27 CT and 97 radiographs; lumbar lordosis MAE 2.90 degrees (MR), 2.26 (CT), 3.60 (radiograph); height error 1.5 to 1.9 percent. [abstract, Europe PMC record] The full-text XML was refused (500), so its data and code statement and its level-labelling method were not read.
- The Europe PMC searches for an alignment angle measured automatically on VerSe, CTSpine1K or TotalSegmentator returned reviews, a scoliosis-CT segmentation paper and a 2026 PLoS One paper on generated CT for back-shape-to-spine Cobb estimation, none confirming a Cobb or lordosis computed on those public sets. [page, titles only] VerSe, CTSpine1K and TotalSegmentator carry masks and labels, no alignment truth.
- SPIDER (lumbar MRI, PMC10908819): 218 patients, 447 sagittal series, masks of vertebrae, discs and canal, per-level gradings (Modic, Pfirrmann, listhesis, herniation, narrowing, bulging), no alignment measure; vertebrae numbered from the most caudal lumbar body upward and the paper says that label "should not be interpreted as an anatomical label". [full, Europe PMC XML] MRSpineSeg was not reached in this session.
- The Europe PMC query for automatic lumbar lordosis on MRI returned only reviews and radiograph papers in its first page. [page, titles only]
- DXA: the query for an automatic kyphosis, lordosis or Cobb on lateral DXA returned nothing on topic. [page, titles only]

## 4. Level identification beside alignment

- VertXNet (Chen Y, Mo Y et al., Sci Rep 2024;14:3341, PMC10858234): a U-Net plus Mask R-CNN ensemble that names levels from a reference vertebra, C2 on cervical and S1 on lumbar lateral films, moving along the spine; MEASURE 1 (512 in-house films), PREVENT (226) and 445 NHANES II films with reference vertebrae annotated by its radiologists; Dice 0.912 cervical overall, 0.90 on PREVENT, 0.88 on NHANES II, with T1 at 0.542 and T11 at 0.716; computes no angle; the reference vertebra is assumed visible and transitional vertebrae are not discussed in the part read. [full, first 100,000 characters]

## 6. What the family's prior art settles and does not

- Settled: the only public benchmark with reader truth for an alignment angle is AASCE, coronal, 609 films, 17 vertebrae as positions, three Cobb angles; the state of the art there is MAE about 3.2 to 3.7 degrees on the provider's split and SMAPE 7.3 percent, with code under MIT for one method (Yi 2020). Every sagittal spinopelvic tool, academic or commercial, is validated on private films against one to four readers, and the field's best figures cluster at PT 1.1 to 1.3 degrees, SVA 1.2 to 2.2 mm, PI 2.2 to 4.6 degrees, SS 1.7 to 5.0 degrees and LL 2.0 to 5.1 degrees, with kyphosis the weakest (ICC 0.58, MAE 8.7 in Zerouali; SE 11.5 for lordosis in Galbusera 2019). Those figures are of the same order as the readers' own disagreement, which Chai 2024 quantifies for pelvic tilt alone (95 percent clouds up to 17.9 mm, up to 16.55 degrees).
- Settled: no public set carries sagittal truth for the whole family. The public pieces are pelvic tilt landmarks (Chai, 115 pelves), cervical keypoints with computed rather than measured parameters (CSXA), lumbar corners without angles (Mendeley, BUU-LSPINE), and NHANES II corners held by a company. CT and MRI public sets (VerSe, CTSpine1K, TotalSegmentator, SPIDER) carry masks and no alignment truth, and no paper read computes an alignment angle on them.
- Settled for naming levels: the alignment literature does not name levels; it counts them. AASCE assumes 17, Yeh excludes every spine without exactly 25 vertebrae, the Zurich pipeline needs at least 18 bodies from C7 to L5, VertXNet counts from a visible C2 or S1, NHANES II's labels are machine-made and unchecked, SPIDER refuses to call its numbers anatomical, and Yuan's authors say their tool cannot identify a lumbosacral transitional vertebra. Yeh names thoracic mis-levelling as the main source of large errors, and the Cobb meta-analysis says end-vertebra selection goes unassessed. So an alignment angle in this literature is a number over a column whose level names are assumed, not a measure from which a level is read; nothing read uses an angle, a slope or an incidence to decide which vertebra a body is.
- Not settled: whether per-level alignment quantities (a body's wedge, a disc angle, a slope at each level, which Cina, Yuan and CSXA compute) carry level information; nobody read tests it. Whether the sagittal tools generalise is unknown: the Cureus review finds external validation absent in all 16 studies, and the only external Cobb validation read (Acta Neurochir 2025) moved from AASCE to one institution. What the sagittal truth's own reproducibility is at whole-spine scale, beyond Chai's pelvis, is not quantified in anything read. Magnification is handled by none of the angle papers and by none of the SVA papers read (Yeh reports SVA in mm with no conversion described; see the earlier note's section 4).
