## What the existing notes already settle for this family

- The six-point convention and Genant's grade thresholds (20-25 / 25-40 / over 40 percent height loss) are fixed; every automatic six-point system reports percent or ratio agreement, not mm, except Yao 2024 (VBH MAE 0.61 mm, T12 to L5, 291 test films, institutional) (masks_corners_magnification.md, section 1) [full there].
- Mask-derived corners are coarser than regressed landmarks: minAreaRect cannot express wedging or biconcavity, a four-vertex polygon loses the middle height, and the only readable mask-to-landmark sagittal pipeline had median landmark errors of 1.7 to 4.1 mm (masks_corners_magnification.md, section 3).
- Ratios and angles (body_index, wedge) cancel uniform magnification; absolute heights, widths and depths on radiographs need a magnification estimate, and measured lateral spine magnification runs 1.21 cervical to 1.31 lumbar with a range to 1.63 (masks_corners_magnification.md, section 4).
- On CT, no training-free method reports automated end-plate width or depth against manual in mm (ct_intensity_methods.md, section 6, Gaps).
- The posterior body height measured from CT masks ran 2.3 to 7.5 mm above Panjabi's dry-bone table at lumbar levels; the cause is not settled (morphometry-beyond-the-42-papers.md, The assumptions).

## What the family's prior art settles and does not

Settled:

- Anterior, middle and posterior heights are the measured quantities everywhere; width, depth and area are almost never measured automatically. The one automated width-and-depth validation found (Siemionow 2020) is lumbar cadaveric intraoperative CT reported in percent, and no automated end-plate or mid-body cross-sectional area validation was found at all. Hipp 2022 is the only large series with an end-plate width ratio and a height-over-width ratio, and it is a projection on lateral films, so its "width" is the sagittal depth in this vocabulary's terms.
- Accuracy of heights, when it is given in mm, is sub-millimetre to a millimetre on good images: 0.61 mm MAE on lateral lumbar films (Yao 2024), 0.78 to 0.86 mm median on lumbar MRI (Al-Haidri 2025), 0.3 mm repeat difference on chest CT (ImaLife), about 1.5 to 1.9 percent of height across modalities (Suri 2021). Most CT work (Palm, Ma, Nadeem, Germann) gives ICC or r without mm, and the fracture-grading work (VerSe, Riedel 2026, HealthiVert) gives grades without heights.
- Ratios (wedge Ha/Hp, biconcavity Hm/Hp, A/P, C/A, C/P, compression ratio) agree with readers at Spearman 0.6 to 0.8 and Bland-Altman limits of about +/-0.1 (Arakawa 2025), so body_index from an automatic pipeline carries about 10 percent of ratio uncertainty, which is the whole range between Genant grade 0 and grade 1.
- Every method takes the level name from somewhere else: an operator's seeds (SpineAnalyzer), a segmenter's labels (TotalSegmentator in Palm, nnU-Net in HealthiVert), the annotation (Suri), or a reference vertebra in the field (VertXNet's C2 or S1, Hipp's assumption of L5 or C2). None names a level from the measure, and the only report of how often the segmenter's naming fails is ImaLife's 95 of 632 participants.
- Public truth for this family is thin: VerSe gives Genant grades per vertebra but no heights; NHANES II gives films, 600 films with corner landmarks and the C2/S1 reference labels, but Hipp's landmarks and metrics are on request only; no public set carries reader-measured heights, widths or depths by level on CT. So a benchmark of this family would have to make its own truth, as Palm (336 vertebrae) and Yao (291 films) did.
- Transitional vertebrae and L6 are excluded or unaddressed everywhere (Suri did not annotate L6; Palm excluded variants; Hipp names mislabelling as a limitation; VertXNet does not discuss it), which is the case this project cares about most.

Not settled:

- Whether CT masks give Panjabi's heights: the CTSpinoPelvic1K gap of 2.3 to 7.5 mm (existing note) is unexplained, and no paper here measured mask heights against calipers.
- Within-person level-to-level ratios of heights exist only as Palm's T1 to L5 matrices in 262 healthy adults (data on request) and Hipp's adjacent-level differences on films; nothing from C2 to L5 in one person.
- Middle-height placement differs by pipeline (Nadeem: minimum in the central third; Palm: Bresenham-adjusted midpoint; six-point: the end-plate midpoints), and no study compared the conventions.
- How the deep landmark systems convert pixels to mm on radiographs (Yao, Suri) is not described, so their mm figures may be at detector scale.
