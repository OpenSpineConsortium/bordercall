## What the existing notes already settle for this family

- The six-point convention and Genant's grade thresholds (20-25 / 25-40 / over 40 percent height loss) are fixed; every automatic six-point system reports percent or ratio agreement, not mm, except Yao 2024 (VBH MAE 0.61 mm, T12 to L5, 291 test films, institutional) (masks_corners_magnification.md, section 1) [full there].
- Mask-derived corners are coarser than regressed landmarks: minAreaRect cannot express wedging or biconcavity, a four-vertex polygon loses the middle height, and the only readable mask-to-landmark sagittal pipeline had median landmark errors of 1.7 to 4.1 mm (masks_corners_magnification.md, section 3).
- Ratios and angles (body_index, wedge) cancel uniform magnification; absolute heights, widths and depths on radiographs need a magnification estimate, and measured lateral spine magnification runs 1.21 cervical to 1.31 lumbar with a range to 1.63 (masks_corners_magnification.md, section 4).
- On CT, no training-free method reports automated end-plate width or depth against manual in mm (ct_intensity_methods.md, section 6, Gaps).
- The posterior body height measured from CT masks ran 2.3 to 7.5 mm above Panjabi's dry-bone table at lumbar levels; the cause is not settled (morphometry-beyond-the-42-papers.md, The assumptions).

## What the family's prior art settles and does not

Settled:

- Anterior, middle and posterior heights are the measured quantities everywhere; width, depth and area are almost never measured automatically. Two automated width-and-depth validations were found: Siemionow 2020, lumbar cadaveric intraoperative CT reported in percent, and Ichikawa 2025, thoracic CT of 42 forensic patients, whose abstract gives Spearman above 0.900 for depth and width and whose mm errors (about 1.5 to 1.7 mm MAE) come only from a generated summary of the paper. No automated end-plate or mid-body cross-sectional area validation was found at all, in this sweep or in the second one (Europe PMC, OpenAlex and a web search). Hipp 2022 is the only large series with an end-plate width ratio and a height-over-width ratio, and it is a projection on lateral films, so its "width" is the sagittal depth in this vocabulary's terms.
- Accuracy of heights, when it is given in mm, is sub-millimetre to a millimetre on good images: 0.61 mm MAE on lateral lumbar films (Yao 2024), 0.78 to 0.86 mm median on lumbar MRI (Al-Haidri 2025), 0.3 mm repeat difference on chest CT (ImaLife), about 1.5 to 1.9 percent of height across modalities (Suri 2021), and Bland-Altman limits of +/-1.5 to 2.9 mm against two readers on lumbar MRI (Germann 2023). Most CT work (Palm, Ma, Nadeem) gives ICC or r without mm; the CT exceptions are Alukaev 2022's body-height MAE of 1.17 mm (which height and which levels not stated) and Ichikawa 2025's summary figures; the fracture-grading work (VerSe, Riedel 2026, HealthiVert) gives grades without heights.
- Ratios (wedge Ha/Hp, biconcavity Hm/Hp, A/P, C/A, C/P, compression ratio) agree with the annotator who made the training data at Spearman 0.605 (C/A), 0.721 (C/P) and 0.798 (A/P) with Bland-Altman limits of about +/-0.1 (test set, 982 vertebrae), and with three external readers at 0.519 to 0.589 (C/A), 0.558 to 0.647 (C/P) and 0.735 to 0.770 (A/P), which the authors call comparable with the readers' agreement among themselves (1,753 vertebrae; Arakawa 2025), so body_index from an automatic pipeline carries about 10 percent of ratio uncertainty, which is the whole range between Genant grade 0 and grade 1.
- Every method takes the level name from somewhere else: an operator's seeds (SpineAnalyzer), a segmenter's labels (TotalSegmentator through Comp2Comp in Palm, SCNet in the HealthiVert paper and CTSpine1K's nnU-Net in its README), the annotation (Suri), a reference vertebra in the field (VertXNet's C2 or S1), or the landmarking networks' own labels (Hipp, whose networks were trained to recognise L1 to S1 and C2 to C7 only, and whose text says definitive labelling needs whole-spine imaging). None of these methods names a level from the measure it reports; the one measure shown to separate a level is Hipp's FBDR, whose cut-off of 1.0574 misclassified 278 of 42,980 lumbar bodies as S1 versus L1 to L5 (end-plates.md gives the diagonal ratio's AUC). The reports of how often the naming fails are ImaLife's 95 of 632 participants and Nadeem's 100 percent labelling on its 100 manually outlined scans, with full agreement across its 236 standard-and-low-dose pairs [full on PMC].
- Public truth for this family is thin: VerSe gives Genant grades per vertebra but no heights; NHANES II gives films, 544 with corner landmarks (complete only for C3 to C7 and L1 to L5) and 445 of them with C2 or S1 reference labels by VertXNet's count, 600 of each by its Zenodo record's description, but Hipp's landmarks and metrics are on request only, and the UK Biobank's 31,798 DXA vertebrae (Wilson 2025) release none; no public set carries reader-measured heights, widths or depths by level on CT. So a benchmark of this family would have to make its own truth, as Palm (336 vertebrae) and Yao (291 films) did.
- Transitional vertebrae and L6 are excluded or unaddressed everywhere (Suri did not annotate L6; Palm excluded variants; Hipp names mislabelling as a limitation; VertXNet does not discuss it), which is the case this project cares about most.

Not settled:

- Whether CT masks give Panjabi's heights: the CTSpinoPelvic1K gap of 2.3 to 7.5 mm (existing note) is unexplained, and no paper here measured mask heights against calipers.
- Within-person level-to-level ratios of heights exist only as Palm's T1 to L5 matrices in 262 healthy adults (data on request) and Hipp's adjacent-level differences on films; nothing from C2 to L5 in one person.
- Middle-height placement differs by pipeline (Nadeem: minimum in the central third; Palm: Bresenham-adjusted midpoint; six-point: the end-plate midpoints), and no study compared the conventions.
- How the deep landmark systems convert pixels to mm on radiographs (Yao, Suri) is not described, so their mm figures may be at detector scale.

## Gaps (what could not be read)

- pubs.rsna.org (Suri 2021, Loeffler 2020, Burns 2017) refused the fetch; Suri and Loeffler were read on PMC, Burns stays as the existing note has it.
- link.springer.com and nature.com redirect to a sign-in; Palm 2025 was read on PMC, Germann 2023 as the PubMed abstract at first and in full on PMC in the review of 2026-10-30, Engelke 2019 only as its abstract (its per-level tables are in no open copy: Europe PMC lists no PMCID and no open full text).
- Ma 2024 (Calcif Tissue Int): abstract only, and no open copy exists (Europe PMC: no PMCID, not open access; Springer redirects to a sign-in); the patient count, which correlation belongs to which height, and the agreement plots were not read.
- Al-Haidri 2025: an aggregator record only; Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex and Crossref confirm the journal, volume 106, article 107770 and closed access but carry no abstract, so its 200 subjects, split, mm errors and Dice stay unchecked.
- Hipp 2022 Appendices S1 to S3 were read in the second sweep from Europe PMC's supplementary files; none gives a landmark accuracy in mm (the row above has what they give).
- The 2024 pedicle screw paper (PMC11345936) was read in full in the review and measures no body dimension; the 2024 MRI-versus-CT planning paper (Abel 2024) is manual measurement, read as its abstract.
- Comp2Comp's spine module, automated body width and depth on CT, and automated end-plate or body area were searched in the second sweep (2026-10-30): Comp2Comp computes no dimension, the width and depth search found Ichikawa 2025 and Abel 2024, and the area search found nothing. Ichikawa 2025's own text was not readable (the Niigata University repository answered 503 under a maintenance notice of 28 October 2026), so its mm figures rest on a generated summary until the paper is read. Zenodo, Hugging Face and the reachable part of Grand Challenge hold no reader-measured heights, widths or depths by level.
- No Grand Challenge task on vertebral morphometry was found; only VerSe (segmentation and labelling, with grades as metadata) and SPIDER (MRI segmentation) exist.
- Nothing was downloaded; no file under /home/user was changed.
