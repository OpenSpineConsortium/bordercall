## What the existing notes already say

- The 42-paper table and its gaps (/home/user/SchwingNet/docs/research/morphometry-beyond-the-42-papers.md): the pedicle rows of Panjabi's tables are manual, on dry bone; Lien 2007 measured pedicles from T1 to L5 in 15 sets of vertebrae and Zindrick 1987 from T1, counted in studies, not people (section on the whole-column series); Busscher 2010 (six cadavers, CT, C3 to S1) plots but does not tabulate its pedicle values (item 4 of the closing list); Shin 2024 measured L1 to L5 interpedicular distance and pedicle length in 1,000 CT patients aged 18 to 35, read from the abstract (table of recent series); Duman 2026 had one radiologist measure body heights and pedicles from T11 to L2 in 517 adults. The same note records that the mask-to-measure mapping "is checked only in the lumbar spine and refuses the pedicle", and that SPINEPS has no pedicle label (its section 'What more data fixes, and what it does not', citing bordercall archive/bordercall/SPINEPS_MAPPING.md). Nothing in it is an automatic pedicle measure.
- The benchmark and model notes (/home/user/SchwingNet/docs/research/benchmarks-to-beat.md, /home/user/SchwingNet/docs/research/public-spine-and-pelvis-models-to-benchmark.md) name no pedicle term; grep finds none.
- The four notes under "/home/user/bordercall/research_notes/Vertebra measures without masks/" name the pedicle only as the plane in which canal width is taken (ct_intensity_methods.md, Inferences, step 6 of its deterministic pipeline) and as a source of metal streaks (its Gaps). So the whole family is new ground for the project: no earlier note reads a method that measures a pedicle or a pars automatically.

## Findings

### 1. Automatic pedicle morphometry on CT, as measures (the family's own keys)

- Knez, Likar, Pernus, Vrtovec 2016, IEEE Trans Med Imaging 35(6):1420-1430, PMID 26742125 [abstract, Europe PMC record]: 3D parametric modelling of the vertebral body and the pedicles on CT of 11 patients, 81 thoracic pedicles; modelling accuracy 0.39 +/- 0.31 mm (body) and 0.31 +/- 0.25 mm (pedicle); against manual plans, screw diameter 0.4 +/- 0.4 mm, length 5.8 +/- 4.2 mm, pedicle crossing point 2.0 +/- 1.4 mm, insertion angle 7.6 +/- 5.8 degrees; fastening strength 48 +/- 26 percent higher in 99 percent of cases. Private data, no code named. The pedicle model yields the family's width, height and axis, but the abstract reports them only through the screw.
- Knez and Vrtovec 2020, Image Analysis and Stereology 39(2):101-109, doi 10.5566/ias.2349, CC BY-NC 4.0 [full, the journal PDF read with pdftotext in the review]: the same parametric method on preoperative thoracic CT of 26 patients (13 male, 13 female, mean age 17.1, 12 to 34; 23 adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, 3 Scheuermann's kyphosis; Orthopaedic Hospital Valdoltra, GE LightSpeed VTC, 0.6 mm slices), 270 vertebrae T1 to T12, 540 pedicles, 20 (T1) to 52 per level; computes pedicle width, height, length, chord length, transverse angulation, sagittal angulation and cross-sectional area, that is pedicle_w, pedicle_h, pedicle_length, pedicle_chord, pedicle_angle_transverse, pedicle_angle_sagittal and pedicle_area at once. Against manual measurements on oblique multiplanar reconstructions, mean absolute difference 0.44 +/- 0.35 mm (width), 0.56 +/- 0.52 mm (height), 1.72 +/- 1.29 mm (chord), no significant difference (p >= 0.12); manual orthogonal reconstructions differ significantly (p <= 1.1 x 10^-3) from the oblique ones by 0.77 +/- 0.56 mm (width) and 1.31 +/- 1.08 mm (height), and from the computerized measures by 0.74 +/- 0.57 mm and 1.45 +/- 1.10 mm. The manual truth is one blinded observer, a medical imaging researcher. Per-level means and SDs are tabulated for T1 to T12 (Tables 1 and 2): computerized width 7.9 +/- 1.4 mm at T1, 5.4 +/- 1.0 at T4, 8.8 +/- 1.7 at T12; height 9.4 +/- 2.2 at T1 to 16.9 +/- 2.4 at T12; transverse angulation 25.4 +/- 5.7 degrees at T1 to 1.1 +/- 7.3 at T12; area 62.8 +/- 20.0 to 121.0 +/- 31.6 mm2. The angles and the area have no manual comparator. No data or code availability statement; Europe PMC has no record of it. This is the one paper that reports the family's measures in their own units from an automatic method.
- Knez et al. 2018, Spine, PMID 30325346 [abstract]: 256 thoracic screws, 1,024 manual plans by two observers and 256 computer-assisted ones; the sagittal inclination's intra-observer variability was 18.3 +/- 7.6 degrees and the difference between one observer and the computer 12.3 +/- 6.5 degrees. It says how loose a manual truth for pedicle_angle_sagittal is.
- Knez et al. 2019, Medical Physics, PMID 31087326 [abstract]: 25 patients, 204 screws planned from CT with bone-density maps; against postoperative reconstructions, entry point 3.4 +/- 2.5 mm, crossing point 2.7 +/- 1.6 mm, sagittal inclination 7.4 +/- 5.3 degrees; 96.6 percent of plans acceptable.
- Zhang, Zhao, Zhang, Huang, Gong, Deng 2024, Journal of Bone Oncology 47:100627, PMID 39188420, PMC11345936 [full]: a heatmap landmark network (the text names a U-Net with a ResNet34 encoder and, inconsistently, VGG16 weights) on 1,410 axial CT slices of 282 patients, five slices per patient aligned to the L1 to L5 pedicles, one hospital, one Siemens scanner, split by image (847/363/200), patient independence of the split not stated. Outputs: left and right pedicle width (pedicle_w), left and right axial angle (pedicle_angle_transverse), left and right screw path length (pedicle_chord) and interpedicular distance. Truth: the mean of two radiologists and one surgeon. ICC 0.97 and 0.98 for width, 0.92 and 0.93 for the axial angle, 0.82 and 0.87 for the path, 0.91 for interpedicular distance; mean absolute errors about 0.33 to 2.2 mm for distances and about 1.6 degrees for angles; landmark PCK at 3 mm 93.5 to 99.0 percent. Lumbar only, fractures (989), prior lumbar surgery or marked vertebral calcification (1,245), bone defects from surgery or congenital variation (52) and significant congenital variation or acquired deformity of the vertebral body (21) excluded from 2,589 patients, leaving 282; transitional vertebrae are not mentioned; levels taken from the slice order, no code or data statement.
- Lee, Kim, Kim, Chung 2011, IEEE Trans Biomed Eng 58(7):2051-2063, PMID 21693388 [abstract]: a classical pipeline (dynamic thresholding, canal reference, edge segmentation) that segments the lumbar pedicle on CT of 19 fusion patients, 84 vertebrae; success 93.22 percent, final mean error 0.14 +/- 0.05 mm, pedicle volume precision under 1 percent. It gives a pedicle mask and volume, not width or height; private data.
- Siemionow, Luciano, Forsthoefel, Aydogmus 2020, J Craniovertebr Junction Spine 11(2):99-103, PMC7462134 [full]: a HoloSurgical network segments intraoperative lumbar CT of cadaveric specimens and a surgeon measures pedicle diameter and angulation on the input and on the segmented output; the accuracy is the output's reading as a percent of the input's (pedicle angulation 96.53, diameter 96.93), not an error in mm or degrees, and the abstract, methods and results give inconsistent counts. Private, no code.
- Burstrom et al. 2019, J Neurosurg Spine 31(1):147-154, PMID 30901757 [abstract]: model-based segmentation with machine learning on cone-beam CT of 21 cadavers plus 20 clinical cases; the automatic pedicle midpoint against a user-defined one; pedicle identification and screw planning correct in 86.1 percent, 95.4 percent once severe deformity (Cobb over 75 degrees) and prior surgery are excluded; 11 +/- 4 s for five vertebrae. No millimetre figure in the abstract, no code.
- Vosko et al. 2026, J Clin Med 15(15):5972, PMC13467386 [full]: pedicle width cut from TotalSegmentator v2.13.0 whole-vertebra masks by a custom pipeline started from hand-placed landmarks, used only as a feature of a cervical screw malposition model (ROC AUC 0.69, 862 screws); no width in mm is printed and its reproducibility was not evaluated. The one pedicle width taken from a public segmenter's masks found.

### 2. Automatic pedicle screw planning (the pedicle axis and its bore, as a screw)

- Goerres et al. 2017, Phys Med Biol 62(7):2871-2891, PMC9148916 [full]: deformable atlas registration (coherent point drift) of 40 vertebral surfaces, 20 at T7/T8 merged and 20 at L3, taken from two public SpineWeb CT segmentation sets (dataset 2, 10 thoracolumbar cases; dataset 5, 10 lumbar cases), with 60 expert trajectories; leave-one-out. Pedicle-centre deviation 0.56 mm (T7/T8), 1.16 and 1.09 mm (L3 left, right); angular error 2.27 degrees (T7/T8), 3.13 and 3.60 degrees (L3), pooled 3.01 +/- 2.81 degrees; maximum screw diameter deviation 0.45 mm at T7/T8 and 1.09 to 1.58 mm at L3 (the paper's own figures disagree between sections); no collision with 6 mm screws inside the vertebra at L3, the only level tested for collisions; the abstract says collisions were avoided in all cases, while section 3.4 finds overlaps for wider and longer screws (none at 7.0 mm diameter and 50 mm length or less) and slightly more often than the reference for long screws; the abstract gives the L3 pedicle-centre deviation as 1.12 +/- 0.67 mm and the diameter difference as 1.26 +/- 1.19 mm. The method needs the level label as input and covers three levels; no code statement. Section 2.4 names the sources: dataset 2 is Yao 2012 (MICCAI, 10 thoracic and lumbar segmentations, the only thoracic source) and dataset 5 Ibragimov 2014 (IEEE TMI 33:861-874, 10 lumbar cases); no code, data or atlas statement anywhere in the article. SpineWeb's host does not resolve from the session, but both sets have mirrors (section 3).
- Vijayan et al. 2019, Phys Med Biol 64(16):165020, PMID 31247607, PMC8650759 [full]: a 40-member lumbar statistical atlas (L1 to L5) from CT of 20 TCIA, 10 SpineWeb and 10 institutional patients, segmented by hand, with one neurosurgeon's trajectories, registered as an active shape model to unsegmented CT; registration succeeded in 191/200 vertebrae (9 outliers, 2 of them lumbosacral transitional vertebrae), RMSE 2.0 +/- 0.5 mm; centrelines Grade A in 94.7 percent (362/382); screw diameter and length conformant in 98.7 percent (Grade A/B); 5 O-arm CBCT patients at L3 to L5, 28 of 30 trajectories (about 93 percent). No code statement found.
- Scherer, Kausch et al. 2022, Spine J 22(10):1666-1676, PMID 35584757 [abstract]: a U-Net per vertebra instance trained on 155 CT-navigated cases with 1,052 planned lumbosacral screws (the levels are not in the abstract), validated on 24 cases and 130 screws; head 4.61 +/- 2.27 mm, tip 3.96 +/- 2.19 mm, direction 5.51 +/- 3.64 degrees from manual plans; Dice 0.61 +/- 0.16 against inter-rater 0.56 +/- 0.18; diameter thinner by 0.3 +/- 0.6 mm; Gertzbein-Robbins A in 96.2 percent. Private registry, no code statement.
- Scherer, Kausch et al. 2023, J Clin Med 12(7):2646, PMC10094754 [full]: the same nnU-Net tool, integrated into MITK, against Brainlab Elements' atlas planning on 50 private cases, 256 lumbar screws; head 3.93 +/- 2.08 mm, tip 3.49 +/- 1.80 mm, angle 4.46 +/- 2.86 degrees (inter-rater 4.89 mm, 4.36 mm, 5.27 degrees); Gertzbein-Robbins A 97 percent against 65 percent; the atlas tool failed on 34 percent of screws in the most altered anatomy. Data on request only; the tool's intellectual property is held by three authors and no licence is stated, so the public MITK platform does not make the planner public.
- Siemionow, Forsthoefel, Foy, Gawel, Luciano 2021, J Craniovertebr Junction Spine 12(3):223-227, PMC8501821 [full]: an autonomous lumbar planner (Holo Surgical) on 20 patient CTs and 208 screws L1 to S1, all Zdichavsky IA and Gertzbein A or B; the screw diameter by level (L1 5.5 mm to S1 7.8 mm) stands in for a width the paper never reports.
- Wang et al. 2024, Bioengineering 11(11):1094, PMC11592283 [full]: AISPINE, a 3D U-Net with edge attention and a Hourglass keypoint model, on 316 PLIF patients (228 training, 88 validation), L2 to S1, one scanner; Dice 0.9459; pedicle transverse and sagittal angles 1.63 +/- 0.83 and 1.39 +/- 1.03 degrees from manual (inter-rater 1.54 +/- 0.84); screws shorter (46.2 against 49.5 mm) and thinner (6.24 against 6.51 mm) than manual, p < 0.001; Gertzbein-Robbins A 97.7 percent, every B at L5. Commercial software, data on request, no code.
- Yang et al. 2025, World Neurosurg 201:124302, PMID 40683465 [abstract]: a 3D U-Net planner trained on 1,005 public CTSpine1K and 230 hospital cases, 840 screws T12 to S1 on 70 validation cases, Gertzbein A 98.8 percent; the first planner trained on a public set, though its test cases are not said to be public; no pedicle width.
- Jia et al. 2023, Frontiers in Surgery 10:1247527, PMC10518392 [full]: Surgiplan, a 3D U-Net, on 45 patients and 208 screws from T10 to L5; the diameter is capped at 90 percent of the pedicle width, but no width is reported; Gertzbein-Robbins A 85.1 percent for planned screws against 64.9 percent for freehand screws read on postoperative CT, an unequal comparison the paper acknowledges. Data "without undue reservation" on request, no code.
- Gotschi et al. 2026, J Spine Surg 12(4):51 [full, publisher HTML]: SpineR (commercial) masks, level-specific template fitting and a multi-objective grid search (safety, HU along the screw, rod alignment) on 20 CTs and 238 screws, T9 to L5, one surgeon's plans as truth; Gertzbein-Robbins A 87.8 percent, median breach 0.8 mm; HU coverage up 74.9 percent; 5.4 min per case (median, IQR 4.6 to 6.9). PMC13181670, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Data sharing statement not readable in the page, no code named.
- Massalimova et al. 2025, Sci Rep 15:37617, PMC12569008 [full]: shape completion of RGB-D vertebra point clouds with constrained trajectory fitting on the public SpineDepth set (9 cadavers, L1 to L5), pedicle-centre offset 1.58 +/- 0.58 mm from CT and 1.68 +/- 0.59 mm from RGB-D against surgeons' plans; the pedicle radius is an inscribed circle and no width is reported. The one planner validated on a public set with a pedicle-centre error in mm.
- Caprara et al. 2022, Comput Methods Biomech Biomed Engin 25(4):464-474, PMID 34369827 [abstract]: a genetic algorithm with finite elements on cadaveric L1 to L5 of four spines (2 female, 2 male, 60 to 78 years), 40 screws, 26 percent higher simulated pull-out. Tsagkaris et al. 2023, Front Bioeng Biotechnol 11:1270522, PMID 37954015, PMC10639121 [full]: the same algorithm tested by pull-out on 25 lumbar vertebrae (five each of L1 to L5) from five fresh-frozen cadavers, a gain only above 3,500 MPa cm3 of E-modulus, the optimised screws shorter (p < 0.001); data on request to the Balgrist Spine Biomechanics Unit; no code found for either.
- Bao et al. 2026, PLoS One 21(2):e0342349, PMID 41671245, PMC12893610 [full], with the IEEE DataPort record "C2-Net database", doi 10.21227/1j3d-6j19 [page] and github.com/Ingram66/C2-NET [code]: C2 only; a segmentation module outlines the C2 pedicle and a second module gives a feasibility probability; truth is screws placed by hand in 3D-printed models; accuracy 89.4 percent, sensitivity 90.0, specificity 89.0. The data are private by the paper's own statement (requests to the hospital's medical ethics committee): 405 patients and 490 split C2 pedicles in a figure caption, while Table 1 gives 320 patients and 440 pedicles for 10-fold training and validation and 37 patients and 50 pedicles for the 3D-printed test set (320 + 37 = 357); CT (Philips iCT 256, 2 mm); the labels come from a 4.78 mm narrowest-diameter cutoff set on 3D-printed models in earlier work. The DataPort test set (13.42 MB of JPEG) is behind a subscription; the GitHub repository holds train.py and C3D_model.py under a one-line README, no LICENSE file.
- Code without a paper: github.com/nishyrut/PedicleScrewGuide [code], GPL-3.0, TotalSegmentator plus SimpleITK, computes a trajectory and a 3D-printable jig, measures no pedicle, reports no validation, "not been tested widely"; github.com/grotyx/pedicle-screw-simulator [code], MIT, research desktop software with a Gertzbein-Robbins grader that finds a geometric pedicle isthmus (the coronal cross-section minimum on the TotalSegmentator mask, optionally refined by a pedicle subregion nnU-Net) to size screws, and says clinical accuracy has not been established and that deformity, implants and transitional anatomy may invalidate its proposals. SonoGym (arXiv 2507.01152 [full, arXiv HTML], CC BY 4.0, NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks Track [page, OpenReview], github.com/SonoGym/SonoGym [code]) simulates robotic ultrasound on CT and label maps of 10 TotalSegmentator subjects and sets the surgical target pose at L4 with in-house planning software; its code and assets are public on GitHub and Hugging Face. SafeRPlan (Ao et al., Med Image Anal 2025;99:103345, PMID 39293187 [abstract]; arXiv 2305.05354 [full, ar5iv]) plans a drill path by safe deep reinforcement learning from a partial observation, the vertebra's dorsal surface as an ultrasound scan would reconstruct it, simulated on 5 MRI-derived human models at L1 to L5 (25 environments) with gold standards from in-house expert planners; no code or data statement found. Neither measures a pedicle.
- Thintharua and others 2026, Neurosurg Rev 49(1):283, PMC12992485 [full], the systematic review of AI in lumbar and lumbosacral screw placement the sweep could not reach: 14 studies from 1,492 hits (searched January 2025). Of them the note lacked Esfandiari 2018, Siemionow 2021, Roberts 2023, Abel 2024, Luchmann 2024, Liebmann 2024, von Atzigen 2022, Yang 2021, Thies 2020 and Da Mutten 2024; none measures a pedicle, and the review names no pedicle width or height in mm from any method beyond Zhang 2024's.

### 3. Pedicle labels in public datasets

- VerSe 2019/2020 (github.com/anjany/verse [page]): 374 scans of 355 patients, whole-vertebra masks and centroids, labels for L6 (25) and T13 (28), CC BY-SA 4.0; no pedicle or substructure label (its "seg-subreg" filenames are not explained on the page).
- VID vertebra identification dataset (github.com/ImFusionGmbH/VID-vertebra-identification-dataset [page], LICENSE.txt CC BY-SA 4.0 [page]; Burgin, Prevost, Stollenga 2023, arXiv 2308.02509, MICCAI 2023 [full, arXiv HTML]; the MICCAI page's code link is the dataset repository itself, so no method code exists): three keypoints per vertebra, body, left pedicle and right pedicle, on 2,118 scans (360 VerSe, 1,676 TCIA CT Colonography, 82 TCIA Pancreas), 1,949 training and 169 validation; the pedicle points were "determined through post-processing of ground truth segmentations" on VerSe, the steps not described, then bootstrapped to the other sets; ground-truth keypoints made by the authors in a bootstrap and validation process (README [page]), the pedicle points first from post-processing of VerSe ground-truth segmentations and then bootstrapped to the other sets (arXiv 2308.02509 [full]), each keypoint carrying a spine_level field; neither source says expert.; the GNN identifies 93.26 percent (validation) and 93.02 percent (test) on VerSe; training adds synthetic spines missing T13, L6 or S2, and off-by-one errors at the thoracolumbar junction are reported, but transitional vertebrae are not otherwise handled. A pedicle centre is not a width, height or angle, but two pedicle points per level on 2,118 public scans is the largest pedicle truth found.
- LumASe (zenodo.org/records/7181338 [page]), CC BY 4.0: 663 lumbar vertebrae, L1 to L5, cropped from CT of ShengJing Hospital (Philips, Siemens, Toshiba), voxel masks of seven regions by three physicians: superior articular process, body, transverse process, lamina, pedicle, spinous process, inferior articular process; fractures, implants and tumours excluded; no paper, no baseline, no measurements. The only public voxel truth of the pedicle found.
- LumbarSeg-6K (Zenodo 21704319, v3 of 2026-07-30, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; Xu et al., Sci Data 2026, doi 10.1038/s41597-026-07986-7) [page; Crossref record]: 5,952 single-vertebra lumbar CT volumes (3,882 CT-COLON, 1,407 VerSe, 663 LumASe) relabelled to one seven-class scheme with a pedicle class, about nine times LumASe's pedicle truth; levels, annotators and any baseline are not on the record and the paper was not readable here.
- Da Mutten et al. 2026, J Imaging Inform Med, PMID 42573685, doi 10.1007/s10278-026-02169-7, CC BY [full, the open-access PDF read in the posterior-elements review]: 118 scans of 115 patients from the public VerSe 2020 set, labelled T1 to L5 (the sacrum excluded) by two resident doctors and two medical students under a spine-surgery fellow, segmented into corpus, lamina, pedicle, superior and inferior articular processes, transverse and spinous processes; nnU-Net, ResEnc nnU-Net and SwinUNETR in five folds and on an external vertebral subset of the public TotalSegmentator set; external Dice 0.989 +/- 0.023 for the pedicles with the standard nnU-Net (0.980 to 0.993 over the seven regions); the geometric method of Blomenkamp et al. (its reference 33, EMBC 2025) reached 0.382 for the lamina to 0.889 for corpus and pedicle together. The labels are on reasonable request and the trained models on GitHub (MICN-Lab/Spine_Subregions, no licence file); the authors say an additional lumbar vertebra would not be recognised. A pedicle Dice of 0.99 would bound a width error at well under the slice thickness, but no width is reported.
- Blomenkamp, Kramer, Bauer, Paulus 2025, EMBC, doi 10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11254383, PMID 41336765 [abstract], Da Mutten 2026's reference 33: a training-free skeletonization partition of a vertebra surface into body, laminae and the spinous, transverse and articular processes, shipped as SpineToolkit's ShapeSegmentation module (github.com/VisSim-UniKO/SpineToolkit, no licence file [code]) and used by SLD (arXiv 2601.16782 [full]). It has no pedicle class, which is why Da Mutten scores corpus and pedicle jointly for it (Dice 0.889), so no geometric baseline for a pedicle width exists.
- TotalSegmentator (github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator [code], Apache-2.0 code and weights for the spine tasks): whole vertebrae in total, bodies without the arch in vertebrae_body, vertebrae_pp and vertebrae_pp_refined; no pedicle, lamina or pars class.
- SpineWeb datasets 2 and 5, Goerres 2017's atlas sources, are obtainable again as mirrors: CSI 2014 on Zenodo 7049844 (20 CTs, ODC-By, labels T1 to L5) and the Ljubljana lumbar vertebra CT database (lit.fe.uni-lj.si, CC BY-NC-ND) [page]. Whole-vertebra masks only.
- CTSpine1K, SPIDER, MRSpineSeg, BUU-LSPINE, NHANES II and AASCE carry no pedicle truth: the searches on their names returned only whole-vertebra masks or vertebral-body corners (BUU-LSPINE: 400 AP and lateral pairs with corner coordinates and an LSTV ground truth, request form [search]; NHANES II: 17,000 films with 600 annotated for body corners, Zenodo record 10223910 [search]). Their pages were not fetched in this sweep, since the question was pedicles and none names one.

### 4. Pedicles on radiographs

- Wu, Liu, Ling, Huang, Liu, Duan 2024, PLoS One 19(6):e0295196, PMID 38870237, PMC11175444 [full]: transverse pedicle angle (pedicle_angle_transverse) as arctan of the pedicle half-width on the AP view over the pedicle length on the lateral view; 66 patients, L3 to L5, but the radiographs are simulated from CT in 3-matic and the landmarks are placed by hand by one surgeon; against the 3D angle, ICC 0.815 and 0.862 at L3, 0.637 and 0.622 at L4, 0.670 and 0.577 at L5, mean differences about 0.5 to 1 degree; no code, no magnification or positioning effects tested. It is the one formula found that reads a pedicle angle off two films.
- Kim, Wang, Park, Chang 2025, Sci Rep 15:26647, PMID 40695965, PMC12283916 [full]: a two-stage rotated detector (MMRotate) finds vertebrae then pedicles on 1,080 posteroanterior radiographs of patients aged 4 to 18 (14,608 pedicle crops for training) and grades Nash-Moe rotation from a pedicle width ratio; mAP 0.905 for pedicles, grade accuracy 0.74; private data, no code. It measures a pedicle shadow's width only as a ratio within one vertebra.
- Aubert, Bakhous et al. 2018, SPIE Medical Imaging 10575 [search only]: a CNN regresses pedicle positions on biplanar radiographs of 476 scoliosis and normal cases (380/48/48), cutting axial rotation error by about 10 percent and 3D pedicle error by about 17 percent; EOS data, private. Ebrahimi et al. 2019, Eur Spine J [search only]: a quasi-automated pedicle detector feeding Stokes' rotation method on frontal films of 149 subjects.
- vertebra-xray-core v0.1.0 (Zenodo 22893478, github.com/Institute-of-One/vertebra-xray-core, MIT, 2026-09-22) [page, code]: a normative pedicle table built from 394 VerSe vertebrae, used as two frontal pedicle centroids beside four end-plate corners to make 3D vertebral rotation identifiable from films; it takes landmarks as input and carries pedicle centroids only, no width, with no validation against a reader.
- No method was found that measures pedicle width, height or interpedicular distance on a plain AP or lateral radiograph automatically and reports it in millimetres.

### 5. The pars interarticularis

- Achar et al. 2023, Sensors 23(18):8001, PMID 37766055, PMC10538057 [full, PMC]: 4 cadaveric lumbar spines (3 female, 1 male, 54 +/- 18 years) with 20 saw-cut pars defects; contrast of bone against defect at 11 defects 35 (UTE), 97 (CT-like), 146 (CT); defect width on CT-like against CT slope 0.86, R2 0.73 (UTE 0.63, R2 0.21); all data used for both training and testing; data not publicly available.; deep learning turns ultrashort-echo-time MRI into CT-like images with saliency maps at the probable pars defect. A Europe PMC search for the pars with "automatic", "deep learning", "thickness", "morphometry" or "segmentation" returned 71 records, 25 read as titles, of which only this one is an automatic method, and it measures a defect, not pars_w or pars_area.
- No automatic measure of pars width, thickness or cross-sectional area was found on any modality; the pars appears in the segmentation datasets only inside the lamina or the articular processes (LumASe and Da Mutten 2026 have no pars class).

### 6. Inner and outer widths and the cortex

- No automatic method reports pedicle_w_inner, pedicle_h_inner, pedicle_w_outer, pedicle_h_outer or pedicle_cortex. The one source that separates cortex from medullary cavity at the isthmus is Li et al. 2019, Folia Morphol (Warsz) 78(3):476-480, PMID 30687913 [full, journal PDF]: 30 adult spine specimens (15 male, 15 female) from C2 to L5, the widths and heights of the pedicle cortex and medullary cavity and the cortex thickness at four borders measured at the isthmus on CT (no AutoCAD named); medial-to-lateral cortex thickness ratio near 3:1 cervical, 2:1 thoracic, 1:1 lumbar, a manual series. Knez 2016 and 2019 use CT-inferred bone density along the screw, not a cortical boundary.

### 7. MRI and synthetic CT

- Ogihara, Fukuda, Katsumi, Ojiri 2026, Tomography 12(3):40, PMC13030672 [full]: two radiologists read pedicle width and length by hand on a CT-like 3D T1 gradient-echo MRI and on CT in 51 patients, 448 pedicles L1 to L5; width agrees to a mean difference of 0.09 and 0.10 mm (ICC 0.985 and 0.968, limits about -1.6 to +1.8 mm), length less well (limits about -7.4 to +5.3 mm). A modality agreement read by hand, not a measurer.
- Morbee 2021, Eur J Radiol 144:109999 [abstract]: synthetic CT from lumbar MRI in 30 participants, pedicle width by readers "statistically equivalent" to CT, no number in the abstract.
- Abel 2024, Eur Spine J 33(11):4144-4154 [abstract]: deep-learning reconstructed 3D MRI with CT-like contrast in 16 patients, pedicle height and width read by three radiologists at L1 to L5 against CT; inter-rater ICC as low as 0.60 on CT itself, the reader floor any pedicle measurer is judged against.
- Roberts 2023, Spine J 23(11):1602-1612 [abstract]: a 3D CycleGAN turns lumbar MRI into CT; sagittal measurements within 10 percent of true CT, axial pedicle measurements off by up to 34 percent, so the pedicle is where a generated CT fails worst.

## What the family's prior art settles and does not

Settled. An automatic measure of pedicle width and height on CT can agree with a manual oblique-plane measure to about half a millimetre (Knez and Vrtovec 2020: 0.44 and 0.56 mm MAD on 540 thoracic pedicles), and a lumbar landmark network reaches ICC 0.97 for width and 0.92 for the transverse angle against three readers (Zhang 2024). Pedicle Dice of 0.99 is reachable with nnU-Net on hand-drawn subregions (Da Mutten 2026). The manual truth itself is loose for the sagittal angle: 18 degrees intra-observer and 12 degrees against the computer in Knez 2018, so pedicle_angle_sagittal and pedicle_incl_trans cannot be judged to a degree by any reader truth found. Automatic screw planners land within 3.5 to 4.6 mm and 4.5 to 5.5 degrees of a surgeon's plan, which is the size of the inter-rater difference (Scherer 2022, 2023; Wang 2024), so the pedicle axis is known to about the precision surgeons agree on. Readers themselves set the floor: pedicle width read by hand agrees between a CT-like MRI and CT to about 0.1 mm on average with limits near 1.5 mm (Ogihara 2026), while inter-rater ICC on CT pedicle measures falls to 0.60 (Abel 2024), and a generated CT misreads the axial pedicle by up to 34 percent (Roberts 2023).

Not settled. No method found measures the family at every level from C2 to S1 in one person: Knez is thoracic, Zhang, Lee, Scherer, Wang and LumASe are lumbar or lumbosacral, Goerres has three levels, Bao has C2. No method reports a per-vertebra pedicle measure on a public test set: the only public pedicle truths are VID's two keypoints per vertebra on 2,118 scans (positions, not sizes), LumASe's 663 lumbar voxel masks (no paper, no baseline) and LumbarSeg-6K's 5,952 lumbar vertebrae relabelled with a pedicle class (no baseline on the record). No method addresses transitional vertebrae; VerSe carries L6 and T13 labels but no pedicle label, and VID handles missing levels only synthetically. No method separates inner from outer width or measures the cortex automatically; none measures the pars at all. On radiographs nothing automatic gives a pedicle width in millimetres; Wu 2024's arctan formula for the transverse angle is manual and on simulated films, and the scoliosis detectors (Kim 2025, Aubert 2018) read the pedicle shadow only as a position or a ratio. No validated code that computes a pedicle measure is public: github.com/grotyx/pedicle-screw-simulator [code], MIT, research desktop software with a Gertzbein-Robbins grader, finds a geometric pedicle isthmus (the coronal cross-section minimum on the TotalSegmentator mask, optionally refined by a pedicle subregion nnU-Net) to size screws, and says clinical accuracy has not been established and that deformity, implants and transitional anatomy may invalidate its proposals; PedicleScrewGuide and SonoGym plan a screw without measuring; C2-Net is two scripts with no licence file. For naming levels from a pedicle measure the field therefore offers a level-by-level pedicle table only from manual series (Panjabi, Lien 2007, Zindrick 1987, Tan 2004, Busscher 2010, Shin 2024, all cited in the 42-paper note) and no automatic measurer validated outside the thoracic (Knez) or lumbar (Zhang) spine.

Two things the family could use at once: VID's pedicle keypoints on VerSe give, with VerSe's whole-vertebra masks, a public starting truth for the pedicle axis (two points) at every labelled level, L6 and T13 included; and LumASe's seven-region lumbar masks give a public truth for a pedicle width and height estimator in L1 to L5, which the parametric model of Knez or an nnU-Net of Da Mutten's kind could be checked against before any grid run.
