## What the existing notes already say

- The 42-paper table and its gaps (/home/user/SchwingNet/docs/research/morphometry-beyond-the-42-papers.md): the pedicle rows of Panjabi's tables are manual, on dry bone; Lien 2007 measured pedicles from T1 to L5 in 15 sets of vertebrae and Zindrick 1987 from T1, counted in studies, not people (section on the whole-column series); Busscher 2010 (six cadavers, CT, C3 to S1) plots but does not tabulate its pedicle values (item 4 of the closing list); Shin 2024 measured L1 to L5 interpedicular distance and pedicle length in 1,000 CT patients aged 18 to 35, read from the abstract (table of recent series); Duman 2026 had one radiologist measure body heights and pedicles from T11 to L2 in 517 adults. The same note records that the mask-to-measure mapping "is checked only in the lumbar spine and refuses the pedicle", and that SPINEPS has no pedicle label (its last section, citing bordercall archive/bordercall/SPINEPS_MAPPING.md). Nothing in it is an automatic pedicle measure.
- The benchmark and model notes (/home/user/SchwingNet/docs/research/benchmarks-to-beat.md, /home/user/SchwingNet/docs/research/public-spine-and-pelvis-models-to-benchmark.md) name no pedicle term; grep finds none.
- The four notes under "/home/user/bordercall/research_notes/Vertebra measures without masks/" name the pedicle only as the plane in which canal width is taken (ct_intensity_methods.md, Inferences, step 6 of its deterministic pipeline) and as a source of metal streaks (its Gaps). None measures the pedicle itself.

So the whole family is new ground for the project: no earlier note reads a method that measures a pedicle or a pars automatically.

## Findings

### 1. Automatic pedicle morphometry on CT, as measures (the family's own keys)

- Knez, Likar, Pernus, Vrtovec 2016, IEEE Trans Med Imaging 35(6):1420-1430, PMID 26742125 [abstract, Europe PMC record]: 3D parametric modelling of the vertebral body and the pedicles on CT of 11 patients, 81 thoracic pedicles; modelling accuracy 0.39 +/- 0.31 mm (body) and 0.31 +/- 0.25 mm (pedicle); against manual plans, screw diameter 0.4 +/- 0.4 mm, length 5.8 +/- 4.2 mm, pedicle crossing point 2.0 +/- 1.4 mm, insertion angle 7.6 +/- 5.8 degrees; fastening strength 48 +/- 26 percent higher in 99 percent of cases. Private data, no code named. The pedicle model yields the family's width, height and axis, but the abstract reports them only through the screw.
- Knez and Vrtovec 2020, Image Analysis and Stereology 39(2):101-109, doi 10.5566/ias.2349 [abstract; the journal's PDF was fetched but came back as unreadable binary, the abstract page was read]: the same parametric method on preoperative thoracic CT of 26 subjects, 540 pedicles; computes pedicle width, height, length, chord length, transverse angulation, sagittal angulation and cross-sectional area, that is pedicle_w, pedicle_h, pedicle_length, pedicle_chord, pedicle_angle_transverse, pedicle_angle_sagittal and pedicle_area at once. Against manual measurements on oblique multiplanar reconstructions, mean absolute difference 0.44 +/- 0.35 mm (width), 0.56 +/- 0.52 mm (height), 1.72 +/- 1.29 mm (chord), no significant difference (p >= 0.12); manual orthogonal reconstructions differ from both by 0.77 mm (width) and 1.31 mm (height), p <= 0.0011. The angles and the area have no manual comparator in the abstract. Private data, no code named. This is the one paper that reports the family's measures in their own units from an automatic method.
- Knez et al. 2018, Spine, PMID 30325346 [abstract]: 256 thoracic screws, 1,024 manual plans by two observers and 256 computer-assisted ones; the sagittal inclination's intra-observer variability was 18.3 +/- 7.6 degrees and the difference between one observer and the computer 12.3 +/- 6.5 degrees. It says how loose a manual truth for pedicle_angle_sagittal is.
- Knez et al. 2019, Medical Physics, PMID 31087326 [abstract]: 25 patients, 204 screws planned from CT with bone-density maps; against postoperative reconstructions, entry point 3.4 +/- 2.5 mm, crossing point 2.7 +/- 1.6 mm, sagittal inclination 7.4 +/- 5.3 degrees; 96.6 percent of plans acceptable.
- Zhang, Zhao, Zhang, Huang, Gong, Deng 2024, Journal of Bone Oncology 47:100627, PMID 39188420, PMC11345936 [full]: a heatmap landmark network (the text names a U-Net with a ResNet34 encoder and, inconsistently, VGG16 weights) on 1,410 axial CT slices of 282 patients, five slices per patient aligned to the L1 to L5 pedicles, one hospital, one Siemens scanner, split by image (847/363/200), patient independence of the split not stated. Outputs: left and right pedicle width (pedicle_w), left and right axial angle (pedicle_angle_transverse), left and right screw path length (pedicle_chord) and interpedicular distance. Truth: the mean of two radiologists and one surgeon. ICC 0.97 and 0.98 for width, 0.92 and 0.93 for the axial angle, 0.82 and 0.87 for the path, 0.91 for interpedicular distance; mean absolute errors about 0.33 to 2.2 mm for distances and about 1.6 degrees for angles; landmark PCK at 3 mm 93.5 to 99.0 percent. Lumbar only, fractures, prior surgery and deformity excluded, no transitional vertebrae, levels taken from the slice order, no code or data statement.
- Lee, Kim, Kim, Chung 2011, IEEE Trans Biomed Eng 58(7):2051-2063, PMID 21693388 [abstract]: a classical pipeline (dynamic thresholding, canal reference, edge segmentation) that segments the lumbar pedicle on CT of 19 fusion patients, 84 vertebrae; success 93.22 percent, final mean error 0.14 +/- 0.05 mm, pedicle volume precision under 1 percent. It gives a pedicle mask and volume, not width or height; private data.
- Burstrom et al. 2019, J Neurosurg Spine 31(1):147-154, PMID 30901757 [abstract]: model-based segmentation with machine learning on cone-beam CT of 21 cadavers plus 20 clinical cases; the automatic pedicle midpoint against a user-defined one; pedicle identification and screw planning correct in 86.1 percent, 95.4 percent once severe deformity (Cobb over 75 degrees) and prior surgery are excluded; 11 +/- 4 s for five vertebrae. No millimetre figure in the abstract, no code.

### 2. Automatic pedicle screw planning (the pedicle axis and its bore, as a screw)

- Goerres et al. 2017, Phys Med Biol 62(7):2871-2891, PMC9148916 [full]: deformable atlas registration (coherent point drift) of 40 vertebral surfaces, 20 at T7/T8 merged and 20 at L3, taken from two public SpineWeb CT segmentation sets (dataset 2, 10 thoracolumbar cases; dataset 5, 10 lumbar cases), with 60 expert trajectories; leave-one-out. Pedicle-centre deviation 0.56 mm (T7/T8), 1.16 and 1.09 mm (L3 left, right); angular error 2.27 degrees (T7/T8), 3.13 and 3.60 degrees (L3), pooled 3.01 +/- 2.81 degrees; maximum screw diameter deviation 0.45 mm at T7/T8 and 1.09 to 1.58 mm at L3 (the paper's own figures disagree between sections); no collisions. The method needs the level label as input and covers three levels; no code statement. The public source data could not be checked: spineweb.digitalimaginggroup.ca does not resolve from the session.
- Vijayan et al. 2019, Phys Med Biol 64(16):165020, PMID 31247607 [abstract]: a 40-member lumbar statistical atlas registered as an active shape model to unsegmented CT; surface RMSE 2.0 +/- 0.5 mm in 96 percent of cases, centrelines within the pedicle in 95 percent, screw diameter and length conformant in 98.7 percent; 5 clinical CBCT patients at 93 percent. No code, atlas source not stated.
- Scherer, Kausch et al. 2022, Spine J 22(10):1666-1676, PMID 35584757 [abstract]: a U-Net per vertebra instance trained on 155 CT-navigated cases with 1,052 planned screws (L2 to S1), validated on 24 cases and 130 screws; head 4.61 +/- 2.27 mm, tip 3.96 +/- 2.19 mm, direction 5.51 +/- 3.64 degrees from manual plans; Dice 0.61 +/- 0.16 against inter-rater 0.56 +/- 0.18; diameter thinner by 0.3 +/- 0.6 mm; Gertzbein-Robbins A in 96.2 percent. Private registry, no code statement.
- Scherer, Kausch et al. 2023, J Clin Med 12(7):2646, PMC10094754 [full]: the same nnU-Net tool, integrated into MITK, against Brainlab Elements' atlas planning on 50 private cases, 256 lumbar screws; head 3.93 +/- 2.08 mm, tip 3.49 +/- 1.80 mm, angle 4.46 +/- 2.86 degrees (inter-rater 4.89 mm, 4.36 mm, 5.27 degrees); Gertzbein-Robbins A 97 percent against 65 percent; the atlas tool failed on 34 percent of screws in the most altered anatomy. Data on request only; the tool's intellectual property is held by three authors and no licence is stated, so the public MITK platform does not make the planner public.
- Wang et al. 2024, Bioengineering 11(11):1094, PMC11592283 [full]: AISPINE, a 3D U-Net with edge attention and a Hourglass keypoint model, on 316 PLIF patients (228 training, 88 validation), L2 to S1, one scanner; Dice 0.9459; pedicle transverse and sagittal angles 1.63 +/- 0.83 and 1.39 +/- 1.03 degrees from manual (inter-rater 1.54 +/- 0.84); screws shorter (46.2 against 49.5 mm) and thinner (6.24 against 6.51 mm) than manual, p < 0.001; Gertzbein-Robbins A 97.7 percent, every B at L5. Commercial software, data on request, no code.
- Jia et al. 2023, Frontiers in Surgery 10:1247527, PMC10518392 [full]: Surgiplan, a 3D U-Net, on 45 patients and 208 screws from T10 to L5; the diameter is capped at 90 percent of the pedicle width, but no width is reported; Gertzbein-Robbins A 85.1 percent for planned screws against 64.9 percent for freehand screws read on postoperative CT, an unequal comparison the paper acknowledges. Data "without undue reservation" on request, no code.
- Gotschi et al. 2026, J Spine Surg 12(4):51 [full, publisher HTML]: SpineR (commercial) masks, level-specific template fitting and a multi-objective grid search (safety, HU along the screw, rod alignment) on 20 CTs and 238 screws, T9 to L5, one surgeon's plans as truth; Gertzbein-Robbins A 87.8 percent, median breach 0.8 mm; HU coverage up 74.9 percent; 5.4 min per case. Data sharing statement not readable in the page, no code named.
- Caprara et al. 2022, Comput Methods Biomech Biomed Engin 25(4):464-474, and Tsagkaris et al. 2023, Front Bioeng Biotechnol 11:1270522 [search only]: a genetic algorithm with finite elements on four cadaveric lumbar spines, 40 screws, 26 percent higher simulated pull-out; no code found.
- Bao et al. 2026, PLoS One 21(2):e0342349, PMID 41671245 [abstract], with the IEEE DataPort record "C2-Net database", doi 10.21227/1j3d-6j19 [page] and github.com/Ingram66/C2-NET [page, named by DataPort, not opened]: C2 only; a segmentation module outlines the C2 pedicle and a second module gives a feasibility probability; truth is screws placed by hand in 3D-printed models; accuracy 89.4 percent, sensitivity 90.0, specificity 89.0. The test set (13.42 MB of JPEG) is behind a DataPort subscription; a core script is on GitHub; licence not stated.
- Code without a paper: github.com/nishyrut/PedicleScrewGuide [code], GPL-3.0, TotalSegmentator plus SimpleITK, computes a trajectory and a 3D-printable jig, measures no pedicle, reports no validation, "not been tested widely"; github.com/grotyx/pedicle-screw-simulator [search only], a desktop planner with a Gertzbein-Robbins grader. SonoGym (arXiv 2507.01152 [abstract], NeurIPS 2025, github.com/SonoGym/SonoGym, CC BY 4.0 for the paper) and SafeRPlan (Ao et al., Medical Image Analysis 2024/2025 [search only]) plan an L4 screw from robotic ultrasound in simulation on CT-derived models; the data and code are public but the target is a screw pose from in-house planning software, not a pedicle measure.

### 3. Pedicle labels in public datasets

- VerSe 2019/2020 (github.com/anjany/verse [page]): 374 scans of 355 patients, whole-vertebra masks and centroids, labels for L6 (25) and T13 (28), CC BY-SA 4.0; no pedicle or substructure label (its "seg-subreg" filenames are not explained on the page).
- VID vertebra identification dataset (github.com/ImFusionGmbH/VID-vertebra-identification-dataset [page], LICENSE.txt CC BY-SA 4.0 [page]; Burgin, Prevost, Stollenga 2023, arXiv 2308.02509 [full, arXiv HTML]): three keypoints per vertebra, body, left pedicle and right pedicle, on 2,118 scans (360 VerSe, 1,676 TCIA CT Colonography, 82 TCIA Pancreas), 1,949 training and 169 validation; the pedicle points were "determined through post-processing of ground truth segmentations" on VerSe, the steps not described, then bootstrapped to the other sets; expert-validated ground truth with spine levels; the GNN identifies 93.26 percent (validation) and 93.02 percent (test) on VerSe; training adds synthetic spines missing T13, L6 or S2, and off-by-one errors at the thoracolumbar junction are reported, but transitional vertebrae are not otherwise handled. A pedicle centre is not a width, height or angle, but two pedicle points per level on 2,118 public scans is the largest pedicle truth found.
- LumASe (zenodo.org/records/7181338 [page]), CC BY 4.0: 663 lumbar vertebrae, L1 to L5, cropped from CT of ShengJing Hospital (Philips, Siemens, Toshiba), voxel masks of seven regions by three physicians: superior articular process, body, transverse process, lamina, pedicle, spinous process, inferior articular process; fractures, implants and tumours excluded; no paper, no baseline, no measurements. The only public voxel truth of the pedicle found.
- Da Mutten et al. 2026, J Imaging Inform Med, PMID 42573685, doi 10.1007/s10278-026-02169-7 [abstract, Europe PMC record; Springer refused]: 118 thoracolumbar CTs segmented by hand into corpus, lamina, pedicle, superior and inferior articular processes, transverse and spinous processes; nnU-Net, ResEnc nnU-Net and SwinUNETR in five folds and on an external set of unstated size; external Dice 0.989 +/- 0.023 for the pedicles with the standard nnU-Net (0.980 to 0.993 over the seven regions); a geometric method (Blomenkamp et al., not read) reached 0.382 to 0.889. The record lists no data; the article is subscription-only. A pedicle Dice of 0.99 would bound a width error at well under the slice thickness, but no width is reported.
- TotalSegmentator (github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator [code], Apache-2.0 code and weights for the spine tasks): whole vertebrae in total, bodies without the arch in vertebrae_body, vertebrae_pp and vertebrae_pp_refined; no pedicle, lamina or pars class.
- CTSpine1K, SPIDER, MRSpineSeg, BUU-LSPINE, NHANES II and AASCE carry no pedicle truth: the searches on their names returned only whole-vertebra masks or vertebral-body corners (BUU-LSPINE: 400 AP and lateral pairs with corner coordinates and an LSTV ground truth, request form [search]; NHANES II: 17,000 films with 600 annotated for body corners, Zenodo record 10223910 [search]). Their pages were not fetched in this sweep, since the question was pedicles and none names one.

### 4. Pedicles on radiographs

- Yu et al. 2024, PLoS One 19(6):e0295196, PMC11175444 [full]: transverse pedicle angle (pedicle_angle_transverse) as arctan of the pedicle half-width on the AP view over the pedicle length on the lateral view; 66 patients, L3 to L5, but the radiographs are simulated from CT in 3-matic and the landmarks are placed by hand by one surgeon; against the 3D angle, ICC 0.815 and 0.862 at L3, 0.637 and 0.622 at L4, 0.670 and 0.577 at L5, mean differences about 0.5 to 1 degree; no code, no magnification or positioning effects tested. It is the one formula found that reads a pedicle angle off two films.
- Kim, Wang, Park, Chang 2025, Sci Rep 15:26647, PMID 40695965 [abstract]: a two-stage rotated detector (MMRotate) finds vertebrae then pedicles on 1,080 posteroanterior radiographs of patients aged 4 to 18 (14,608 pedicle crops for training) and grades Nash-Moe rotation from a pedicle width ratio; mAP 0.905 for pedicles, grade accuracy 0.74; private data, no code. It measures a pedicle shadow's width only as a ratio within one vertebra.
- Aubert, Bakhous et al. 2018, SPIE Medical Imaging 10575 [search only]: a CNN regresses pedicle positions on biplanar radiographs of 476 scoliosis and normal cases (380/48/48), cutting axial rotation error by about 10 percent and 3D pedicle error by about 17 percent; EOS data, private. Ebrahimi et al. 2019, Eur Spine J [search only]: a quasi-automated pedicle detector feeding Stokes' rotation method on frontal films of 149 subjects.
- No method was found that measures pedicle width, height or interpedicular distance on a plain AP or lateral radiograph automatically and reports it in millimetres.

### 5. The pars interarticularis

- Achar et al. 2023, Sensors 23(18):8001, PMID 37766055 [abstract; MDPI refused]: deep learning turns ultrashort-echo-time MRI of cadaveric spines into CT-like images with saliency maps at the probable pars defect; bone-to-defect contrast 35 (UTE), 97 (CT-like), 146 (CT); ex vivo only, and the paper's section on the width of pars defects could not be read. A Europe PMC search for the pars with "automatic", "deep learning", "thickness", "morphometry" or "segmentation" returned 71 records, 25 read as titles, of which only this one is an automatic method, and it measures a defect, not pars_w or pars_area.
- No automatic measure of pars width, thickness or cross-sectional area was found on any modality; the pars appears in the segmentation datasets only inside the lamina or the articular processes (LumASe and Da Mutten 2026 have no pars class).

### 6. Inner and outer widths and the cortex

- No automatic method reports pedicle_w_inner, pedicle_h_inner, pedicle_w_outer, pedicle_h_outer or pedicle_cortex. The one source that separates cortex from medullary cavity at the isthmus is a 2019 Folia Morphologica study of 30 cervical-to-lumbar specimens traced by hand in AutoCAD (cortex ratio medial to lateral near 3:1 cervical, 2:1 thoracic, 1:1 lumbar) [search only]. Knez 2016 and 2019 use CT-inferred bone density along the screw, not a cortical boundary.

## What the family's prior art settles and does not

Settled. An automatic measure of pedicle width and height on CT can agree with a manual oblique-plane measure to about half a millimetre (Knez and Vrtovec 2020: 0.44 and 0.56 mm MAD on 540 thoracic pedicles), and a lumbar landmark network reaches ICC 0.97 for width and 0.92 for the transverse angle against three readers (Zhang 2024). Pedicle Dice of 0.99 is reachable with nnU-Net on hand-drawn subregions (Da Mutten 2026). The manual truth itself is loose for the sagittal angle: 18 degrees intra-observer and 12 degrees against the computer in Knez 2018, so pedicle_angle_sagittal and pedicle_incl_trans cannot be judged to a degree by any reader truth found. Automatic screw planners land within 3.5 to 4.6 mm and 4.5 to 5.5 degrees of a surgeon's plan, which is the size of the inter-rater difference (Scherer 2022, 2023; Wang 2024), so the pedicle axis is known to about the precision surgeons agree on.

Not settled. No method found measures the family at every level from C2 to S1 in one person: Knez is thoracic, Zhang, Lee, Scherer, Wang and LumASe are lumbar or lumbosacral, Goerres has three levels, Bao has C2. No method reports a per-vertebra pedicle measure on a public test set: the only public pedicle truths are VID's two keypoints per vertebra on 2,118 scans (positions, not sizes) and LumASe's 663 lumbar voxel masks (no paper, no baseline). No method addresses transitional vertebrae; VerSe carries L6 and T13 labels but no pedicle label, and VID handles missing levels only synthetically. No method separates inner from outer width or measures the cortex automatically; none measures the pars at all. On radiographs nothing automatic gives a pedicle width in millimetres; Yu 2024's arctan formula for the transverse angle is manual and on simulated films, and the scoliosis detectors (Kim 2025, Aubert 2018) read the pedicle shadow only as a position or a ratio. No code that computes a pedicle measure is public under any licence; the public code either plans a screw without measuring (PedicleScrewGuide, SonoGym) or is a fragment behind a subscription (C2-Net). For naming levels from a pedicle measure the field therefore offers a level-by-level pedicle table only from manual series (Panjabi, Lien 2007, Zindrick 1987, Tan 2004, Busscher 2010, Shin 2024, all cited in the 42-paper note) and no automatic measurer validated outside the thoracic (Knez) or lumbar (Zhang) spine.

Two things the family could use at once: VID's pedicle keypoints on VerSe give, with VerSe's whole-vertebra masks, a public starting truth for the pedicle axis (two points) at every labelled level, L6 and T13 included; and LumASe's seven-region lumbar masks give a public truth for a pedicle width and height estimator in L1 to L5, which the parametric model of Knez or an nnU-Net of Da Mutten's kind could be checked against before any grid run.
