## What the paper is

- Design: morphometric study on preoperative CT of consecutive patients at the authors' hospital (p. 1517, PDF 2).
  Inference: retrospective, since the scans were taken for surgical planning; the paper does not use the word.
- Question: how far the conventional transverse pedicle width (TPW) and sagittal pedicle width (SPW) differ from the
  minimum pedicle diameter (MPD) measured in the plane perpendicular to the pedicle axis, and whether an index can
  predict the optimal screw diameter from TPW or SPW (p. 1516-1517, PDF 1-2).
- Population: 50 consecutive living patients, 23 men and 27 women, who had preoperative multiplanar reconstruction
  CT (MPR-CT) for lumbar surgery at the authors' hospital between August 2008 and July 2010 (p. 1517, PDF 2); the
  authors' affiliation is a department of orthopaedic surgery in Kawachinagano, Osaka, Japan (p. 1516, PDF 1).
  Ethnicity is not stated (Inference: Japanese). Ages 52 to 85 years, mean 70.1 at the time of CT; no SD, no
  height, weight or body-size data (p. 1517, PDF 2).
- Diagnoses: 16 lumbar spinal canal stenosis (9 men, 7 women) and 34 lumbar degenerative spondylolisthesis
  (14 men, 20 women) (p. 1517, PDF 2). The slipped level, the stenotic level and any per-diagnosis result are not
  reported.
- Exclusion: spondylolysis, and lumbar scoliosis over 10 deg (p. 1516-1517, PDF 1-2). No other criterion is stated
  (nothing on prior surgery, implants, fracture, osteoporosis or bone density).
- Stated purpose (abstract and introduction, p. 1516-1517, PDF 1-2): the axial TPW is still the recommended sizing
  index although, per the abstract, it is only weakly correlated with the MPD outside the upper lumbar spine; the
  study measures how far TPW and SPW differ from the MPD and builds a regression index from them.
- Denominators: 4 parameters at L1 to L5, measured on 3 planes, 100 pedicles per level, 500 pedicles in all
  (p. 1517, PDF 2). Inference: 100 pedicles per level from 50 patients means both pedicles of every patient at every
  level, ten pedicles per patient; the paper does not say "both sides", and no pedicle is reported missing or
  excluded.
- Modality: GE LightSpeed Plus helical CT; slice thickness 1.25 mm; 120 kVp; 300 mA; window centre 300, window
  width 2,000 (p. 1517, PDF 2). DICOM data reconstructed in Exa Vision LITE (Ziosoft, Tokyo); stated linear precision
  0.01 mm and angular precision 0.01 deg (p. 1517, PDF 2). These are display precisions of the software, not
  accuracy (Inference).
- Statistics: StatView 5.0; Mann-Whitney U test between parameters; significance at p < 0.05; simple linear
  regression of MPD on TPW and on SPW at each level (p. 1517, PDF 2).
- Level identification: not described. How L1 and L5 were found, whether the count ran from the sacrum or from the
  twelfth rib, is not said (p. 1517, PDF 2).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib
  counts or block vertebrae. Inference: since every one of the 50 patients gave exactly 100 pedicles per level
  L1-L5, no case was dropped for a transitional segment, and a transitional L5 (if any) was measured as L5 without
  comment.
- Spondylolysis (pars defect) was an exclusion criterion; its count among screened patients is not given
  (p. 1517, PDF 2).
- Lumbar scoliosis over 10 deg was excluded; how many were screened out is not given (p. 1517, PDF 2).
- Case mix, not a prevalence: 34 of the 50 patients (14 men, 20 women) had lumbar degenerative spondylolisthesis and
  16 (9 men, 7 women) lumbar spinal canal stenosis (p. 1517, PDF 2). The slipped level, the grade of slip and how it
  was diagnosed are not given, and no measure is reported separately for slipped and unslipped levels. These are
  surgical candidates selected for diagnosis, so the 68% is not a population rate of spondylolisthesis.

## Measures, each as a recipe

The reference line for all measures is the pedicle axis: the line that bisects the pedicle isthmus on the axial
and on the sagittal plane (p. 1517, PDF 2). All widths are endosteal, that is inner cortex to inner cortex
(p. 1517, PDF 2; Fig. 1, p. 1518, PDF 3). Fig. 2 (p. 1518, PDF 3) shows three planes on a 3D rendering of a lumbar
vertebra: plane A, an axial plane containing the pedicle axis of the sagittal view; plane B, a sagittal plane; plane
C, the oblique coronal plane perpendicular to both, in which MPD and CI are taken. The caption's wording for plane B
("the sagittal plane including the axial plane") is garbled; Inference: it means the sagittal plane that contains
the pedicle axis drawn on the axial view. TPW and SPW are defined in planes oriented to the vertebra (the axial plane
parallel to the upper endplate, the sagittal plane parallel to the body's anteroposterior midline), while only MPD
and CI use the plane perpendicular to the pedicle axis (p. 1517, PDF 2). The paper does not say whether the TPW and
SPW planes are the same as planes A and B, which contain the pedicle axis; Inference: where the pedicle axis tilts
craniocaudally against the endplate, or converges medially against the midline, they are not.

### Transverse pedicle width (TPW)

- Definition: on an axial plane laid parallel to the upper end-plate, the narrowest inner-cortex (endosteal)
  width of the pedicle, taken at right angles to the pedicle axis (p. 1517, PDF 2; Fig. 1a, p. 1518, PDF 3). Fig. 1a draws the pedicle axis obliquely
  (converging anteromedially) and the width as a double arrow across the pedicle at its narrowest, perpendicular to
  that axis, not along the coronal (left-right) direction.
- Plane: axial MPR parallel to the upper endplate. Instrument: Exa Vision LITE on 1.25 mm helical CT.
- Levels: L1 to L5, 100 pedicles each.
- Values, mean +/- SD in mm (Table 1, p. 1518, PDF 3): L1 5.46 +/- 1.69; L2 5.76 +/- 1.73; L3 7.25 +/- 1.82;
  L4 9.01 +/- 1.91; L5 12.86 +/- 2.47.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: estimate the upper endplate plane from the cranial surface of the body
  subregion (a plane fit); find the pedicle as the narrowest bridge between the body and the posterior-element
  subregion on each side (Inference from memory, not from this paper: SPINEPS has no pedicle label of its own, the
  pedicle falling in the arch subregion next to the body); find the pedicle axis as the line through the midpoints
  of the isthmus in the axial and sagittal projections; then, in the slice of the mask parallel to the endplate
  plane that contains the axis, take the minimum width perpendicular to the axis. A binary bone mask gives the
  periosteal (outer) border; the paper's value is endosteal, so either threshold the cancellous core inside the mask
  by HU or subtract two cortical thicknesses (not given in this paper). Without that, a mask value runs larger than
  the paper's.
- From a radiograph alone: not measured in this paper; earlier studies measured pedicles indirectly on plain
  radiographs (refs 8 and 10, p. 1519, PDF 4). Inference: the AP radiograph shows the pedicle oval, whose transverse
  width is an outer-cortex projection along the beam, not perpendicular to the pedicle axis, and magnified (about
  1.1 to 1.2 without calibration); it is a different quantity from this endosteal TPW, and at L5, where the
  pedicle section is strongly inclined, the projected oval is a poor guide.
- Across levels: rises steadily L1 to L5; L1 and L2 barely differ (0.30 mm), the steps L2-L3 (1.49 mm), L3-L4
  (1.76 mm) and L4-L5 (3.85 mm) grow (Table 1, p. 1518, PDF 3). Inference: adjacent-level separation, as mean
  difference over pooled SD, is about 0.18 (L1-L2), 0.84 (L2-L3), 0.94 (L3-L4) and 1.74 (L4-L5); TPW separates L4
  from L5 well and L1 from L2 not at all.
- Sex, age, size, population: no breakdown by sex, side, age or diagnosis. Elderly degenerative patients at a
  Japanese hospital (presumably Japanese, Inference), ages 52 to 85, mean 70.1 (p. 1517, PDF 2).
- Error: no intra- or inter-observer data; software precision 0.01 mm; slice thickness 1.25 mm (p. 1517, PDF 2).

### Sagittal pedicle width (SPW)

- Definition: on a sagittal plane laid parallel to the body's front-to-back midline, the narrowest
  inner-cortex (endosteal) height of the pedicle, taken at right angles to the pedicle axis (p. 1517, PDF 2; Fig. 1b, p. 1518, PDF 3). Fig. 1b shows a
  parasagittal cut through the pedicle with the pedicle axis drawn horizontal and the width as a vertical double
  arrow. Inference: despite its name, SPW is the endosteal pedicle height (craniocaudal), the quantity other
  authors, Panjabi among them, call pedicle height.
- Plane: parasagittal MPR through the pedicle. Levels L1 to L5.
- Values, mean +/- SD in mm (Table 1, p. 1518, PDF 3): L1 11.89 +/- 1.57; L2 10.44 +/- 1.75; L3 10.23 +/- 1.63;
  L4 9.36 +/- 1.36; L5 8.95 +/- 1.54.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: in the parasagittal slice of the mask that contains the pedicle axis,
  the minimum craniocaudal extent of the pedicle perpendicular to that axis, taken on the endosteal border (HU
  threshold inside the mask) or corrected for cortex as for TPW.
- From a radiograph alone: not measured here. Inference: a lateral radiograph shows the pedicle height between the
  superior and inferior notches, left and right superimposed, outer cortex, magnified; an endosteal value cannot be
  read on it.
- Across levels: falls L1 to L5; the largest step is L1-L2 (1.45 mm), L2 and L3 nearly equal (0.21 mm)
  (Table 1, p. 1518, PDF 3). Inference: adjacent-level separation about 0.87 (L1-L2), 0.12 (L2-L3), 0.58 (L3-L4)
  and 0.28 (L4-L5).
- TPW against SPW (Fig. 3, p. 1519, PDF 4): significant at L1, L2, L3 and L5 (p < 0.0001); no bracket is drawn at
  L4, where the means are 9.01 and 9.36 mm. The text does not report these tests. Inference: the ratio TPW/SPW is
  0.46, 0.55, 0.71, 0.96 and 1.44 from L1 to L5, a shape index that crosses 1 between L4 and L5.
- Sex, age, size, population: no breakdown.
- Error: as TPW; no reliability data.

### Minimum pedicle diameter (MPD)

- Definition: the maximum diameter of the circle whose centre lies on the pedicle axis and which does not cross the
  inner cortex of the pedicle, in the oblique coronal planes perpendicular to the pedicle axis on both the axial and
  sagittal planes (p. 1517, PDF 2; Fig. 1c and plane C of Fig. 2, p. 1518, PDF 3). Fig. 1c draws the MPD as the
  circle's diameter between two tangent lines, the circle sitting inside an inclined oval section. Inference: in
  plane C the pedicle axis is a single point, so the MPD is twice the distance from that point to the nearest inner
  cortex; it is therefore at most the minor (narrowest) width of the section, and it is the largest screw diameter
  that fits a screw driven along the pedicle axis, the assumption the authors state as a limitation (p. 1519-1520,
  PDF 4-5). The text says "planes" and does not state explicitly that the minimum over positions along the axis (the
  isthmus) was taken; the rationale is that the narrowest section of the lumbar pedicle lies on the plane
  perpendicular to the pedicle axis (p. 1516-1517, PDF 1-2, citing Li et al., ref 5), and Li et al. are said to have
  measured the isthmus section perpendicular to the axis (p. 1519, PDF 4).
- Plane: oblique coronal MPR (plane C). Levels L1 to L5.
- Values, mean +/- SD in mm (Table 1, p. 1518, PDF 3): L1 5.09 +/- 1.60; L2 5.39 +/- 1.64; L3 6.52 +/- 1.57;
  L4 6.83 +/- 1.45; L5 7.36 +/- 1.46.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: build the endosteal (cancellous) core of the pedicle inside the mask, fit
  the pedicle axis, step along the axis through the isthmus, and at each axis point take twice the 2D distance, in
  the plane perpendicular to the axis, from the axis point to the nearest endosteal border; the MPD is the minimum
  of that over the isthmus. A 3D Euclidean distance transform of the core, read at the axis points, gives nearly
  the same value. On a periosteal mask the result is larger by about two cortical thicknesses.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible on a standard AP or lateral view (Inference). Inference: a fluoroscopic
  en-face (down-the-pedicle) view with the beam along the pedicle axis projects approximately plane C, so the
  outer-cortex oval seen there approximates the section, magnified and blurred by the pedicle's length; it cannot
  give an endosteal diameter.
- Across levels: rises slowly L1 to L5; L1-L2 0.30 mm, L2-L3 1.13 mm, L3-L4 0.31 mm, L4-L5 0.53 mm (Table 1,
  p. 1518, PDF 3). Inference: adjacent-level separation about 0.19, 0.70, 0.21 and 0.36; MPD alone names no level.
- Against TPW (Fig. 3, p. 1519, PDF 4; text p. 1517, PDF 2): not different at L1 and L2; smaller at L3
  (p = 0.0015), L4 and L5 (p < 0.0001). Mean TPW minus MPD: 0.37, 0.37, 0.73, 2.18 and 5.50 mm from L1 to L5
  (Table 1; the paper rounds the L4 and L5 gaps to 2.2 and 5.5 mm, p. 1519, PDF 4).
- Against SPW: smaller at every level (p < 0.0001) (p. 1517, PDF 2; Fig. 3, p. 1519, PDF 4).
- Regressions of MPD (y) on TPW (x), p < 0.0001 at every level (text p. 1517, PDF 2, for all five levels; only L3,
  L4 and L5 are plotted, Fig. 5a-c, p. 1520, PDF 5):
  L1 y = 0.911x + 0.108, R2 0.932; L2 y = 0.913x + 0.134, R2 0.925; L3 y = 0.813x + 0.631, R2 0.891;
  L4 y = 0.611x + 1.322, R2 0.653; L5 y = 0.395x + 2.281, R2 0.447.
- Regressions of MPD (y) on SPW (x), p < 0.0001 at every level (text p. 1517, PDF 2; only L5 is plotted, Fig. 5d,
  p. 1520, PDF 5):
  L1 y = 0.617x - 2.254, R2 0.368; L2 y = 0.621x - 1.092, R2 0.440; L3 y = 0.642x - 0.041, R2 0.444;
  L4 y = 0.750x - 0.193, R2 0.496; L5 y = 0.776x + 0.416, R2 0.670.
- So TPW predicts MPD well in the upper lumbar spine and poorly at L5, while SPW predicts it best at L5 and
  worst at L1 (R2 above). Inference by calculation (r = sqrt(R2), positive slopes): the within-level correlation of
  MPD with TPW is 0.97, 0.96, 0.94, 0.81 and 0.67 from L1 to L5, and of MPD with SPW 0.61, 0.66, 0.67, 0.70 and 0.82;
  the correlation of TPW with SPW is not reported.
- Scatter (Fig. 5, p. 1520, PDF 5, read from the plots at 300 dpi, approximate): at L3 TPW spans about 1.7 to
  13 mm and MPD about 2 to 12.3 mm, tight about the line (one very small L3 pedicle, TPW about 1.7 and MPD about
  2 mm); at L4 TPW about 4 to 14 mm, MPD about 2.7 to 11 mm; at L5 TPW about 7 to 20 mm and MPD about 4.6 to
  11.2 mm, with two outlying pedicles of TPW about 18 and 20 mm whose MPD is only about 7.4 mm, so the widest L5
  pedicles gain little usable diameter; at L5 SPW spans about 5.4 to 12.4 mm. No L5 pedicle has an MPD below about
  4.5 mm.
- Sex, age, size, population: no breakdown.
- Error: no reliability data; Inference: residual SD of MPD given TPW, from SD(MPD) x sqrt(1 - R2), is about 0.42,
  0.45, 0.52, 0.85 and 1.09 mm from L1 to L5; given SPW about 1.27, 1.23, 1.17, 1.03 and 0.84 mm.

### Cephalocaudal inclination (CI)

- Definition: the angle between the major axis of the pedicle section in plane C (perpendicular to the pedicle
  axis in both the axial and sagittal views) and a normal to the upper end-plate (p. 1517,
  PDF 2; angle alpha in Fig. 1c, p. 1518, PDF 3). 0 deg means the long axis of the oval is craniocaudal (upright);
  larger angles mean the oval lies over towards horizontal. The direction of tilt (medial or lateral) is not
  defined; Inference from the discussion (p. 1519, PDF 4), which puts lower-lumbar perforations at the
  inferomedial and superolateral walls, the nearest walls along the minor axis: the long axis runs superomedial to
  inferolateral. Whether CI is signed is not stated either: Fig. 4's axis runs down to -10 deg and the mean minus
  one SD falls below zero at L1 (about -1.3) and L2 (about -2.6) (Fig. 4, p. 1519, PDF 4; Table 1), which an
  unsigned angle with a skewed spread can also give, so negative readings can be neither assumed nor ruled out.
- Plane: oblique coronal MPR (plane C). Levels L1 to L5.
- Values, mean +/- SD in deg (Table 1, p. 1518, PDF 3): L1 3.47 +/- 4.75; L2 5.01 +/- 7.60; L3 18.04 +/- 9.39;
  L4 39.06 +/- 8.36; L5 51.81 +/- 5.18.
- Level change: each of L3, L4 and L5 differs significantly from the level above (p < 0.0001); L2 does not differ
  from L1 (Fig. 4, p. 1519, PDF 4; text p. 1517, PDF 2, says the increase is significant below L3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: in plane C at the isthmus, take the principal axes of the pedicle section
  (second moments of its pixel coordinates, or a fitted ellipse); project the vertebra's craniocaudal axis (the
  normal of the upper endplate plane fitted to the body subregion) into plane C; CI is the angle between the first
  principal axis and that projected line, folded into 0 to 90 deg. A near-round section (L1, L2) gives an unstable
  major axis; the large SD at L2 (7.60 deg on a mean of 5.01) reflects that.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible on standard AP or lateral views (Inference). Inference: an en-face
  fluoroscopic view along the pedicle axis shows the pedicle oval's inclination directly, outer cortex only.
- Across levels: near zero at L1 and L2, then a steep monotone climb through L3, L4 and L5 (Fig. 4, p. 1519,
  PDF 4). Inference: adjacent-level separation about 0.24 (L1-L2), 1.53 (L2-L3), 2.36 (L3-L4) and 1.83 (L4-L5); CI
  is the best single discriminator of L3, L4 and L5 in this paper, and useless for L1 against L2.
- Sex, age, size, population: no breakdown.
- Error: software angular precision 0.01 deg; no reliability data.

### Screw-diameter index (derived)

- The authors round the TPW regressions into an index of optimal screw diameter y from TPW x: y = 0.9x + 0.1 at
  L1 and L2; y = 0.8x + 0.6 at L3; y = 0.6x + 1.3 at L4; y = 0.4x + 2.3 at L5 (p. 1519, PDF 4). Because the TPW
  fit is weakest at L5, they suggest the SPW form there: y = 0.8x + 0.4 with x the SPW (p. 1519, PDF 4). They call
  this the first regression-based index of its kind (p. 1519, PDF 4), and the conclusions call the study the first
  to relate TPW to an MPD measured on an MPR plane in the lumbar spine (p. 1520, PDF 5). The index predicts the
  mean MPD; it does not subtract any safety margin, and the index was not validated on a separate sample or against
  screw outcomes (Inference: no such test is reported). The authors label y "optimal screw diameter" in the index
  although the fitted y was the MPD, so they equate the two (p. 1517 and 1519, PDF 2 and 4).
- Inference by calculation: at the Table 1 mean TPW the rounded index gives 5.01, 5.28, 6.40, 6.71 and 7.44 mm
  against mean MPDs of 5.09, 5.39, 6.52, 6.83 and 7.36 mm, within about 0.1 mm; the rounded SPW form at the L5 mean
  SPW gives 7.56 mm, 0.2 mm above the mean MPD.
- Error: the index inherits the regression scatter: residual SD of MPD about 0.4 to 0.5 mm at L1 to L3, 0.85 mm at
  L4 and 1.09 mm at L5 from TPW, 0.84 mm at L5 from SPW (Inference, from SD(MPD) x sqrt(1 - R2)).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1 and L2: pedicle section's long axis nearly the sagittal (craniocaudal) axis, CI about 3 to 5 deg; MPD about
  equal to TPW (no significant difference), both about 5 to 6 mm; SPW 10 to 12 mm, so the pedicle is tall and
  narrow (p. 1517-1519, PDF 2-4; Table 1, p. 1518, PDF 3). TPW predicts MPD with R2 0.932 at L1 and 0.925 at L2
  (p. 1517, PDF 2).
- L3: the intermediate level, where the section begins to tilt: CI 18 deg, the first significant rise over the
  level above (Fig. 4, p. 1519, PDF 4); MPD 0.73 mm below TPW (p = 0.0015); TPW still predicts MPD well (R2 0.891)
  (p. 1517, PDF 2; Table 1, p. 1518, PDF 3).
- L4: CI 39 deg; TPW and SPW nearly equal (9.01 and 9.36 mm; the only level with no TPW-SPW bracket in Fig. 3);
  MPD 2.2 mm below TPW; 7 of 67 pedicles (10.4%) with TPW 8 mm or more had a minimum width under 6 mm (p. 1519,
  PDF 4). Inference from the 100 pedicles per level: 67 of 100 L4 pedicles had a TPW of 8 mm or more.
- L5: CI 52 deg; pedicle wider than tall (TPW 12.86, SPW 8.95 mm); MPD 5.5 mm below TPW; 16 of 96 pedicles
  (16.7%) with TPW 8 mm or more had a minimum width under 6 mm (p. 1519, PDF 4); TPW predicts MPD poorly
  (R2 0.447), SPW better (R2 0.670) (p. 1517, PDF 2). Inference from the 100 pedicles per level: 96 of 100 L5
  pedicles had a TPW of 8 mm or more, so at L5 an 8 mm axial width is nearly universal and tells little.
- T12/L1 and L5/S1 junctions: T12 and S1 were not measured; nothing is said about either junction. Inference
  only: the trend suggests T12 behaves like L1 (upright oval, small CI), but the paper gives no data.

## Measurement error and reliability

- No intra-observer or inter-observer study, no repeat measurement, no statement of how many observers measured
  or whether they were blinded (whole paper).
- Stated software precision: 0.01 mm and 0.01 deg (p. 1517, PDF 2). Effective resolution is set by the 1.25 mm
  slice thickness and in-plane pixel size (not given) and by the bone window (centre 300, width 2,000), which
  moves the apparent endosteal edge (Inference).
- The authors cite that CT inner-cortex measurements correlate well with direct cadaveric ones (refs 3 and 4,
  p. 1519, PDF 4) and that MPR-CT pedicle measurement is useful and accurate (refs 5 and 13, p. 1519, PDF 4).
- Inference (statistics): the Mann-Whitney U test is for independent samples, whereas TPW, SPW and MPD are three
  measures of the same pedicle; left and right pedicles of one patient are pooled as 100 independent units per
  level. The p values are therefore not exact, though the large differences at L4 and L5 would survive a paired
  test.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication: preoperative CT planning of lumbar pedicle screw diameter in elderly degenerative patients
  (stenosis, degenerative spondylolisthesis) (p. 1516-1517, PDF 1-2). The introduction sets this in pedicle-screw
  instrumentation as the standard way to fuse or correct deformity in lumbar degenerative disorders such as
  spondylolisthesis and scoliosis, with preoperative CT sizing held to be essential (p. 1516, PDF 1).
- Background the authors rely on: the lumbar pedicle section is oval rather than round, more so in the lower lumbar
  spine, and its major axis is inclined towards horizontal (p. 1516, PDF 1); recent research (ref 14, Sugisaki
  2009, an in vivo 3D study of the isthmus by its title, p. 1521, PDF 6) found significant differences between TPW
  and MPD, and Li et al. (ref 5) held the isthmus section perpendicular to the axis better than the axial plane for
  the narrowest diameter (p. 1519, PDF 4).
- Pitfall: the axial-plane TPW, still the recommended index (refs 1-4), overestimates the usable pedicle diameter
  at L3 to L5 because the section perpendicular to the axis is oval with its long axis inclined towards horizontal
  (p. 1516, PDF 1; p. 1518-1519, PDF 3-4). The abstract warns against sizing screws from "plain CT scans",
  especially in the lower lumbar spine (p. 1516, PDF 1); Inference: "plain" means the unreformatted axial images.
- Why CT at all: the authors note that pedicle morphology has been studied on cadavers, on plain radiographs and on
  CT, and that CT is recommended for choosing screw length and diameter because its inner-cortex measures correlate
  well with direct cadaveric ones (refs 3 and 4) (p. 1519, PDF 4).
- Where the MPD is much smaller than the TPW, a screw sized by TPW can breach the pedicle wall and injure the nerve
  root (p. 1516, PDF 1).
- Pearl: at L1 and L2 the axial TPW is a fair proxy for MPD, since the oval stands nearly upright (p. 1518-1519,
  PDF 3-4).
- Pitfall: a large TPW is no guarantee: 10.4% of L4 and 16.7% of L5 pedicles with TPW 8 mm or more had a minimum
  width under 6 mm (p. 1519, PDF 4).
- Perforation pattern: in the lower lumbar spine perforation tends to occur through the inferomedial or
  superolateral wall because of the inclined long axis; inferomedial breach endangers the nerve root running along
  that wall, superolateral breach weakens pullout strength and load sharing (ref 15) (p. 1519, PDF 4).
- Advice: do not choose the largest screw from the TPW in the lower lumbar spine (p. 1519, PDF 4); use the index
  above, and at L5 the SPW form (p. 1519, PDF 4).
- Pitfall (limitation): the MPD assumes a screw along the pedicle axis; a different entry point or trajectory
  meets a smaller effective diameter, so a smaller screw is needed (p. 1519-1520, PDF 4-5).
- Measuring the MPD for each pedicle is the ideal but costs effort in reconstruction and measurement (p. 1519,
  PDF 4).
- Degeneration: the cohort is degenerative by design and the title names it, but the paper never compares with
  normal spines, never analyses by diagnosis or by slipped level, and never names a degenerative change (osteophyte,
  facet hypertrophy, endplate sclerosis) as a source of measurement error. Inference: in degenerative spines the
  upper endplate used to orient the axial plane and the CI reference may be irregular or tilted by
  spondylolisthesis, which would shift TPW and CI; the paper does not test this. Inference: because spondylolysis
  and scoliosis over 10 deg were excluded, isthmic spondylolisthesis and degenerative scoliosis, common in the same
  age group, are not represented, and these norms should not be carried to them without checking. The paper cites a
  CT comparison of pedicle morphology across degenerative diseases (Nojiri 2005, ref 12, p. 1520, PDF 5) only in a
  list of CT studies and does not discuss its findings (p. 1519, PDF 4).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Before any pedicle measure, define the pedicle axis on each side as the line bisecting the isthmus in both the
   axial and the sagittal view; every width is taken perpendicular to it (p. 1517, PDF 2).
2. Orient the reference planes to the vertebra, not to the scanner: the axial plane for TPW parallel to the upper
   endplate, the sagittal plane for SPW parallel to the anteroposterior midline of the body, and the CI reference
   line perpendicular to the upper endplate (p. 1517, PDF 2). Inference: on a lordotic lumbar spine, scanner axes
   would fold each level's tilt into TPW, SPW and CI.
3. To measure the true narrowest pedicle diameter, cut the plane perpendicular to the pedicle axis (plane C, Fig. 2,
   p. 1518, PDF 3) and take the largest circle centred on the axis inside the inner cortex (p. 1517, PDF 2); place
   the cut at the isthmus (Inference from the axis definition and the narrowest-section rationale, p. 1516-1517,
   PDF 1-2; the paper does not say how the position along the axis was chosen). Do not substitute the axial width
   at L3 to L5 (p. 1517-1519, PDF 2-4).
4. Measure endosteally (inner cortex); when working from a periosteal bone mask, threshold the cancellous core or
   correct for two cortical thicknesses before comparing with this paper's values (p. 1517, PDF 2; Inference for
   the correction).
5. In plane C, take the long axis of the pedicle section and its angle to the line perpendicular to the upper
   endplate (CI) (p. 1517, PDF 2; Fig. 1c, p. 1518, PDF 3). Expect about 3.5 and 5 deg at L1 and L2, 18 at L3, 39
   at L4 and 52 at L5, each of L3, L4 and L5 significantly above the level over it (Table 1, p. 1518, PDF 3;
   Fig. 4, p. 1519, PDF 4). Inference: use it as a level cue for L3 to L5; the paper itself does not propose CI
   for naming levels. Report CI as an unsigned angle unless a side convention is fixed, since the paper defines
   none (Gaps).
6. Inference from Table 1 (p. 1518, PDF 3): use the shape of the pedicle as a second cue. TPW/SPW rises from about
   0.46 at L1 through about 1 at L4 to 1.44 at L5, so a pedicle wider than tall with a steep CI points to L5, and a
   pedicle about as wide as tall with CI near 40 deg to L4.
7. Do not try to tell L1 from L2 by these pedicle measures: TPW and MPD differ by 0.30 mm and CI by 1.5 deg, and
   Fig. 4 marks no significant CI difference between L1 and L2 (Table 1, p. 1518, PDF 3; Fig. 4, p. 1519, PDF 4);
   SPW differs by about 1.45 mm but was not tested between levels. Inference: name L1 and L2 from ribs, transverse
   processes or the count instead.
8. Expect MPD to sit close to TPW at L1-L2 and far below it at L4-L5 (mean gaps 2.2 and 5.5 mm, p. 1519, PDF 4).
   Inference for the check: compare a mask-derived MPD with the level's regression prediction from its own TPW
   (Measures, MPD), not with TPW itself, since the L5 gap grows with TPW: small L5 pedicles (TPW about 7 to 8 mm)
   have MPD about 5 to 6 mm in Fig. 5c (p. 1520, PDF 5); a value far above the prediction at a candidate L5
   (more than about 2 residual SD, 2 x 1.09 mm) questions the level or the axis.
9. When only TPW is at hand, predict MPD with the level's regression and allow the residual spread (about 0.4 mm at
   L1 rising to about 1.1 mm at L5, Inference from R2 and SD); at L5 prefer the SPW regression (p. 1517 and 1519,
   PDF 2 and 4).
10. Flag a lower-lumbar pedicle with TPW of 8 mm or more as possibly narrow (under 6 mm) in plane C: 7 of 67 (10.4%)
    at L4, 16 of 96 (16.7%) at L5 (p. 1519, PDF 4).
11. When checking a planned or placed screw at the lower lumbar levels, look first at the inferomedial wall (nerve
    root at risk) and the superolateral wall (loss of pullout strength), where the inclined section makes breaches
    likeliest (p. 1519, PDF 4).
12. On radiographs, do not report MPD or CI from a plain AP or lateral film; only an en-face view along the pedicle
    axis approximates plane C, and then only the outer cortex (Inference; the paper measured none on radiographs).
13. Treat these values as elderly degenerative norms from one Japanese hospital (ages 52 to 85, mean 70.1, 23 men and
    27 women, 34 with degenerative spondylolisthesis and 16 with stenosis), with spondylolysis and lumbar scoliosis
    over 10 deg excluded (p. 1517, PDF 2); Inference: do not apply them to young, non-Asian, isthmic or scoliotic
    spines without a size adjustment and a check.
14. Where the trajectory or entry point departs from the pedicle axis, report that the usable diameter is smaller
    than the MPD (p. 1519-1520, PDF 4-5).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13, rib anomalies or
spondylolysis; spondylolysis and scoliosis over 10 deg were excluded without counts (p. 1517, PDF 2). The 34 of 50
with degenerative spondylolisthesis and 16 of 50 with stenosis are a surgical case mix, not population rates, and
the slipped level is not given (p. 1517, PDF 2).

(b) Measures:
- TPW and SPW correspond to pedicle width and pedicle height in the generator (Inference), but here they are
  endosteal and taken perpendicular to the pedicle axis in planes oriented to the upper endplate. Inference from
  memory, not checked against the PDFs: Panjabi's lumbar pedicle width and height were external (outer-cortex)
  dimensions of cleaned cadaveric vertebrae, so they should exceed these values by about two cortical thicknesses;
  a periosteal SPINEPS mask will likewise read larger than this paper. Calibrate one to the other before pooling.
  The cohort here is also elderly (mean 70.1) and degenerative, unlike a mixed cadaver series.
- MPD (minimum diameter in the plane perpendicular to the pedicle axis) and CI (inclination of the long axis of the
  pedicle section) are candidates to add. CI is the strongest level discriminator for L3/L4/L5 in this paper
  (Inference: separation about 1.5 to 2.4 pooled SD between adjacent levels) and is cheap to compute from a mask's
  pedicle cross-section. It is nearly blind between L1 and L2.
- Inference from memory: CI is not Panjabi's sagittal (or transverse) pedicle inclination. Those angles give the
  direction of the pedicle axis itself; CI is the roll of the cross-section about that axis, the tilt of the oval's
  long axis within plane C. A generator that holds only pedicle-axis angles has no CI, and a mask feature named
  "pedicle inclination" must say which of the two it is.
- Inference by calculation: within a level, MPD correlates with TPW at r = 0.97, 0.96, 0.94, 0.81 and 0.67 from L1
  to L5, and with SPW at r = 0.61, 0.66, 0.67, 0.70 and 0.82 (from the R2 on p. 1517, PDF 2); a generator that adds
  MPD should draw it conditional on TPW (or SPW at L5) with these correlations rather than independently.
- Inference: a single-ellipse model of the section, with MPD as the minor axis, does not reproduce the L5 means
  jointly (a central horizontal chord of 12.86 mm at CI 51.8 deg needs a minor axis of at least about 7.95 mm,
  against a mean MPD of 7.36 mm). The argument is rough: it puts means into a nonlinear relation and assumes the
  TPW plane passes through the axis point of plane C. Still, the MPD circle is constrained to the axis and the
  sections need not be ellipses, so draw MPD from the level-wise regressions on TPW (or SPW at L5) rather than
  deriving it geometrically.
- The TPW axial plane is parallel to the upper endplate, and the CI reference is the normal to that endplate; a
  mask-based implementation must use the same reference, not the scanner axes, or CI will absorb the lordosis of
  each level (Inference). In a degenerative spine the upper endplate itself may be irregular, and a slip tilts it
  against its neighbours; the paper does not test either (Inference).

(c) Measurement error to use: the paper gives none. Inference: use at least the slice thickness (1.25 mm) as a
scale for edge uncertainty on width measures, and treat CI at L1-L2 as poorly determined (SD 4.75 and 7.60 deg on
means of 3.5 and 5.0 deg). Residual SD of MPD given TPW (Inference): 0.42, 0.45, 0.52, 0.85, 1.09 mm at L1-L5;
given SPW 1.27, 1.23, 1.17, 1.03, 0.84 mm. The Table 1 SDs (p. 1518, PDF 3) are between-pedicle spreads of 100
pedicles from 50 patients, with whatever measurement error there is folded in; they are population spreads, not
error terms.

(d) Covariates: none reported by sex, age, height or weight; the cohort is 23 men and 27 women aged 52 to 85,
mean 70.1, degenerative, presumably Japanese (p. 1517, PDF 2). The SDs in Table 1 (p. 1518, PDF 3) mix men and
women and both sides. For population priors the reference list points to pedicle studies in Korean (ref 7), Indian
(refs 2 and 8), Japanese (refs 9 and 12) and Asian (ref 11) series (titles, p. 1520, PDF 5), but the paper draws no
comparison between populations.

## Gaps and conflicts

- No reliability study, no observer count, no blinding; no pixel size; no per-side or per-sex results; no results
  by diagnosis or by slipped level, so the "degenerative" in the title is a population label, not a tested factor.
- No account of level identification or of transitional segments.
- MPD is defined over "planes" perpendicular to the axis; whether the minimum along the axis was taken is implied
  by the isthmus rationale but not stated (p. 1516-1517, PDF 1-2).
- The 10.4% and 16.7% counts speak of a "minimum pedicle width" of under 6 mm (p. 1519, PDF 4); Inference: this
  means the MPD.
- The text says CI increases significantly "below L3" (p. 1517, PDF 2), while Fig. 4 (p. 1519, PDF 4) marks L3
  itself as different from L2; the figure is the more specific statement.
- TPW against SPW comparisons appear only in Fig. 3 (p. 1519, PDF 4), not in the text or tables.
- The MPD against TPW comparisons at L1 and L2 are reported only as "not different", with no p value (p. 1517,
  PDF 2). The abstract gives the significant differences as p < 0.01 (p. 1516, PDF 1), coarser than the results'
  p = 0.0015 and p < 0.0001 (p. 1517, PDF 2); this is rounding, not a conflict.
- No pixel size, field of view, reconstruction kernel or MPR slab thickness; no description of how the isthmus and
  the pedicle axis were placed by hand, of who measured, or of whether left and right differed. The sign of CI
  (medial or lateral tilt of the long axis) is not defined, nor whether CI was recorded as a signed angle (Fig. 4's
  axis runs to -10 deg, p. 1519, PDF 4). The age SD is not given.
- The authors admit one limitation only, the assumption that the screw follows the pedicle axis (p. 1519-1520,
  PDF 4-5). Inference: unmentioned are the single centre, the consecutive surgical (not population) sample, the
  absence of a normal or younger comparison group, the absence of any reliability study and the untested index.
- The Fig. 2 caption describes plane B as the sagittal plane "including the axial plane" (p. 1518, PDF 3), which
  cannot be meant literally; Inference: the sagittal plane containing the pedicle axis drawn on the axial view.
- No correction for multiple comparisons and no sample-size reasoning are stated (p. 1517, PDF 2).
- The pullout-strength consequence of a superolateral breach is supported by ref 15 (Brasiliense 2010), whose
  title names the thoracic spine (p. 1521, PDF 6); Inference: the lumbar claim rests on a thoracic study.
- The abstract's L1 to L5 TPW/SPW/MPD triplets (p. 1516, PDF 1) match Table 1 (p. 1518, PDF 3) exactly.
- Internal checks pass: each regression line passes through the Table 1 means, and slope = sqrt(R2) x SD(MPD) /
  SD(x) holds at every level, so Table 1's SDs and the regressions agree (Inference, by calculation). The rounded
  differences 2.2 and 5.5 mm (p. 1519, PDF 4) match Table 1; 7/67 = 10.4% and 16/96 = 16.7% are correct.
- Inference from memory of other papers in this set: Li et al. 2004 (ref 5) and Sugisaki et al. 2009 (ref 14)
  make the same point that the isthmus perpendicular to the axis is narrower than the axial width (this paper
  cites ref 5 for it on p. 1516-1517 and p. 1519, PDF 1-2 and 4, and ref 14 on p. 1519, PDF 4, for significant
  TPW-MPD differences); Zindrick 1987 (ref 10) and Panjabi 1992 report transverse pedicle widths that rise from L1
  to L5, the same direction as here but larger in absolute value, probably because they are outer-cortex measures.
  These are from memory and not checked against the PDFs here.
- Statistical choice: unpaired Mann-Whitney on paired measures, and pedicles pooled across sides (Inference).
