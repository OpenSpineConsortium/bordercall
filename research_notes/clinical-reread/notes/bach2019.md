## What the paper is

- Design: cross-sectional normative imaging study; the authors call the collection prospective, of anonymized CTs,
  after University of South Florida IRB approval (p. e107, PDF 2).
- Purpose: normal values for lumbar disc height by level, sex and decade, to guide interbody cage height (p. e106,
  PDF 1). Motivation: oversized cages raise distractive and compressive endplate loads and predispose to subsidence,
  body fracture, loss of correction, failure and revision (p. e106, PDF 1, refs 1-7). The authors list restoring disc
  height among the main goals of spine surgery, beside decompression, sagittal and coronal balance and fusion (p. e106,
  PDF 1), and claim the study is the first and most extensive of its kind and the largest of its kind (p. e110, PDF 5;
  p. e114, PDF 9).
- Population: 240 anonymized abdominal CT scans, 131 women and 109 men, of living patients, collected at the University
  of South Florida (Tampa, Florida, USA) from January 2010 to August 2012 (p. e107, PDF 2; the abstract also says
  abdominal CT, p. e106, PDF 1). Ethnicity is not reported. Inclusion: people aged 10-80 years presenting with a
  non-spinal complaint in whom a thoracic and lumbar CT was obtained (p. e107, PDF 2). Exclusion: every CT was reviewed
  for spinal pathology and people with "incidental spinal pathologies" were excluded (p. e107, PDF 2), with no list of
  what counted and no count of how many were excluded.
- Table 6 gives N = 109 men and N = 131 women for every measure at every level (p. e113, PDF 8). Inference from
  arithmetic: the decade means of Tables 2-5 weighted by the Table 1 counts reproduce the Table 6 means to within
  0.04 mm, except where a decade cell is itself a slip (see Gaps), so each subject seems to have contributed all six
  discs; the pooled Table 6 values include the 16 teenagers (8 per sex) of the first decade.
- Ages as printed, which disagree with each other (see Gaps): abstract 45 (14-83) years for women and 48 (14-89) for
  men (p. e106, PDF 1); results 45 (14-79) for women and 47 (14-79) for men (p. e108, PDF 3); methods 10-80 years
  (p. e107, PDF 2).
- Decade groups (Table 1, p. e108, PDF 3), women / men: 10s 8/8, 20s 25/19, 30s 18/17, 40s 23/8, 50s 25/23,
  60s 17/22, 70s 15/12; totals 131/109 (the columns sum correctly). The disc tables call these the "First" to
  "Seventh Decade" (Tables 2-5); Inference: "First Decade" is the 10-19 group, not ages 0-9, since the youngest
  subject is 14.
- Height, weight and BMI were available for only 81 women and 69 men (p. e109, PDF 4; Tables 9-11, pp. e115-e117,
  PDF 10-12). That subset: women 46.5 +/- 17.8 years, 163.8 +/- 6.93 cm, 76.2 +/- 17.5 kg, BMI 28.4 +/- 6.13; men
  48.5 +/- 19.3 years, 179.1 +/- 6.71 cm, 90 +/- 20.2 kg, BMI 27.9 +/- 5.4; men taller and heavier (P < 0.0005 each),
  age and BMI not different (P 0.502, 0.66) (Table 11, p. e117, PDF 12). The subject lists of Tables 9 and 10 run from
  18 to 77 years (women) and 18 to 80 years (men); a recount of their rows on the page images gives 81 and 69 subjects,
  and their means reproduce Table 11 (women 46.5 years, 163.8 cm). No one under 18 is in this subset. Inference: about
  nine heights in ten are whole inches converted to cm (157.5, 160, 162.6, 165.1, 167.6, ...), so stature was probably
  recorded or self-reported in inches, not measured. Inference from arithmetic: the printed BMI disagrees with the
  printed weight and height by more than 1 kg/m2 in at least five rows of each table (for example a woman of 41 years,
  156.2 cm, 97.8 kg, BMI printed 21.6 where 40.1 follows; Table 9, p. e115, PDF 10).
- Modality and instrument: CT data modelled in Mimics Innovation Suite 19.0 (Materialise, Leuven, Belgium); bone
  thresholded at 226-3071 HU; hand segmentation tools isolated each vertebral body by cutting the superior and inferior
  articular processes away from the adjacent body, and the 12th rib from T12 (p. e107, PDF 2). Scanner, slice
  thickness, reconstruction kernel and voxel size are not reported.
- Observer: one dedicated neuroradiologist, blinded to age and sex, reviewed every CT and made every measurement
  (p. e107, PDF 2). No repeat measurement and no second observer.
- Levels: six discs, T12/L1, L1/2, L2/3, L3/4, L4/5, L5/S1 (Tables 2-8). How the levels were identified and counted is
  not stated. Figure 1C (p. e107, PDF 2) shows the measured model as six bodies above the sacrum with points at six
  disc spaces; Inference: T12 to L5 over S1, a 12-thoracic, 5-lumbar column.
- Statistics: independent t-tests by sex per measure; a paired t-test of left against right foraminal height (its
  result is never reported); Pearson correlation with age, strength graded weak for R 0.1-0.39, moderate 0.4-0.59,
  strong > 0.6 (after Evans 1996, ref 15); height, weight and BMI by sex (t-test) and against age (Pearson); SPSS 24.0,
  P < 0.05 (pp. e107-e108, PDF 2-3; footnotes of Tables 7-8, p. e114, PDF 9).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib-count
  variants or block vertebrae: no prevalence, no handling rule, no statement of how levels were counted.
- The only numbering-related detail: the 12th rib was cut away from T12 during segmentation (p. e107, PDF 2).
  Inference: T12 was the vertebra carrying the 12th rib, so levels were probably counted with the usual 12 thoracic and
  5 lumbar convention (Figure 1C shows six bodies above the sacrum); whether a transitional spine was excluded as
  "spinal pathology" or measured silently under that convention cannot be told.
- Inference (prevalence from memory, not from this paper): since roughly one person in ten to one in five carries a
  transitional lumbosacral segment in most series, the "L5/S1" row may include some L5/L6 discs or discs above a
  sacralised L5. The L5/S1 anterior height has the widest SD of Table 6 (2.59 men, 2.94 women; p. e113, PDF 8), which
  such mixing would produce, though variation of lumbosacral lordosis would produce it too.

## Measures, each as a recipe

All disc measures are taken on the Mimics 3D surface model of thresholded bone, on points placed in the midsagittal
plane (Figure 1A-C, p. e107, PDF 2). The methods name only three midsagittal points, the "anterior edge, center, and
posterior edge of each vertebra" (p. e107, PDF 2); that the points lie on the two facing endplates is read from
Figure 1A. The figure shows, on a lateral view of two adjacent bodies, three points on the inferior endplate of the
upper body and three facing points on the superior endplate of the lower body, plus, as read from the figure, a point
under the upper pedicle and one on the lower pedicle; on the superior view of an endplate, three midline points
(posterior rim, centre, anterior rim) and one point on each pedicle base (Figure 1A-B); and, on a lateral view of the
whole model, the same points at all six disc spaces (Figure 1C). The caption only says these are points used to
measure heights. Whether each height is the straight distance between the two facing points or a distance
perpendicular to the disc plane is not stated.

### Anterior disc height (no abbreviation)

- Landmarks: the anterior edge of the inferior endplate of the cranial body and the anterior edge of the superior
  endplate of the caudal body, in the midsagittal plane (p. e107, PDF 2; the endplate assignment is read from
  Figure 1A).
- Plane: midsagittal; instrument: point placement on a 3D CT bone model; levels T12/L1 to L5/S1.
- Values, all ages pooled, men vs women (mm, mean +/- SD; Table 6, p. e113, PDF 8): T12/L1 6.29 +/- 1.49 vs
  4.81 +/- 1.09; L1/2 7.59 +/- 1.58 vs 5.92 +/- 1.27; L2/3 8.70 +/- 1.67 vs 7.13 +/- 1.51; L3/4 9.66 +/- 1.75 vs
  8.11 +/- 1.62; L4/5 10.94 +/- 2.15 vs 9.82 +/- 2.28; L5/S1 11.92 +/- 2.59 vs 11.36 +/- 2.94. Sex P: 0.000 at every
  level except L5/S1 (0.123).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: take the two consecutive vertebral body instances (body subregion if the segmenter gives
  one, else the whole instance cut anterior to the pedicles). Inference: find the vertebra's own midsagittal plane
  (left-right symmetry plane through the body centroid), take on that plane the anteriormost point of the caudal
  surface of the cranial body and of the cranial surface of the caudal body (the ring apophysis rims), and take the
  distance between them; report the cranio-caudal component along the mean of the two bodies' long axes as well, since
  the paper does not say which. An intervertebral disc label, where given, can be measured at its anterior margin the
  same way.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, anterior rim to anterior rim; magnification must be corrected or a
  ratio used (Inference; the paper only says radiograph-based disc heights need complex corrections, p. e106, PDF 1).
  An AP radiograph cannot give it.
- Across levels: rises steadily and monotonically from T12/L1 to L5/S1 in both sexes (Table 6); of the four directly
  measured heights (anterior, middle, posterior, FH) it is the only one that keeps rising at L5/S1, which carries the
  AP average up with it. Inference (from Table 6 means and SDs): adjacent levels differ by 1.0-1.7 mm,
  about 0.4-0.9 pooled SD, so it orders levels on average but does not separate any single adjacent pair reliably.
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger at every level, by a ratio (men/women) of 1.31 at T12/L1 falling to 1.22
  (L2/3), 1.11 (L4/5) and 1.05 (L5/S1) (Inference: ratios computed from Table 6). Age, men: no correlation except L4/5
  (R -0.202, P 0.035) (Table 7, p. e114, PDF 9). Age, women: weak negative at L3/4 (R -0.265), L4/5 (-0.387) and L5/S1
  (-0.388), none above (Table 8, first column, p. e114, PDF 9; that column is mislabelled, see Gaps). Women's lower
  lumbar anterior heights fall between the 30s and the 40s: L4/5 11.33 +/- 1.8 to 9.06 +/- 1.6, L5/S1 13.8 +/- 2.4 to
  10.32 +/- 2.1 (Table 3, p. e111, PDF 6). Not tested against body height.
- Error: not reported. Between-subject SD 1.1-2.9 mm; coefficient of variation about 18-26 percent (Inference from
  Table 6).

### Middle disc height (centre)

- Landmarks: the centre of the inferior endplate of the cranial body and the centre of the superior endplate of the
  caudal body, midsagittal (p. e107, PDF 2; Figure 1B shows the centre point midway between the anterior and posterior
  rim points).
- Values, men vs women (Table 6, p. e113, PDF 8): T12/L1 6.66 +/- 1.34 vs 6.13 +/- 1.04 (P 0.001); L1/2 8.20 +/- 1.82 vs
  7.18 +/- 1.22; L2/3 9.79 +/- 1.87 vs 8.51 +/- 1.46; L3/4 10.41 +/- 1.86 vs 9.16 +/- 1.54; L4/5 10.64 +/- 1.76 vs
  9.70 +/- 1.79 (P 0.000 for these four); L5/S1 9.34 +/- 2.12 vs 9.13 +/- 2.00 (P 0.444).
- Plane and instrument as for anterior height; levels T12/L1 to L5/S1.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: on the midsagittal plane, the gap between the two bodies at the
  antero-posterior midpoint of the endplate; because the bony endplate is concave, this point sits in the endplate's
  dome and the value includes both concavities. Use the outer bone surface (the 226 HU boundary), not the cortical
  shell's inner face; an endplate subregion, if the segmenter labels one, belongs to the bone side.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: a lateral radiograph projects the endplate rims, not the midsagittal dome, so a
  radiographic "middle" height is not the same quantity; it can be approximated only as the gap between the projected
  endplate lines at their midpoint, which underestimates this CT value where the endplate is concave. Not available on
  AP.
- Across levels: rises from T12/L1 to a peak at L4/5 (men 10.64, women 9.70), with L3/4 almost as high, then falls at
  L5/S1. Middle exceeds anterior from T12/L1 to L3/4 (middle/anterior ratio 1.06-1.13 men, 1.13-1.27 women), is about
  equal at L4/5 (0.97, 0.99) and is well below anterior at L5/S1 (0.78, 0.80) (Inference: ratios from Table 6).
  Inference: the step L2/3 to L3/4 to L4/5 is 0.1-0.45 SD, so the middle height cannot tell these three discs apart.
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger by 9-15 percent at T12/L1 to L4/5, 2 percent and not significant at L5/S1
  (Inference from Table 6). Age, men: positive at T12/L1 (R 0.403, moderate, P 0.000), L1/2 (0.341, P 0.000) and L2/3
  (0.258, P 0.007), none at L3/4 to L5/S1 (Table 7, p. e114, PDF 9); the men's T12/L1 middle height rises from
  5.63 +/- 1.9 (10s) to 7.48 +/- 1 (70s) (Table 2, p. e110, PDF 5). Age, women: weak positive only at L1/2 (R 0.195,
  P 0.025) (Table 8, p. e114, PDF 9).
- Error: not reported. CV about 16-23 percent (Inference from Table 6).

### Posterior disc height

- Landmarks: posterior edge of the inferior endplate of the cranial body to posterior edge of the superior endplate of
  the caudal body, midsagittal (p. e107, PDF 2; endplate assignment read from Figure 1A). Plane and instrument as for
  anterior height; levels T12/L1 to L5/S1.
- Values, men vs women (Table 6, p. e113, PDF 8): T12/L1 3.75 +/- 1.00 vs 3.29 +/- 0.82 (P 0.000); L1/2 4.96 +/- 1.27
  vs 4.31 +/- 0.92 (P 0.000); L2/3 5.67 +/- 1.45 vs 5.14 +/- 1.04 (P 0.001); L3/4 5.90 +/- 1.50 vs 5.59 +/- 1.22
  (P 0.079); L4/5 6.05 +/- 1.42 vs 5.98 +/- 1.41 (P 0.705); L5/S1 5.27 +/- 1.38 vs 5.23 +/- 1.43 (P 0.851).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: on the midsagittal plane, the posteriormost points of the two facing body
  surfaces at the canal side, excluding the pedicle bases and any posterior osteophyte; the cut between body and
  posterior elements must not leave pedicle in the body mask.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, posterior rim to posterior rim (Inference). Not available on AP.
- Across levels: smallest at T12/L1, rises to L4/5 (peak), falls at L5/S1; L2/3, L3/4 and L4/5 lie within 0.4 mm of
  each other in men (5.67, 5.90, 6.05) and within 0.85 mm in women (5.14, 5.59, 5.98) (Table 6).
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger only at T12/L1 to L2/3; no sex difference at L3/4 to L5/S1 (Table 6). Age,
  men: weak positive at T12/L1 (R 0.193, P 0.044), weak negative at L4/5 (R -0.246, P 0.010) (Table 7). Age, women:
  negative at L1/2 (R -0.186, P 0.033), L2/3 (-0.315), L3/4 (-0.424, moderate) and L4/5 (-0.248) (Table 8, p. e114,
  PDF 9); women's L3/4 posterior height falls from 6.2 +/- 1 (20s) to 4.56 +/- 1 (70s) (Table 3, p. e111, PDF 6).
- Error: not reported. CV about 20-27 percent, the largest relative spread of the disc measures (Inference).

### Average disc height (three-point mean)

- Definition: the mean of anterior, middle and posterior heights (p. e107, PDF 2).
- Values as printed in the abstract, men vs women (p. e106, PDF 1; repeated on p. e108, PDF 3): T12/L1 5.6 +/- 1.1 vs
  4.8 +/- 0.8; L1/2 6.9 +/- 1.3 vs 5.8 +/- 0.9; L2/3 8.1 +/- 1.4 vs 6.9 +/- 1.1; L3/4 8.7 +/- 1.5 vs 7.6 +/- 1.2;
  L4/5 9.2 +/- 1.6 vs 8.5 +/- 1.6; L5/S1 8.8 +/- 1.6 vs 8.6 +/- 1.8. Figure 8 (p. e113, PDF 8) plots the same means:
  men 5.66, 6.94, 8.08, 8.72, 9.27, 8.90; women 4.73, 5.84, 6.94, 7.61, 8.46, 8.57.
- Across levels: men peak at L4/5; women are flat from L4/5 to L5/S1, numerically highest at L5/S1 (Figure 8).
- Sex: disc height smaller in women at every level except L5/S1, the text says P < 0.001 (p. e108, PDF 3); but see
  Gaps.
- Age: in men weak positive at T12/L1 (R 0.265, P 0.005) and L1/2 (0.211, P 0.027) (Table 7); in women weak negative
  at L3/4 (-0.304), L4/5 (-0.291) and L5/S1 (-0.252) (Table 8, p. e114, PDF 9). By decade: Tables 2-3 "Average" rows
  (p. e110-e111, PDF 5-6) and Figures 2-7 (p. e108-e109, PDF 3-4).
- Error: not reported; between-subject SD as in the abstract, 0.8-1.8 mm (p. e106, PDF 1).
- From a 3D CT instance mask or a lateral radiograph: the mean of the three heights above, so it inherits the middle
  height's dependence on the endplate concavity (Inference).
- Caution: the Table 6 row labelled "Average disc height" is not this quantity. Inference from arithmetic: every Table 6
  "Average" equals (anterior + posterior)/2 of the same table (men T12/L1 (6.29 + 3.75)/2 = 5.02; women L5/S1
  (11.36 + 5.23)/2 = 8.30), while the three-point means computed from Table 6 reproduce the abstract to one decimal
  (men L4/5 9.21, women L5/S1 8.57) and Figure 8 to within 0.1 mm (the men's Figure 8 points run 0.02-0.09 mm above
  the computed means, for example T12/L1 5.66 against 5.57). The Table 6 "Average" row is therefore the AP average.
  In the decade tables (Tables 2-3) the rows are labelled correctly: "Average" is the three-point mean and "AP Avg"
  the anterior-posterior mean, cell for cell, apart from the slips listed under Gaps.
- The abstract's SDs for the three-point average (1.1, 0.8 at T12/L1 and so on) lie within 0.1 mm of the Table 6 SDs
  of the AP average at every level; no table gives the SD of the three-point mean, so they cannot be checked
  (Inference). The abstract's women's T12/L1 mean of 4.8 mm sits beside Figure 8's 4.73 and a mean of 4.74 computed
  from Table 6 (p. e106, PDF 1; p. e113, PDF 8).

### Anterior-posterior average disc height (AP Avg)

- Definition: mean of anterior and posterior heights only (p. e107, PDF 2; footnote of Tables 2-3).
- Values, men vs women (Table 6 row labelled "Average", p. e113, PDF 8; see the caution above): T12/L1 5.02 +/- 1.13 vs
  4.05 +/- 0.81; L1/2 6.28 +/- 1.26 vs 5.12 +/- 0.95; L2/3 7.18 +/- 1.39 vs 6.14 +/- 1.10; L3/4 7.78 +/- 1.44 vs
  6.85 +/- 1.21; L4/5 8.50 +/- 1.61 vs 7.90 +/- 1.62 (P 0.005); L5/S1 8.59 +/- 1.65 vs 8.30 +/- 1.87 (P 0.198); P 0.000
  at T12/L1 to L3/4.
- From a 3D CT instance mask or a lateral radiograph: Inference: this is the measure closest to what a lateral
  radiograph gives, since it uses only the rims.
- Across levels: rises monotonically T12/L1 to L5/S1 in both sexes (Table 6).
- Age: men weak negative at L4/5 only (R -0.243, P 0.010); women negative at L2/3 (-0.221), L3/4 (-0.390), L4/5
  (-0.380), L5/S1 (-0.320) (Tables 7-8, p. e114, PDF 9). Men's L4/5 AP average falls from 9.2 +/- 1.4 (20s) to
  7.98 +/- 1.8 (70s) (Table 2, p. e110, PDF 5).
- Sex: men larger at every level except L5/S1 (P 0.198); P 0.005 at L4/5 (Table 6). Error: not reported; SD
  0.81-1.87 mm between subjects (Table 6).

### Derived: disc wedge (anterior minus posterior, anterior/posterior ratio)

- The paper does not report a disc angle or wedge. Inference, from the Table 6 means (not from individual data, so no
  SD can be given): anterior minus posterior is, men / women, T12/L1 2.54 / 1.52 mm, L1/2 2.63 / 1.61, L2/3 3.03 /
  1.99, L3/4 3.76 / 2.52, L4/5 4.89 / 3.84, L5/S1 6.65 / 6.13; anterior/posterior ratio men 1.68, 1.53, 1.53, 1.64,
  1.81, 2.26, women 1.46, 1.37, 1.39, 1.45, 1.64, 2.17. The L5/S1 disc is distinctly more wedged than every disc above
  it, and L4/5 is second; this is the strongest level cue among the disc measures, and the ratio (not the difference
  in mm) is free of magnification. Women's discs are less wedged than men's above L5/S1 and nearly as wedged at it.
- From a mask or a lateral radiograph: the same anterior and posterior points; a disc angle needs the endplate depth
  as well, which this paper does not give.

### Foraminal height (FH), left and right

- Landmarks: the greatest distance between the inferior border of the cranial pedicle and the superior border of the
  caudal pedicle, on each side (p. e107, PDF 2; the authors say the distances "furthest apart along the superior and
  inferior pedicle borders"). As read from the figure, Figure 1A shows one point under the upper pedicle and one on the
  lower pedicle, and Figure 1B one point at each pedicle base where it joins the body, beside the canal (p. e107,
  PDF 2). Whether "greatest distance" is a vertical span or the longest chord of the foramen is not stated.
- Plane and instrument: not stated beyond the pedicle borders; Inference: a parasagittal view of the same 3D model.
  Levels T12/L1 to L5/S1, left and right.
- Values, left-right average, men vs women (Table 6, p. e113, PDF 8): T12/L1 16.27 +/- 1.56 vs 15.35 +/- 1.86
  (P 0.000); L1/2 18.34 +/- 1.50 vs 17.29 +/- 1.87 (P 0.000); L2/3 19.46 +/- 1.66 vs 18.63 +/- 2.02 (P 0.001); L3/4
  18.58 +/- 1.81 vs 18.00 +/- 2.12 (P 0.025); L4/5 17.65 +/- 1.91 vs 17.43 +/- 2.06 (P 0.387); L5/S1 14.30 +/- 2.05 vs
  14.36 +/- 1.80 (P 0.806). Left and right by decade: Tables 4 (men) and 5 (women), p. e112, PDF 7. The paired
  t-test result is not given. Inference from Tables 4-5: left minus right is within 1 mm in 81 of the 84 decade cells
  (largest 1.47 mm, men L4/5 fourth decade); left exceeds right at men's L4/5 in all seven decades (0.18-1.47 mm) and
  right exceeds left at women's T12/L1 and L5/S1 in all seven decades (0.09-0.81 mm); whether this side pattern is
  real or a measuring habit cannot be told.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: needs the posterior-element subregion (pedicles) of both instances. On each
  side, take the pedicle of the cranial vertebra and of the caudal vertebra (at L5/S1 the caudal "pedicle" is the S1
  pedicle, so the sacrum mask must carry it), find the lowest point of the cranial pedicle's inferior border (the roof
  of the foramen, the inferior vertebral notch) and the highest point of the caudal pedicle's superior border, and take
  the maximum cranio-caudal distance between the two borders in the parasagittal plane through the pedicles.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: the classical lateral-radiograph foraminal height (pedicle to pedicle); on a
  lateral the two sides superimpose, so a radiograph gives roughly the left-right average; magnification must be
  corrected or the value used as a ratio. Not on AP.
- Across levels: rises from T12/L1 to a peak at L2/3 (men 19.46, women 18.63), declines through L3/4 and L4/5, and
  drops sharply at L5/S1 to the smallest value of all six levels. Inference (Table 6 means and SDs): the L4/5 to L5/S1
  drop is 3.3 mm (men) and 3.1 mm (women), about 1.6-1.7 pooled SD, the largest adjacent-level contrast of any measure
  in the paper; T12/L1 to L1/2 is about 1.0-1.35 SD. FH has the smallest relative spread (CV 8-14 percent).
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger at T12/L1 to L3/4, not at L4/5 or L5/S1 (Table 6; p. e108-e109, PDF 3-4).
  Age: men negative at L2/3 (R -0.247), L3/4 (-0.355), L4/5 (-0.430) and L5/S1 (-0.415), none at T12/L1 or L1/2
  (Table 7); women negative at every level, R -0.275 (T12/L1), -0.345, -0.413, -0.447 (L3/4), -0.412, -0.241 (L5/S1)
  (Table 8, p. e114, PDF 9). By decade, men's L4/5 FH falls from 18.77 +/- 1.4 (10s) to 16.39 +/- 2 (70s) and women's
  L3/4 from 18.89 +/- 2.43 to 15.49 +/- 2.3 (Tables 4-5, p. e112, PDF 7).
- Error: not reported.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- T12/L1 (thoracolumbar junction): the smallest of the six discs in every disc height: anterior 6.29 / 4.81 mm, middle
  6.66 / 6.13, posterior 3.75 / 3.29, AP average 5.02 / 4.05 (men / women); its FH, 16.27 / 15.35, is the second
  smallest, above only L5/S1 (Table 6, p. e113, PDF 8). Biggest sex gap at the anterior rim (ratio 1.31, Inference
  from Table 6). In women no disc height at T12/L1 correlates with age, though FH does (R -0.275) (p. e109, PDF 4;
  Table 8, p. e114, PDF 9). The men's middle height here correlates positively with age (R 0.403), the
  strongest positive age effect in the paper (Table 7, p. e114, PDF 9); the men's three-point average in Figure 2
  rises from 5.04 at the "10" point to 7.77 mm at the "80" point (p. e108, PDF 3). The 12th rib had to be cut from T12
  when segmenting (p. e107, PDF 2).
- L1/2: second smallest in every disc height (Table 6); men's middle and three-point average rise with age (R 0.341,
  0.211; Table 7). Women's middle rises (0.195) while posterior falls (-0.186) (Table 8, p. e114, PDF 9).
- L2/3: FH peaks here (Table 6). Men's middle height rises with age (R 0.258); women's posterior height (R -0.315) and
  AP average (-0.221) fall (Tables 7-8, p. e114, PDF 9). Men's middle height 11.02 +/- 1.5 in the 40s is Table 2's
  largest cell for this level (p. e110, PDF 5).
- L3/4: no age correlation of any disc height in men (p. e109, PDF 4; Table 7), though FH falls (R -0.355); in women
  the posterior height has the strongest age effect of any disc height in the paper (R -0.424) and FH the strongest of
  all (R -0.447) (Table 8, p. e114, PDF 9). Posterior height no longer differs by sex (P 0.079).
- L4/5: highest middle and posterior heights and highest three-point average in men (9.2 mm), which the authors name
  the maximum height of the adult lumbar interbody space (p. e106, PDF 1; p. e116-e117, PDF 11-12). FH no longer
  differs by sex (P 0.387) and has the strongest age decline in men (R -0.430). Anterior height now exceeds middle
  (Table 6). In men, L4/5 is the only level where anterior (R -0.202), posterior (-0.246) and AP average (-0.243) fall
  with age (p. e109, PDF 4; Table 7, p. e114, PDF 9).
- L5/S1 (lumbosacral junction): largest anterior height (11.92 / 11.36), middle falls back to 9.34 / 9.13, posterior
  5.27 / 5.23, most wedged disc (Inference above); no sex difference in any measure (P 0.123-0.851, Table 6), the
  authors saying men's and women's L5/S1 discs are about the same height (p. e108, PDF 3); no age
  correlation of any measure in men except FH (R -0.415) (p. e109, PDF 4; Table 7); FH is the smallest of all levels
  (14.30 / 14.36). Widest SDs of the anterior height (2.59 / 2.94). In women the anterior height (R -0.388), AP
  average (-0.320), three-point average (-0.252) and FH (-0.241) fall with age here, the posterior height does not
  (-0.041) (Table 8, p. e114, PDF 9). Women's L5/S1 anterior height in the 30s is 13.8 +/- 2.4 mm, the largest cell in
  either disc table, and in their 70s 9.35 +/- 3.5 mm, the widest SD (Table 3, p. e111, PDF 6). The authors exclude
  L5/S1 from their elliptization pattern of the lower lumbar discs (p. e110, PDF 5).
- C2/C3 and C7/T1: not studied.

## Measurement error and reliability

- None reported: one neuroradiologist measured every scan once; no intra- or inter-observer agreement, no repeat
  measurement, no phantom (p. e107, PDF 2).
- Resolution: slice thickness and voxel size not given; the bone model is a 226-3071 HU threshold with manual
  separation of the articular processes and the 12th rib (p. e107, PDF 2). Inference: the 226 HU boundary sets where
  the endplate surface lies, so a different threshold or a learned mask boundary shifts every height systematically by
  a fraction of a voxel per surface. Inference: disc height runs along the scanner's z axis, so on axial abdominal CT
  the slice thickness and spacing, not the in-plane pixel, limit its precision; the paper gives neither.
- Positional limitation named by the authors: the recumbent position may have changed the heights, and MRI may give
  different heights; they expect the trends by level and decade to hold (p. e116, PDF 11).
- Sample-size limitation named by the authors: 2 sexes x 7 decades gives 14 groups, which may be too few to show trends
  a larger sample would; statistical and clinical significance need not coincide; further subgrouping (by stature,
  weight, BMI) would make the groups too small; height, weight and BMI were missing for many (only 81 women, 69 men)
  (p. e114, PDF 9).
- Internal slips found by arithmetic (Inference): nine of the 252 derived decade cells (168 disc averages, 84 FH
  averages) disagree with their own components by 0.09-0.63 mm; they are listed under Gaps.
- Between-subject SDs (Table 6) are 0.8-2.9 mm for disc heights and 1.5-2.1 mm for FH; these are population spread,
  not measurement error.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication: choosing interbody cage height in lumbar fusion; the aim is to restore disc height and foraminal height
  (indirect decompression of the neural elements) without oversizing (p. e106, PDF 1; p. e110, PDF 5). By their titles,
  the cited complication papers are mostly on lateral interbody fusion (refs 2, 5-7: stand-alone LLIF, XLIF), with one
  on PEEK cages in circumferential fusion (3), one finite-element study of PEEK spacers (4) and one anterior cervical
  graft model (1); the one cage-height study is of TLIF (ref 20) (p. e117-e118, PDF 12-13).
- The authors conclude that a cage taller than 10 mm restores the interbody space beyond physiological height and may
  predispose to complications (abstract, p. e106, PDF 1).
- Recommended practice (p. e114, PDF 9): cages of 6-10 mm chosen by the patient's sex and overall body height, placed
  mid-vertebral body, as wide as possible, and lordotic rather than square in profile, to restore height and lordosis
  while limiting subsidence.
- The one study found on average cage heights used reported 11.25 +/- 1.32 to 12.38 +/- 1.43 mm (ref 20, a TLIF
  study); the authors set this against their largest average disc height of 9.87 +/- 0.7 mm (men, "eighth decade")
  and warn that oversizing raises implant-endplate pressure and may cost regional lordosis (p. e113, PDF 8). They
  conclude that the disc heights they measured were smaller in both sexes than the cage sizes the literature reports
  (p. e117, PDF 12). Inference: this comparison holds only for averages; the mean anterior height at L5/S1 is 11.9 mm
  in men and 11.4 mm in women, within the cited cage range, and 13.2-13.8 mm in women in their 20s and 30s, above it
  (Tables 3 and 6, p. e111, PDF 6; p. e113, PDF 8).
- Subsidence is the most obvious complication of excessive endplate pressure, which can also lead to failure and
  reoperation; how subsidence relates to loss of correction and return of pain is poorly understood; there is no
  unified grading system for it; besides cage height, anterior versus posterior placement, cage width and cage
  lordosis contribute (p. e113-e114, PDF 8-9). The authors call for a prospective study of restored disc height
  against subsidence, controlling for lordosis and cage position (p. e114, PDF 9).
- Healthy discs do not lose height uniformly with age. Upper lumbar: middle height rises, anterior and posterior
  unchanged; lower lumbar except L5/S1: middle unchanged, anterior and posterior fall. The authors call this
  "elliptization", a rectangular disc cross-section becoming elliptical (p. e110, PDF 5; Figure 9, p. e117, PDF 12),
  say it agrees with earlier smaller studies (refs 8, 10, 12, 13, 16) and concede it is not unequivocally shown
  (p. e116, PDF 11). Their own tables support it only in part (see Gaps).
- FH declines with age more strongly and more often significantly than disc height, which the authors read as bony
  remodelling of the bodies (greater endplate concavity, loss of body height) in normal ageing, citing endplate
  deflection with trabecular deformation under axial load and trabecular damage at small strains (p. e110-e111,
  PDF 5-6, refs 17-19). The stiffening of the ageing nucleus and the loss of body mass and density contribute
  (p. e111, PDF 6).
- Pitfall named by the authors: the surgeon's experience of near-universal disc degeneration conflates two separate
  processes, ageing and degeneration, into a belief that discs simply narrow with age (p. e111, e113, PDF 6, 8).
- Sex matters more than age for disc height (p. e106, PDF 1; p. e113, PDF 8; p. e116, PDF 11); the cause of the sex
  difference is unclear and may relate to BMI or muscle mass; men were taller and heavier, but whether stature or
  weight affects disc height is left open (p. e113, PDF 8; p. e117, PDF 12).
- Literature pitfalls named in the introduction: plain-radiograph disc heights need complex corrections to be accurate;
  MRI, though easier to read, rests on a two-dimensional sagittal section and is not consistently broken down by level
  or decade; CT data are scarce (p. e106, PDF 1). The authors also note that very little literature gives normal disc
  heights or guides implant size (p. e113, PDF 8).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Measure each lumbar disc at three midsagittal points, anterior rim, endplate centre and posterior rim, between the
   facing endplates of the two bodies, and keep all three; do not reduce to one average (p. e107, PDF 2).
2. When you compare with Bach's numbers, use the three-point averages of the abstract and Figure 8 for the overall
   height and the Table 6 "Average" row only as the anterior-posterior average (Inference from arithmetic; p. e106,
   PDF 1; p. e113, PDF 8).
3. On a lateral radiograph or fluoroscopy, measure only the rim heights (anterior, posterior) and the pedicle-to-pedicle
   foraminal height; do not treat a radiographic mid-disc height as the CT centre height, since the CT centre sits in
   the endplate concavity (Inference; p. e106, PDF 1 on radiograph corrections).
4. Use ratios rather than millimetres on radiographs and fluoroscopy, because magnification is unknown: the
   anterior/posterior ratio is about 1.4-1.8 from T12/L1 to L4/5 and about 2.2 at L5/S1 (Inference from Table 6,
   p. e113, PDF 8). These are ratios of means; the paper gives no per-subject ratio or its spread, so treat them as
   centres only.
5. To find the lumbosacral junction, look for the disc with the greatest anterior height, the greatest wedge and a
   middle height lower than its anterior height by about 20 percent, and whose foraminal height is about 3 mm
   (17-19 percent) smaller than that of the disc above (Inference from Table 6, p. e113, PDF 8). Inference: confirm
   by the sacrum's shape, never by disc numbers alone, since these are population means with SDs of 1.4-2.9 mm at
   L5/S1.
6. At the thoracolumbar junction, expect T12/L1 to be the smallest of the six measured discs at every point
   (posterior height about 3.3-3.8 mm, anterior 4.8-6.3 mm; Table 6, p. e113, PDF 8), and separate the 12th rib from
   T12 before measuring (p. e107, PDF 2). Inference: the paper measured no disc above T12/L1, so it cannot say how
   T12/L1 compares with T11/T12; do not use small disc height alone to place the junction, and confirm it by the
   last rib-bearing vertebra.
7. Do not try to separate L2/3, L3/4 and L4/5 by middle or posterior disc height: their means lie within 0.4-1.2 mm of
   each other, with SDs of 1.0-1.9 mm (Table 6, p. e113, PDF 8). Use anterior height and wedge, which keep rising
   from level to level (Inference from Table 6), together with vertebral measures.
8. Condition every disc height on sex: men's discs are 5-31 percent taller at the anterior rim depending on level, but
   posterior heights at L3/4 to L5/S1 and every L5/S1 measure do not differ by sex (Table 6, p. e113, PDF 8).
9. Do not read a narrow disc as age-normal: in this healthy sample the three-point average changed little with age
   (no correlation in men below L1/2; in women only weak negative correlations at L3/4 to L5/S1, R -0.25 to -0.30),
   and the authors separate ageing from degeneration (p. e110, e113, PDF 5, 8; Tables 7-8, p. e114, PDF 9).
   Inference: a markedly narrowed disc therefore suggests degeneration or pathology rather than age. Expect foraminal
   height to fall with age, in women at every level and in men from L2/3 down (Tables 7-8, p. e114, PDF 9).
10. Expect teenagers (the 10-19 group, 8 per sex) to have a smaller three-point average than adults in their 20s at
    every level in both sexes (for example men L2/3 6.61 vs 8.17 mm; Tables 2-3, p. e110-e111, PDF 5-6); do not use
    adult norms below 20 years (Inference from the decade tables; the paper does not discuss growth).
11. Remember these are recumbent CT values; the authors say position may have changed them and MRI may differ
    (p. e116, PDF 11). Inference: standing radiographs load the disc and may read lower.
12. When the spine may be transitional, do not apply the L5/S1 row without first deciding which disc is the last
    mobile one above a fused sacrum; this paper gives no rule (Inference; the paper is silent).
13. Measure foraminal height on each side as the greatest distance between the lower border of the cranial pedicle
    and the upper border of the caudal pedicle, and average left and right before comparing with Table 6 (p. e107,
    PDF 2; p. e113, PDF 8). Expect it to peak at L2/3 (19.5 / 18.6 mm, men / women) and to be smallest at L5/S1
    (14.3 / 14.4 mm).
14. Build the bone surface at a bone threshold (Bach used 226-3071 HU) and cut each vertebra free of its neighbours'
    articular processes, and T12 free of its rib, before placing points, so that a facet or rib does not bridge two
    instances (p. e107, PDF 2).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13 or rib-count
variants and does not say how such spines were handled. Use other papers in the set.

(b) Measures and definition differences:
- The generator (Panjabi's four papers) draws vertebral dimensions only; Inference from the panjabi1991c note in this
  set and from memory: Panjabi does not measure disc heights or foraminal heights. This paper offers two pairwise
  (junction) features the generator could add as emissions on transitions between consecutive instances: disc height
  at anterior, middle and posterior midsagittal points (T12/L1 to L5/S1) and the left-right foraminal height
  (pedicle to pedicle). Suggested per-junction means and SDs are those of Table 6 (p. e113, PDF 8), split by sex,
  remembering that its "Average" row is the AP average; for adult-only or age-specific priors use the decade rows of
  Tables 2-5 (p. e110-e112, PDF 5-7), with the Table 1 counts as weights. Inference: within a spine, disc heights of
  neighbouring levels are likely correlated, which the paper does not report; a generator that draws them
  independently from Table 6 will give too much level-to-level jitter.
- No cervical or thoracic disc values: the generator's C3-T11 junctions get nothing from this paper.
- Definition biases when extracting from a CT mask (all Inference):
  - Bach's points sit on a 226 HU surface of a hand-cleaned 3D model; a SPINEPS mask boundary may sit a fraction of a
    voxel inside or outside it on each endplate, biasing every disc height by up to about one voxel in total.
  - Whether Bach's height is a straight point-to-point distance or perpendicular to the disc is not stated; a mask
    method should report the straight distance between facing rim points and note the difference where the disc is
    strongly wedged (L5/S1), where the two can differ.
  - The middle height depends on placing the centre point in the endplate concavity; a mask method that takes the
    minimum gap or the gap between fitted planes will read lower than Bach's middle height at T12/L1 to L3/4.
  - Osteophytes and the ring apophysis: Bach's rim points are on the body edge; a mask that includes a marginal
    osteophyte will shorten the anterior or posterior height.
  - Foraminal height needs pedicles; if the segmenter does not separate pedicles from the rest of the posterior
    elements, take the narrowest part of the posterior-element mask just behind the body as the pedicle.
- The L5/S1 row is the disc above the first sacral body in a five-lumbar spine; with a transitional segment the
  numbering of the decoder decides which disc it describes.

(c) Measurement error: none reported (p. e107, PDF 2). Inference, an assumption and not from the paper: until a
reliability study in the set gives a figure, take a per-height error of about one slice spacing of the CT used (often
0.6-1.25 mm on thin reconstructions, more on routine 2.5-5 mm abdominal series), since disc height runs along z; the
Table 6 SDs (0.8-2.9 mm disc, 1.5-2.1 mm FH) are between-subject spread and already contain this paper's unknown
measurement error.

(d) Covariates:
- Sex: strong at T12/L1 to L3/4 (anterior ratio men/women 1.31 to 1.19, middle 1.09-1.15, posterior 1.06-1.15, FH
  1.03-1.06), fading to none at L5/S1 (ratios 1.00-1.05) (Inference: ratios computed from Table 6).
- Age: small. Men: middle height rises with age at T12/L1, L1/2 and L2/3 (R 0.403, 0.341, 0.258), the three-point
  average at T12/L1 and L1/2 (0.265, 0.211) and the posterior height at T12/L1 (0.193); anterior, posterior and AP
  average fall weakly at L4/5 only (R -0.20 to -0.25); FH falls at L2/3 to L5/S1 (R -0.247 to -0.430). Women: anterior
  height falls at L3/4 to L5/S1 (R -0.265 to -0.388), posterior at L1/2 to L4/5 (R -0.186 to -0.424), AP average at
  L2/3 to L5/S1 (R -0.221 to -0.390), three-point average at L3/4 to L5/S1 (R -0.252 to -0.304), middle rises weakly at
  L1/2 (0.195); FH falls at every level (R -0.241 to -0.447) (Tables 7-8, p. e114, PDF 9). The per-decade means and SDs
  of Tables 2-5 (p. e110-e112, PDF 5-7) can replace a linear age term.
- Teenagers: the pooled Table 6 values include 8 men and 8 women aged 10-19 whose discs are smaller at most levels
  (Tables 1-3); Inference: an adult prior should use the second to seventh decades of Tables 2-5 instead.
- Body size: the authors say they tried to assess height, weight and BMI but the data were incomplete (81 women,
  69 men), and they report no relation of disc height to any of them (p. e114, PDF 9); stature, weight and BMI were
  compared only by sex and against age (Tables 11-12, p. e117, PDF 12). Women's stature fell with age (R -0.332,
  P 0.002). The paper's advice to size cages by sex and overall body height is not backed by a disc-height-on-stature
  analysis here. Inference: stature was probably recorded in inches, not measured (Tables 9-10).
- Population: one US centre (Tampa, Florida), ethnicity not reported, supine CT of patients with non-spinal complaints.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Which "average" Table 6 shows: its "Average disc height" row equals (anterior + posterior)/2 at every level and sex,
  not the three-point mean the text defines and the abstract and Figure 8 report (Inference from arithmetic; Table 6,
  p. e113, PDF 8 against p. e106, PDF 1).
- Maximum height: the abstract conclusion and the conclusions give L4/5 as the maximum, 8.9 +/- 1.7 mm (men) and
  8.6 +/- 1.8 mm (women) (p. e106, PDF 1; p. e116-e117, PDF 11-12), but the results give L4/5 as 9.2 +/- 1.6 (men) and
  8.5 +/- 1.6 (women), and 8.6 +/- 1.8 is the women's L5/S1 value; in women L5/S1 (8.57) slightly exceeds L4/5 (8.46)
  (p. e106, PDF 1; Figure 8, p. e113, PDF 8). Inference: the men's 8.9 matches Figure 8's men's L5/S1 point (8.90),
  so both conclusion figures look like L5/S1 values printed as L4/5.
- Largest disc height "9.87 +/- 0.7 mm for men in their eighth decade" (p. e113, PDF 8): the decade tables stop at the
  seventh decade (70s) and Table 1 lists no subject in their 80s, yet Figures 2-7 (p. e108-e109, PDF 3-4) plot an
  "80" point for both sexes (L4/5 men 9.87). The figures' points match the tables' "Average" rows for the 20s to 70s
  only approximately, and the figures' "10" point differs from the tables' first decade (L4/5 men 9.16 in Figure 6 vs
  8.73 in Table 2). The figure captions promise both the average and the AP average but each plots one line per sex,
  which follows the three-point "Average" rows. Inference: with the abstract's age ranges up to 83 and 89 years, the
  figures were probably drawn from a data set that kept subjects of 80 and over, whom the tables and Table 1 leave out.
- Ages: methods 10-80, abstract 14-83 (women) and 14-89 (men), results 14-79 for both, men's mean 48 (abstract) vs 47
  (results) (pp. e106-e108, PDF 1-3); the subset with stature runs 18-77 and 18-80 (Tables 9-10), so it holds a man
  of 80, older than the results' upper limit of 79.
- Decade-table slips (Inference from arithmetic; Tables 2-5, p. e110-e112, PDF 5-7): men T12/L1 first decade
  "Average" 4.72 and "AP Avg" 4.26 where the components give 4.81 and 4.41; men L3/4 first decade 7.62 and 6.67 where
  they give 7.90 and 7.04; men L4/5 first decade "AP Avg" 8.7 where they give 8.07; men L5/S1 sixth decade "AP Avg"
  8.71 (the seventh decade's value) where they give 8.14; women L1/2 seventh decade "Average" 5.81 where they give
  5.59; FH "Average" men T12/L1 third decade 16.43 against (L+R)/2 16.34 and women L4/5 first decade 17.63 against
  17.45. Every other derived cell agrees within 0.05 mm.
- Tables 9-10 (p. e115-e116, PDF 10-11): the printed BMI disagrees with the printed weight and height by more than
  1 kg/m2 in at least five rows of each table (Inference from arithmetic).
- Elliptization against the tables: in women the posterior height falls with age at L1/2 and L2/3, upper lumbar levels
  where the authors say anterior and posterior heights do not change; in men L3/4 shows no age change in any disc
  height and the lower-lumbar fall is limited to L4/5 (Tables 7-8, p. e114, PDF 9; p. e110, PDF 5).
- The authors speak of a strong correlation between disc height and sex (p. e113, PDF 8) but test sex only by t-tests;
  no correlation coefficient or effect size for sex is given.
- Sex test: the text says women's discs are smaller at P < 0.001 at all levels except L5/S1 (p. e108, PDF 3), but
  Table 6 shows posterior height not different at L3/4 (P 0.079) and L4/5 (P 0.705), the L4/5 "Average" at P 0.005 and
  T12/L1 middle at P 0.001.
- Age-correlation wording against the table's own scale: women's FH is called "moderate to strong" at all levels
  (p. e109, PDF 4) but |R| is 0.241-0.447 (weak to moderate, none strong); women's L4/5 posterior height is called
  moderate (p. e109, PDF 4) but R is -0.248 (weak) (Table 8, p. e114, PDF 9).
- Table 8's first column is headed "Average Disc Height" (it appears twice); the text assigns its values to anterior
  disc height (p. e109, PDF 4), so the first column is anterior height.
- Table 12: the men's age-to-weight correlation is printed as -1.67, an impossible R; probably -0.167 (Inference)
  (p. e117, PDF 12).
- Table 4: the men's L1/2 left FH in the fifth decade has a truncated SD, "17.91 +/- 1." (p. e112, PDF 7).
- The paired left-right FH test is in the methods but its result is never given (p. e107, PDF 2).
- Not reported at all: scanner, slice thickness, voxel size, the number excluded and why, ethnicity, level counting,
  transitional anatomy, measurement reliability, whether heights are perpendicular to the disc, disc angle, endplate
  depth or width, vertebral body heights, lordosis, and any relation of disc height to stature.
- Inference (other papers): radiograph series of disc height (for example Frobin and Brinckmann, refs 9 and 11) and
  MRI series often report the tallest disc at L4/5 with L5/S1 smaller in the middle; this paper agrees for the middle
  and posterior heights but shows L5/S1 tallest at the anterior rim, as expected from lumbosacral lordosis. The cited
  cage heights come from a TLIF study (ref 20); lateral and anterior cages are sized against anterior height, which
  this paper shows exceeds 10 mm at L4/5 and L5/S1 in many people, so the conclusion that a cage over 10 mm is
  supraphysiologic should be read for the three-point average only.
