# Alonge 2021: Lumbar spinal canal dimensions in Nigerians on CT

Source: thread "Reread every paper for clinical detail", project symphony_4, 2026-10-28.
Citation as printed on the first page: OJ Alonge, AA Adeolu, AB Omololu and OM Atalabi. Lumbar spinal canal dimensions in Nigerians using computed tomography scan. Afr. J. Med. Med. Sci. (2021) 50, 69-76. No issue number is printed.
Copy read: publisher's free copy (Afr J Med Med Sci), project files literature/synthetic_spine/alonge2021.pdf; sha256 begins 80d8ad2c4793cfca. All 8 pages read as page images (journal pp. 69-76 = PDF 1-8); every number below was taken from the page image and rechecked against it by a second reader; the PDF's text layer agrees for the values searched.

## What the paper is

- Design: retrospective, cross-sectional measurement study of the bony lumbar canal on existing abdominal CT drawn from one hospital group's CT database (the paper does not say how many sites), by surgeons and a radiologist of University College Hospital and the College of Medicine, University of Ibadan. p. 69 (PDF 1), p. 70 (PDF 2).
- Why abdominal CT: the lumbar vertebrae are in the field of every abdominal scan, the patients are not known to have lumbar symptoms and so are taken as representative of the normal population, and no extra cost or radiation is added. p. 70 (PDF 2). The authors contrast this with a Togolese CT study that scanned 500 asymptomatic people of reproductive age prospectively, which they call an ethical concern. p. 74 (PDF 6).
- Source and period: the CT database of Lagoon Hospitals, Lagos, Nigeria; scans of 1 July 2017 to 30 June 2019. p. 69 (PDF 1), p. 70 (PDF 2).
- Population: 120 abdominal CT examinations of living Nigerian adults, taken as 120 patients (Obs 120 in Tables 1 and 2; the paper does not say whether any patient contributed two scans), 66 women (55%) and 54 men (45%); Yoruba 51%, Igbo 21%, the rest other or unknown tribes; age 18 to 65 years, mean 40.2 (SD 10.07). p. 71 (PDF 3); the sex counts 66 and 54 are from Tables 1 to 4, p. 73-74 (PDF 5-6). Inference: 51% and 21% of 120 are about 61 and 25 people; the paper prints only percentages.
- Data recorded per patient: initials, sex, age, tribe and hospital number only; no height, weight or other body-size measure. p. 71 (PDF 3).
- Cohort against the two African normative studies the authors lean on: Amadou et al. (Togo, prospective CT of 500 asymptomatic adults of reproductive age, mean age 38 years, 54% men) and Elhassan et al. (Sudan, MRI of 128 adults aged 20 to 28, 57% men); this cohort is older (mean 40.2) and has fewer men (45%). p. 74-75 (PDF 6-7).
- Inclusion: every abdominal CT of a patient of Nigerian heritage, judged by one native Nigerian name. p. 70 (PDF 2).
- Exclusion: age below 18 (skeletal maturity) or above 65 (degeneration "florid beyond this age"); degenerative change at the bony landmarks; spondylolisthesis; signs of spondylodiscitis or other infection; images that did not clearly show all lumbar vertebrae. p. 70 (PDF 2). In the discussion the excluded degenerative features are named as marginal and posterior osteophytes. p. 74 (PDF 6). The number screened and the number excluded for each reason are not given.
- Modality and instrument: hospital CT workstation; sagittal and axial reconstructions shown side by side with linked reference lines; bone window; display magnification 4.00x; electronic cursor, values in mm. p. 71 (PDF 3). Scanner, slice thickness, kernel and pixel size are not reported. Inference: the axial screen photograph of Fig. 2 carries overlay text that reads like "1.25sp", possibly a 1.25 mm slice spacing; this is not stated in the text. p. 72 (PDF 4).
- Measures: canal anteroposterior diameter (APD), canal transverse diameter (TD), vertebral body anteroposterior diameter (VAPD), the ratio APD/VAPD called the vertebral Torg ratio, and canal shape (oval or trefoil). p. 71 (PDF 3). Fig. 2 also labels a "VTD" line that the text never defines or reports. p. 72 (PDF 4).
- Levels: L1 to L5, each measured in every patient: 600 level measurements of each measure (120 x 5). p. 73-74 (PDF 5-6).
- Level identification and counting: not described. The only related rule is the exclusion of images that did not clearly show all lumbar vertebrae. p. 70 (PDF 2).
- Statistics: Epi Info 7.2.2.6; descriptive means, cross-tabulated by sex; p values printed for the per-patient sex comparison (Tables 1 and 2) but the test is not named. p. 71 (PDF 3), p. 73 (PDF 5).
- Observers: not stated; the ethics paragraph says only the researcher had access to the data, which suggests a single measurer. p. 71 (PDF 3). Inference: one observer, single reading.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts, cervical ribs or block vertebrae. It assumes five lumbar vertebrae L1 to L5 in every patient and does not say how L1 or L5 was identified on the abdominal CT. p. 70-71 (PDF 2-3).
- Inference: a patient with a transitional segment would either have been numbered by an unstated rule or dropped under "did not clearly show all lumbar vertebrae"; the paper gives no count either way, so it yields no transitional prior.
- Spondylolisthesis was an exclusion criterion; its frequency among the screened scans is not given. p. 70 (PDF 2).
- Developmental (constitutional) narrowing, the one anatomical variant the paper counts: 16 of 600 level measurements (2.67%) had APD below 12.3 mm, which the authors call 2 SD below the mean; 11 of 600 (1.83%) were below Verbiest's 12 mm; none was below 10 mm. The denominator is level measurements, not patients; how many patients carried them is not given. p. 74 (PDF 6), p. 75 (PDF 7).
- Canal shape is a level-dependent variant: oval at L1 and L2, trefoil at L4 and L5, and at L3 about two thirds oval and one third trefoil. p. 74 (PDF 6), p. 75 (PDF 7).

## Measures, each as a recipe

### Canal anteroposterior diameter (APD)

- Definition: the bony canal's midline sagittal depth, measured on the mid-sagittal reconstruction with the cursor drawn perpendicular from the posterior border of each vertebra (the paper's word; Fig. 1 shows the posterior wall of the body) to the lamina; the operator checked on the linked axial view that the cursor and reference line lay in the midline at each level. p. 71 (PDF 3), Fig. 1 p. 71 (PDF 3).
- Plane: mid-sagittal CT, cross-checked on axial. Instrument: workstation cursor at 4.00x display magnification, bone window. Levels: L1 to L5. p. 71 (PDF 3).
- Inference from Fig. 1: the five APD lines start on the posterior wall at roughly mid-height of each body and run back to the lamina, so the measure is taken at the vertebral (pedicle) level, not at the disc. p. 71 (PDF 3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Take the instance's own axes (craniocaudal axis through the body end-plate centroids, left-right axis normal to the mid-sagittal plane through the body centroid and the spinous process). In the mid-sagittal plane, at the craniocaudal mid-height of the body subregion, cast a line from the posterior surface of the body subregion backwards, perpendicular to the posterior wall, and record the length of the empty gap until it meets the anterior surface of the posterior-element subregion (lamina or spinolaminar junction). Without subregions, fill the canal hole in each axial slice (the hole enclosed by body, pedicles and laminae) and take its midline AP extent. Report the bony canal, not the dural sac.
- From a radiograph alone: not addressed by the paper. Inference: a lateral radiograph gives the depth from the posterior body wall to the lamina or spinolaminar line, magnified by the film geometry (unknown magnification unless calibrated); dividing by the body depth (the Torg ratio below) removes magnification. The lumbar lamina's anterior surface is harder to see on a lateral film than in the neck.
- Across levels (Table 3, p. 73, PDF 5): a shallow V with its floor at L3 in both sexes.
  - Women (n = 66): L1 16.16 (SD 1.95), L2 15.82 (1.87), L3 15.25 (2.39), L4 16.00 (2.82), L5 16.45 (3.40) mm.
  - Men (n = 54): L1 16.43 (1.32), L2 15.42 (1.48), L3 14.92 (1.68), L4 15.40 (2.07), L5 16.12 (2.61) mm.
  - Single-level ranges: women 11.20 (L5) to 29.20 (L5), with minima of 11.90 at L3 and L4; men 11.10 (L3) to 24.40 (L5). Medians 14.70 to 16.25.
  - Inference (computed from Table 3): pooled per-level means L1 16.28, L2 15.64, L3 15.10, L4 15.73, L5 16.30 mm, pooled SDs L1 1.69, L2 1.71, L3 2.10, L4 2.51, L5 3.06 mm; adjacent levels differ by 0.5 to 0.7 mm, about 0.2 to 0.4 SD, so APD alone barely separates any two adjacent lumbar levels; its spread widens caudally (SD largest at L5).
  - Per-patient mean of L1 to L5 (Table 1, p. 73, PDF 5): 15.81 (SD 1.98), range 12.30 to 26.5, median 15.57 mm.
- Sex, age, size, population: no sex difference: women 15.94 (SD 2.28), men 15.66 (SD 1.53), p = 0.4429 (Table 1, p. 73, PDF 5); no per-level test is printed, and the text calls the sex difference not significant, consistent with the Togolese study. p. 75 (PDF 7). Inference (Welch t from Table 3 summaries): no level reaches p < 0.05 (|t| <= 1.4). Women's SDs exceed men's at every level. Age and body size are not analysed; height and weight were not recorded (p. 71, PDF 3). Population comparisons: 15.8 mm here against 16 mm reported by Eisenstein for Caucasians and 15.41 +/- 0.55 mm for Togolese on CT; the authors say that without those studies' SDs no firm conclusion on a difference can be drawn, although they print the Togolese +/- 0.55 (its meaning, SD or SE, is not stated). p. 75 (PDF 7). The abstract cites 16 mm as the classic average AP diameter from bone and radiograph studies. p. 69 (PDF 1).
- Error: no intra- or inter-observer data, no repeat measurement. Values recorded to 0.1 mm. p. 71 (PDF 3), Table 3 p. 73 (PDF 5).

### Canal transverse diameter (TD)

- Definition: on the axial cut at the point of maximum pedicle width, the distance between the inner (medial) borders of the two pedicles: an interpedicular distance. p. 71 (PDF 3), p. 75 (PDF 7), Fig. 2 p. 72 (PDF 4).
- Plane: axial CT. Instrument and levels as for APD.
- Inference from Fig. 2: the TD line is horizontal across the canal between the medial pedicle cortices, posterior to a second line labelled VTD that crosses the back of the vertebral body. p. 72 (PDF 4).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Resample the instance into slices perpendicular to its craniocaudal axis; through the pedicle span, find the slice where the pedicles' cross-section is widest (the paper's "point of maximum pedicle width"; Inference: read as the slice where each pedicle is most fully cut, measured mediolaterally); in that slice, measure along the left-right axis the gap between the medial surfaces of the left and right pedicles (or the left-right width of the filled canal hole at the pedicle level).
- From a radiograph alone: not addressed by the paper. Inference: the AP radiograph gives the classic interpedicular distance between the medial pedicle cortices, magnified by the film geometry; the lateral radiograph cannot give it.
- Across levels (Table 4, p. 74, PDF 6): rises monotonically from L1 (narrowest) to L5 (widest) in both sexes. p. 73 (PDF 5).
  - Women (n = 66): L1 21.75 (SD 1.88), L2 22.36 (1.86), L3 23.78 (2.10), L4 26.47 (2.99), L5 31.42 (4.00) mm.
  - Men (n = 54): L1 23.33 (1.90), L2 23.62 (1.97), L3 25.08 (2.16), L4 27.65 (3.01), L5 33.50 (4.62) mm.
  - Single-level ranges: women 17.00 (L1) to 41.40 (L5); men 19.30 (L1, L2) to 45.90 (L5).
  - Inference (computed): pooled per-level means L1 22.46 (SD 2.05), L2 22.93 (2.01), L3 24.37 (2.21), L4 27.00 (3.05), L5 32.36 (4.40) mm. Steps between adjacent levels: L1-L2 +0.5 mm (about 0.2 SD, nearly inseparable), L2-L3 +1.4 (about 0.7 SD), L3-L4 +2.6 (about 1 SD), L4-L5 +5.4 (about 1.4 SD): TD is the best single separator of L4 from L5 in this paper and the worst of L1 from L2.
  - Possible table error (Inference): the men's L3 median is printed 23.70, the same as the men's L2 median and 1.4 mm below the men's L3 mean of 25.08, while every other median lies within 0.6 mm of its mean. Table 4, p. 74 (PDF 6).
  - Per-patient mean of L1 to L5 (Table 2, p. 73, PDF 5): 25.82 (SD 2.44), range 20.06 to 32.58, median 25.56 mm.
- Sex, age, size, population: men wider: per-patient mean 26.64 (SD 2.38) against 25.16 (SD 2.29), p = 0.0007 (Table 2, p. 73, PDF 5); per level men exceed women by 1.2 to 2.1 mm. The text says the difference is significant at L1 to L3 but not at L4 and L5, without printing per-level p values. p. 73 (PDF 5). Inference (Welch t from Table 4 summaries): L1 t 4.6, L2 3.6, L3 3.3, L4 2.1 (p about 0.03), L5 2.6 (p about 0.01), so the summaries do not support "not significant" at L4 and L5 unless a different test or correction was used. TD here (25.82 +/- 2.4) is wider than the Togolese CT value 23.27 +/- 1.67 (range 13.68 to 33.68); the authors attribute this to the Togolese study not stating where on the pedicle TD was taken. p. 75 (PDF 7). The abstract gives 20 mm as the classic transverse average. p. 69 (PDF 1).
- Error: none reported. Inference: the slice choice (maximum pedicle width) is the main source of variation, largest at L5 where the SD doubles.

### Vertebral body anteroposterior diameter (VAPD)

- Definition: the AP depth of the vertebral body on the mid-sagittal cut, measured at the same level as the APD. p. 71 (PDF 3), Fig. 3 p. 72 (PDF 4). Inference from Fig. 3: a horizontal line from the anterior to the posterior cortex at about mid-height of the body.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the mid-sagittal plane of the body subregion, at mid-height between the end-plates, the AP extent of the body along the vertebra's AP axis (anterior cortex to posterior wall).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: lateral radiograph, mid-body AP depth, magnified like the APD.
- Values: not reported for any level. Inference: implied by APD / Torg ratio from pooled means, about L1 23.3, L2 24.4, L3 26.0, L4 27.1, L5 28.1 mm; a ratio of means is not a mean of ratios, so these are rough.
- Sex, error: not reported.

### Vertebral Torg ratio (APD/VAPD)

- Definition: canal APD divided by VAPD at the same level (the authors call it the vertebral Torg ratio). p. 71 (PDF 3), p. 73 (PDF 5).
- Values: mean 0.62 over L1 to L5; L1 0.70, L2 0.64, L3 0.58, L4 0.58, L5 0.58. p. 69 (PDF 1), p. 73-74 (PDF 5-6). No SD, no sex split, no table.
- Across levels: falls from L1 to L3, then flat; separates L1 from L2 and L2 from L3 but not L3, L4 and L5. The text says it rises after L3 while printing 0.58 for L3, L4 and L5. p. 74 (PDF 6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the ratio of the two mask measures above.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: a lateral radiograph or lateral fluoroscopic frame gives both terms at the same magnification, so the ratio is magnification-free; this is the reason the ratio exists in the neck, though the paper does not say so. No lumbar stenosis threshold for the ratio is given.
- Error: none reported.

### Canal shape (oval or trefoil)

- Definition: the axial outline of the canal at each level, recorded as oval or trefoil; a trefoil is a central canal plus two lateral recesses. p. 70 (PDF 2), p. 71 (PDF 3). No criterion separating the two is given.
- Across levels: oval at L1 and L2, trefoil at L4 and L5; L3 is the transition, about two thirds oval and one third trefoil. No other type was seen. p. 74 (PDF 6), p. 75 (PDF 7). The authors contrast this with a Turkish study that found three main types with sub-variants. p. 75 (PDF 7).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Fill the canal hole in the axial slice at the pedicle level and test the contour for lateral recess lobes (concavities at the posterolateral corners, a three-lobed outline) against a convex oval.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (axial property). Inference.
- Sex, error: not reported.

### VTD (labelled only)

- Fig. 2 labels a line "VTD" across the posterior part of the vertebral body in the axial slice. It is not defined in the text and no value is reported. Inference: vertebral body transverse diameter. p. 72 (PDF 4).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1: widest APD in men (16.43 mm), narrowest TD in both sexes (women 21.75, men 23.33 mm); Torg ratio 0.70, the highest; canal oval. p. 73-74 (PDF 5-6).
- L2: APD women 15.82, men 15.42 mm; TD women 22.36, men 23.62 mm; Torg 0.64; oval. Table 3-4.
- L3: narrowest APD in both sexes (Table 3: women 15.25, men 14.92 mm; the text on p. 73, PDF 5 prints the sexes the other way round, see Gaps); the lowest single value of the whole table (men 11.10 mm), women's minimum here 11.90 mm (women's lowest single value, 11.20 mm, is at L5); Torg ratio 0.58, its lowest; shape mixed, about two thirds oval, one third trefoil. TD women 23.78, men 25.08 mm. p. 73-74 (PDF 5-6).
- L4: APD women 16.00, men 15.40 mm; TD women 26.47, men 27.65 mm; Torg 0.58; trefoil. The introduction names L4/L5 as the commonest site of lumbar stenosis (citing [8]). p. 70 (PDF 2), Tables 3-4.
- L5: widest APD in women (16.45 mm) and widest TD in both sexes (women 31.42, men 33.50 mm); the largest spread of both measures (APD SD 3.40 women, range 11.20 to 29.20; TD SD 4.00 women, 4.62 men; TD up to 45.90 mm); Torg 0.58; trefoil. p. 73-74 (PDF 5-6).
- Mid-lumbar narrowing: the APD falls from L1 to L3 and rises from L4 to L5 in both sexes, as also reported by Amonoo-Kuofi (Nigerian), Azu (South African) and Kang (Korean, PET/CT). Different patterns elsewhere: Eisenstein's dry bone of both Caucasian and Negroid spines narrowest at L2 and L4; Sudanese MRI decreasing steadily from L1 to L5; Indian MRI narrowest at L4. p. 75 (PDF 7).
- Junctions: the paper says nothing about T12/L1 or L5/S1, nor about the sacrum.
- Inference from the minima in Table 3: every APD below 12 mm (11 of 600) must lie at L3, L4 or L5, since the minima at L1 (13.00, 14.40) and L2 (12.70, 12.20) are all above 12 mm. p. 73 (PDF 5).

## Measurement error and reliability

- No intra-observer, inter-observer or repeat-measurement data; one researcher appears to have measured everything. p. 71 (PDF 3).
- Display at 4.00x on the workstation, bone window; values to 0.1 mm per level. p. 71 (PDF 3), Table 3 p. 73 (PDF 5).
- Slice thickness, pixel size and reconstruction plane alignment (beyond the midline check on axial) are not reported.
- The per-level SDs in Tables 3 and 4 are population spread plus measurement error, not error alone.
- The authors flag the method of TD (which slice of the pedicle) as the likely cause of disagreement with the Togolese study, i.e. a definitional error source. p. 75 (PDF 7).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- The lumbar canal holds the conus and cauda equina; it is oval over most of its length and trefoil lower down, the trefoil being a central canal plus lateral recesses; a narrow lateral recess of the trefoil canal is implicated in stenosis, though the central canal appears more involved than the lateral canal. p. 70 (PDF 2).
- The midline AP diameter is the most important and most commonly implicated dimension in stenosis (citing Eisenstein). p. 70 (PDF 2).
- Lumbar stenosis is developmental (congenital), presenting in young adults, or degenerative, presenting in middle and old age. The syndrome: low back ache, bilateral leg pain, numbness, weakness and neurogenic claudication, from compression in the central canal, lateral recess and foramina; the authors call neurogenic claudication fairly specific for canal stenosis. The less common congenital type gives the same symptoms at a younger age. Commonest site L4/L5. p. 70 (PDF 2).
- CT and MRI are named the most accurate modalities for canal dimensions and part of the work-up of degenerative spine disease; older African data are radiographic (Amonoo-Kuofi's interpedicular distances in Nigerian adults) or from bone specimens, and some were from symptomatic patients. p. 70 (PDF 2), p. 74 (PDF 6).
- In congenital stenosis the AP diameter, cross-sectional area and transverse diameters are smaller, and so are the vertebral body width and pedicle length at all lumbar levels (citing Singh 2005); these patients have more degenerative disease (citing [10]). p. 70 (PDF 2).
- No radiological criterion of stenosis is agreed; a systematic review found up to 10 criteria. p. 70 (PDF 2).
- Verbiest's thresholds, from Caucasian studies: normal APD 15 to 27 mm, below 12 mm evidence of stenosis, below 10 mm definite stenosis. p. 70 (PDF 2). Here 11 of 600 level measurements were below 12 mm and none below 10 mm. p. 74-75 (PDF 6-7).
- Racial reference values: the canal is marginally smaller in black than in white people; reference values come largely from Caucasian studies, so surgeons lack consensus on what counts as narrowing in people of black heritage. p. 69 (PDF 1), p. 70 (PDF 2), p. 75 (PDF 7). The developmental stenosis prevalence of 2.67% is twice Eisenstein's 1.3%. p. 75 (PDF 7).
- Pitfall: TD depends on where on the pedicle it is taken; define the slice (maximum pedicle width). p. 75 (PDF 7).
- Pitfall: the abdominal CT cohort may include people with back pain, since abdominal symptoms bring them to CT and some will also have back pain, though few from stenosis; patients with lumbar abnormalities, including marginal and posterior osteophytes, were removed. Abdominal CT has been used before as the control arm of an East African study of stenosis prevalence in low back pain, and the authors take the closeness of their APD to the Togolese value as support for the method. p. 74 (PDF 6).
- Pitfall: the sample is too small to separate Nigerian ethnic groups, and regional differences within a country have been reported; Nigeria has three main, phenotypically distinct ethnic groups. p. 75 (PDF 7).
- Pitfall: CT on asymptomatic people carries radiation, so most CT normative studies are small: 60 adults in one cited Caucasian CT study, 34 and 39 adults in two other cited studies of asymptomatic people (the paper does not call those two Caucasian). p. 74 (PDF 6).
- The authors call their 2.67% the prevalence of developmental (constitutional) narrowing because the subjects were not known to be symptomatic and showed no degenerative change; it agrees with Eisenstein's finding of a marginally smaller canal in black people. p. 75 (PDF 7).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Measure the canal AP diameter in the true mid-sagittal plane of each lumbar vertebra, confirming the midline on the axial view, as a line perpendicular to the posterior border of the vertebra from that border to the lamina, at each vertebral level. p. 71 (PDF 3), Fig. 1. Inference from Fig. 1: take it at about the body's mid-height, not at the disc.
2. Measure the canal transverse diameter on the axial slice where the pedicles are widest, between their medial cortices; always state the slice rule, since a different pedicle slice gives a different value. p. 71 (PDF 3), p. 75 (PDF 7).
3. Use the transverse diameter to place the lowest lumbar levels: in this cohort it rises from about 22.5 mm at L1 to about 32 mm at L5 (pooled means computed from Table 4), with the largest step (about 5 mm) between L4 and L5; treat it as weak evidence between L1 and L2, where it changes by about 0.5 mm. Table 4, p. 74 (PDF 6).
4. Do not use the canal AP diameter to tell adjacent lumbar levels apart: its per-level means span only about 1.2 mm in women and 1.5 mm in men across L1 to L5 (floor at L3), against per-level SDs of 1.3 to 3.4 mm. Table 3, p. 73 (PDF 5).
5. Use the APD/VAPD ratio (vertebral Torg ratio) as a level cue: 0.70 at L1, 0.64 at L2, 0.58 from L3 down; it separates L1, L2 and L3 but not L3 to L5, and no SD is given. p. 73-74 (PDF 5-6). Inference: both terms lie in one lateral projection at one magnification, so the ratio should carry over to a lateral radiograph or fluoroscopic frame where the lamina can be seen; the paper measured it only on mid-sagittal CT.
6. Read the axial canal shape: an oval canal points to L1 or L2, a trefoil to L4 or L5, and L3 may be either (about two thirds oval). p. 74 (PDF 6).
7. Condition transverse-diameter priors on sex (men about 1.2 to 2.1 mm wider per level); do not condition the AP diameter on sex. Tables 2-4, p. 73-74 (PDF 5-6).
8. Flag a level as developmentally narrow when its canal AP diameter is below 12 mm (Verbiest) and as definitely stenotic below 10 mm; in this Nigerian cohort values below 12 mm were 11 of 600 level measurements (1.83%) and none was below 10 mm. p. 70 (PDF 2), p. 74-75 (PDF 6-7). Inference from the minima of Table 3: all of them lay at L3 to L5.
8a. Do not flag a wide L5 interpedicular distance as abnormal on its own: in this cohort the L5 transverse diameter reached 41.4 mm in women and 45.9 mm in men, with SDs of 4.0 and 4.6 mm. Table 4, p. 74 (PDF 6).
9. Before measuring, check for spondylolisthesis, osteophytes at the measured landmarks and infection, which this paper excluded and which distort the canal; report such levels separately rather than as normal anatomy. p. 70 (PDF 2), p. 74 (PDF 6).
10. Never mix the per-patient average of L1 to L5 (Tables 1 and 2) with per-level values (Tables 3 and 4) when comparing with a threshold or another study. p. 73 (PDF 5).
11. When comparing a Nigerian or other African patient with Caucasian norms, expect a marginally smaller AP canal (cohort mean 15.8 mm, SD 2.0, against 16 mm), and do not read a 15 mm canal as narrow: it lies inside Verbiest's 15 to 27 mm range and within one SD of this cohort's mean. p. 70 (PDF 2), p. 73 (PDF 5), p. 75 (PDF 7).
12. Do not take this paper's level labels as checked for transitional anatomy: it says nothing on how L1 to L5 were counted, so confirm the count yourself (ribs at T12, the sacrum) before applying its level values. p. 70-71 (PDF 2-3).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13, rib-count variants or block vertebrae, and does not say how levels were numbered. Spondylolisthesis was excluded without a count. The only level-dependent variant counted is canal shape (L3 about one third trefoil, L1-L2 oval, L4-L5 trefoil) and developmental narrowing (11/600 level measurements below 12 mm, 16/600 below 12.3 mm).

(b) Measures:
- Canal depth: this APD is a bony mid-sagittal depth at the vertebral level from the posterior body wall to the lamina. Inference: compare with Panjabi's lumbar canal depth definition in the set before pooling; any difference in the posterior landmark (lamina against spinolaminar junction or spinous process base) or in the height of the line shifts a mask value by a millimetre or more. Living Nigerian CT here, not cadaver.
- Canal width: this TD is the interpedicular distance at the slice of maximum pedicle width. Inference: Panjabi's canal width and the classic radiographic interpedicular distance are close relatives but the slice rule differs; this paper's L5 values (pooled about 32 mm, up to 45.9 mm) are large, and the L5 jump may partly reflect the slice rule at the short, wide L5 pedicles. A mask-derived width taken at mid-pedicle may come out smaller at L5 than this table.
- Body depth (VAPD) and canal-to-body ratio: the generator could add the ratio APD/VAPD (L1 0.70, L2 0.64, L3 to L5 0.58) as a size-free lateral feature for radiographs and fluoroscopy; no SDs are given, so its spread must come from elsewhere.
- Canal shape (oval or trefoil) as a categorical feature by level: L1-L2 oval, L3 mixed (about 2:1 oval), L4-L5 trefoil.
- Inference: the ratio TD/APD from pooled means is about 1.38 at L1, 1.47 L2, 1.61 L3, 1.72 L4, 1.98 L5, a size-free canal-shape index on CT (not on a single radiograph, since TD and APD come from different projections).
- Inference, set beside this set's Panjabi 1992 note (cadaver lumbar spines, not read again here): Panjabi's canal depth (SCD) has the same shape with its floor at L3 (19.0, 18.2, 17.5, 18.6, 19.7 mm), about 2.4 to 3.4 mm deeper than this APD at every level; Panjabi's canal width (SCW) rises only from 23.7 at L1 to 27.1 at L5, against 22.5 to 32.4 here, and his width-to-depth ratio stays 1.25 to 1.39 against 1.38 to 1.98 here. So the generator's lumbar canal width cannot take this paper's L4 and L5 values without first settling whether the gap is the slice rule (maximum pedicle width), the posterior landmark, living CT against cadaver, or population.

(c) Measurement error: none given; no reliability data. The per-level SDs (APD 1.3 to 3.4 mm, TD 1.9 to 4.6 mm) are population spread plus error. Resolution: 0.1 mm recorded, 4.00x display magnification.

(d) Covariates:
- Sex: TD larger in men (per-patient means 26.64 against 25.16 mm, p = 0.0007; per level +1.2 to +2.1 mm); APD not (15.66 against 15.94 mm, p = 0.4429). Women's per-level SDs exceed men's for APD. Tables 1-4.
- Age: 18 to 65, mean 40.2 (SD 10.07); not analysed against any measure.
- Size: height and weight not recorded; no size factor. The cited congenital-stenosis literature says a narrow canal travels with smaller body width and pedicle length (p. 70, PDF 2), which supports a shared size factor between canal and body dimensions.
- Population: Nigerian adults in Lagos, 51% Yoruba, 21% Igbo; canal AP marginally smaller than Caucasian norms (15.8 against 16 mm), similar to Togolese CT (15.41); TD wider than Togolese (25.82 against 23.27 mm).

## Gaps and conflicts

- Text against table at L3 (the special attention item): the text on p. 73 (PDF 5) gives the narrowest APD at L3 as 15.3 mm in males and 14.9 mm in females; Table 3 (p. 73, PDF 5) gives L3 women 15.2515 and men 14.9204. The table is internally consistent (totals / n give the means, and the per-level totals average to Table 1's per-patient totals exactly), so the text has the sexes swapped; men are narrower at L3.
- The text says TD is significantly wider in men at L1 to L3 and not at L4 and L5 (p. 73, PDF 5) but prints no per-level test; Inference: Welch t from Table 4's summaries gives p about 0.03 at L4 and 0.01 at L5.
- The Torg ratio text says it rises after L3 while printing 0.58 at L3, L4 and L5. p. 74 (PDF 6). No VAPD values, SDs or sex split are given for VAPD or the ratio.
- The 12.3 mm cut-off is called 2 SD below the mean (p. 74, PDF 6), but 15.81 - 2 x 1.98 = 11.85 mm from Table 1; Inference: 12.3 mm equals Table 1's minimum per-patient mean, not a 2 SD limit. The prevalence 2.67% counts level measurements (16/600), not people, although the authors call it the prevalence of developmental stenosis, which reads as a rate in people. p. 74-75 (PDF 6-7). Inference (from this set's Eisenstein 1977 note): Eisenstein's 1.3% is also a rate in vertebrae (28 stenotic vertebrae in 21 skeletons, 4.7% of skeletons), so the "twice as high" comparison is like for like in its denominator but not in its threshold, since Eisenstein called stenosis against each group's own lower limit and this paper against 12.3 mm.
- "The mean APD from L1 to L5 ranged from 12.3 to 26.5 mm" (p. 73, PDF 5) is the range of per-patient means; single-level values range from 11.10 to 29.20 mm (Table 3).
- Table 1 and 2 "Mode" values for continuous data (male TD mode equal to its maximum, 32.58) carry no meaning. p. 73 (PDF 5).
- Fig. 2's "VTD" is never defined or reported. p. 72 (PDF 4).
- The methods say results were shown in tables and pie charts (p. 71, PDF 3); no pie chart is printed, and the ethnic split and shape counts appear only as percentages or fractions in the text.
- The Torg ratio, VAPD and canal shape have no table, no SD and no sex split, although the methods say every mean was cross-tabulated by sex. p. 71 (PDF 3), p. 73-74 (PDF 5-6).
- Table 4's men's L3 median (23.70) sits 1.4 mm below its mean (25.08) and repeats the men's L2 median; Inference: a likely transcription error. p. 74 (PDF 6).
- The authors say they lack the SDs of the comparison studies yet print the Togolese APD as 15.41 +/- 0.55 and TD as 23.27 +/- 1.67; whether these are SDs is not said. p. 75 (PDF 7).
- No criterion for oval against trefoil, no count of shapes beyond "about two-thirds" at L3. p. 74 (PDF 6).
- No level-counting method, no transitional anomaly handling, no exclusion counts, no scanner or slice parameters, no reliability study, no body size, no age analysis.
- The first page carries a foot folio of 88 although the running header gives pages 69-76; pages are cited here by the header numbering.
- Inference (from this set's Hinck 1966 note, not this paper): the monotonic caudal rise of the interpedicular distance with the largest step at L4-L5 agrees in shape with Hinck's adult radiographic interpedicular distances (L1 25.0 to L5 29.7 mm), but the rise here is steeper (22.5 to 32.4 mm) and the absolute values are not comparable because radiographs are magnified. Eisenstein's lateral-radiograph method for the sagittal canal is the radiograph counterpart of this APD; the paper itself reports Eisenstein's dry-bone narrowest levels as L2 and L4 against L3 here (p. 75, PDF 7). Check both against their own notes before pooling.
