# Abbas 2021: The role of vertebral morphometry in degenerative lumbar spinal stenosis

Source: thread "Reread every paper for clinical detail", project symphony_4, 2026-10-28.

Citation as printed on p. 1 (PDF 1): Janan Abbas, Natan Peled, Israel Hershkovitz, and Kamal Hamoud. "The Role of Vertebral Morphometry in the Pathogenesis of Degenerative Lumbar Spinal Stenosis." BioMed Research International (Hindawi), Volume 2021, Article ID 7093745, 8 pages. https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/7093745. Received 9 May 2021, revised 1 July 2021, accepted 16 August 2021, published 6 September 2021. No issue number is printed; the journal numbers articles by ID.

Copy read: PMC open-access Cloud Service (pmc-oa-opendata), PMC8437646.1, fetched 2026-10-28 by this thread; sha256 begins 0bcff4a020aedca7. Printed page numbers equal PDF page numbers (p. 1 is PDF 1, through p. 8 is PDF 8). Every page was read as an image; the figures and tables were also read at higher resolution.

## What the paper is

- Design: retrospective CT study comparing lumbar bony morphometry (L1 to L5) between patients with symptomatic degenerative lumbar spinal stenosis (DLSS) and people without stenosis symptoms, each sex analysed separately (in effect a case-control design; the paper calls it a retrospective study). Participants enrolled 2008 to 2012, following the authors' earlier project (their ref. 16, Abbas et al. 2020, on spinous process inclination in DLSS). p. 2 (PDF 2), section 2.1.
- Aim: whether vertebral morphometry (body size, laminar inclination) is associated with DLSS; hypothesis that it may have a role in its pathogenesis. p. 1 to 2 (PDF 1 to 2).
- Stenosis group: 165 living patients with DLSS-related symptoms, 80 men and 85 women, age 40 to 88 years. Each had stenosis at one or more levels, defined as a dural sac cross-sectional area below 100 mm2 (after Schonstrom, their ref. 8), caused by degeneration of the segment (facet hypertrophy, thickening of the ligamentum flavum). Diagnosis combined clinical symptoms and signs with CT findings (ref. 7); the clinical inclusion criteria followed SPORT (refs. 17, 18) and were applied by one co-author, a spine surgeon (KH). The levels at which each patient's stenosis lay are not given. p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Control group: 180 living patients without stenosis-related symptoms, referred to the radiology department of Carmel Medical Center, Haifa, for abdominal CT because of renal colic symptoms and/or abdominal pain; 90 men and 90 women, age 40 to 99 years. p. 2 (PDF 2). The abstract instead calls them individuals "from the general population" (p. 1, PDF 1).
- Population: Israel; the ethics committee of Carmel Medical Center, Haifa, approved the study (0083-07-CMC). Where the stenosis group was scanned is not stated; the same scanner model and patient position are given for both groups. Ethnicity is not stated. p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Mean age and BMI (Table 1, p. 4, PDF 4): control men 62.8 +/- 12 years, BMI 27.3 +/- 4; stenosis men 66.2 +/- 11 years (P = 0.066), BMI 28.9 +/- 4 (P = 0.021); control women 62 +/- 12 years, BMI 27.6 +/- 5; stenosis women 62 +/- 8 years (P = 0.800), BMI 31.4 +/- 5 (P < 0.001).
- Exclusions: listed after the stenosis group's description as exclusions "from this study" and not restated for the controls: age under 40; congenital stenosis, defined as a bony canal anteroposterior diameter below 12 mm (Verbiest, ref. 19); fractures; spondylolysis; tumours; Paget's disease; steroid treatment; severe lumbar scoliosis (over 20 deg); iatrogenic conditions. p. 2 (PDF 2). Inference: the controls' age range (from 40) and Table 3's lowest control band (12.1 to 14 mm) suggest the age and canal exclusions were applied to them too. The number excluded for each reason is not given. Degenerative spondylolisthesis is not listed as an exclusion.
- Modality and instrument: CT on a Philips Brilliance 64, 120 kV, slice thickness 0.9 to 3 mm, tube current 150 to 570 mA, allowing processing in all planes; supine with knees extended; all measurements in bone window. p. 2 (PDF 2). The measurement software, the in-plane pixel size and whether axial slices were realigned to each vertebra are not given.
- Levels: L1 to L5 only. The vertebral body and canal measures follow a 1987 morphometric study adapted to CT; the text names it "Perry el al." but the reference list (ref. 20) is Berry, Moran, Berg and Steffee 1987, a morphometric study of human lumbar and selected thoracic vertebrae, Spine 12(4):362-367. p. 2 (PDF 2), p. 8 (PDF 8).
- How levels were identified and counted: not described. No word on how L1 or L5 was found, on the count of ribs or lumbar segments, or on transitional segments.
- Statistics: SPSS 20; sample size from a power analysis (alpha 0.05, printed as "beta = 0.8"; Inference: meant as power 0.8); normality checked; independent t-tests between groups within each sex; forward logistic regression (printed "forward R-L") with DLSS as the dependent variable, adjusted for age and BMI (p. 3, PDF 3). Significance P < 0.05. p. 2 (PDF 2), p. 3 (PDF 3).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts or block vertebrae: no prevalence, no numbering method and no rule for a transitional segment. p. 1 to 8 (PDF 1 to 8).
- Exclusions that touch anatomy: spondylolysis, congenital stenosis (bony AP canal < 12 mm), scoliosis over 20 deg and fractures, with no counts of how many were excluded for each. p. 2 (PDF 2).
- The introduction names osteoarthritis, spondylolisthesis and Scheuermann's disease as spine conditions others have related to vertebral body morphometry (refs. 12 to 15), with no prevalence and no data here. p. 1 (PDF 1).
- Inference: since L5 carries the sharpest level signatures in this paper (the jump of the interpedicular width, the canal area, the body width and the beveling ratio, below), an unrecognised sacralised L5, a lumbarised S1 counted as L5, or an L6 would shift exactly the values that most separate L5 from L4.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### Vertebral body length (VL)

- Definition: on an axial slice at mid vertebral height, the longest line joining the anterior and posterior borders of the body. Figure 1a draws it as a midline arrow from the anterior cortex to the posterior cortex of the body, on a slice that also shows the pedicles, a closed canal and the spinous process. p. 2 (PDF 2), section 2.2; p. 3 (PDF 3), Figure 1a.
- Projection: axial CT, bone window. Levels L1 to L5. Instrument: CT software, not named.
- Values (Table 1, p. 4, PDF 4), mm, mean +/- SD, control then stenosis:
  - Men: L1 29.9 +/- 2.7 vs 31.3 +/- 2.9 (P = 0.003); L2 31.1 +/- 2.2 vs 32.6 +/- 2.8 (P < 0.001); L3 32.6 +/- 2.3 vs 34 +/- 2.5 (P < 0.001); L4 32.8 +/- 2.3 vs 34.1 +/- 2.6 (P = 0.001); L5 32.9 +/- 2.3 vs 34.5 +/- 2.7 (P < 0.001).
  - Women: L1 26.4 +/- 2.1 vs 27.8 +/- 2.1; L2 24.5 +/- 2.2 vs 28.7 +/- 1.9; L3 29.3 +/- 2.3 vs 30.9 +/- 2.5; L4 30 +/- 2.4 vs 31.7 +/- 2.2; L5 30.5 +/- 2 vs 32.1 +/- 2.4 (all P < 0.001).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Take the body subregion (or the vertebra mask anterior to the canal), find the superior and inferior end-plate surfaces, and take the plane halfway between them, perpendicular to the vertebra's own craniocaudal axis rather than the scanner z axis. In that plane, measure the extent of the body along the vertebra's anteroposterior axis in the midsagittal line (anterior cortex to posterior cortex). "Longest line" could be read as a maximal Feret diameter; Figure 1a supports the midline anteroposterior reading.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. A lateral radiograph gives the anteroposterior depth of the body at mid height, but needs magnification correction (a calibration marker or known source-to-image distance); the paper uses no radiographs.
- Across levels: in control men it rises L1 to L3 (+1.2, +1.5 mm) and then plateaus (L3 32.6, L4 32.8, L5 32.9): it separates L1 from L3 but not L3, L4 and L5 from each other (Inference, arithmetic from Table 1). In control women the printed L2 value (24.5) is below L1 (26.4) and far below L3 (29.3), which breaks the pattern and is probably a misprint (see Gaps); without it the women's series rises L1 to L5 (26.4, ?, 29.3, 30, 30.5).
- Sex, age, size, population: in controls men are larger than women by 2.4 to 3.5 mm at L1, L3, L4 and L5 (Inference, from Table 1; L2 left out as suspect; no sex test reported). Larger in stenosis patients at every level in both sexes (1.3 to 1.6 mm in men, 1.4 to 1.7 mm in women, L2 women aside); stenosis patients also had higher BMI. The authors note lumbar vertebral size depends on age, sex, race and level (p. 7, PDF 7, refs. 22, 27, 39, 40) and cite work linking higher BMI with vertebral cross-sectional area (ref. 45), calcium intake and physical activity with vertebral size in women (refs. 43, 44), and suggest heavy manual work as a contributor (ref. 46). p. 7 (PDF 7).
- Error: no measure-specific value; the pooled ICCs are given under Measurement error below.

### Vertebral body width (VW)

- Definition: on the same axial mid-height slice, the longest line joining the right and left borders of the body. Figure 1a draws it straight across the body at about its mid depth, ending on the lateral cortices, anterior to the pedicle roots. p. 2 (PDF 2), section 2.2; Figure 1a, p. 3 (PDF 3).
- Projection: axial CT. L1 to L5.
- Values (Table 1, p. 4, PDF 4), mm, control then stenosis:
  - Men: L1 39.7 +/- 2.9 vs 40.9 +/- 3.1 (P = 0.009); L2 40.9 +/- 2.8 vs 42.8 +/- 3.5 (P < 0.001); L3 42.8 +/- 2.9 vs 44.8 +/- 3.1 (P < 0.001); L4 44.7 +/- 2.9 vs 46.6 +/- 3.2 (P < 0.001); L5 49.1 +/- 3.8 vs 51.3 +/- 5 (P = 0.002).
  - Women: L1 35 +/- 2.4 vs 36.1 +/- 2.4 (P = 0.006); L2 36.8 +/- 2.6 vs 37.9 +/- 2.3 (P = 0.003); L3 38.8 +/- 2.8 vs 40.1 +/- 2.6 (P = 0.002); L4 40.8 +/- 2.8 vs 42.2 +/- 8 (P = 0.001; the SD of 8 is out of line with every other cell); L5 45 +/- 3.4 vs 47.1 +/- 3.3 (P < 0.001).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Same mid-height plane as VL; the maximal left-right extent of the body subregion, excluding the pedicle roots.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. An AP radiograph shows the body width at mid height (the waist), with magnification correction; rotation about the long axis shortens it little.
- Across levels: monotonic increase L1 to L5. Control men steps +1.2, +1.9, +1.9, then +4.4 mm at L4 to L5; women +1.8, +2.0, +2.0, then +4.2 mm (Inference, arithmetic from Table 1). With SDs near 3 mm, single steps of about 2 mm separate adjacent levels poorly; the L4 to L5 step is the clearest.
- Sex, age, size, population: men 3.9 to 4.7 mm wider than women at each level in controls (Inference, from Table 1); stenosis patients wider at every level (1.1 to 2.2 mm).
- Error: pooled ICCs only.

### Anterior and posterior vertebral body heights (AH, pH) and the beveling ratio

- Definition: on the midsagittal plane, AH is the distance between the anterosuperior and anteroinferior corners of the body; pH is the distance between the posterosuperior and posteroinferior corners. The vertebral beveling ratio is AH/pH x 100. p. 2 (PDF 2), section 2.3; Figure 1b, p. 3 (PDF 3), which draws one arrow along the anterior cortex and one along the posterior cortex of one body.
- Projection: midsagittal CT reformat. L1 to L5.
- Values: AH and pH themselves are not tabulated. Table 1's caption lists beveling, but the table has no beveling rows; the ratio appears only in Figures 5 and 6 (p. 4 and p. 5, PDF 4 and 5) as plotted means without SDs. Read from the plots (approximate, my reading at enlargement): control men about 93, 97, 99, 103, 117 for L1 to L5 and stenosis men about 90, 94, 96, 99, 113; control women about 96, 99, 102, 104, 117 and stenosis women about 94, 99, 100, 102, 114.
- Meaning: the authors read a lower ratio in stenosis as a more kyphotic body at L1 to L3 and a less lordotic one at L4 and L5 (p. 3, PDF 3, section 3.1). Inference: a ratio below 100 is a body lower in front (wedge), above 100 one taller in front (lordotic). Stenosis men differed at all five levels (asterisks under L1 to L5 in Figure 5); women only at L4 (Figure 6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. On the vertebra's midsagittal plane (through the body centroid and the spinous process), find the four corner points of the body outline (the extreme points of the superior and inferior end plates at the anterior and posterior cortex) and take the two craniocaudal distances; the ratio needs no scale.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. A lateral radiograph gives AH and pH directly (the classic wedge measurement), and the ratio cancels uniform magnification; beam divergence and rotation blur the end-plate corners, worst at L5 and at the ends of the field.
- Across levels: rises steadily from L1 (low 90s) to L4 (about 100 to 104) and jumps by about 12 to 14 points at L5 (about 117 in controls of both sexes), the largest step in the series (Inference, from the figures). The control ratio crosses 100 between L3 and L4 in men and between L2 and L3 in women.
- Sex, age, size, population: similar shape in both sexes; women's control L1 to L3 values sit 2 to 3 points higher than men's (my reading of the figures, not tested).
- Error: pooled ICCs only; the plotted values have no dispersion.

### Bony canal anteroposterior diameter (AP)

- Definition: on an axial slice at the mid vertebral body, at the level of the basivertebral foramen, the longest line from the posterior border of the vertebral body to the posterior border of the canal. Figure 2a draws it in the midline from the posterior body wall to the inner margin of the arch at the spinolaminar junction. p. 2 (PDF 2), section 2.4; Figure 2a, p. 3 (PDF 3).
- Projection: axial CT. L1 to L5.
- Values (Table 2, p. 5, PDF 5), mm, control then stenosis:
  - Men: L1 18.7 +/- 1.4 vs 17.8 +/- 1.4; L2 17.8 +/- 1.6 vs 16.6 +/- 1.5; L3 16.9 +/- 1.7 vs 15.4 +/- 1.5; L4 17.6 +/- 1.9 vs 15.6 +/- 1.6 (P printed "< 0.00"); L5 18.8 +/- 2.2 vs 16.1 +/- 1.9 (P < 0.001 at the other levels).
  - Women: L1 18.7 +/- 1.4 vs 17.6 +/- 1.4; L2 18 +/- 1.6 vs 16.6 +/- 1.3; L3 17.3 +/- 1.5 vs 15.5 +/- 1.5; L4 18 +/- 1.6 vs 15.5 +/- 1.6; L5 18.8 +/- 2.2 vs 15.9 +/- 1.8 (all P < 0.001).
- Distribution (Table 3, p. 5, PDF 5), percent of subjects, sexes pooled. Control (n = 180) in bands 12.1-14 / 14.1-17 / > 17 mm: L1 0 / 13 / 87; L2 0 / 31.7 / 68.3; L3 1.7 / 50 / 48.3; L4 1.1 / 32.8 / 66.1; L5 0.6 / 22.2 / 80. Stenosis (n = 165) in bands 12-14 / 14.1-17 / > 17 mm: L1 0 / 32.1 / 67.1; L2 4.8 / 58 / 36.6; L3 17 / 67 / 15.8; L4 17.6 / 63 / 18.8; L5 13.9 / 59.8 / 22.8. No subject is below 12 mm because congenital stenosis (< 12 mm) was excluded. The text gives 25.4 mm as the top of the control range (p. 3, PDF 3), in no table.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Find the mid-height plane of the body as for VL. In that plane the vertebra mask (body plus pedicles plus laminae) encloses the canal as a hole (Figure 2a shows a closed ring at this level); fill the hole, subtract the bone, and measure the hole along the vertebra's midsagittal anteroposterior axis, from the posterior body wall to the spinolaminar junction. Check that the arch is closed in that plane; where it is not, move to the nearest plane with a closed ring and record the offset. The basivertebral foramen notches the posterior cortex at this level, so decide whether the anterior end point is the notch floor or the line of the posterior cortex on either side (the paper does not say).
- From a radiograph alone: not done in this paper. Inference: a lateral radiograph approximates it as the distance from the posterior body wall to the spinolaminar line at mid body, with magnification correction; the paper gives no validation of that. The paper cites Jones and Thomson (1968, ref. 34) for ratios of canal to body dimensions that sort large from small canals (p. 6, PDF 6); Inference (from memory): that is a plain-radiograph index, which cancels magnification.
- Across levels: U-shaped, smallest at L3 in both sexes (control men 16.9, women 17.3) and largest at L1 and L5 (18.7 to 18.8). Adjacent steps are 0.7 to 1.2 mm against SDs of 1.4 to 2.2 mm, so the AP diameter barely separates adjacent levels; it separates L3 from L1 or L5 best (Inference, arithmetic from Table 2). In stenosis patients the minimum spreads over L3 and L4.
- Sex, age, size, population: control men and women are almost identical (women equal or up to 0.4 mm larger), although the women's bodies are 7 to 12 percent smaller (Inference, from Tables 1 and 2). Stenosis patients have smaller AP diameters at every level (0.9 to 2.9 mm) while having larger bodies. The authors note the midsagittal diameter is largely fixed before or soon after birth and may be stunted by pre- and perinatal illness, malnutrition or toxins (p. 5 to 6, PDF 5 to 6, refs. 32, 33).
- Error: pooled ICCs only.

### Bony canal mediolateral diameter (ML; interpedicular width)

- Definition: on the same axial slice, the longest line joining the medial borders of the two pedicles. Figure 2a draws it across the widest, anterior part of the canal. p. 2 (PDF 2), section 2.4; Figure 2a, p. 3 (PDF 3).
- Projection: axial CT. L1 to L5.
- Values (Table 2, p. 5, PDF 5), mm, control then stenosis:
  - Men: L1 25.5 +/- 1.8 vs 25.6 +/- 1.8 (P = 0.725); L2 26 +/- 2.1 vs 26.2 +/- 2.1 (0.478); L3 27 +/- 2.2 vs 27.3 +/- 2.6 (0.526); L4 28.6 +/- 3 vs 29.4 +/- 4.1 (0.141); L5 34.9 +/- 3.9 vs 35.3 +/- 4.9 (0.566).
  - Women: L1 24.5 +/- 2.4 vs 24.2 +/- 2.2 (0.479); L2 25.4 +/- 2.5 vs 24.7 +/- 2.1 (0.086); L3 26.5 +/- 2.4 vs 25.9 +/- 2.2 (0.064); L4 28.7 +/- 2.7 vs 28.6 +/- 2.9 (0.663); L5 34.8 +/- 4.1 vs 34.3 +/- 3.9 (0.483).
  - No group difference at any level.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the plane used for AP, the maximal left-right width of the canal hole between the medial pedicle cortices.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. The AP radiograph's interpediculate distance is the classic analogue, but it is the narrowest distance between the pedicle shadows over their whole height, magnified, while this CT line is the longest line in one axial slice; the two are not interchangeable without a correction.
- Across levels: rises slowly L1 to L4 (men +0.5, +1.0, +1.6 mm; women +0.9, +1.1, +2.2 mm) and then jumps by about 6 mm at L5 (men +6.3, women +6.1), against SDs of 2 to 4 mm. It is the clearest single L4 versus L5 separator in the paper (Inference, arithmetic from Table 2).
- Sex, age, size, population: women 0.5 to 1 mm narrower at L1 to L3, equal at L4 and L5 (Inference, from Table 2). The authors note the transverse diameter keeps growing until adulthood, unlike the midsagittal one, and read the equal ML values in both groups as pointing to genetic or early developmental control of canal size (p. 6, PDF 6, ref. 33).
- Error: pooled ICCs only.
- Inference (from the Panjabi 1992, Eisenstein 1977 and Hinck 1966 notes of this set): this paper's L5 ML (34.9 mm in control men) is far above Panjabi's dry-bone canal width at L5 (27.1 mm), Eisenstein's greatest interpedicular distance at L5 (24 to 26 mm) and even Hinck's magnified film mean (29.7 mm); at L1 it is 0.8 to 1.8 mm above Panjabi's 23.7 mm (12 mixed-sex cadaver spines). The mid-body "longest line" at L5 may reach into the lateral recesses of the trefoil canal. Do not pool this ML with the others.

### Bony canal cross-sectional area (CSA)

- Definition: the area of the bony canal on the same axial slice, bounded in front by the posterior cortex of the body, at the sides by the medial edges of both pedicles and behind by the neural arch's anterior border. p. 2 (PDF 2), section 2.4; Figure 2b, p. 3 (PDF 3), which shades the canal (its arrow is not explained).
- Projection: axial CT. L1 to L5.
- Values (Table 2, p. 5, PDF 5), mm2, control then stenosis (all P < 0.001):
  - Men: L1 296 +/- 41 vs 274 +/- 36; L2 283 +/- 40 vs 256 +/- 39; L3 279 +/- 45 vs 252 +/- 41; L4 305 +/- 52 vs 268 +/- 53; L5 370 +/- 66 vs 310 +/- 59.
  - Women: L1 285 +/- 42 vs 256 +/- 36; L2 283 +/- 45 vs 243 +/- 34; L3 282 +/- 74 vs 236 +/- 38 (the SD of 74 is out of line); L4 308 +/- 51 vs 253 +/- 39; L5 374 +/- 70 vs 297 +/- 57.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. The area of the filled canal hole in the plane used for AP and ML; better on a plane perpendicular to the local canal axis, since an oblique slice through a tilted vertebra inflates the area.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible from one projection. Inference: an ellipse from AP and ML would overestimate the trefoil canal of L5.
- Across levels: smallest at L3 (control men 296, 283, 279; women 285, 283, 282 for L1 to L3), then rises at L4 (+26 mm2) and sharply at L5 (+65 to +66 mm2) in both sexes (Inference, arithmetic from Table 2); SDs of 40 to 70 mm2 make it weak for adjacent levels except L4 to L5.
- Sex, age, size, population: men and women almost equal in controls. The authors cite a study finding canal area on CT reliable and more sensitive than the AP and interpedicular measurements (p. 4, PDF 4, ref. 29).
- Error: pooled ICCs only.

### Interlaminar angle (ILA)

- Definition: on an axial slice through the upper third of the laminae, the angle between the long axes of the two laminae. Figure 3 draws both axes meeting at the spinolaminar junction, with the angle opening anteriorly toward the canal. p. 2 (PDF 2), section 2.5; Figure 3, p. 3 (PDF 3).
- Projection: axial CT. L1 to L5.
- Values (Table 4, p. 6, PDF 6), degrees, control then stenosis:
  - Men: L1 116 +/- 10 vs 116 +/- 9 (P = 0.889); L2 116 +/- 9 vs 114 +/- 10 (0.265); L3 113 +/- 11 vs 111 +/- 11 (0.291); L4 102 +/- 11 vs 98 +/- 12 (0.025); L5 98 +/- 11 vs 90 +/- 13 (< 0.001).
  - Women: L1 114 +/- 10 vs 117 +/- 11 (0.111); L2 112 +/- 9 vs 117 +/- 9 (0.002); L3 108 +/- 10 vs 116 +/- 12 (< 0.001); L4 98 +/- 11 vs 104 +/- 11 (0.001); L5 94 +/- 10 vs 92 +/- 11 (0.225).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Find the craniocaudal extent of the laminae (the posterior arch between the pedicles and the spinous process), take the axial plane one sixth of the way down from their top (the middle of the upper third), fit a line to each lamina's cross-section (principal axis of the left and of the right lamina pixels), and take the angle between the two lines on the canal side.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (an axial angle).
- Across levels: decreases caudally, with the largest step between L3 and L4 (men -11 deg, women -10 deg) and small steps elsewhere (0 to 4 deg) against SDs of 9 to 13 deg; it separates the upper three from the lower two levels, not neighbours within them (Inference, arithmetic from Table 4).
- Sex, age, size, population: control women 2 to 5 deg smaller than men at every level, L1 to L5 (Inference, from Table 4). The direction of the stenosis effect differs by sex: smaller in stenosis men at L4 and L5, larger in stenosis women at L2 to L4.
- Error: pooled ICCs only.

### Laminar inclination (LI)

- Definition: on a parasagittal plane through the middle of the lamina, the angle that the mid lamina's long axis makes with the posterior cortex of that vertebra's own body. The authors modified Xu et al. 1999 (ref. 21), who referred the lamina to the horizontal (mid) plane of the vertebral body; they use the posterior cortex because, unlike the upper end plate, it is not altered by osteoporotic or degenerative change. p. 2 (PDF 2), section 2.6; p. 6 (PDF 6), Discussion; Figure 4, p. 4 (PDF 4).
- Figure 4b places the sagittal section through the middle of one lamina, a little lateral to the midline, a plane that still cuts the vertebral body; Figure 4a draws one line along the posterior body wall and one along the lamina running posteroinferiorly, extended forward to meet it, with the angle marked on the caudal side of the meeting point. p. 4 (PDF 4).
- Projection: parasagittal CT reformat. L1 to L5.
- Values (Table 4, p. 6, PDF 6), degrees, control then stenosis:
  - Men: L1 22.6 +/- 5 vs 21.4 +/- 5 (P = 0.120); L2 26.4 +/- 6 vs 23.7 +/- 5 (0.003); L3 31.4 +/- 6 vs 27.5 +/- 4 (< 0.001); L4 39.5 +/- 6 vs 36.7 +/- 7 (0.001); L5 46.7 +/- 8 vs 47.4 +/- 7 (0.599).
  - Women: L1 26.5 +/- 4 vs 23.7 +/- 4; L2 29.5 +/- 5 vs 26.5 +/- 4; L3 34 +/- 5 vs 29 +/- 5; L4 42.3 +/- 6 vs 37.8 +/- 6 (all < 0.001); L5 49.8 +/- 6 vs 50.4 +/- 6 (0.518).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Take the sagittal plane through the centre of one lamina (left or right; the paper does not say which or whether both were averaged). In it, fit a line to the posterior cortex of the body (the posterior boundary of the body subregion between the end plates) and a line to the lamina's long axis, and take the angle between them on the caudal side, as Figure 4a draws it.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. A lateral radiograph superimposes both laminae and the facets, so the lamina axis is hard to draw; not validated here.
- Across levels: the measure with the steadiest level gradient in the paper: control men 22.6, 26.4, 31.4, 39.5, 46.7 (steps +3.8, +5.0, +8.1, +7.2 deg); women 26.5, 29.5, 34, 42.3, 49.8 (+3.0, +4.5, +8.3, +7.5) with SDs of 4 to 8 deg (Inference, arithmetic from Table 4). The L3 to L4 and L4 to L5 steps exceed one SD. The authors note that Xu's laminar slope angle, too, varies across vertebral levels (p. 6, PDF 6, ref. 21).
- Sex, age, size, population: control women 2.6 to 3.9 deg larger than men at each level (Inference, from Table 4). Smaller in stenosis at L2 to L4 in men (by 2.7 to 3.9 deg) and L1 to L4 in women (by 2.8 to 5.0 deg); no difference at L5.
- Error: pooled ICCs only.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- No junction outside L1 to L5 is studied (no T12/L1 or L5/S1 measure, no sacrum, no thoracic level).
- L1: VL at L1 is the body measure that entered the adjusted model in both sexes: OR 1.273 (95 percent CI 1.094-1.481, P = 0.002) in men and 1.473 (1.164-1.864, P = 0.001) in women. Women's LI at L1 also entered (OR 0.856, 0.761-0.963, P = 0.010). Table 5, p. 6 (PDF 6). At L1 the ML and ILA (both sexes) and the men's LI do not differ by group (Tables 2 and 4).
- L2: beveling at L2 entered the men's model (OR 0.919, 0.852-0.991, P = 0.028). Table 5, p. 6 (PDF 6). The suspect women's control VL value lies here (Table 1).
- L3: the narrowest bony AP canal and the smallest CSA in controls of both sexes (Table 2, p. 5); the level where half of the controls have an AP of 14.1 to 17 mm (Table 3); men's LI at L3 entered the model (OR 0.901, 0.822-0.987, P = 0.025). Table 5, p. 6 (PDF 6).

## Measurement error and reliability

- Reliability was tested on repeated measurements of 20 individuals (from which group is not stated): intratester by one author (JA) measuring twice 3 to 5 days apart; intertester by two testers (JA and KH) measuring within an hour of each other; both blinded to the results. p. 2 (PDF 2), section 2.7.
- Intratester ICC 0.782 to 0.985 and intertester ICC 0.721 to 0.980 (printed high to low), across all measurements, with no breakdown by measure or level. p. 2 (PDF 2), section 3.
- Not reported: standard error of measurement, limits of agreement, pixel size, the slice actually used for 3 mm studies, and which measures sit at the low end of the ICC range.
- Inference: with slices up to 3 mm thick, "mid vertebral height", "the basivertebral foramen level" and "the upper third of the laminae" may each fall up to half a slice from the intended plane; the angles (ILA, LI) and the canal AP, whose between-level steps are small, are the measures this hurts most.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Lumbar stenosis is developmental (congenital) or acquired; the congenital form is multilevel and presents at 30 to 40 years, while the degenerative form is by far the commonest, usually single level, and presents in the 50s and 60s. p. 1 (PDF 1), Introduction (refs. 1 to 3).
- Degenerative stenosis comes from disc degeneration anteriorly and the two facet joints and the ligamentum flavum posteriorly; it is central, lateral recess or foraminal, with different symptoms and operations; its main symptoms are neurogenic claudication and radicular pain. p. 1 (PDF 1).
- The stenosis definition used: a dural sac cross-sectional area below 100 mm2 at one or more levels. It is accepted that DLSS goes with the area left for the dural sac rather than with bony canal diameters (p. 1, PDF 1, refs. 8, 9); Schonstrom found the dural sac area more reliable than bony diameters, a critical size below 100 mm2, and a normal bony AP (14.1 mm) in patients who had undergone decompression. p. 2 (PDF 2), p. 4 (PDF 4).
- Thresholds quoted: Verbiest first set normal and pathological ranges of canal dimensions (refs. 25, 26); later dry-bone and CT studies put the normal lumbar midsagittal diameter above 12 mm (refs. 8, 22 to 24, 27, 28, Eisenstein's among them); congenital stenosis was defined here as below 12 mm (ref. 19). p. 2 (PDF 2), p. 4 (PDF 4).
- The authors' conclusion on the canal: although DLSS AP diameters stay above 12 mm, almost half of the stenosis patients (the results text says half) had an AP of 14.1 to 17 mm at L2 to L5 while half of the controls were between 17 and 25.4 mm; Table 3 gives 58 to 67 percent of stenosis patients in the 14.1 to 17 mm band at L2 to L5 and 48 to 87 percent of controls above 17 mm. So the borderline AP value should be revised and the AP canal diameter considered when evaluating DLSS. p. 3 (PDF 3), sections 3.2 and 4; p. 5 (PDF 5), Table 3; p. 7 (PDF 7).
- Other cited markers: the ratio of dural sac area to canal area for evaluating stenosis (Zheng, ref. 9); correlation of dural sac area with walking distance (refs. 30, 31); ratios of canal to body dimensions to tell large from small canals (Jones and Thomson 1968, printed in the text as "Johns and Thompson", ref. 34); Zheng found that the AP diameters of the vertebral body give no evaluation of DLSS. pp. 4 to 6 (PDF 4 to 6).
- In congenital stenosis the canal AP, pedicle length and body width are expected to be smaller (ref. 35, a radiographic cohort), the reverse of the larger bodies found here in degenerative stenosis. p. 6 (PDF 6).
- The canal's midsagittal diameter is set before or soon after birth and can be stunted by early illness; the transverse diameter keeps growing until adulthood. p. 5 to 6 (PDF 5 to 6).
- Laminar orientation: a smaller laminar slope angle has been linked to ligamentum flavum hypertrophy (Yu 2021, ref. 36); a smaller thoracic laminar slope angle to greater tension of the ligamentum flavum (ref. 37, a study of thoracic ossification of the ligamentum flavum); horizontal laminae and facets predispose to degenerative spondylolisthesis (Nagaosa 1998, ref. 38, a radiographic study). p. 6 (PDF 6).
- Larger bodies in DLSS may reflect higher BMI and heavy manual work; low calcium intake and high physical activity may affect vertebral size in women; smaller vertebrae carry a higher risk of osteoporotic fracture (Ruyssen-Witrand 2007, ref. 42), so the authors ask, as a limitation needing a prospective study, whether DLSS patients have a lower fracture risk. p. 7 (PDF 7).
- The authors' mechanism: a larger body together with altered laminar inclination and AP canal diameter may change the path of axial load, alter beveling, and destabilise the segment, leading to ligamentum flavum thickening, facet arthrosis, degenerative listhesis and osteophytes; DLSS is multifactorial, genetic and environmental (ref. 47). p. 7 (PDF 7).
- Pitfall (the authors'): end-plate-based references drift with osteoporotic and degenerative end-plate change; refer angles to the posterior body cortex instead. p. 6 (PDF 6).
- Limitation (the authors'): a retrospective design cannot show causation. p. 7 (PDF 7), section 4.1.
- Pitfall, Inference: the controls are abdominal CT patients (renal colic, abdominal pain) aged 40 and over, not a population sample, despite the abstract's wording; the values apply to adults over 40 without scoliosis over 20 deg, spondylolysis or fracture. p. 1 and p. 2 (PDF 1 and 2).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Measure body length and width on an axial plane at the body's mid height, not at the end plates, when comparing with this paper's values; record which plane you used (p. 2, PDF 2, section 2.2; Figure 1a, p. 3).
2. To call L5 against L4 on CT, compare the interpedicular width (expect a jump of about 6 mm at L5), the canal area (about +65 mm2) and the body width (about +4 mm) (Tables 1 and 2, pp. 4 and 5, PDF 4 and 5). Inference: each alone overlaps between people; ML and CSA are both canal measures and not independent, so count them as one piece of evidence with the body width as a second.
3. On a midsagittal reformat compute the anterior over posterior body height ratio; expect about 93 to 104 at L1 to L4 and about 117 at L5 in people without stenosis, read from plots without SDs (Figures 5 and 6, pp. 4 and 5, PDF 4 and 5). Inference: a lateral radiograph gives the same ratio free of magnification, and a ratio well above L4's is the expected L5 signature.
4. Measure laminar inclination on a parasagittal plane through the middle of one lamina, against the posterior body cortex, not the end plate (p. 2 and p. 6, PDF 2 and 6; Figure 4). Expect about 23 to 27 deg at L1 rising to about 47 to 50 deg at L5 in controls (Table 4, p. 6). In a stenosis patient expect values smaller by about 3 to 5 deg at L2 to L4 (men) or L1 to L4 (women), and none at L5. Inference: it is the steadiest monotonic level gradient here, so it is a candidate level marker.
5. Measure the interlaminar angle in the axial plane through the upper third of the laminae (p. 2, PDF 2; Figure 3). Inference from Table 4: use it to tell L1 to L3 (about 108 to 116 deg in controls) from L4 and L5 (about 94 to 102 deg), not neighbours within those groups.
6. Do not use the bony canal AP diameter alone to name a level: it varies by about 1 mm between neighbours with an SD near 1.5 to 2 mm, smallest at L3 (Table 2, p. 5). Use it to flag a narrow canal (below 12 mm congenital, p. 2; 14.1 to 17 mm common in degenerative stenosis, Table 3, p. 5).
7. Inference from Tables 1 and 2: do not scale canal dimensions by body size; in this sample women's bodies are 7 to 12 percent smaller but their canal AP, ML and area equal men's.
8. When a patient has degenerative stenosis, expect larger bodies (VL and VW about 1 to 2 mm above controls at every level) and a smaller canal AP and area; a level prior built from controls will be biased in such a patient (Tables 1 and 2, pp. 4 and 5).
9. Take canal measures on the axial slice through the basivertebral foramen at mid body (p. 2, PDF 2, section 2.4; Figure 2, p. 3). Inference: check the arch is closed in that slice and record whether the anterior end point is the floor of the foramen notch or the cortical line beside it.
10. Before using any value from this paper, check the level count independently: the paper gives no numbering method and no transitional-anomaly handling, so its L5 values assume a normal five-lumbar spine (p. 2, PDF 2).
11. Inference: treat the women's control VL at L2 (24.5 mm), the stenosis women's VW SD at L4 (8 mm) and the control women's CSA SD at L3 (74 mm2) as probable misprints; interpolate rather than use them (Table 1, p. 4; Table 2, p. 5).
12. Inference (comparing the set's Panjabi, Eisenstein and Hinck notes): do not mix this paper's canal ML with dry-bone canal widths or film interpediculate distances; at L5 it runs about 8 to 11 mm above dry-bone widths and about 5 mm above Hinck's magnified film mean (Table 2, p. 5).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper reports no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13, rib anomalies or block vertebrae, and no numbering method.

(b) Measures the generator holds or could add, and definition differences:
- Body depth and width: this paper measures at mid height on an axial slice (the waist). Panjabi's end-plate depth and width (EPD, EPW) are taken across the upper and lower end plates (from the set's Panjabi 1992 note). Inference: mid-height width runs below end-plate width because of the concave waist, so a mask value taken at mid height must not be compared with an end-plate prior, and vice versa. The paper's "longest line" wording may also be read as a maximal Feret diameter, which runs slightly above a strict midline depth.
- Canal depth (AP) and canal width (ML): taken at mid body, at the basivertebral foramen; ML is the longest line between the medial pedicle borders, not the narrowest interpediculate distance of a radiograph. Inference: Panjabi's canal depth (SCD) follows the same U with an L3 minimum (19.0, 18.2, 17.5, 18.6, 19.7 mm in the set's Panjabi note) and agrees with this paper's AP within about 1 mm, but this paper's ML runs about 1 to 2 mm above Panjabi's canal width at L1 (SCW 23.7 mm) and about 8 mm above it at L5 (27.1 mm); check the plane before pooling.
- Canal area (CSA) and the beveling ratio AH/pH are candidates the generator could add: CSA gives a strong L4 to L5 step, and the ratio is scale-free, so it survives an unknown patient size factor and radiograph magnification. Inference: if the generator draws anterior and posterior body heights, the ratio follows from them.
- Laminar inclination (against the posterior body cortex) and interlaminar angle are new angular measures; LI has a monotonic gradient of about 3 to 8 deg per level and is scale-free. Xu's original laminar slope angle uses the horizontal plane of the body, so values from the two methods are not interchangeable.
- Within this sample the canal does not scale with sex while the body does: Inference, a single shared patient size factor applied to both body and canal dimensions would over-scale the canal in small (female) patients.
- Levels covered are L1 to L5 only, against the generator's C3 to L5.

(c) Measurement error to use: only pooled ICCs (intratester 0.782 to 0.985, intertester 0.721 to 0.980, 20 subjects). Inference: in the absence of per-measure values, treat the between-subject SDs in Tables 1, 2 and 4 as an upper bound for measurement plus biological variation; no SEM is given.

(d) Covariates: sex (bodies larger in men by 2.4 to 4.7 mm; canal equal; LI larger in women by 2.6 to 3.9 deg; ILA smaller in women by 2 to 5 deg); disease (DLSS: larger body, smaller canal AP and area, smaller LI, lower beveling ratio); BMI higher in DLSS and an independent term in the model; age mean about 62 to 66 with range 40 to 99, entered the model but not analysed per measure; population: Israeli hospital patients, ethnicity not given. The authors cite age, sex, race and level as determinants of lumbar vertebral size (refs. 22, 27, 39, 40; ref. 27 is Eisenstein's South African study and ref. 39 a CT osteometry of the Asian lumbar spine). p. 7 (PDF 7).

## Gaps and conflicts

- No transitional-anomaly or numbering information at all; levels L1 to L5 are taken as given.
- The level of each patient's stenosis is not given, so the stenosis-group values at each level mix stenotic and non-stenotic segments.
- AH and pH themselves are never tabulated; the beveling ratio is only plotted, without SDs, and Table 1's caption promises beveling it does not contain (p. 4, PDF 4).
- Abstract versus Table 1: the abstract gives control men's VL at L2 as 31.3 mm, Table 1 gives 31.1 mm (p. 1 vs p. 4, PDF 1 vs 4).
- Abstract versus methods: the abstract calls the controls "general population", the methods abdominal CT referrals for renal colic or abdominal pain (p. 1 vs p. 2).
- Conclusion versus results: the conclusion names vertebral body width, but no VW term entered the regression (Table 5 has VL L1; its footnote defines VW, which is not in the table) (p. 7 vs p. 6). The discussion says VL, AP and LI "increase the likelihood" of DLSS, while the ORs for AP and LI are below 1, so it is a smaller AP and a smaller LI that go with DLSS (p. 3 vs p. 6).
- Table 1, control women VL L2 = 24.5 +/- 2.2 mm, below L1 (26.4) and L3 (29.3) while every other series rises and the stenosis women's L2 (28.7) sits between their L1 and L3; probably a misprint. Stenosis women VW L4 SD = 8 mm, out of line with all others (about 2 to 5); probably a misprint.
- Table 2, control women CSA L3 SD = 74 mm2, against 34 to 70 elsewhere; possibly a misprint. Men's AP at L4 has P printed "< 0.00".
- Printed "+" for "+/-": Table 2 ML L1 women control (24.5 + 2.4) and ML L3 men control (27 + 2.2); Table 4 ILA L3 men stenosis (111 + 11).
- Text versus Table 4: section 3.3 says women's interlaminar angles at L1 to L4 were significantly greater in stenosis, but Table 4 gives P = 0.111 at L1, so only L2 to L4 differ (p. 3 vs p. 6, PDF 3 vs 6).
- Table 3: sexes pooled; the control L5 row sums to 102.8 percent, the stenosis rows to 96.5 to 99.8 percent; the band edges differ between groups (12.1-14 vs 12-14 mm). The results say almost 50 percent of controls were between 17 and 25.4 mm and half of the stenosis group between 14.1 and 17 mm, the discussion swaps "almost 50 percent" and "half"; the 48.3 percent above 17 mm matches only L3 in Table 3; the upper bound 25.4 mm appears in no table (p. 3, PDF 3).
- Table 5: men's age OR 1.066 has a printed CI of 1.022-1.629, far wider above than below on the log scale; probably a misprint. The units of the ORs are not stated.
- The text names the morphometric method's source "Perry el al. (1987)" while ref. 20 is Berry et al. 1987; "Johns and Thompson (1968)" is ref. 34, Jones and Thomson.
- Which lamina (left, right or both) LI was taken on, and whether ILA and LI were averaged across sides, is not stated. No per-measure reliability.
- Spondylolysis was excluded, but degenerative spondylolisthesis was not, and its frequency in either group is not given.
- Inference: the canal-growth argument is hard to follow: the authors say the transverse diameter grows until adulthood, yet read equal ML values as pointing to genetic or early developmental control (p. 6, PDF 6).
- The direction of LI is not reconciled with the literature cited: Inference, with the posterior body wall as reference, a smaller LI means a lamina closer to parallel with the wall (more vertical), while the cited work speaks of horizontal laminae and smaller slope angles as the risk; the authors do not say how their angle maps onto Xu's slope angle.
- Inference (from the set's Eisenstein 1977 note): Eisenstein's overall lower limit of normal for the midsagittal canal is 13 mm on dry bone and 15 mm on film, slightly above the 12 mm this paper adopts, though the paper cites Eisenstein (refs. 22, 27, 28) among the sources for 12 mm. Eisenstein's canal AP is a minimum and his interpedicular a maximum, measured by caliper; this paper's are CT longest lines on one mid-body slice. From the set's Hinck 1966 note, Hinck's interpediculate distance is a magnified minimum on AP films, not this paper's CT longest line.
- Inference (from the set's Panjabi 1992, Eisenstein 1977 and Hinck 1966 notes): this paper's ML at L5 (34.8 to 34.9 mm in controls) is about 8 to 11 mm above dry-bone canal widths at L5 (Panjabi 27.1, Eisenstein 24 to 26) and about 5 mm above Hinck's magnified film mean (29.7); its control men's VL and VW lie within about 1 to 3 mm of Eisenstein's mid-height caliper values for Caucasoid men (VW at L5 49.1 against 46).
