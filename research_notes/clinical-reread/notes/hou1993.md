# Hou 1993: Pedicle morphology T9 to L5 in a Chinese population

Source: thread "Reread every paper for clinical detail", project symphony_4, 2026-10-28.

Citation as printed on the first page: Shuxun Hou, MD; Richard Hu, MD, FRCSC; and Yamin Shi, MD. "Pedicle Morphology of the Lower Thoracic and Lumbar Spine in a Chinese Population." Spine, Volume 18, Number 13, pp 1850-1855, 1993 (J. B. Lippincott Company). Accepted for publication August 17, 1992 (p. 1850, PDF 1).

## What the paper is

- Design: a descriptive dry-bone morphometric study of the pedicles from T9 to L5, written to guide pedicle screw size and entry point, with a comparison of means against three published white series (Zindrick et al, Berry et al, Olsewski et al). p. 1850 (PDF 1); p. 1854 (PDF 5).
- Material: disarticulated vertebrae from the Anatomy Department of the 4th Military Medical Institute, Xi'an, China: 40 sets of "normal spinal columns from T9 to L5", 25 male and 15 female. The vertebrae were examined grossly and had no bony disease or deformity. pp. 1850-1851 (PDF 1-2). Inference: 40 columns x 9 levels = 360 vertebrae.
- Population: Chinese (title), called an oriental population in the Introduction, p. 1850 (PDF 1); the Discussion offers smaller vertebrae in "orientals" than in whites as a possible reason for the differences from Olsewski, p. 1855 (PDF 6). Where the skeletons came from beyond the anatomy department's collection is not stated. Ages, statures and body weights are not given; the authors say height and weight "were not available", p. 1852 (PDF 3). How sex was assigned to a skeleton is not stated.
- Observer and instrument: all caliper measures by one person (Y.S.), p. 1850 (PDF 1), with Vernier calipers of 0.1 mm resolution, p. 1851 (PDF 2). Entry-point relations were judged visually. Pedicle inclination was measured in "a further 10 specimens", method and instrument not described, p. 1851 (PDF 2).
- Statistics: Statview SE on an Apple Macintosh; two-tailed Student t test, significance at P <= 0.05, p. 1851 (PDF 2). Comparison with the published series was by comparing means (test not named), p. 1854 (PDF 5).
- Counts: "six measurements" per vertebra, 40 specimens, a total of 1,440 data points, p. 1851 (PDF 2). Inference: 1,440 = 40 x 9 x 4, i.e. the four caliper measures (A, B, LI, LII); the two visual entry-point codes are not in that count. Whether one or both sides of each vertebra were measured is not stated; Inference: 1,440 implies one value per measure per vertebra.
- Levels: T9 to L5 only. How levels were identified in disarticulated bone, and how a column with a transitional segment would have been handled, is not described. The figures plot mean +/- 1 SD (the bars of Figures 3 to 5 match the table SDs; Inference for Figure 7).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts, cervical ribs or block vertebrae (pp. 1850-1855, PDF 1-6). Inference: the selection of "normal" columns "T9 to L5" without disease or deformity may have excluded transitional columns, but this is not stated, so no prevalence can be taken from it.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### A. Pedicle width (medial-lateral diameter, "A")

- Definition: the medial-lateral width of the pedicle "taken at the narrowest section in the mid-point of the pedicle", by caliper (Inference: a caliper on dry bone reads outer cortex to outer cortex; the paper does not say outer or inner). Figure 1 (left, an axial outline seen from above, spinous process at the top) marks A with two arrows, one from the canal side and one from the lateral surface, across the narrowest part of the pedicle. p. 1851 (PDF 2), Figure 1.
- Plane: transverse. Instrument: Vernier caliper, 0.1 mm. Levels T9 to L5.
- Values, Table 1, p. 1852 (PDF 3), mean +/- SD mm, male / female / pooled 40:
  - T9 6.0 +/- 1.1 / 5.2 +/- 0.9 / 5.7 +/- 1.1
  - T10 7.0 +/- 1.3 / 6.4 +/- 1.4 / 6.8 +/- 1.4
  - T11 8.6 +/- 1.7 / 7.5 +/- 1.7 / 8.1 +/- 1.8
  - T12 8.8 +/- 1.6 / 7.5 +/- 2.3 / 8.3 +/- 1.9
  - L1 7.2 +/- 1.3 / 6.6 +/- 1.0 / 7.0 +/- 1.2
  - L2 7.6 +/- 1.2 / 7.1 +/- 1.5 / 7.4 +/- 1.3
  - L3 9.4 +/- 1.6 / 9.0 +/- 1.8 / 9.2 +/- 1.6
  - L4 10.8 +/- 1.4 / 10.2 +/- 2.2 / 10.5 +/- 1.8
  - L5 12.8 +/- 2.7 / 13.0 +/- 2.7 / 12.9 +/- 2.7
- Extremes: the smallest single widths were in the lower thoracic spine and in females, 3 mm at T9 and 4.2 mm at T10; in males the smallest L1 widths were "in the low 5-mm range". p. 1852 (PDF 3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): build a vertebra frame (end-plate normal as z, midsagittal AP as y, left-right as x). SPINEPS gives no pedicle label; take the pedicle as the bony bridge of the posterior-element part next to the body, between the posterior body wall and the branching of the transverse and articular processes. Step along the pedicle axis, and in each section perpendicular to it take the medial-lateral chord; A is the smallest such chord (the isthmus), measured to the mask's outer boundary, because a caliper reads the outer cortex. Do not measure along the vertebra's x axis at L4 and L5, where the axis turns 18 to 30 deg medially (Figure 7): an x-axis chord reads about 1/cos(angle) too wide.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): an AP radiograph shows the pedicle as an oval whose medial-lateral width approximates A after dividing by the magnification, but at L4 and L5 the beam crosses an obliquely running pedicle and the oval overstates the isthmus. A lateral radiograph cannot give A. The paper itself notes that Zindrick's radiographic technique, thought free of magnification, gave widths larger than these caliper values at most levels, p. 1854 (PDF 5).
- Across levels: rises from T9 (5.7) to T11-T12 (8.1-8.3), drops at L1 (7.0), then rises steeply L3 to L5 (9.2, 10.5, 12.9). The L1 pedicle "was consistently smaller" than the pedicles above and below "in each spinal column", p. 1851 (PDF 2), i.e. a local minimum at L1 in all 40 columns. Inference, my effect sizes (difference of pooled means over pooled SD): T12 vs L1 about 0.8, L2 vs L3 1.2, L3 vs L4 0.8, L4 vs L5 1.0, T9 vs T10 0.9, T10 vs T11 0.8, T11 vs T12 and L1 vs L2 below 0.35. Width alone cannot tell T10 (6.8) from L1 (7.0) or T11-T12 (8.1-8.3) from L3 (9.2) well. The Discussion's summary of all the series compared is a gradual rise in pedicle size toward the L1 region, a sharp fall, then a gradual rise distally, p. 1854 (PDF 5); in Hou's own table the rise ends at T12 and the fall is at L1. Pedicle height was always greater than pedicle width, p. 1852 (PDF 3).
- Sex, age, size, population: female narrower at T9 (P = 0.031) and T12 (P = 0.043); no sex difference in the lumbar spine, p. 1851 (PDF 2). Male minus female 0.4 to 1.3 mm at T9 to L4, but female wider by 0.2 mm at L5 (Table 1). Population: Olsewski's white males had significantly wider pedicles at every lumbar level and his white females at L1, L4 and L5 (Olsewski male L1 9.5 against 7.2 here, L5 21.1 against 12.8), Table 1 and p. 1854 (PDF 5); Berry's series differed only at L5, where Berry's was smaller (10.9 against 12.9), p. 1854 (PDF 5); Zindrick's differed at every level except T11, p. 1854 (PDF 5); Zindrick's means are larger at T9 and L1 to L5 but smaller at T10, T11 and T12 (Table 1). The authors say their specimens were measured much as Berry measured his and that Berry's figures agree closely with theirs; Olsewski used a similar direct technique yet found significantly larger pedicles, less so in females (three of five lumbar levels), which they suggest may reflect smaller vertebrae in orientals than in whites, pp. 1854-1855 (PDF 5-6). No age or size data.
- Error: no repeatability or inter-observer data; single observer; caliper resolution 0.1 mm. The SDs (0.9 to 2.7 mm) are between-specimen spread plus unknown observer error.

### B. Pedicle height (cephalo-caudal diameter, "B")

- Definition: the cephalad-caudad diameter of the pedicle at its narrowest section at the mid-point of the pedicle; Figure 1 (right, a lateral outline drawn turned on its side, body below and posterior elements above) marks B with two arrows across the narrow neck of the pedicle. Inference: that neck lies between the superior and inferior vertebral notches. p. 1851 (PDF 2), Figure 1.
- Plane: sagittal. Instrument: Vernier caliper. Levels T9 to L5.
- Values, Table 2, p. 1852 (PDF 3), mean +/- SD mm, male / female / pooled:
  - T9 12.5 +/- 1.2 / 12.2 +/- 1.3 / 12.9 +/- 1.2 (pooled printed; see Gaps)
  - T10 14.4 +/- 1.3 / 14.2 +/- 1.7 / 14.3 +/- 1.4
  - T11 16.4 +/- 1.6 / 16.0 +/- 1.6 / 16.2 +/- 1.6
  - T12 17.1 +/- 1.3 / 15.9 +/- 1.1 / 16.6 +/- 1.4
  - L1 15.9 +/- 1.4 / 15.5 +/- 1.5 / 15.8 +/- 1.4
  - L2 15.4 +/- 1.6 / 14.7 +/- 1.0 / 15.1 +/- 1.5
  - L3 15.3 +/- 1.7 / 14.3 +/- 1.4 / 14.7 +/- 1.6
  - L4 15.3 +/- 1.9 / 14.5 +/- 2.6 / 15.0 +/- 2.2
  - L5 20.5 +/- 3.6 / 18.7 +/- 3.9 / 19.8 +/- 3.7
- Extremes: heights were always greater than widths and never below 9.2 mm in males or 9.3 mm in females, p. 1852 (PDF 3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): in the same pedicle sections as A, take the craniocaudal chord; B is its minimum along the pedicle (A and B may each be taken at their own narrowest section, since the paper measures each "at the narrowest section"). Alternatively, in the parasagittal plane through the pedicle axis, the narrowest chord between the superior and inferior vertebral notches.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): lateral radiograph, the narrowest vertical dimension of the pedicle between the notches, both sides superimposed, divided by the magnification; AP radiograph, the vertical extent of the pedicle oval.
- Across levels: rises from T9 (12.9 pooled) through T10 (14.3) to a thoracic peak at T11-T12 (16.2-16.6), falls gently through L1 (15.8) and L2 (15.1) to the lumbar minimum at L3 (14.7; text: "The pedicle of L3 was narrowest in the lumbar spine" in females, and 15 mm (SD 1.7) at L3 in males), then jumps at L5 (19.8). pp. 1851 (PDF 2); Table 2. Inference, my effect sizes: T9 vs T10 about 1.1 (1.5 if the pooled T9 value is a misprint for about 12.4, see Gaps), T10 vs T11 1.3, L4 vs L5 1.6; the lumbar pairs L1 to L4 below 0.5. B marks the lower thoracic climb and L5 but does not separate L1 to L4.
- Sex, age, size, population: the text finds the only sex difference at T12 (P = 0.005), p. 1851 (PDF 2); Table 2 also puts the sex marker on L4 (see Gaps). Male minus female 0.2 to 1.8 mm, largest at L5. Population: Olsewski's white males differed significantly at L1, L3, L4 and L5 and were similar at L2; his females differed at L2 and L5, p. 1854 (PDF 5). Berry's differed at T12 and L4; Zindrick's at T9 to T12 and L5 (Table 2 markers). At L5 Hou's 19.8 is far above Zindrick's 14.0 and Berry's 13.8 (Table 2).
- Error: as for A.

### LI. Pedicle length parallel to the midline ("Length I", also called pedicle depth)

- Definition: from the posterior cortex to the anterior vertebral cortex, "parallel to the sagittal diameter" of the body. Figure 1 (left) draws LI from a point on the posterior outline lateral to the lamina, near the root of the transverse process and superior articular process (a drawn point; the text names no landmark), straight forward past the lateral wall of the canal, through the pedicle, to the anterior cortex. p. 1851 (PDF 2), Figure 1. The Results call it pedicle depth, "Length 1", and the Methods print its abbreviation as "(L1)", which reads like the level L1, p. 1851 (PDF 2). Inference: this is a whole-vertebra chord (posterior elements plus pedicle plus body), not the pedicle alone.
- Plane: transverse, parallel to the midsagittal plane. Instrument: caliper. Levels T9 to L5.
- Values, Table 3, p. 1853 (PDF 4), mean +/- SD mm, male / female / pooled:
  - T9 39.2 +/- 3.2 / 37.7 +/- 2.7 / 38.6 +/- 3.0
  - T10 39.4 +/- 3.0 / 37.6 +/- 2.5 / 38.8 +/- 2.9
  - T11 39.6 +/- 3.1 / 38.0 +/- 4.3 / 39.0 +/- 3.6
  - T12 43.5 +/- 4.9 / 41.9 +/- 5.8 / 42.9 +/- 5.2
  - L1 50.7 +/- 3.4 / 47.6 +/- 2.3 / 49.5 +/- 3.4
  - L2 50.3 +/- 3.9 / 48.6 +/- 2.2 / 49.7 +/- 3.4
  - L3 49.7 +/- 3.8 / 48.9 +/- 2.6 / 49.4 +/- 3.4
  - L4 47.9 +/- 3.9 / 46.3 +/- 2.4 / 47.3 +/- 3.4
  - L5 43.9 +/- 4.1 / 43.1 +/- 4.9 / 43.6 +/- 4.4
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): in the axial plane through the pedicle isthmus centre, cast a ray parallel to the vertebra's own midsagittal plane (not the scanner's y axis) through the pedicle centre; LI is the extent of the instance mask along that ray, from its most dorsal voxel (the posterior surface at the lamina-transverse process junction) to its most ventral voxel (anterior body cortex). Record the start point used, since the Figure 1 start is a drawn point, not a defined landmark.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): on a lateral radiograph, the distance from the posterior surface of the posterior elements at the pedicle level to the anterior body cortex approximates LI, divided by the magnification; the facets and transverse processes overlap the start point. An AP radiograph cannot give it.
- Across levels: a thoracic plateau T9 to T11 (38.6 to 39.0), a step at T12 (42.9) and a larger step at L1 (49.5), a plateau L1 to L3 (49.4 to 49.7), then a decline at L4 (47.3) and L5 (43.6). The authors describe length as falling from L1 to L5, p. 1852 (PDF 3), and as greatest in the upper lumbar spine and shortening distally to L5, much as Berry found, pp. 1852-1853 (PDF 3-4). Inference, my effect sizes: T11 vs T12 about 0.9, T12 vs L1 1.5, L3 vs L4 0.6, L4 vs L5 0.9; T9 to T11 and L1 to L3 essentially 0. LI is the clearest marker of the thoracolumbar junction in this paper.
- Sex, age, size, population: males longer at every level (0.8 to 3.1 mm), significant only at L1, 50.7 (SD 3.4) against 47.6 (SD 2.3), P = 0.003, p. 1851-1852 (PDF 2-3). Population: Table 3's last column, "Zindrick et al", is printed beside the LII columns, but its significance markers sit on the LI cumulative column and the text compares Zindrick with the length parallel to the midline, so it is the LI comparator; Zindrick differed significantly at every level except T12 (Table 3, p. 1853, PDF 4; p. 1854, PDF 5). Inference: its values (41.2 at T9 to 33.7 at L5) are those the zindrick1987 note in this set gives for Zindrick's depth parallel to the midline, which starts at the posterior aspect of the transverse process. The authors say Zindrick's lengths are shorter, p. 1854 (PDF 5); that holds at L1 to L5 (L5 33.7 against 43.6 here) but Zindrick's are longer at T9 to T11 (41.2, 40.1, 41.2 against 38.6, 38.8, 39.0) and about equal at T12 (43.2 against 42.9), Table 3.
- Error: as for A; SDs 2.2 to 5.8 mm.

### LII. Pedicle length to the anterior midline ("Length II")

- Definition: from the posterior cortex to the mid-point of the anterior vertebral cortex, measured "along the pedicle" (Results); the Figure 1 caption calls it the depth of the body from posterior cortex to anterior midline cortex. Figure 1 (left) draws LII from the posterior outline near the lamina-transverse process junction on the other side from LI, obliquely through the pedicle to the anterior cortex in the midline. p. 1851 (PDF 2); p. 1852 (PDF 3), Figure 1. Inference: the length of a convergent screw path that ends at the anterior midline. Table 3 gives no Zindrick comparator for LII (see LI).
- Values, Table 3, p. 1853 (PDF 4), mean +/- SD mm, male / female (no pooled column):
  - T9 43.6 +/- 2.9 / 41.9 +/- 3.0
  - T10 44.2 +/- 2.5 / 47.7 +/- 2.7 (female value suspect, see Gaps)
  - T11 44.6 +/- 3.2 / 43.6 +/- 4.6
  - T12 49.1 +/- 5.3 / 47.8 +/- 5.7
  - L1 55.6 +/- 3.2 / 52.7 +/- 2.7
  - L2 55.4 +/- 3.8 / 53.2 +/- 2.3
  - L3 55.6 +/- 3.5 / 53.7 +/- 2.6
  - L4 53.9 +/- 4 / 51.8 +/- 2.3
  - L5 51.9 +/- 3.9 / 50.6 +/- 4.5
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the same dorsal start point as LI; the end point is the anterior body surface where the midsagittal plane meets it at the height of the pedicle axis; LII is the straight distance between the two (equivalently, the mask's extent along that line).
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (needs the axial angle). Inference.
- Across levels: same shape as LI, about 4 to 8 mm longer; LII minus LI (male) 4.4 at T9 rising to 6.0 at L4 and 8.0 at L5 (Inference, from Table 3 means); Inference: the growth tracks the growing medial inclination.
- Sex: significant only at L1 (P = 0.005), females smaller, p. 1852 (PDF 3).
- Error: as for A.

### Pedicle inclination from the midline

- Definition: the pedicle's inclination relative to the sagittal plane of the vertebral body, measured in "a further 10 specimens", p. 1851 (PDF 2); Figure 7, "Inclination of pedicle from midline", p. 1854 (PDF 5). Method, instrument, side and sign convention not stated. Inference: the transverse-plane angle between the pedicle axis and the midsagittal plane, the same quantity as Zindrick's transverse angle and Panjabi's PDIs; positive means the axis runs anteromedially (converging), since L5 is strongly positive.
- Values read from Figure 7 (approximate, to about 0.5 deg; bars taken as +/- 1 SD, Inference): T9 about 4 (bar 1.5 to 6), T10 about 4.5 (1 to 8), T11 about 0 (-5 to 4.5), T12 about -2.5 (-8 to 3), L1 about 3 (-1.5 to 7), L2 about 5.5 (1 to 10), L3 about 11 (5.5 to 16.5), L4 about 18.5 (11 to 26), L5 about 30.5 (20 to 41). p. 1854 (PDF 5).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): per side, the pedicle axis as the line through the centres of successive isthmus sections (or the normal to the narrowest section), its angle in the axial plane to the vertebra's own midsagittal plane, which must come from the vertebra (canal centre, spinous process, body symmetry), not the scanner, or rotation adds to one side and subtracts from the other.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): not measurable from an AP or a lateral; needs an axial image.
- Across levels: near zero or slightly lateral at T11 and T12, small at T9-T10 and L1-L2, then climbing L3 to L5. "Progression distally produces more inclination away from the midline", p. 1853 (PDF 4); the values are said to resemble earlier reports. Inference, effect sizes from the read values: L4 vs L5 about 1.3, L3 vs L4 about 1.1, L2 vs L3 about 1.1; T12 vs L1 about 1.1.
- Sex, population: not reported (n = 10, sexes not given).
- Error: not reported; values read from a figure add about +/- 0.5 deg.

### Entry point: pedicle centre relative to the transverse process (T, U, M, B)

- Definition: the centre of the pedicle was related visually to posterior landmarks. Viewing laterally gave the centre of the narrowest cephalad-caudad diameter; viewing from cephalad along the canal gave the medial-lateral centre. The centre was coded as at the top of the transverse process (T), its upper half (U), its midpoint, the mid-transverse process line (M), or its bottom half (B). p. 1851 (PDF 2), Figure 2.
- Counts per level of 40, Figure 6 left, p. 1854 (PDF 5) (T / U / M / B): T9 36/4/0/0; T10 36/4/0/0; T11 32/8/0/0; T12 24/11/4/0 (sums to 39); L1 4/23/13/0; L2 1/14/25/0; L3 0/8/29/3; L4 0/4/35/1; L5 0/2/37/1. Text: all 40 cephalad to the mid-TP at T9; 37 of 40 at the mid-TP at L5, p. 1850 (PDF 1).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): project the instance onto the coronal plane of the vertebra frame (a posterior view); find the transverse process's craniocaudal extent at its base and its midline (M), and the pedicle centre (the centroid of the isthmus section projected posteriorly); code the centre's height relative to the TP's top, midpoint and bottom.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): an AP radiograph or AP fluoroscopy shows the pedicle oval and the transverse process together; the oval centre's height relative to the TP's mid-line can be read directly, with no magnification issue since it is a ratio.
- Across levels: migrates caudally from the top or upper half of the TP (T9 to T11, top in 32 to 36 of 40) to the mid-TP (L3 to L5), with T12 to L2 transitional. Figure 6 caption: position becomes more consistently mid-TP going caudally, p. 1854 (PDF 5).
- Error: visual judgement by one observer; none reported.

### Entry point: pedicle centre relative to the superior facet (zones 1 to 5)

- Definition: the superior facet was divided by four vertical lines into three segments; a fifth vertical line, parallel to them and one segment's width (one third of a facet width) lateral to the facet, made a fourth segment. The pedicle centre was related to these zones, which the authors call arbitrary. p. 1851 (PDF 2), Figure 2 and caption. Figure 2 (a posterior view, the midline to the right) numbers the lines 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 from lateral to medial: Inference from the drawing, line 1 near the medial edge and line 4 at the lateral edge of the facet, line 5 beyond it. Figure 6's right panel numbers the same lines 1 to 5 from left to right, so there the lateral side is on the right, p. 1854 (PDF 5).
- Counts per level, Figure 6 right, p. 1854 (PDF 5). The numbers sit staggered beside the lines; my reading of their positions (Inference): T9 middle third 2, lateral third 38; T10 middle 7, lateral 33; T11 middle 3, lateral 36, at line 4 (the lateral border) 1; T12 middle 1, lateral third 26, at line 4 12, beyond line 5 1; L1 at line 3 1, lateral third 3, at line 4 12, between lines 4 and 5 5, at line 5 19; L2 at line 3 2, lateral third 5, between 4 and 5 5, at line 5 27, beyond 5 1; L3 at line 4 3, between 4 and 5 1, at line 5 29, beyond 5 7; L4 between 4 and 5 2, at line 5 22, beyond 5 16; L5 at line 5 1, beyond line 5 39. Each level sums to 40.
- Text: at T9 the starting points lay opposite the middle or lateral third of the superior facet; at L5 at least one third of a facet width lateral to the facet's lateral border, p. 1850 (PDF 1); "almost all" L5 points are well lateral to the lateral border of the facet; the mean distance lateral to the most lateral part of the facet at L5 is 4.9 mm (SD 2), p. 1852 (PDF 3); how and on how many specimens that distance was measured is not stated. The Results also say the medial-lateral position starts at the mid-portion of the facet at T9, p. 1852 (PDF 3), but Figure 6 puts 38 of 40 T9 centres in the lateral third (see Gaps). Figure 6 caption: all but one L5 centre are lateral to the facet joint by one facet segment, p. 1854 (PDF 5).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): in the posterior (coronal) projection, take the superior articular process's medial and lateral borders, divide into thirds, and measure the pedicle centre's medial-lateral offset from the lateral border in units of facet width and in mm.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): AP radiograph or fluoroscopy, the pedicle oval centre against the facet's lateral border; the ratio is magnification-free, the 4.9 mm needs correction.
- Across levels: moves steadily lateral going caudally, from the middle-lateral facet third at T9-T10 to about one third of a facet width beyond the lateral border at L4-L5. p. 1852 (PDF 3).
- Error: visual, single observer, none reported.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- T9: narrowest pedicle of the series, 5.7 mm pooled (female 5.2, male 6.0, sex difference P = 0.031); smallest height 12.9 (printed pooled); shortest LI 38.6; entry cephalad to the TP midline in all 40 (36 at the top, 4 in the upper half) and opposite the middle (2) or lateral (38) third of the facet; inclination about 4 deg. Tables 1-3, pp. 1852-1853 (PDF 3-4); Figure 6, p. 1854 (PDF 5). Minimal single width 3 mm (female, implied), p. 1852 (PDF 3).
- T10: width 6.8, height 14.3, LI 38.8; entry 36 T, 4 U; facet middle 7, lateral 33; minimal single width 4.2 mm. Tables 1-3; p. 1852 (PDF 3).
- T11: width 8.1 (the one level where Hou and Zindrick widths do not differ), height 16.2, LI 39.0; entry 32 T, 8 U; inclination about 0. p. 1854 (PDF 5); Tables 1-3.
- T12: widest lower thoracic pedicle (8.3; male 8.8, female 7.5, P = 0.043); tallest thoracic pedicle (16.6; male 17.1, female 15.9, P = 0.005); LI 42.9 with the largest LI SD (5.2); inclination slightly lateral, about -2.5 deg (sign convention Inference); entry still mostly cephalad to mid-TP (24 T, 11 U, 4 M); facet zone begins to spread laterally (12 at the lateral border). The authors note the sex difference at T12 matters when stabilising these segments, p. 1852 (PDF 3).
- T12/L1 junction: width drops (8.3 to 7.0), LI steps up (42.9 to 49.5), inclination turns from slightly lateral to slightly medial (about -2.5 to 3 deg), the entry point moves down from T to U on the TP and laterally on the facet. Tables 1, 3; Figures 6-7.
- L1: the narrowest lumbar pedicle and smaller than both neighbours in every column (7.0; male 7.2, female 6.6; male minima low 5 mm), p. 1851-1852 (PDF 2-3); the Discussion calls it the smallest vertebral pedicle, though T9 and T10 are narrower in Table 1 (see Gaps); LI longest of the male series (50.7, male-female P = 0.003), LII 55.6 / 52.7 (P = 0.005); entry 4 T, 23 U, 13 M.
- L2: width 7.4, height 15.1, LI 49.7 (the longest pooled LI); entry mostly mid-TP (25 M).
- L3: lowest lumbar height (14.7); width 9.2; LI 49.4; inclination about 11; entry mid-TP in 29, below in 3.
- L4: width 10.5, height 15.0, LI 47.3, inclination about 18.5; entry mid-TP in 35; 16 of 40 more than one third of a facet width lateral to the facet.
- L5: widest (12.9), tallest (19.8) and most inclined (about 30 deg) pedicle, the largest SDs of the series (2.7, 3.7, about 10 deg); LI back down to 43.6; entry at mid-TP in 37 of 40, lateral to the facet's lateral border by 4.9 mm (SD 2), 39 of 40 beyond line 5. Female width exceeds male here (13.0 against 12.8). Tables 1-3; p. 1852 (PDF 3); Figures 6-7.
- L5/S1, C2/C3, C7/T1: no data; the study stops at T9 and L5.

## Measurement error and reliability

- No intra-observer or inter-observer data, no repeat measurements. One observer for all caliper measures (p. 1850, PDF 1); the inclination subset is unattributed.
- Instrument: Vernier calipers, resolution 0.1 mm (p. 1851, PDF 2). Dry bone, so no magnification.
- Entry-point codes and facet zones are visual judgements; Figure 6's T12 TP counts sum to 39, not 40.
- Between-specimen SDs (pooled): width 1.1 to 2.7 mm (coefficient of variation about 17 to 23 percent, my arithmetic), height 1.2 to 3.7 mm (8 to 11 percent at T9 to L3, 15 percent at L4, 19 percent at L5), LI 2.9 to 5.2 mm (7 to 12 percent).
- Inter-study disagreement is larger than these SDs: L5 width ranges from 10.9 (Berry) to 21.1 (Olsewski males) among the white series quoted in Table 1, p. 1852 (PDF 3), and the authors attribute part of the difference with Zindrick to "subtle differences in positions measured", p. 1854 (PDF 5).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Knowledge of pedicle size and position is crucial for safe screw placement; early European screws were small, so morphology mattered less, p. 1850 (PDF 1).
- Published starting points: Roy-Camille, the junction of the mid-transverse process line and the mid-facet line; Magerl, the lateral border of the superior facet at the mid-transverse process; Weinstein, more lateral, at the "nape of the neck" of the superior articular process, to avoid impinging on the adjoining facet and for accuracy. Most authors advocate one universal entry point, which the authors call contrary to accurate placement, p. 1850 (PDF 1).
- No single entry point suits T9 to L5: the pedicle centre starts medial and cephalad (above the mid-TP, opposite the middle or lateral facet third) in the lower thoracic spine and moves to the mid-TP and well lateral to the facet in the lower lumbar spine. Weinstein found Roy-Camille's method better in the lower thoracic and upper lumbar spine and his own lateral method better in the lower lumbar spine. p. 1853 (PDF 4).
- Use a smaller-diameter screw in the lower thoracic and upper lumbar spine; Weinstein had a 40 percent failure rate with 7.0 mm screws in the upper lumbar spine, much improved by changing to 5.5 mm, p. 1852 (PDF 3).
- The authors call the L1 pedicle the smallest and say it needs added care; with male minima in the low 5 mm range, a 5 mm screw seems prudent there, p. 1852 (PDF 3). (In Table 1 it is the smallest lumbar pedicle; T9 and T10 are smaller.)
- Earlier size ranges came mostly from white series and shaped much of instrument and screw design, which is why the authors measured an oriental population, p. 1850 (PDF 1).
- Female lower thoracic pedicles can be as small as 3 mm (T9) and 4.2 mm (T10), p. 1852 (PDF 3). Inference: these are outer widths; the cancellous channel is narrower still.
- Screw length should be limited to 45 mm in the lumbar spine, depending on the screw's inclination, p. 1853 (PDF 4).
- Account for the increasing medial inclination distally when preparing the pedicle, p. 1853 (PDF 4).
- Screw choice depends on the patient's sex and size and the level, p. 1850 (PDF 1), abstract; the Conclusion places the sex differences in width in the lower thoracic and upper lumbar spine and asks for care that screw diameter fits the pedicle there, p. 1855 (PDF 6) (the Results find width sex differences only at T9 and T12, none in the lumbar spine; see Gaps).
- The sex difference at T12 matters when stabilising the thoracolumbar segments; given a choice, use a smaller-diameter screw at the lower thoracic and upper lumbar levels, p. 1852 (PDF 3).
- Values depend on measurement method and population, p. 1855 (PDF 6); the authors suggest the differences from Olsewski may come from orientals having smaller vertebrae than whites, pp. 1854-1855 (PDF 5-6). Inference: white-derived instrument and screw sizes may then be too large for Chinese patients.
- Pitfall: radiographic measurements (Zindrick) and caliper measurements (here) differ significantly even with similar landmarks; the authors summarise them as wider widths and shorter lengths by radiograph and suspect small differences in the points measured, p. 1854 (PDF 5). The tables show exceptions (Zindrick narrower at T10 to T12, longer at T9 to T11; Tables 1 and 3).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. On a CT instance mask, take pedicle width as the narrowest medial-lateral chord of the pedicle (the paper's definition, p. 1851, PDF 2), outer boundary to outer boundary; Inference: measure it perpendicular to the pedicle axis, not along the scanner or vertebral x axis, since at L4-L5 (axis about 18 to 30 deg from the midline, Figure 7, p. 1854, PDF 5) an x-axis chord is too wide by about 1/cos(angle).
2. Look for the L1 dip in pedicle width: in all 40 columns L1 was narrower than T12 and L2. Use a local width minimum flanked by wider pedicles above and below as evidence for L1, together with the LI step. p. 1851 (PDF 2).
3. Locate the thoracolumbar junction by the sagittal chord LI: about 39 mm at T9-T11, 43 at T12, 49.5 at L1-L3 (pooled, Chinese dry bone). A jump of 6 to 7 mm between consecutive instances marks T12 to L1. Table 3, p. 1853 (PDF 4); Figure 5, p. 1854 (PDF 5). Inference: compare the step, not the absolute value, since LI from a mask depends on the dorsal start point and on body size.
4. Separate L3, L4 and L5 by pedicle width (9.2, 10.5, 12.9) and, more strongly, by transverse inclination (about 11, 18.5 and 30 deg, read from the figure; 10 specimens); L5 also has the tallest pedicle (19.8) and a shorter LI (43.6). Tables 1-3, pp. 1852-1853 (PDF 3-4); Figure 7, p. 1854 (PDF 5).
5. Do not use pedicle width alone between T10 and L1 (6.8 against 7.0) or between T11-T12 and L3 (8.1-8.3 against 9.2); combine with LI (39 against 49.5) and height. Tables 1-3.
6. Do not use pedicle height to order L1 to L4 (15.8, 15.1, 14.7, 15.0, overlapping SDs); use it for the T9 to T11 climb (12.9, 14.3, 16.2) and for L5. Table 2, p. 1852 (PDF 3).
7. Read the pedicle centre's height against its transverse process: cephalad to the TP midline (at the TP top or in its upper half) at T9 to T11 in all 40 columns (top in 32 to 36), at the TP midline at L3 to L5 (29, 35 and 37 of 40); T12 to L2 are mixed (T12 24 top, 11 upper half, 4 midline; L1 4, 23, 13; L2 1, 14, 25). Figure 6, p. 1854 (PDF 5). These were judged by eye on dry bone viewed from behind and from the side (p. 1851, PDF 2); Inference: on AP radiographs or AP fluoroscopy the pedicle oval and the TP give the same reading only when the beam is square to the vertebra, so use it as soft evidence.
8. Read the pedicle centre against the superior facet in the same posterior view: in the middle or lateral third of the facet at T9 to T11 (38, 33 and 36 of 40 in the lateral third); at or beyond line 5, one third of a facet width lateral to the facet, in 36 of 40 at L3, 38 at L4 and 40 at L5 (L5 mean 4.9 mm, SD 2, lateral to the facet's lateral border). Figure 6, p. 1854 (PDF 5); p. 1852 (PDF 3). Inference: on an AP image the ratio to facet width needs no magnification correction; the 4.9 mm does.
9. Scale all lengths and widths for sex before comparing with a level template: females narrower by about 0.4 to 1.3 mm at T9 to L4 and shorter in LI by 0.8 to 3.1 mm (most at L1); at L5 female width is 0.2 mm larger than male. Only width at T9 and T12, height at T12 and LI and LII at L1 reached significance. Tables 1-3, pp. 1852-1853 (PDF 3-4); pp. 1851-1852 (PDF 2-3).
10. Scale for population: these Chinese widths are about 20 to 40 percent below Olsewski's white males at L1 to L5. Do not apply this table to a white patient or a white table to a Chinese patient without a size factor. Table 1, p. 1852 (PDF 3); p. 1855 (PDF 6).
11. Give L5 the widest likelihoods for pedicle width, height and inclination: its SDs are the largest of the series there (width 2.7, height 3.7, inclination about 10 deg); for LI the widest is T12 (SD 5.2, L5 4.4), so widen T12's LI likelihood too. Tables 1-3, pp. 1852-1853 (PDF 3-4); Figure 7, p. 1854 (PDF 5).
12. Measure LI along the vertebra's own midsagittal direction, and inclination from the vertebra's own midsagittal plane; rotation or scoliosis taken from the scanner frame biases both. Inference from the definitions on p. 1851 (PDF 2).
13. When checking a planned or seen screw, flag a lumbar screw longer than about 45 mm, a screw over 5 mm at a small male L1 or a narrow female T9-T10, and one universal entry point used at every level from T9 to L5. pp. 1852-1853 (PDF 3-4).
14. Check every extracted pedicle: in this series height always exceeded width, and no height was below 9.2 mm (male) or 9.3 mm (female), p. 1852 (PDF 3). Inference: at T9 to L4 a width at or above the height points to an extraction error (a chord taken across the transverse process root or along an oblique axis) before it points to an unusual level; do not apply the check at L5, where Zindrick's series in this set has width above height (18.0 against 14.0, Tables 1-2 here).
15. When parsing this paper or data copied from it, read "(L1)" in the Methods list of measures as Length I (LI), not as the level L1; and take Table 3's "Zindrick et al" column as the comparator for LI, not LII. p. 1851 (PDF 2); Table 3, p. 1853 (PDF 4).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of any transitional segment, rib anomaly or block vertebra (pp. 1850-1855, PDF 1-6).

(b) Measures:
- Pedicle width and height: the generator holds these (Panjabi). Hou's are the narrowest-section values by caliper (outer cortex, Inference). Per the panjabi1991t and panjabi1992 notes in this set (not reread here), Panjabi digitised the pedicle section without saying it was the narrowest; his widths run larger than Hou's (T9 7.7, T10 9.0, T11 9.8, T12 8.7, L1 8.6, L5 about 18.6 against Hou 5.7, 6.8, 8.1, 8.3, 7.0, 12.9), while his heights agree closely in the lumbar spine (L1 15.9, L3 14.4, L5 19.6 against Hou 15.8, 14.7, 19.8). Inference: a mask extractor that takes the isthmus minimum will read nearer Hou than Panjabi for width; partial volume at 1 mm voxels changes a 5 to 7 mm width by 10 to 20 percent.
- Could add: transverse pedicle inclination (Hou Figure 7; Panjabi's PDIs; Zindrick's transverse angle), a strong L3/L4/L5 separator; LI, the sagittal chord through the pedicle to the anterior cortex, a strong T11/T12/L1 separator; LII; and two AP-projection features, the pedicle centre's height relative to the TP and its lateral offset from the superior facet, which need no magnification correction and so suit radiographs and fluoroscopy.
- LI is a whole-vertebra chord; from a mask its value depends on the dorsal start point (near the lamina-TP junction in Figure 1, p. 1851, PDF 2). Zindrick's depth parallel to the midline, quoted in Table 3 (p. 1853, PDF 4), is about 10 mm shorter at L5 (33.7 against 43.6) and 4.2 to 6.6 mm shorter at L1 to L4, but 1.3 to 2.6 mm longer at T9 to T11 (my arithmetic); Inference: a different start point or a ray that leaves the rounded anterolateral body explains differences of that order, so fix and record the start point and the ray before using these values as a template.
- Inference: LII is the screw-path length to the anterior midline; it is not the same as Zindrick's depth along the pedicle axis (which ends wherever the axis leaves the anterior cortex), so do not pool the two.
- Inference: Hou's starting-point codes (TP and facet zones) are qualitative, posterior-view features of the dry vertebra; a generator would need a continuous version (pedicle centre height as a fraction of TP height; lateral offset from the facet's lateral border in facet widths), which this paper gives only as counts.

(c) Measurement error: none reported. Use the caliper resolution (0.1 mm) as a floor and add an observer error from another paper; the printed SDs are between-specimen spread (with observer error inside) and can serve as the generator's per-level spread for this population.

(d) Covariates: sex, with per-level male minus female differences of width 0.4 to 1.3 mm (female 0.2 wider at L5), height 0.2 to 1.8 mm, LI 0.8 to 3.1 mm (Tables 1-3); the significant ones are width at T9 and T12, height at T12 (and possibly L4), LI and LII at L1. Population: Chinese dry bone, smaller than Olsewski's white series in lumbar width. No age, stature or weight, so no size covariate can be fitted from this paper; Inference: a female/male width ratio of about 0.85 to 0.96 (L5 1.02) is a crude size factor.
