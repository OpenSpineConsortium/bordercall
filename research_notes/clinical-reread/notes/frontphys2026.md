## What the paper is

- Design: retrospective, single-centre, cross-sectional CT study; ethics approval No. 2025KY10001 (p. 3 (PDF 3); p. 8 (PDF 8)).
- Setting and population: 100 "healthy volunteers" who had a lumbar spine CT at the Department of Health Examination of an orthopaedic hospital in Zhengzhou, Henan, China, September 2023 to September 2025; called "healthy Chinese adults" (p. 2 to 3 (PDF 2 to 3); p. 7 (PDF 7)). The ethics statement describes the data as anonymised clinical CT from routine health examinations (p. 8 (PDF 8)). Ethnicity beyond "Chinese" is not given. Living people, not cadavers. Inference: "healthy" means only that the exclusion criteria were met; why a health check-up included a lumbar CT (back pain or screening) is not stated.
- Sex and age: text says 50 men and 50 women, aged 20 to 70 (p. 1 (PDF 1); p. 4 (PDF 4)). Table 1 (p. 5 (PDF 5)) gives five decade groups of 20 people each, with mean ages 25.1 +/- 3.4, 35.6 +/- 2.9, 45.2 +/- 2.5, 55.7 +/- 3.1, 65.4 +/- 2.3 years; its men by group are 10, 8, 6, 13, 9 and women 10, 12, 14, 7, 11, which sum to 46 men and 54 women, not 50 and 50 (see Gaps).
- Denominator for every value: 100 people x 5 levels (L1 to L5) = 500 vertebral bodies; each table row is n = 100 (Inference: no missing levels are reported).
- Inclusion (p. 3 (PDF 3)): age 20 to 70; standard lumbar spine CT; no lumbar surgery or trauma.
- Exclusion (p. 3 (PDF 3)): vertebral fracture on CT by a semiquantitative method; "congenital vertebral deformity or acquired short vertebral body"; spinal metal implants; drugs affecting bone metabolism (for example corticosteroids) or bone-metabolic disease (myeloma, rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, lupus, metabolic or endocrine disorders, bone tumours); adult degenerative scoliosis or severe osteophytosis. No count of screened or excluded people is given.
- Instrument (p. 3 (PDF 3)): Siemens Somatom Definition 64 AS+ spiral CT; acquisition slice 3.0 mm, detector collimation 128 x 0.6 mm, pitch 0.8, "scan angle" 0 deg (Inference: gantry tilt), 120 kV, spiral volumetric scanning, no contrast, the same scanner and kV for every subject. Reconstruction: 1 mm slices at 1 mm interval, kernel B60s (sharp), bone window; multiplanar reformation (MPR) on a syngo MMWP VE36A workstation. Tube current (mAs), field of view and pixel spacing are not given.
- Measures (p. 3 to 4 (PDF 3 to 4)): seven morphological ones (VBHa, VBHm, VBHp, EPWu, EPWl, EPTA, EPTP) and CT attenuation (HU) in 15 regions per body (3 sagittal thirds + 4 axial quadrants in each of the 3 thirds), after Banse et al. 2001.
- Observer (p. 3 (PDF 3)): one trained observer, named, blinded to subject characteristics; each parameter measured three times and averaged (morphology p. 3, HU p. 4 (PDF 4)).
- Statistics (p. 4 (PDF 4)): SPSS 23.0; mean +/- SD; Shapiro-Wilk, Levene; t-test or one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post hoc; Mann-Whitney U or Kruskal-Wallis when non-normal; p < 0.05. No sex-stratified analysis, no a priori power calculation and no adjustment for height, weight or BMI (limitations, p. 8 (PDF 8)); no analysis by age group is reported either, although Table 1 groups the sample by decade (Inference from the Results, p. 4 to 7 (PDF 4 to 7)).
- Levels covered: L1 to L5 only, the vertebral body only (no posterior elements); T12 and S1 are not measured.
- How levels were identified and counted: not stated. No counting rule (from C2, from the last rib or from the sacrum), no definition of L1 or L5, no word on transitional vertebrae. Inference: "congenital vertebral deformity" as an exclusion may have removed some transitional spines, but the paper does not say so.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts, cervical ribs or block vertebrae. Its only related statement is the exclusion of "congenital vertebral deformity or acquired short vertebral body" (p. 3 (PDF 3)), with no count and no definition.
- Inference: the reference values therefore describe five-lumbar-vertebra spines named by an unstated rule; an L5 that is partly sacralised, or a sixth lumbar segment, is not represented.

## Measures, each as a recipe

General conventions (p. 3 (PDF 3), Figure 1): the list of all seven parameters opens by saying they were measured on mid-sagittal images; a later sentence says all were measured on "mid-sagittal and mid-coronal planes" after Liu 2018 and Han 2023. Widths are defined as the "horizontal distance between the outermost lateral edges" of the endplate. The paper never says which parameter uses which plane; Inference: heights and cortical thicknesses on the mid-sagittal MPR, widths on the mid-coronal MPR (see Gaps). Figure 1A shows the three heights as vertical calipers on a sagittal slice; Figure 1B, labelled as the endplate widths, shows horizontal calipers on a sagittal slice (see Gaps); Figure 1C is a 3D sketch with a band on the superior endplate and two dashed lines marking where EPTA and EPTP are taken.

### Anterior vertebral body height (VBHa)

- Definition (p. 3 (PDF 3)): on the mid-sagittal image, the length of the body's front edge, from the upper end-plate's anterior rim down to the lower end-plate's anterior rim.
- Values, Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5), mm, n = 100 per level: L1 25.1 +/- 1.4; L2 26.5 +/- 1.3; L3 27.1 +/- 1.4; L4 27.6 +/- 0.7; L5 27.7 +/- 0.6.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Build the vertebra's own frame (craniocaudal axis normal to the mean endplate plane; AP axis from the body centroid toward the posterior-element centroid; LR the cross product). Take the body subregion in the plane through the body centroid normal to LR. Find the anterior-most points of the superior and inferior surfaces and measure their distance along the craniocaudal axis. Step 1 to 2 mm inward from the anterior-most voxel column, because the rounded anterior rim and partial volume shorten the height at the very edge; exclude osteophyte voxels (the reference excluded severe osteophytosis).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference (the paper uses CT only): lateral radiograph, the anterior height of six-point vertebral morphometry; absolute mm need magnification correction (the reference is true CT mm); the ratio VBHa/VBHp is magnification-free.
- Across levels: rises monotonically L1 to L5, steeply L1 to L2 (+1.4 mm) then flattening (L3 to L5 +0.6 mm in total); L4 and L5 differ by 0.1 mm, so VBHa alone does not separate L4 from L5 (Inference from Table 2).
- Sex, age, size, population: no breakdown. Chinese adults 20 to 70.
- Error: intra-observer ICC > 0.90 (all parameters, 20 vertebrae re-measured after 2 weeks); no inter-observer value (p. 3 (PDF 3); p. 8 (PDF 8)).

### Middle vertebral body height (VBHm)

- Definition (p. 3 (PDF 3)): on the mid-sagittal image, the length joining the centre of the upper end-plate to the centre of the lower one.
- Values, Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5), mm: L1 23.5 +/- 1.3; L2 24.3 +/- 1.4; L3 24.4 +/- 1.2; L4 23.7 +/- 1.2; L5 23.0 +/- 0.6.
- Always less than both VBHa and VBHp at every level (biconcave body), the authors' "slightly concave central region" (p. 6 (PDF 6)). Inference from Table 2: the mean rim height minus VBHm is 2.65, 3.00, 2.80, 3.25 and 3.15 mm at L1 to L5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the same mid-sagittal plane, at the AP midpoint of the endplate, the distance between the superior and inferior surfaces of the body mask along the craniocaudal axis. Schmorl nodes or endplate defects (not discussed in the paper) would shorten it.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference (the paper uses CT only): lateral radiograph, the middle height of six-point morphometry; the central endplate is blurred by the projected rims, especially with tilt; the paper does not discuss radiographs.
- Across levels: peaks at L2 to L3 (24.3, 24.4) and falls to its smallest at L5 (23.0). Ratio VBHm/VBHa (Inference from Table 2): 0.94, 0.92, 0.90, 0.86, 0.83 at L1 to L5, a size-free cue that falls steadily caudally.
- Sex, age, size, population: none given.
- Error: as above (ICC > 0.90 intra-observer only).

### Posterior vertebral body height (VBHp)

- Definition (p. 3 (PDF 3)): on the mid-sagittal image, the length of the body's back edge, upper end-plate's posterior rim to lower end-plate's posterior rim.
- Values, Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5), mm: L1 27.2 +/- 1.3; L2 28.1 +/- 2.6; L3 27.3 +/- 2.2; L4 26.3 +/- 2.1; L5 24.6 +/- 2.5.
- Anterior minus posterior (p. 5 to 7 (PDF 5 to 7)): posterior > anterior at L1 and L2 (L1 by 2.1 mm); about equal at L3 (27.1 vs 27.3); anterior > posterior at L4 and L5 (L5 by 3.1 mm). The Discussion gives the range as -2.1 mm at L1 to +3.1 mm at L5 (p. 7 (PDF 7)). Inference from Table 2: VBHa/VBHp = 0.92, 0.94, 0.99, 1.05, 1.13 at L1 to L5. The abstract says the L3 difference is not significant (p. 1 (PDF 1)); no p-value is given for the other levels.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. At the posterior wall of the body in the mid-sagittal plane (which passes through the canal, not the pedicles, so the wall is clean); step 1 to 2 mm anterior to the posterior-most voxel column to avoid the posterior rim and any marginal osteophyte. Where the segmenter gives no body/posterior-element split, the posterior wall is the anterior border of the canal in that plane.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference (the paper uses CT only): lateral radiograph; the ratio VBHa/VBHp is magnification-free.
- Across levels: peaks at L2 (28.1) and falls to its minimum at L5 (24.6); the L4 to L5 drop (1.7 mm) is the largest step (Inference from Table 2). Combined with VBHa, the wedge ratio separates L5 (1.13) from L4 (1.05) and L3 (0.99), and the upper lumbar levels (0.92 to 0.94) from the lower (Inference).
- Sex, age, size, population: none given.
- Error: ICC > 0.90 intra-observer; SD 2.1 to 2.6 mm at L2 to L5, larger than for VBHa.

### Superior endplate width (EPWu)

- Definition (p. 3 (PDF 3)): horizontal distance between the outermost lateral edges of the superior endplate. Plane: stated globally as mid-sagittal and mid-coronal; Inference: a mediolateral width can only come from the coronal or axial plane, and the values (42 to 51 mm) match a mediolateral width, not an AP depth.
- Values, Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5), mm: L1 41.8 +/- 1.0; L2 43.4 +/- 0.7; L3 45.2 +/- 0.6; L4 47.7 +/- 0.8; L5 50.7 +/- 2.4. Increase L1 to L5 21.3% (p. 5 (PDF 5); p. 7 (PDF 7)).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the vertebra's frame, take the slab of body voxels within 1 to 2 mm of the superior surface and measure the LR extent (the maximum LR Feret of that slab, or its extent on the coronal plane through the body centroid if one wants the paper's mid-coronal reading). Exclude lateral osteophytes; partial volume adds about one voxel.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference (the paper uses CT only): AP radiograph, the distance between the lateral margins of the body at the superior endplate; needs magnification correction for mm; axial rotation and lateral bending shorten it; ratios between adjacent levels are magnification-free.
- Across levels: rises at every step, by 1.6, 1.8, 2.5 and 3.0 mm from L1-L2 to L4-L5 (Inference from Table 2); the steps are larger than the printed SDs at L1 to L4, so in this cohort width ranks levels well, but the printed SDs look too small (see Error and Gaps).
- Sex, age, size, population: none given. The authors compare with Shrestha 2022 (a Nepalese anatomical study of dried lumbar vertebrae by its title, p. 9 (PDF 9); mean L5 width 42.59 mm), Liu 2018, Han 2023 and an MRI study at L4-L5 (p. 5 (PDF 5)).
- Error: ICC > 0.90 intra-observer only. Inference: SDs of 0.6 to 1.0 mm in a mixed-sex sample of 100 adults aged 20 to 70 are implausibly small; Panjabi's 12 autopsy spines give SEM 0.74 to 1.20 mm for lumbar EPWu, that is SD about 2.6 to 4.2 mm.

### Inferior endplate width (EPWl)

- Definition (p. 3 (PDF 3)): horizontal distance between the outermost lateral edges of the inferior endplate.
- Values, Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5), mm: L1 44.8 +/- 1.1; L2 45.7 +/- 0.8; L3 48.7 +/- 0.8; L4 51.3 +/- 1.7; L5 52.4 +/- 0.6. Increase L1 to L5 17.0% (p. 7 (PDF 7)).
- EPWl exceeds EPWu at every level, by 3.0, 2.3, 3.5, 3.6 and 1.7 mm at L1 to L5 (Inference from Table 2). The inferior endplate of each vertebra is also wider than the superior endplate of the one below (L1 inf 44.8 vs L2 sup 43.4, and so on to L4 inf 51.3 vs L5 sup 50.7).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: as EPWu, on the slab next to the inferior surface.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: AP radiograph, lateral margins at the inferior endplate; as EPWu.
- Across levels: steps of 0.9, 3.0, 2.6 and 1.1 mm (L1-L2 to L4-L5) (Inference from Table 2). Inference: the two small steps separate poorly, L1 from L2 (0.9 mm against SD 1.1 at L1) and L4 from L5 (1.1 mm against SD 1.7 at L4); the middle steps (L2-L3, L3-L4) are the informative ones.
- Sex, age, size, population: none given.
- Error: as EPWu.

### Anterior 1/3 cortical thickness of the superior endplate (EPTA)

- Definition (p. 3 (PDF 3)): the superior endplate is divided into three equal parts (AP) and the "vertical cortical bone thickness" is measured in the anterior third, mid-sagittal.
- Values, Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5), mm: 0.8 at every level, SD 0.1 at L1 to L2 and 0.2 at L3 to L5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: not possible from a binary mask. Inference: take the CT intensity profile along the craniocaudal axis through the centre of the anterior third of the superior endplate, inside the mask, and measure the high-attenuation layer (for example full width at half maximum); the paper gives no threshold or window rule.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible.
- Across levels: flat; no level information.
- Error: ICC > 0.90 intra-observer. Inference: 0.8 to 1.0 mm is at the resolution of 1 mm reconstructed slices with a sharp kernel; partial volume dominates.

### Posterior 1/3 cortical thickness of the superior endplate (EPTP)

- Definition (p. 3 (PDF 3)): as EPTA, in the posterior third.
- Values, Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5), mm: L1 0.9 +/- 0.2; L2 1.0 +/- 0.2; L3 1.0 +/- 0.2; L4 0.9 +/- 0.2; L5 1.0 +/- 0.2.
- EPTA < EPTP at all levels, p < 0.05 (abstract p. 1 (PDF 1); Results p. 5 (PDF 5)); the Discussion calls it a 10 to 20% difference, the same at every level, and gives the ranges as EPTA 0.8 +/- 0.1 to 0.8 +/- 0.2 mm and EPTP 0.9 +/- 0.2 to 1.0 +/- 0.2 mm (p. 6 (PDF 6); p. 7 (PDF 7)). The Results text says EPTP 0.9 +/- 0.2 at all levels (p. 5), which does not match Table 2 (see Gaps). The authors read the thinner anterior cortex as the anterior endplate being mechanically weaker and more fracture-prone (p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- Mask, radiograph, levels, error: as EPTA.

### CT attenuation in sagittal thirds (HU, upper, middle, lower third)

- Definition (p. 4 (PDF 4), Figures 2 and 3A): the body is divided into upper, middle and lower thirds in the sagittal plane; a circular or elliptical ROI of about 1 cm^2 is placed in each, avoiding cortex, the basivertebral foramen, osteophytes and calcifications; mean HU in the ROI, three measurements averaged; ROI size and shape kept "as uniform as possible" across regions and levels. Figure 3A (p. 4 (PDF 4)) actually shows three stacked rectangular ROIs in the centre of the body on a slice labelled "Sagittal view", with printed areas 123.0, 107.5 and 110.1 mm^2. Its annotation boxes give, for that one unnamed example, mean 162, 126 and 217 HU (upper, middle, lower, matched by the leader lines) with an in-ROI SD of 56.6, 69.9 and 72.9 HU; in that example the middle third is the lowest, unlike the cohort means.
- Values, Table 3, p. 5 (PDF 5), HU, upper / middle / lower: L1 167.4 +/- 26.6 / 174.3 +/- 21.2 / 180.6 +/- 20.1; L2 162.9 +/- 30.4 / 165.1 +/- 29.2 / 172.3 +/- 13.3; L3 156.5 +/- 24.6 / 158.9 +/- 17.5 / 165.8 +/- 14.5; L4 153.9 +/- 21.2 / 157.2 +/- 13.2 / 164.3 +/- 10.9; L5 158.7 +/- 24.2 / 160.8 +/- 13.9 / 166.6 +/- 8.4. Figure 4 (p. 6 (PDF 6)) plots the same.
- Pattern: upper < middle < lower at every level; lower > upper p < 0.05 (p. 5 (PDF 5)). Gradient 13 HU (7.9%) at L1 and 10.4 HU (6.8%) at L4 (p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Erode the body subregion by 2 to 3 mm to drop the cortex, split it into thirds along the vertebra's craniocaudal axis, and on the mid-sagittal plane take a 1 cm^2 central ROI in each third; avoid the posterior midline at mid-height (basivertebral foramen). A volume mean of each third will differ from the paper's single ROI.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (no calibrated density).
- Across levels: the authors say the levels "roughly" follow L1 > L2 > L5 > L3 > L4 (abstract, p. 1 (PDF 1); p. 5 to 6 (PDF 5 to 6)); Table 3 shows that order exactly in all three thirds (Inference from Table 3). L1 vs L4 in the upper third 13.5 HU (8.1%), p < 0.05 (p. 6 (PDF 6)); no other level contrast is tested. Inference from Table 3: adjacent-level gaps are 1.5 to 9.2 HU (L3 to L4 is the smallest, 1.5 to 2.6 HU) and the widest gap, L1 to L4, is 13.5 HU (upper), 17.1 HU (middle) and 16.3 HU (lower), while the between-person SDs are 8.4 to 30.4 HU, so HU cannot name a level on its own.
- Sex, age, size, population: no breakdown; the authors cite Liu 2005 (168 healthy men, BMD falling from L1 to L4 in every age group; a Chinese journal by its title, p. 9 (PDF 9)) as agreeing (p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- Error: no reliability statistic specific to HU beyond the global ICC > 0.90 (Inference: the ICC sentence sits under the morphology section, p. 3, and may not cover HU). HU depend on kernel (B60s), kV (120) and contrast; no phantom calibration (p. 3 (PDF 3); p. 8 (PDF 8)).

### CT attenuation in axial quadrants (12 regions)

- Definition (p. 4 (PDF 4), Figures 2 and 3B to D): in each third, an axial slice divided into four quadrants: Q1 left anterior, Q2 right anterior, Q3 left posterior, Q4 right posterior (Table 6 footnote, p. 7 (PDF 7)); one ROI per quadrant, as above. Which axial slice within each third is used is not stated. Figure 3B to D (p. 4 (PDF 4)) shows rectangular ROIs of visibly different sizes; the annotation boxes (low resolution, readings approximate) print areas of about 85 to 211 mm^2, against the stated 1 cm^2. In the figure the quadrant-1 ROI sits at the image left and anterior, quadrant 2 at the image right and anterior, quadrants 3 and 4 behind them, and the middle-third slices pass through the pedicles.
- Values (Tables 4 to 6, p. 6 to 7 (PDF 6 to 7)), the ones that matter:
  - Lowest region of the 12 at every level is an upper-third anterior quadrant: L1 Q2 157.5 +/- 30.9; L2 Q2 151.7 +/- 26.7; L3 Q2 149.3 +/- 23.9; L4 Q1 146.7 +/- 17.8 (lowest of all); L5 Q1 152.0 +/- 27.7.
  - Highest region at every level is a lower-third posterior quadrant: L1 Q4 185.5 +/- 16.1; L2 Q4 179.2 +/- 26.4; L3 Q3 172.9 +/- 21.8; L4 Q4 168.6 +/- 20.1; L5 Q3 174.0 +/- 20.4.
  - Upper third, L1: posterior Q3 174.8 +/- 23.3 and Q4 175.3 +/- 23.3 vs anterior Q1 158.1 +/- 26.2 and Q2 157.5 +/- 30.9, p < 0.01 (p. 5 (PDF 5); p. 7 (PDF 7)).
  - Upper third, L5: anterior Q1 152.0 +/- 27.7, Q2 156.9 +/- 21.8 vs posterior Q3 164.0 +/- 26.9, Q4 167.2 +/- 24.5, not significant (p. 7 (PDF 7)).
  - Inference from Tables 4 to 6: posterior-minus-anterior mean gap in the upper third is 17.3, 16.5, 14.6, 14.2 and 11.2 HU at L1 to L5; in the middle third 16.1, 14.8, 14.0, 10.6, 12.4; in the lower third 11.0, 9.8, 11.2, 9.2, 11.3. The gradient is largest in the upper third and at L1 to L2.
  - Left vs right differences are small: 29 of the 30 left-right pairs differ by under 4 HU, the largest 4.9 HU (L5 upper anterior) (Inference from Tables 4 to 6); the paper draws no conclusion on side.
  - Level order per quadrant (Inference from Tables 4 to 6): L1 > L2 > L5 > L3 > L4 holds in 11 of the 12 regions; in the upper-third right-anterior quadrant (Q2) L5 (156.9) is above L2 (151.7).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. On the axial plane of the vertebra's frame at the centre of each third, split the eroded body by its mid-sagittal and mid-coronal lines; a 1 cm^2 ROI centred in each quadrant, avoiding the posterior midline (basivertebral foramen). Fix "left" as patient left; the paper's convention is unclear (see Gaps).
- From a radiograph alone: not possible.
- Error: as above; quadrant SDs run from 8.5 (L4 upper Q4) to 37.6 (L3 upper Q3).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1 (thoracolumbar junction): posterior height exceeds anterior by 2.1 mm (27.2 vs 25.1), narrowest endplates (41.8 sup, 44.8 inf), highest HU in every third; the authors link the high HU to "relatively stable stress" at the thoracolumbar junction (speculative) (p. 5 to 6 (PDF 5 to 6)). The authors use L1 as their example of the upper-third anterior-posterior HU gap, p < 0.01 (p. 5 (PDF 5); p. 7 (PDF 7)); Inference from Table 4: the gap is in fact largest at L1 (17.3 HU). The Discussion also says L1 "primarily bears the weight above the thorax" (p. 5 (PDF 5)), an odd phrase left unexplained.
- L2: largest VBHp (28.1 +/- 2.6); still posterior > anterior (by 1.6 mm, Inference); second-highest HU (p. 5 (PDF 5)).
- L3: anterior and posterior heights about equal (27.1 vs 27.3), not significant; VBHm at its largest (24.4); HU fourth of five; largest quadrant SD (upper Q3 37.6) (p. 5 (PDF 5); p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- L4: anterior > posterior (27.6 vs 26.3); lowest HU in every third (153.9, 157.2, 164.3); the anterosuperior quadrant (146.7 +/- 17.8) is the lowest region measured, still above the 100 HU threshold in these healthy adults (p. 7 (PDF 7)). The authors suggest L3 to L4, as the centre of lumbar motion, take the greatest dynamic loads with faster bone turnover (speculative, p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- L5 (lumbosacral junction): widest endplates (50.7 sup, 52.4 inf), most wedged (anterior exceeds posterior by 3.1 mm; VBHp 24.6 and VBHm 23.0 are the smallest), largest EPWu SD (2.4). The authors call L5 the "keystone" of the lumbosacral junction under the highest cumulative load, to explain its wide endplates (p. 5 (PDF 5)). Its HU rebound above L3 and L4 they explain by its larger size and "frequent reactive osteosclerosis" (p. 6 (PDF 6)). Upper-third anterior-posterior HU gap smaller (anterior Q1 152.0, Q2 156.9 vs posterior Q3 164.0, Q4 167.2) and not significant (p. 5 (PDF 5); p. 7 (PDF 7)).
- C2/C3, C7/T1, T12/L1 below L1, L5/S1 below L5: no measurement; S1 and T12 not measured.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Intra-observer: one observer, 20 randomly chosen vertebrae re-measured after 2 weeks; ICC > 0.90 for all parameters; exact ICCs not given (p. 3 (PDF 3)).
- Inter-observer: not evaluated, acknowledged (p. 8 (PDF 8)).
- Each value is the mean of three measurements (p. 3 to 4 (PDF 3 to 4)).
- Instrument: 1 mm slices, 1 mm interval, B60s; pixel spacing not given. Inference: cortical thickness (0.8 to 1.0 mm) is at or below the effective resolution.
- HU: kernel, kV and contrast standardised; no phantom calibration, no DXA comparison (p. 3 (PDF 3); p. 8 (PDF 8)).
- Between-person SD (Table 2) is unusually small for widths and VBHa at L4 to L5 (0.6 to 1.0 mm); see Gaps.
- HU noise inside one ROI: the single example of Figure 3A prints an in-ROI SD of 56.6 to 72.9 HU (p. 4 (PDF 4)). Inference: a 1 cm^2 ROI mean is stable, but single voxels are not, so any mask-based reading should average over a region, as the paper does.
- All measurements were manual; the authors name automated deep-learning segmentation (Baur 2022, as cited) as a way to improve reproducibility (p. 8 (PDF 8)).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Cage sizing (p. 2 (PDF 2); p. 7 (PDF 7)): endplate width guides interbody cage size; a cage sized for L1 (about 42 mm) is undersized for L5 (about 51 mm), risking poor endplate coverage and subsidence.
- Thin anterior endplate cortex (0.8 mm vs 0.9 to 1.0 mm posteriorly): avoid excessive distraction or compression during surgery to prevent endplate breach (p. 7 (PDF 7)). Zhao 2009 is cited for the anterior endplate failing first under compression (p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- The anterosuperior quadrant is a low-density "stress-weak zone", matching the usual site of osteoporotic compression fractures (superior and anterior parts of the body) (p. 1 to 2 (PDF 1 to 2); p. 7 (PDF 7)).
- HU thresholds: a mid-sagittal (or mean lumbar) CT value below 100 HU marks poor bone quality and is proposed as a threshold for cement augmentation or more robust fixation (Elarjani 2021, Zhang 2023, as cited, p. 7 (PDF 7)). In healthy adults the lowest region (L4 anterosuperior, 146.7 HU) stays above it, but in osteoporosis that region may fall well below 100 HU even when the global value looks acceptable (p. 7 (PDF 7)).
- HU correlates with DXA T-score and predicts screw loosening, cut-out and vertebral fracture after fixation (Elarjani 2021, as cited, p. 2 (PDF 2)), and screw loosening, cage subsidence and adjacent-segment fracture (Zhou 2021, Zhang 2023, as cited, p. 7 (PDF 7)). Most CT-HU work reads one or a few mid-body ROIs and so misses the heterogeneity inside the body (p. 2 (PDF 2)).
- Morphology and bone quality act together: a wide body with very low density is a poor foundation for fixation, and a body of acceptable density with a weak endplate cortex may still fail early (p. 2 (PDF 2)).
- The roughly 20% widening of the endplates from L1 to L5 is read as spreading the rising axial load (Hussein 2013, Ashish 2023, as cited, p. 5 (PDF 5)); see Gaps on area against width.
- Vertebral body height is preserved in disc degeneration while disc height falls (an MRI study, Nisanci and Yildiz 2026, as cited, p. 2 (PDF 2)). Inference: body heights are the steadier level cue in a degenerate spine.
- The superior part of the body has lower density and fewer, more widely spaced trabeculae on HR-pQCT (as cited, p. 6 (PDF 6)); the same craniocaudal inhomogeneity is reported in osteoporotic compression-fracture patients (Guan 2023, as cited, p. 6 (PDF 6)) and in earlier CT-HU studies (Wang 2022, in single-level disc herniation by its title, and Li 2011, as cited, p. 6 (PDF 6); titles p. 9 to 10 (PDF 9 to 10)).
- Automated reading: a 3D U-Net applied to low-dose chest CT has already given vertebral morphology in a Chinese population, with sex- and age-related trends in vertebral height ratios (Ma 2024, as cited, p. 2 (PDF 2)). Inference: height ratios measured from segmentations are expected to vary with sex and age, which this paper does not report.
- Screw trajectory: intravertebral density distribution (lower anterosuperior density) affects screw purchase and pullout (p. 2 (PDF 2)); the anterosuperior region at L3 to L4 is the critical area for augmentation or screw fixation in osteoporosis (p. 7 (PDF 7)).
- Vertebroplasty and kyphoplasty: aim cement at the anterosuperior zone to restore anterior column support, rather than simply filling the central cavity (p. 7 (PDF 7)).
- Finite-element models should use regional rather than uniform density (Oefner 2022, as cited, p. 7 (PDF 7)).
- Load sharing: the anterior column bears about 80% of the stress (Li 2024, as cited, p. 6 (PDF 6)); postmenopausal women with more heterogeneous body heights are at higher risk of incident lumbar fracture independent of areal BMD (Roux 2016, OFELY, as cited, p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- Lordosis: posterior-higher upper lumbar bodies turning to anterior-higher lower lumbar bodies form the wedge basis of lumbar lordosis (p. 6 (PDF 6); p. 8 (PDF 8)); the authors call this consistent with an MRI study of adult Egyptians (Hegazy and Hegazy 2014) and an anatomical study (Hou 2013), as cited (p. 6 (PDF 6); reference titles p. 9 (PDF 9)).
- Pitfalls of measuring: contrast changes HU, so only non-contrast scans; kernel and slice thickness change HU (p. 3 (PDF 3)); ROIs must avoid cortex, the basivertebral foramen, osteophytes and calcifications (p. 4 (PDF 4)); CT HU are not DXA BMD and vary with scanner, kV and contrast (p. 8 (PDF 8)).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Measure VBHa, VBHm and VBHp on the mid-sagittal plane of each lumbar body (CT), as the paper defines them (p. 3 (PDF 3)), and form VBHa/VBHp; from the Table 2 means expect about 0.92 at L1, 0.94 at L2, 0.99 at L3, 1.05 at L4 and 1.13 at L5 in Chinese adults without fracture (ratios computed from Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5); Inference). Inference: on a true lateral radiograph use the ratio, not mm, since it cancels magnification; the paper does not study radiographs.
2. Inference from Table 2 and p. 5 to 7 (PDF 5 to 7): treat the body with the strongest anterior-over-posterior excess (about +3 mm, VBHp the smallest of the five) as the L5 candidate and the bodies with posterior > anterior as upper lumbar (L1 to L2); the paper does not propose this rule, its VBHp SD is 2.1 to 2.6 mm at L2 to L5, and it never checked numbering, so confirm with the sacrum and the count from C2.
3. Expect VBHm to be about 2.6 to 3.3 mm below the mean of the anterior and posterior heights at every level (computed from Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5); the authors note VBHm is the smallest of the three at every level, p. 6 (PDF 6)); if VBHm equals or exceeds the rims, re-check the landmarks or look for an endplate lesion (Inference).
4. Inference from Table 2 and p. 5 to 6 (PDF 5 to 6): do not call a fracture for an L1 or L2 anterior height 1.5 to 2 mm (about 6 to 8%) below posterior height; that wedge is the cohort mean in spines screened free of fracture. The paper's fracture screen was a semiquantitative grade whose thresholds it does not print (p. 3 (PDF 3)).
5. On the coronal CT plane, measure EPWu and EPWl between the outermost lateral edges of each endplate (p. 3 (PDF 3)); expect a rise at every step from L1 to L5 (EPWu 41.8 to 50.7 mm, EPWl 44.8 to 52.4 mm) and EPWl > EPWu within each body (Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5)). Inference: on an AP radiograph take the same lateral margins, compare adjacent-level ratios rather than absolute mm, and correct for axial rotation.
6. Inference from Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5): do not rely on EPWl to tell L4 from L5 (step 1.1 mm against SD 1.7 at L4) or L1 from L2 (0.9 mm against SD 1.1), nor on VBHa to tell L4 from L5 (step 0.1 mm); use the wedge ratio VBHa/VBHp and EPWu (L4 to L5 step 3.0 mm) instead.
7. Inference: take width as the mediolateral span on the coronal plane, not the AP span drawn in Figure 1B (p. 3 (PDF 3)); the paper names both mid-sagittal and mid-coronal planes without assigning them, Figure 1B's sagittal calipers read 29.6 and 30.3 mm, and the Table 2 widths (41.8 to 52.4 mm) only fit a mediolateral span. An AP span read as a width would mimic a much smaller vertebra.
8. Expect the reference to apply only to spines without fracture, congenital deformity or short body, implants, bone-metabolic disease or drugs, degenerative scoliosis or severe osteophytosis (p. 3 (PDF 3)); Inference: remove osteophytes from a mask before taking any width or height, since the reference had none of note.
9. Do not use absolute HU to name a level: the mean order L1 > L2 > L5 > L3 > L4 holds in every third (Table 3, p. 5 (PDF 5)), but adjacent-level gaps are 1.5 to 9.2 HU and even L1 to L4 only 13.5 to 17.1 HU, against between-person SDs of 8.4 to 30.4 HU (Inference from Tables 3 to 6, p. 5 to 7 (PDF 5 to 7)). Inference: within one patient the HU profile along the column (falling from L1 to a trough at L3 to L4, rising again at L5) may be a weak tie-breaker, and only on non-contrast scans.
10. Read HU only on non-contrast CT with a stated kernel; put ROIs of about 1 cm^2 inside the trabecular bone, away from cortex, the basivertebral foramen and osteophytes, and average three placements (p. 3 to 4 (PDF 3 to 4)).
11. When assessing bone quality for fixation or cement, read the anterosuperior quadrant of each body, not only the mid-body: it is the lowest region at every level (Tables 4 to 6, p. 6 to 7 (PDF 6 to 7)), and a value below 100 HU flags poor bone by the threshold the authors cite (p. 7 (PDF 7)).
12. Do not measure endplate cortical thickness to name levels: it is flat (0.8 anterior, 0.9 to 1.0 posterior mm) across L1 to L5 (Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5)); Inference: it sits at the resolution limit of 1 mm CT.
13. Treat these values as Chinese mixed-sex adult means aged 20 to 70; the paper gives no sex, age, height or weight breakdown, so widen the spread before using them as priors (p. 8 (PDF 8)).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper reports no LSTV, L6, T13, rib-count or block-vertebra prevalence, and excluded "congenital vertebral deformity or acquired short vertebral body" without a count or definition (p. 3 (PDF 3)). Inference: a decoder should not take its absence of anomalies as a prevalence of zero; the cohort was filtered and the numbering rule is unknown.

(b) Measures:
- The generator already holds EPWu, EPWl and VBHp from Panjabi's lumbar paper (panjabi_long.csv, n = 12 autopsy spines, means with SEM). For comparison:
  - EPWu L1 to L5, Panjabi 41.2, 42.6, 44.1, 46.6, 47.3 mm; this paper 41.8, 43.4, 45.2, 47.7, 50.7 mm. Close at L1 to L4 (0.6 to 1.1 mm larger here), 3.4 mm larger at L5.
  - EPWl, Panjabi 43.3, 45.5, 48.0, 49.5, 49.4; this paper 44.8, 45.7, 48.7, 51.3, 52.4. Here the L4 to L5 step is +1.1 mm; Panjabi's is -0.1 mm.
  - VBHp, Panjabi 23.8, 24.3, 23.8, 24.1, 22.9; this paper 27.2, 28.1, 27.3, 26.3, 24.6, about 1.7 to 3.8 mm larger. Inference: Panjabi's posterior height and this paper's (posterior edge to posterior edge on mid-sagittal CT, including the posterior rim) are not defined identically, and living CT vs autopsy specimens and population may add to it; a mask-derived VBHp will sit nearer to whichever definition the code copies.
- Measures the generator could add from this paper: VBHa and VBHm (Panjabi's lumbar table holds neither), and the size-free ratios VBHa/VBHp and VBHm/VBHa, which change monotonically L1 to L5 and are the best L4/L5 separators here (Inference from Table 2). Inference: with Panjabi's upper endplate depth EPDu (34.1 to 35.5 mm at L1 to L5 in panjabi_long.csv), the mean anterior-minus-posterior height difference here corresponds to a body wedge of about -3.5 deg at L1 (posterior higher) and +5.1 deg at L5 (anterior higher), a quantity the generator could draw directly.
- Endplate depth: the generator holds EPDu and EPDl (Panjabi, 33.2 to 35.5 mm); this paper reports no depth. Its Figure 1B, one unnamed example on a sagittal slice, shows AP spans of 29.6 and 30.3 mm (p. 3 (PDF 3)), which is no reference value.
- EPTA/EPTP and HU are not level discriminators here (flat thickness; HU gaps smaller than the spread) and are not morphometric dimensions of the generator (Inference from Tables 2 to 6).
- Definition differences that bias mask values (Inference): (1) the paper's widths are read on a single mid-coronal plane, a mask's maximum LR extent of the endplate slab will be equal or slightly larger; (2) heights at the very anterior or posterior edge of a mask are shortened by rounded rims and partial volume, so step inward; (3) the paper excluded severe osteophytosis, masks include osteophytes unless removed; (4) Figure 1B shows an AP span, so do not copy the figure.

(c) Measurement error: only intra-observer ICC > 0.90 (no SEM, no inter-observer). The printed SDs for EPWu, EPWl and VBHa (0.6 to 1.7 mm, except 2.4 at L5 EPWu) are smaller than Panjabi's implied SDs (SEM x sqrt(12), about 2.6 to 4.9 mm for lumbar EPWu/EPWl); Inference: do not use these SDs as the population spread; use them, if at all, only for the means' shape.

(d) Covariates: none given by sex, age, height, weight or BMI; population is Chinese (Henan), 20 to 70, about half women (46/54 by Table 1, 50/50 by text). The paper gives nothing on body size.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Sex counts: text 50 men and 50 women (p. 1, p. 4 (PDF 1, 4)); Table 1 sums to 46 men and 54 women (p. 5 (PDF 5)).
- Age distribution: the limitation calls it uneven, "only 20 participants in the 41-50 age group" (p. 8 (PDF 8)), but Table 1 has exactly 20 in every decade (p. 5 (PDF 5)).
- Consent: "all volunteers provided written informed consent" (p. 3 (PDF 3)) vs the ethics statement that consent was waived for a retrospective analysis (p. 8 (PDF 8)); "volunteers" were "retrospectively enrolled".
- L5 quadrants: Results say the L5 upper-third anterior quadrants were slightly higher than the posterior ones (p. 5 (PDF 5)); Table 4 (p. 6) and the Discussion (p. 7 (PDF 7)) show them lower (152.0 and 156.9 vs 164.0 and 167.2).
- L4 gradient called "even more pronounced" than L1's (p. 6 (PDF 6)), yet 10.4 HU (6.8%) is smaller than L1's 13 HU (7.9%).
- The Discussion says the anterior-posterior gradient was "most pronounced" at L3 and L4 (p. 7 (PDF 7)); Inference from Table 4: the upper-third gap is largest at L1 and L2 (17.3, 16.5 HU) and smaller at L3 and L4 (14.6, 14.2 HU).
- The Discussion says the anterosuperior region is "often approaching or falling below" the 100 HU threshold (p. 7 (PDF 7)); in this cohort its lowest mean is 146.7 +/- 17.8 HU (L4, Table 4, p. 6 (PDF 6)), so the statement is about osteoporotic patients not measured here.
- The Discussion speaks of endplate "area" increasing about 20% from L1 to L5 (p. 5 (PDF 5)); only widths were measured, and the 21.3% and 17.0% figures are width increases.
- The HR-pQCT finding is cited to "Liu et al., 2018" (p. 6 (PDF 6)), but the only Liu 2018 in the reference list is a CT study of lumbar endplate morphology (p. 9 (PDF 9)). Inference: a citation error.
- Scanner: a "Somatom Definition 64 AS+" with 128 x 0.6 mm collimation (p. 3 (PDF 3)). Inference: the model name and the collimation do not obviously match; the reconstruction (1 mm, B60s) is what matters for the values.
- Limitations the authors admit (p. 8 (PDF 8)): single centre, n = 100, no power analysis, no sex-stratified analysis, no height, weight or BMI, manual measurement with intra-observer reliability only, no DXA comparison, and no validation of the regional HU pattern against biomechanical testing or fracture outcomes.
- The Discussion gives an anterior-posterior HU difference of about 16 to 17 HU, consistent from L1 to L4 (p. 7 (PDF 7)); Inference from Table 4: 17.3 and 16.5 at L1 and L2 but 14.6 and 14.2 at L3 and L4.
- The Results text gives EPTP as 0.9 +/- 0.2 mm and EPTA as 0.8 +/- 0.1 mm at all levels (p. 5 (PDF 5)) vs Table 2: EPTP 1.0 at L2, L3, L5 and EPTA SD 0.2 at L3 to L5.
- Plane of the widths: "measured on mid-sagittal images (Figure 1)" opens the list (p. 3 (PDF 3)), later "mid-sagittal and mid-coronal planes"; Figure 1B labels the endplate widths on a sagittal slice with calipers of 29.6 and 30.3 mm, an AP depth, while Table 2 widths (41.8 to 52.4 mm) fit a mediolateral width. Inference: the figure shows the wrong plane or a depth.
- Figure 1A example calipers: Inference from the leader lines, anterior 30.9 mm, middle 25.2 mm, posterior 29.6 mm, an anterior height above every Table 2 mean (max 27.7 +/- 0.6); it is one unnamed example.
- ROIs: "circular or elliptical", about 1 cm^2, uniform (p. 4 (PDF 4)) vs Figure 3: rectangular ROIs of differing size (sagittal ones labelled 107.5 to 123.0 mm^2, axial ones about 85 to 211 mm^2 by their low-resolution annotation boxes).
- Left and right: Figure 2 draws quadrant 1 (left anterior) at the upper left of the body; Figure 3B places the first ROI on the image left. Inference: in radiological display that is the patient's right, so "left" may mean image-left; the paper does not say.
- Citations: a sentence attributes the 100 HU threshold to "Elarjani et al" but cites Zhang 2023 (p. 7 (PDF 7)); the semiquantitative fracture method is cited to Elarjani 2021 (p. 3 (PDF 3)) while Genant 1993, the semiquantitative method, is cited for reconstruction parameters. Inference: references appear shifted.
- Shrestha 2022's L5 width of 42.59 mm is called consistent (p. 5 (PDF 5)) though this paper's L5 EPWu is 50.7 mm; Inference: the two measure different things or populations.
- Unreported: how levels were identified, any transitional anomaly, sex and age breakdowns, height and weight, pixel spacing and mAs, endplate depth, pedicle, canal or facet measures, statistical tests of adjacent-level morphology, ICC per parameter, HU-specific reliability, number screened and excluded. The data are said to be in the article or supplementary material (p. 8 (PDF 8)); no individual data are printed.
- Inference from Panjabi (bordercall's table): VBHp here is 1.7 to 3.8 mm larger and EPWu/EPWl SDs are about a third of Panjabi's implied SDs; Panjabi has EPWl flat from L4 to L5 where this paper has it rising.
