Source: thread "Reread every paper for clinical detail", project symphony_4, 2026-10-28.

Tomography 2026, 12, 109 (article 109, 16 pages). https://doi.org/10.3390/tomography12080109. Received 3 June 2026, revised 14 July 2026, accepted 16 July 2026, published 28 July 2026 (p. 1 (PDF 1)). ## What the paper is

- Design: retrospective, single-centre, observational review of CT scans taken 1 January 2021 to 31 January 2025; ethics committee approval, protocol 2026/31-25; consent waived (p. 3 (PDF 3); p. 15 (PDF 15)).
- Setting: a tertiary chest diseases and thoracic surgery training hospital in Izmir, Turkey (affiliations p. 1 (PDF 1); the authors call it a specialised tertiary thoracic centre, p. 14 (PDF 14)).
- Population: 517 living adult patients, 277 men and 240 women, described only as Turkish; mean age 48.6 +/- 16.4 years, all >= 18 years, upper age not given (p. 3, p. 6 (PDF 3, 6)). Age groups: 18 to 35 years n = 198, 36 to 55 n = 176, > 55 n = 143 (sum 517), not split by sex (p. 7 (PDF 7)).
- Indications: thoracic or abdominal CT for non-spinal, non-oncological reasons, for example interstitial lung disease, occupational pneumoconiosis screening and pulmonary embolism protocols (p. 3 (PDF 3)). The abstract says thoracic CT only (p. 1 (PDF 1)).
- Exclusions (p. 3 (PDF 3); Figure 1, p. 4 (PDF 4)): prior thoracic or spinal surgery, chest wall intervention or instrumentation; iatrogenic rib resection or rib spreading (Figure 1 only); pathological bone (primary or metastatic tumour; myeloma is named in Figure 1 only); congenital spinal deformity; major traumatic fracture; advanced degenerative disc disease; spondylolysis; spondylolisthesis; scoliosis with Cobb angle > 10 deg; motion artefact or incomplete field of view. The STROBE diagram (Figure 1) calls the patients "consecutive" adults and gives no number screened or excluded; which field of view counted as complete is not defined.
- Objectives (p. 3 (PDF 3)): (1) normal linear and angular dimensions of T11 to L2 and their pedicles as surgical baselines; (2) the prevalence of congenital rib anomalies and their bearing on wrong-level surgery; (3) sex patterns for forensic identification.
- Instrument: 64-slice MDCT (Philips Brilliance CT), 120 kVp, automatic tube current modulation 150 to 250 mA, supine, contiguous axial slices 0.625 mm thick; multiplanar reformats and 3D reconstructions on a PACS workstation and IntelliSpace Portal 11.1; all reads in a bone window, width 1500 HU, level 300 HU (p. 3 to 4 (PDF 3 to 4)).
- Calibration: the landmark protocol was rehearsed on a pilot of 30 random scans that were left out of the cohort (p. 4 (PDF 4)).
- Levels: T11, T12, L1, L2 only, the authors' definition of the thoracolumbar junction (TLJ) (p. 1, p. 2, p. 4 (PDF 1, 2, 4)). Ribs: the CTs were searched for "congenital numerical and structural rib anomalies" (p. 5 (PDF 5)); the extent searched is not stated. Inference: the whole field of view was read, since cervical, 3rd and 12th ribs appear in the figures (Figures 4 and 5, pp. 9 to 10).
- Measures: anterior, middle and posterior corpus heights (ACH, MCH, PCH); transverse pedicle diameter (TPD); sagittal pedicle diameter (SPD); pedicle length (PL); transverse pedicle angle (TPA) (p. 4 (PDF 4)); rib anomalies (p. 5 (PDF 5)).
- Observer: one radiologist with 14 years' experience made every measurement and every anomaly call (p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- How levels were identified and counted: not stated anywhere. The paper gives no counting rule (from C1/C2 or the first rib downward, or from the sacrum upward), no definition of T12 as the last rib-bearing vertebra, and no transverse-process, facet or rib-head criterion for telling T12 from L1. It recorded an "absent 12th rib" and a "lumbar rib" as separate categories; Inference: these only make sense if T12 and L1 were fixed by something other than the ribs, but what that was is not said. Transitional or numeric variants (T13, eleven thoracic vertebrae, L6, LSTV) are neither counted nor said to be excluded; "congenital spinal deformities" is an exclusion (p. 3 (PDF 3)) and may or may not have removed them (Inference).
- Statistics: SPSS 26; normality by Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Shapiro-Wilk and Q-Q plots; independent t-test for sex; paired t-test for right versus left; one-way ANOVA with Tukey for age groups; Pearson chi-square or Fisher for anomaly rates; intraclass correlation for repeatability; p < 0.05; no multiplicity correction, acknowledged (p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- Inference: the 95% intervals in Table 1 match n = 277 and n = 240 at every level (checked at T11 ACH and L2 PCH), so every patient seems to have had all four levels measured, including those scanned for an abdominal or a thoracic indication alone.
- Side: Table 2 gives one value per level and sex without saying whether it is the right side, the left side or their mean. Inference: probably the mean; the side means of Table 3 average to Table 2's sex-pooled mean within 0.01 mm at L1 and L2, 0.03 mm at T11 and 0.08 mm at T12.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- Definitions (p. 5 (PDF 5)): presence, absence and length of the 12th rib were recorded; a hypoplastic rib is one "less than 4 cm in length"; bifid ribs, foramen ribs and supernumerary (cervical or lumbar) ribs were logged "by incidence and laterality". How rib length was measured (straight or along the curve, from the head or the tubercle) is not stated. All calls were made on CT (axial, MPR, 3D); radiographs appear only as illustrations.
- Prevalence, Table 5, p. 10 (PDF 10), text p. 8 to 9 (PDF 8 to 9), Figure 6 p. 10; denominators 517 patients, 277 men, 240 women, all Turkish adults at one chest hospital:
  - Hypoplastic 12th rib: 55/517 (10.64%); men 25/277 (9.03%); women 30/240 (12.50%); chi-square 0.982, p = 0.322. The most frequent anomaly.
  - Absent 12th rib: 22/517 (4.26%); men 4/277 (1.44%); women 18/240 (7.50%); chi-square 11.231, p = 0.001.
  - Lumbar rib: 12/517 (2.32%); men 10/277 (3.61%); women 2/240 (0.83%); chi-square 4.412, p = 0.036.
  - Cervical rib: 7/517 (1.35%); men 1/277 (0.36%); women 6/240 (2.50%); chi-square 4.021, p = 0.045.
  - Any rib anomaly: 96/517 (18.57%); men 40/277 (14.44%); women 56/240 (23.33%); chi-square 6.143, p = 0.013.
  - The footnote of Table 5 says every p-value is from the chi-square test (p. 10), although the methods allow Fisher's exact test where appropriate (p. 6). Inference, recomputed from the printed counts: the printed chi-square values match neither the Pearson nor the Yates-corrected statistic consistently (hypoplastic: printed 0.982, Pearson 1.63, Yates 1.29; absent: 11.231 against 11.58 and 10.14; lumbar: 4.412 against 4.37 and 3.23; cervical: 4.021 against 4.41 and 2.95; overall: 6.143 against 6.73 and 6.15). The cervical rib cells have expected counts of 3.75 and 3.25, where Fisher's exact test is the right test, and give two-sided Fisher p of about 0.053, not significant; the lumbar rib gives Fisher p about 0.042 and the absent 12th rib about 0.0007, which stand.
- The four types add to exactly the overall count (55 + 22 + 12 + 7 = 96; men 40; women 56), so (Inference) each patient sits in one category only. Yet Figure 4A to B (p. 9 (PDF 9)) shows one patient with a hypoplastic right 12th rib and an absent left 12th rib; the rule for filing such a patient is not given (see Gaps).
- Laterality was promised (p. 5) but is never reported, so unilateral and bilateral absence cannot be told apart and "absent 12th rib" cannot be read as "eleven rib pairs". Although the methods name "numerical" rib anomalies (p. 5), no count of rib pairs (11 or 13) and no count of thoracic vertebrae is reported anywhere.
- Bifid and foramen ribs were recorded (p. 5) but no count is given. Figure 5C (p. 10 (PDF 10)) shows a supernumerary rib arising from the posterior aspect of a short left third rib and running inferolaterally toward the lung; it fits none of the Table 5 categories.
- Figures: Figure 4A (axial CT) and 4B (AP radiograph) of one patient: hypoplastic right 12th rib (black arrows), absent left 12th rib (white arrows); Figure 4C, a frontal chest radiograph of another patient with "bilateral costal variations", with the ribs numbered on the image-left side, apparently 1 to 11 (the last label is faint; Inference on the reading) (p. 9 (PDF 9)). Figure 5A axial CT of a cervical rib, a slender bar running laterally from the image-right side of the vertebra; 5B axial CT of a lumbar rib, which appears as a separate slender bony element jointed to the tip of the image-left transverse process of a lumbar-type vertebra, the other transverse process being short and plain (p. 10 (PDF 10)). The paper's figures follow the usual radiological convention, image-left = patient's right (Figure 4A puts the "right" 12th rib on image-left, Figure 5C the "left" third rib on image-right, pp. 9 to 10); Inference on the reading: the cervical rib of 5A is on the patient's left and the lumbar rib of 5B on the patient's right. Neither caption names the side or the vertebra.
- Not discussed at all: T13, eleven or thirteen thoracic vertebrae, L6, lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, block vertebrae, and a thoracolumbar transitional vertebra by facet orientation. The authors call T12 "the T12 transitional segment" (p. 1, p. 7, p. 11 (PDF 1, 7, 11)) in the ordinary anatomical sense, not as a numeric anomaly.
- No criterion is given for a lumbar rib against an elongated or unfused transverse process, or for a rib-bearing vertebra below T12 being L1 with a lumbar rib rather than a T13.
- Effect of an anomaly on the measures: not analysed (no comparison of morphometry between patients with and without anomalies).
- Field of view: the cohort mixes thoracic and abdominal CT (p. 3, Figure 1). Scans with an "incomplete field of view" were excluded (p. 3), but what field was required is not said. Inference: an abdominal CT rarely includes C7 and a chest CT may stop near L1 to L2, yet cervical and lumbar rib rates use all 517 as denominator with no statement that every scan covered C7 and L1, so the cervical rib rate in particular may be an underestimate; if instead the exclusion demanded a field from C7 to L2, the cohort was selected for long scans.
- Clinical reading (p. 12 (PDF 12)): when the 12th rib is rudimentary or absent, counting caudal to cranial and cranial to caudal during planning and on intraoperative fluoroscopy often disagree, which leads to wrong-level surgery, a "never event"; AP radiographs alone should not be relied on for level localisation; preoperative cross-sectional CT that traces the whole rib cage is advised. Proposed as a "Standardized Rib-Tracing Protocol", mandatory before any TLJ procedure and especially in women (p. 13 (PDF 13)).
- The authors say their pattern (hypoplasia commonest, then absence) agrees with Oner et al. 2024, an earlier Turkish MDCT series of congenital rib anomalies (Folia Morphol 2024; 83: 182-191, reference 9, p. 15), without giving its values (p. 12, p. 13 (PDF 12, 13)).
- Introduction (p. 2 (PDF 2)): the TLJ is said to be often associated with costal variants (12th-rib hypoplasia, absent ribs, bifid ribs, supernumerary lumbar or cervical ribs), which matter for preventing surgical mislocalisation and serve as auxiliary markers in skeletal identification.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### Vertebral corpus heights: ACH, MCH, PCH

- Landmarks: anterior, middle and posterior heights of the body, measured in the mid-sagittal plane on sagittal reformats, after Yerli et al. 2024 and Gulec et al. 2017 (p. 4 (PDF 4)). Exactly where the middle height is taken, and whether the ring apophysis or endplate cortex is included, is not said. Figure 2A illustrates it on L2 with three lines labelled 27.0, 24.2 and 27.3 mm; by their position from the anterior to the posterior side these are ACH, MCH and PCH (Inference on the reading) (p. 5 (PDF 5)). The paper says every landmark is an established one and nothing is new (p. 4).
- Values, Table 1, p. 7 (PDF 7), mm, mean +/- SD, men / women, all p < 0.001:
  - T11: ACH 21.43 +/- 1.87 / 19.22 +/- 1.54; MCH 22.11 +/- 1.65 / 20.03 +/- 1.48; PCH 23.08 +/- 1.92 / 20.81 +/- 1.63.
  - T12: ACH 23.61 +/- 2.14 / 21.09 +/- 1.72; MCH 24.04 +/- 1.88 / 21.55 +/- 1.66; PCH 24.83 +/- 2.07 / 22.19 +/- 1.85.
  - L1: ACH 24.78 +/- 2.05 / 22.56 +/- 1.91; MCH 25.12 +/- 1.76 / 23.02 +/- 1.64; PCH 25.94 +/- 1.98 / 23.57 +/- 1.82.
  - L2: ACH 26.31 +/- 2.21 / 23.85 +/- 1.95; MCH 26.55 +/- 1.98 / 24.11 +/- 1.78; PCH 27.12 +/- 2.15 / 24.68 +/- 1.92.
  - Men exceed women by 9.1% to 11.9%; Cohen's d > 0.8 for every height (p. 7 (PDF 7)). Table 1 also prints the 95% interval of each mean. Inference, computed: the "Diff (%)" column is the male-female difference over the female mean (T11 ACH 2.21 / 19.22 = 11.5%).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Take the body (SPINEPS corpus label 49 with endplates 52 and 53) in the vertebra's own frame (scanimport's geometry frame: z along the column, y from canal to body). In a thin mid-sagittal slab through the body's centroid, measure the craniocaudal extent of the body along z at the anterior cortex (just behind the anterior edge), at the antero-posterior midpoint, and at the posterior wall. PCH is the generator's VBHp. Decide once whether the middle height is taken at the central concavity (the usual meaning, which Figure 2A shows) or as the paper's table implies (see Gaps).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference; the paper takes nothing from radiographs. A lateral radiograph gives ACH and PCH as projected heights, needing a magnification correction; a beam not parallel to the endplates doubles their contours and blurs MCH most.
- Across levels: all three heights rise steadily from T11 to L2 in both sexes (p. 6 (PDF 6)). In the table means PCH > MCH > ACH at every level. Inference (ratio of means from Table 1): ACH/PCH is 0.929, 0.951, 0.955, 0.970 in men and 0.924, 0.950, 0.957, 0.966 in women at T11, T12, L1, L2, so anterior wedging lessens down the junction, with the largest change from T11 to T12. Adjacent-level steps are 1.1 to 2.2 mm, about 0.55 to 1.15 SD, largest from T11 to T12 (Inference, computed).
- Sex, age, size, population: men are 2.1 to 2.6 mm taller at every level, about the size of one level's step (Inference: absolute height therefore confounds level with sex and size; a woman's L1 ACH of 22.56 mm lies between a man's T11, 21.43, and T12, 23.61). Age was not analysed for heights. Body height, weight and BMI were not available (p. 14 (PDF 14)). Inference, from this set's Bonczar 2024 note (meta-analysis, pooled): posterior height L1 26.57, L2 27.02 mm, about 1 to 1.7 mm above Duman's sex-pooled PCH (24.84, 25.99), and a wedge ratio ACH/PCH of 0.92 (L1) and 0.95 (L2) against Duman's about 0.96 and 0.97.
- Error: intra-observer ICC 0.912 to 0.965 for all linear measures together, heights included (p. 6 (PDF 6)); not given per measure. Inference: SEM about 0.3 to 0.65 mm (see Measurement error).

### Transverse pedicle diameter, TPD (pedicle width)

- Landmarks: the narrowest point of the pedicle isthmus, outer cortex to outer cortex (p. 4 (PDF 4)), on an axial image (the plane is given only by the caption of Figure 2B, p. 5); the landmark scheme P, Q, R, S, X, Y follows Zindrick et al. 1987 and Kim et al. 1994 (p. 4). The text names the end points X and Y, but in Figure 2E X and Y lie on the sagittal drawing at the superior and inferior notches, while P and Q lie on the axial drawing at the medial and lateral pedicle cortices (p. 5 (PDF 5)); the letters in the text appear swapped (Inference). Figure 2B shows TPD-R 9.02 mm and TPD-L 9.47 mm (green lines) on a lumbar vertebra (p. 5).
- Values, Table 2, p. 8 (PDF 8), mm, men / women: T11 7.62 +/- 1.35 / 7.21 +/- 1.14 (p < 0.001); T12 8.46 +/- 1.42 / 8.28 +/- 1.53 (p = 0.168); L1 9.53 +/- 1.64 / 8.88 +/- 1.72 (p < 0.001); L2 10.08 +/- 1.71 / 9.72 +/- 1.83 (p = 0.021).
- Right and left, whole cohort, Table 3, p. 8 (PDF 8): T11 7.51 +/- 1.22 / 7.42 +/- 1.28 (difference 0.09, p = 0.31); T12 8.32 +/- 1.45 / 8.28 +/- 1.50 (0.04, p = 0.58); L1 9.25 +/- 1.60 / 9.21 +/- 1.65 (0.04, p = 0.65); L2 9.93 +/- 1.70 / 9.89 +/- 1.80 (0.04, p = 0.78).
- By age group, Table 4, p. 8 (PDF 8), 18-35 / 36-55 / > 55: men T11 7.55 / 7.62 / 7.68, T12 8.38 / 8.46 / 8.52, L1 9.45 / 9.53 / 9.62, L2 9.98 / 10.08 / 10.18; women T11 7.15 / 7.21 / 7.28, T12 8.23 / 8.28 / 8.33, L1 8.80 / 8.88 / 8.95, L2 9.65 / 9.72 / 9.80 (SDs 1.10 to 1.88). ANOVA p 0.112 to 0.450, none significant.
- T11 bottleneck: 57 patients, about 11%, had a T11 TPD < 6 mm (p. 7 (PDF 7)); whether one side or either side, and the split by sex, are not given.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the arch (SPINEPS arcus label 41), on each side take the bridge of bone between the body and the transverse and articular processes; in axial slabs of the vertebra's own frame find the smallest mediolateral width of that bridge, measured across the pedicle axis, from the outer boundary of the mask on the canal side to the outer boundary on the lateral side. With voxels of 0.6 to 1 mm, partial volume is a large share of a 7 mm width; use a sub-voxel boundary.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference; not in the paper. An AP radiograph shows the pedicle ovals, whose transverse width approximates TPD but is widened by the pedicle's medial angulation (here 21 to 28 deg) and by any axial rotation; a lateral radiograph cannot give it.
- Across levels: widens steadily from T11 to L2 in both sexes (p. 7 (PDF 7)); T11 is the narrowest. Adjacent steps are 0.55 to 1.07 mm, about 0.3 to 0.8 SD (Inference, computed), so TPD alone separates neighbours weakly.
- Sex, age, size, population: men wider at T11 (+5.7%), L1 (+7.3%) and L2 (+3.7%), not at T12 (+2.2%) (percentages computed from Table 2; Inference). No age effect within sex (Table 4). No body size data.
- Error: ICC as above; Inference: SEM about 0.2 to 0.55 mm. Mean right-left difference 0.04 to 0.09 mm, but Inference from the printed 95% intervals: the SD of the right-left difference within a patient is about 1.3 to 1.9 mm (T11 1.3, T12 1.5, L1 1.7, L2 1.9; half-width x sqrt(517) / 1.96), so individual sides often differ by 1 to 2 mm.
- Against the cited source: Inference, from this set's Zindrick 1987 note: Zindrick's transverse width is T11 7.8, T12 7.1, L1 8.7, L2 8.9 mm (cadaver vertebrae), with a dip at T12; Duman's sex-pooled TPD is 7.43, 8.38, 9.23, 9.91 mm with no dip. Against this set's Bonczar 2024 note (meta-analysis, pooled): L1 7.51, L2 8.23 mm, about 1.7 mm below Duman.
- Shape: Inference, computed from Table 2 sex-pooled means: TPD/SPD is 0.53 (T11), 0.57 (T12), 0.61 (L1), 0.63 (L2), a tall oval that rounds caudally; the authors describe the pedicle as a vertical ellipse (p. 11 (PDF 11)). The ratio is free of scale and magnification.

### Sagittal pedicle diameter, SPD (pedicle height)

- Landmarks: the "maximum vertical height of the pedicle", measured from the superior to the inferior pedicle notch (text: points P and Q; figure: X and Y) (p. 4 (PDF 4)), on a sagittal image through the pedicle (the plane from the caption of Figure 2C, p. 5). Figure 2C shows a blue vertical line labelled "PH: Pedicle Height", 15.3 mm, on a lumbar sagittal image (p. 5 (PDF 5)).
- Values, Table 2, p. 8 (PDF 8), mm, men / women: T11 14.17 +/- 1.42 / 13.93 +/- 1.31 (p = 0.046); T12 14.65 +/- 1.51 / 14.50 +/- 1.44 (p = 0.251); L1 15.21 +/- 1.43 / 14.88 +/- 1.55 (p = 0.012); L2 16.24 +/- 1.72 / 15.19 +/- 1.84 (p < 0.001). Figure 3, p. 9 (PDF 9), plots the same means.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In a parasagittal slab through the pedicle's centre, the craniocaudal extent of the arch bone between the floor of the superior vertebral notch and the roof of the inferior notch. The paper says "maximum", the generator's PDH says "smallest craniocaudal diameter"; the notch-to-notch distance is in practice the narrowest height, but a maximum over several parasagittal slices would read larger. Record which.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. A lateral radiograph shows the pedicle between the notches, with both sides superimposed and magnified; not in the paper.
- Across levels: rises steadily from T11 to L2 (p. 7 (PDF 7)); in men the step from L1 to L2 (1.03 mm) is twice the others, in women the curve flattens (steps 0.57, 0.38, 0.31 mm) (Figure 3, Table 2). Steps are 0.2 to 0.65 SD (Inference, computed).
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger at T11, L1 and L2, not at T12 (p. 7 to 8). Age not analysed for SPD.
- Error: ICC as above; Inference: SEM about 0.2 to 0.55 mm.
- Against other papers in this set: Inference, from the Zindrick 1987 note: Zindrick's sagittal pedicle width is T11 17.4, T12 15.8, L1 15.4, L2 15.0 mm, peaking at T11 and falling caudally, the opposite of Duman's rise (14.06, 14.58, 15.06, 15.75 mm, sex-pooled); the two agree at L1 to L2 and differ by about 3 mm at T11. Panjabi's PDH falls the same way (see bordercall section). Bonczar 2024 (pooled) gives L1 15.40 mm, close to Duman.

### Pedicle length, PL (screw-path chord)

- Landmarks: from the posterior cortical surface of the articular process (R) to the anterior cortex of the vertebral body (S) (p. 4 (PDF 4)), on an axial image (caption of Figure 2B, p. 5). The text does not say "along the pedicle axis"; the figures draw it so: in Figure 2E the dashed R-to-S line passes through the pedicle and in Figure 2B both lines run through the pedicles and meet near the anterior midline (p. 5). Figure 2B shows the right (orange) and left (red) lines on a lumbar vertebra, labelled PL-L 58.69 mm and PL-R 56.43 mm (p. 5 (PDF 5)).
- Values: reported only at T11 and only for the whole cohort by side: right 41.25 +/- 2.14 mm, left 41.08 +/- 2.20 mm, difference 0.17 mm, p = 0.41 (Table 3, p. 8 (PDF 8)). Not given by sex or at T12, L1 or L2, although the abstract lists it among the measures (p. 1).
- Inference: this is the length a transpedicular screw path can take (the chord or "pedicle axis length" of Zindrick's tradition), not the length of the pedicle itself; the paper does not discuss it.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the axial plane of the vertebra's frame, draw the pedicle axis (through the TPD isthmus centre at the TPA angle) and take the length of bone it crosses, from where it enters the posterior surface at the articular process to where it leaves the anterior cortex of the body.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference; not possible from a lateral alone, which shows only its sagittal projection, roughly PL times the cosine of TPA.
- Across levels, sex, age: no data.
- Against the cited source: Inference, from this set's Zindrick 1987 note: Zindrick's depth along the pedicle axis at T11 is 41.8 mm, close to Duman's 41.25 / 41.08 mm (right / left).
- Error: right-left difference only; its printed interval is not centred on its mean (see Gaps).

### Transverse pedicle angle, TPA

- Landmarks: the angle between the central axis of the pedicle and the mid-sagittal axis of the vertebral body, after Zindrick 1987 and Wu 2024 (p. 4 (PDF 4)), on an axial image (caption of Figure 2D, p. 5). The abstract calls it a medial trajectory (p. 1): the two axes converge anteriorly. Figure 2D draws both pedicle axes converging in front of the body on the mid-sagittal line, with example angles 15.12 and 14.50 deg; the level is not given (p. 5 (PDF 5)).
- Values, Table 2, p. 8 (PDF 8), deg, men / women: T11 21.18 +/- 3.20 / 20.98 +/- 2.50 (p = 0.424); T12 23.45 +/- 3.73 / 23.35 +/- 2.21 (p = 0.706); L1 25.55 +/- 5.33 / 25.33 +/- 4.47 (p = 0.610); L2 27.79 +/- 5.55 / 27.32 +/- 4.78 (p = 0.303). In words: about 21 deg at T11 to about 28 deg at L2 (p. 1, p. 11).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the vertebra's axial plane, the angle between each pedicle's long axis (for example the principal axis of the pedicle voxels in an axial slab, or the line from the posterior entry point through the isthmus centre to the body) and the y axis from canal to body. This is the generator's PDIs (pedicle axis inclination to the sagittal plane).
- From a radiograph alone: not from a lateral. The paper cites Wu et al. 2024, a digital-radiography method for TPA of the lower lumbar spine, by title only (p. 15, reference 8); it does not describe it. Inference: on an AP view the position of the pedicle oval relative to the lateral body margin carries the angle, but rotation confounds it.
- Across levels: increases by about 2 deg per level (steps 1.98 to 2.37 deg; Inference, computed), and its SD widens from about 2.2 to 3.7 deg at T11 to T12 to about 4.5 to 5.6 deg at L1 to L2. Steps are 0.4 to 1.0 SD.
- Sex, age, size, population: no sex difference at any level (p = 0.303 to 0.706); the authors read this as sex-independent biomechanics (p. 11, p. 15). Age not analysed.
- Error: intra-observer ICC 0.898 (95% CI 0.872 to 0.924) (p. 6 (PDF 6)). Inference: SEM about 0.7 to 1.8 deg.
- Against its own cited source: Inference, from this set's note on Zindrick 1987 (Table 3 there): Zindrick's transverse angle is T11 1.2, T12 -4.2, L1 10.9, L2 12.0 deg, so Duman's 21 to 28 deg, though said to follow Zindrick's landmarks, is about 15 to 28 deg larger at every level (T11 +19.9, T12 +27.6, L1 +14.6, L2 +15.6, against the sex-pooled means) and shows no near-neutral or lateral T12. Duman's own Figure 2D example (15.12 and 14.50 deg) is nearer Zindrick's lumbar values than to Duman's table. The construction of the axis (convergence point anterior to the body, Figure 2D) is the likeliest source (Inference); do not mix the two series.

### 12th-rib length and the rib anomalies

- Definition: hypoplastic rib < 4 cm (p. 5 (PDF 5)); absent rib; supernumerary cervical or lumbar rib; bifid and foramen rib. Individual rib lengths were recorded but are not reported.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: SPINEPS gives no ribs (scanimport/schemes.py: "ribs": False), so rib length and rib-bearing must come from the independent rib arm. Inference: the SPINEPS costal-process labels (43, 44) mark the transverse processes, beside which a lumbar rib or a short 12th rib would lie.
- From a radiograph alone: an AP radiograph can show a hypoplastic or absent 12th rib (Figure 4B, p. 9), but the authors warn that AP radiographs are not enough for level localisation (p. 12, p. 13). Inference: the 4 cm threshold was set on CT; on an AP projection a rib running obliquely backward and downward is foreshortened, so a projected length under 4 cm does not prove hypoplasia.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- T11: narrowest pedicles of the junction (TPD 7.62 mm men, 7.21 mm women); about 11% (57/517) below 6 mm (p. 7 (PDF 7)); described as the critical bottleneck for transpedicular screws (p. 11 (PDF 11)) and the level where a sub-millimetre side difference could narrow the safe margin (p. 11 to 12); the proposed "Pedicle Safety Threshold" is defined here (p. 13 (PDF 13)). TPA about 21 deg; screw-path length about 41 mm (Table 3).
- T12: the only level where neither TPD (p = 0.168) nor SPD (p = 0.251) differs by sex (p. 7 to 8 (PDF 7 to 8)); the authors attribute this to T12's role in axial load transfer (p. 11), an interpretation not tested. Body heights at T12 still differ by sex by 11.5% to 11.9% (Table 1). T12 lies close to the thoracic aorta, after Korkmaz and Ozevren (p. 12 (PDF 12)). Its rib is hypoplastic in 10.64% and absent in 4.26% (Table 5).
- L1: largest sex difference in TPD (0.65 mm); L1 virtual morphometry is cited as a sex estimator in Turkish people (p. 13 (PDF 13)). Lumbar ribs in 2.32% (Table 5); the paper does not name the vertebra that bears them (Inference: usually L1).
- L2: largest of all four levels in every measure; male SPD jumps by 1.03 mm from L1.
- Thoracolumbar junction: 50% to 60% of spinal column injuries occur here; the commonest site of traumatic and osteoporotic compression fractures (p. 2 (PDF 2)). The authors describe the TLJ as a zone of rapid change in size, shape and spatial orientation, with the pedicles as the main path of load from the posterior elements to the body (p. 2).
- Across the junction in this paper's data (Tables 1 and 2): every height, TPD, SPD and TPA rises monotonically from T11 to L2 in both sexes; nothing marks T12/L1 as a break. Inference: the T12/L1 boundary is not visible in these four measures as a step; a classifier needs the rib, facet or transverse-process evidence that this paper does not measure.
- C7/T1: only the cervical rib rate, 7/517 (1.35%), with a female excess (Table 5).
- C2/C3 and L5/S1: not covered.

## Measurement error and reliability

- One observer; intra-observer only: 50 random CTs re-measured by the same radiologist four weeks later (p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- ICC for all linear measures (TPD, SPD, heights) 0.912 to 0.965 (95% CI 0.895 to 0.978); for TPA 0.898 (0.872 to 0.924); all p < 0.001 (p. 6 (PDF 6)). The ICC model, the per-measure ICCs, the standard error of measurement and any Bland-Altman limits are not given. No reliability is given for the rib anomaly calls or rib lengths.
- Inference: with the per-sex SDs of Tables 1 and 2, SD x sqrt(1 - ICC), taking the smallest SD with the highest ICC and the largest SD with the lowest, gives a repeatability SEM of about 0.2 to 0.55 mm for TPD and SPD (SD 1.14 to 1.84), 0.3 to 0.65 mm for the heights (SD 1.48 to 2.21) and 0.7 to 1.8 deg for TPA (SD 2.21 to 5.55, ICC 0.898). Which ICC belongs to which measure is unknown, and the SD of the 50-scan subsample is unknown.
- Right-left: no significant asymmetry; the mean difference was 0.04 to 0.09 mm in TPD and 0.17 mm in PL (Table 3, p. 8 (PDF 8)). The SD of the paired differences is not printed. Inference, from the printed 95% intervals (SD = half-width x sqrt(517) / 1.96): about 1.3 (T11), 1.5 (T12), 1.7 (L1) and 1.9 mm (L2) for TPD; the printed p-values imply larger SDs still (T11 about 2.0 mm). So the cohort mean hides individual side differences of 1 to 2 mm, larger than the repeatability SEM; the authors' own advice to read each side before instrumentation (p. 12) is the right reading.
- Instrument: 0.625 mm contiguous slices, bone window W 1500 / L 300 HU, pilot-calibrated reformat alignment (p. 3 to 4).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- The TLJ (T11 to L2) is where the rigid kyphotic thoracic spine, held by the ribs and sternum, meets the mobile lordotic lumbar spine; it takes high stress and torsion and is the commonest site of acute spinal injury (p. 2 (PDF 2)).
- Pedicle width is the main limit on screw diameter; a mismatch raises the risk of cortical breach; a medial breach is the most dangerous (dural tear, CSF leak, direct neural injury) (p. 11 (PDF 11)).
- "Pedicle Safety Threshold" (p. 13 (PDF 13)): defined at T11, where 11% of this cohort had a TPD < 6 mm; a TPD < 6 mm is said to carry a high breach risk with a 6.5 mm screw, and any patient having TLJ instrumentation with a TPD < 6 mm should be considered for 4.5 to 5.5 mm screws or an extrapedicular technique.
- Pedicle shape: the pedicles widen craniocaudally and are taller than wide at every level, a "vertical, elliptical configuration" read as adaptation to rising load (p. 11 (PDF 11)); the widening agrees with Koken et al. 2014 and Duzkalir et al. 2015 (p. 11). The T12 equality of the sexes is set against the pan-lumbar dimorphism of Palm et al. 2025 (AI-based CT of 3,117 vertebrae) and Bonczar et al. 2024 (p. 11; references 14 and 15, p. 16).
- Despite cohort-level symmetry, side-specific multiplanar CT should be read before instrumentation, since a sub-millimetre difference can matter in a narrow pedicle such as T11 (p. 11 to 12 (PDF 11 to 12)).
- TPA is predictable and sex-independent, so it can serve as a baseline "safe corridor" for free-hand and 2D-fluoroscopy screw placement where O-arm navigation is too costly or slow (p. 12 (PDF 12)).
- Sex differences in body height call for sex-specific planning of screw length and of vertebral body replacement size (p. 7 (PDF 7)).
- Wrong level: rudimentary or absent 12th ribs make top-down and bottom-up counts disagree on planning images and fluoroscopy; do not rely on AP radiographs alone; trace the whole rib cage on CT before any TLJ procedure, especially in women (p. 12 to 13 (PDF 12 to 13)).
- Forensic (p. 12 to 13 (PDF 12 to 13)): the female excess of an absent 12th rib is proposed as a discriminating marker for sex estimation, to combine with pelvic or cranial markers (p. 12); the male excess of lumbar ribs is reported as a sex-dimorphic pattern (p. 12) and the rib variants as a whole are called potential sex-diagnostic markers (Figure 6 caption, p. 10), but the lumbar rib is not singled out as a marker; an asymptomatic hypoplastic or absent 12th rib seen on antemortem and postmortem images can corroborate a positive identification; population-specific body heights help forensic reports tell a traumatic compression fracture from natural wedging; the authors call the rib findings "radiological fingerprints" (p. 2 (PDF 2)). No discriminant function or classification accuracy is computed. Inference from Table 5: an absent 12th rib is about 5.2 times as common in women (7.50% / 1.44%), a lumbar rib about 4.3 times as common in men (3.61% / 0.83%), a cervical rib about 6.9 times as common in women (2.50% / 0.36%, on 7 cases).
- Pitfalls the authors name (p. 14 (PDF 14)): single-centre chest-hospital sample (selection bias); no body height, weight, BMI or workload; cross-sectional; pathology and scoliosis > 10 deg excluded, so the values describe pristine spines and not the elderly or deformed; supine, non-weight-bearing scans; the age analysis was not powered for small differences. No multiplicity correction (p. 6).
- Future work the authors propose (p. 14 (PDF 14)): multicentre, multi-ethnic cohorts; BMI, standing height, bone mineral density and workload; upright cone-beam CT for weight-bearing anatomy; and deep-learning networks for automated morphometric segmentation and sex estimation (Inference: the same task as bordercall's mask-based measurement).
- Data: the anonymised data are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request (p. 15 (PDF 15)). Inference: per-patient values, rib laterality and the rib-length records could be asked for.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Never name T12 only as "the lowest rib-bearing vertebra": in this cohort the 12th rib was absent in 4.26% (22/517) and hypoplastic (< 4 cm) in 10.64% (55/517), and 2.32% (12/517) carried a lumbar rib (Table 5, p. 10). Count the whole column from a fixed end and check the rib count against it (p. 12).
2. On CT, trace every rib from its head to the vertebra it articulates with, both sides separately, before naming the lowest thoracic level; one patient can have a hypoplastic right and an absent left 12th rib (Figure 4A to B, p. 9).
3. On an AP radiograph or AP fluoroscopy, treat a short or missing lowest rib on either side as a warning that top-down and bottom-up counts may disagree, and do not settle the level from the AP view alone (p. 12 to 13; Figure 4B, p. 9). The < 4 cm cut-off was applied on CT (p. 5); a projected rib length on AP is foreshortened, so do not apply 4 cm to it directly (Inference).
4. Raise the prior of an absent 12th rib in women (7.50% against 1.44% in men) and of a lumbar rib in men (3.61% against 0.83%) (Table 5, p. 10).
5. When a lumbar-type vertebra has a separate costal element jointed to its transverse process (Figure 5B, p. 10), call it a lumbar rib on L1 only if the count from C2 (or from T1) gives twelve thoracic vertebrae above it; otherwise consider T13. The paper gives no rule; this instruction is Inference.
6. Look for cervical ribs on axial CT of the lowest cervical vertebra (Figure 5A, p. 10; the paper does not name the level, Inference: C7); expect them in about 1.35% (7/517), more in women (6/240 against 1/277), though that sex difference rests on 7 cases and is not significant by Fisher's exact test (Inference, recomputed). Confirm that C7 is in the field of view before calling a rib absent (Inference).
7. Measure TPD at the narrowest isthmus, outer cortex to outer cortex, on the axial image; SPD as the notch-to-notch height on a sagittal image through the pedicle; PL from the posterior articular-process cortex to the anterior body cortex, drawn through the pedicle; TPA between the pedicle axis and the mid-sagittal line of the body, on the axial image (p. 4; planes from the captions of Figure 2B to 2D, p. 5).
8. Use TPA as a sex-free level cue between T11 and L2: about 21.1, 23.4, 25.4 and 27.6 deg (sex-pooled; Table 2, p. 8), about 2 deg per level, with SD 2 to 6 deg, so neighbours overlap heavily (0.4 to 1.0 SD apart, Inference). Compare only with values taken by the same construction: this paper's TPA is 15 to 28 deg larger than Zindrick's transverse angle at the same levels (Inference, from this set's Zindrick note).
9. Use TPD and SPD only as weak level cues (neighbours differ by 0.3 to 0.8 SD), and expect TPD near 7.4 mm at T11 rising to about 9.9 mm at L2 (Table 2, p. 8).
10. Before comparing absolute body heights with a level table, normalise for sex and size: the male-female difference (2.1 to 2.6 mm) is as large as one level's step (Table 1, p. 7). Inference, computed from Table 1 means: the ratio ACH/PCH, free of scale and magnification, rises from about 0.93 at T11 to 0.97 at L2, with its largest step between T11 and T12; the paper itself does not propose a ratio.
11. Do not expect sex differences in pedicle size at T12 (p. 8), and do not take their absence as a sign of anything.
12. Read laterality from the image orientation, never from overlay text: in Figure 2B the line the caption calls the right pedicle length is labelled "PL-L" (p. 5). This paper's figures put the patient's right on image-left (Figure 4A, Figure 5C captions, pp. 9 to 10).
13. Do not expect the right and left TPD of one vertebra to match: the cohort mean difference is only 0.04 to 0.09 mm (Table 3, p. 8), but the printed intervals imply a within-patient SD of the side difference of about 1.3 to 1.9 mm (Inference, computed), so differences of 1 to 2 mm are ordinary. Flag only larger asymmetry, and then check for axial rotation or a segmentation error first (Inference).
14. Flag any pedicle at the TLJ with TPD < 6 mm as narrow, the threshold the authors propose for screw sizing; expect it in about 11% (57/517) at T11 (p. 7, p. 13).
15. Expect the T11 to L2 pedicles to be taller than wide at every level, the ratio TPD/SPD rising from about 0.53 at T11 to 0.63 at L2 (Inference, computed from Table 2; the authors describe the vertical ellipse, p. 11). On an AP view this ratio of the pedicle oval needs no magnification correction, though rotation changes it (Inference).
16. Do not look for a step at T12/L1 in body heights, TPD, SPD or TPA: all four rise smoothly from T11 to L2 in this cohort (Tables 1 and 2, pp. 7 to 8); find the junction from the ribs and from features this paper did not measure (Inference).
17. When the lowest rib is short or absent, count in both directions, from the top and from the sacrum, on the same images (the paper reports that these two counts often disagree in this situation; using them as a cross-check is Inference); if they disagree, settle the names on CT with the whole rib cage traced before naming any TLJ level (p. 12 to 13).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder (all from Table 5, p. 10; 517 Turkish adults, 277 men, 240 women, CT at a chest hospital):
- 12th rib absent: 4.26% overall; 1.44% men, 7.50% women; laterality unknown, so this is not a rate of eleven rib pairs. Inference: for the decoder it is the rate at which "lowest rib-bearing vertebra = T12" fails by placing T12 one level too high on that side.
- 12th rib hypoplastic (< 4 cm): 10.64% overall; 9.03% men, 12.50% women. Inference: on radiographs and fluoroscopy a short 12th rib may be missed, so this rate bounds how often a rib-based count can slip on 2D images.
- Lumbar rib: 2.32% overall; 3.61% men, 0.83% women. Inference: this is the rate of a rib-bearing vertebra below T12; the paper does not separate lumbar rib from T13.
- Cervical rib: 1.35% overall; 0.36% men, 2.50% women (denominator possibly including scans without C7; the sex difference rests on 7 cases, Fisher p about 0.053 by recomputation, so a sex-specific prior is weakly supported).
- The categories are mutually exclusive in Table 5 (they sum to 96), so a patient with two kinds of anomaly counts once; the per-anomaly rates may be slight undercounts (Inference).
- Any rib anomaly: 18.57%; 14.44% men, 23.33% women.
- No rates for T13, eleven thoracic vertebrae, L6, LSTV or missing bodies; the paper gives nothing for those transitions.

(b) Measures and definitions against the generator (schwingcheck/mnemonics.py) and its Panjabi data (schwingcheck/data/panjabi_long.csv, cadaver, n = 12 per level):
- TPD is PDW (pedicle_w, smallest transverse diameter): same definition. Panjabi PDW right / left: T11 8.8 / 10.7, T12 8.8 / 8.6, L1 8.0 / 9.2, L2 7.8 / 8.7 mm, flat or falling; Duman sex-pooled TPD 7.43, 8.38, 9.23, 9.91 mm, rising (Inference, pooled from Table 2). The shapes disagree; Duman's narrow T11 and wide L2 would bias a level classifier trained on Panjabi at the TLJ.
- SPD is PDH (pedicle_h, defined in the generator as the smallest craniocaudal diameter): Duman says "maximum" height between the notches. Panjabi PDH right / left: T11 16.9 / 17.8, T12 16.5 / 16.8, L1 15.9 / 15.8, L2 15.0 / 14.9 mm, falling; Duman sex-pooled SPD 14.06, 14.58, 15.06, 15.75 mm, rising (Inference, pooled). The direction is opposite; a mask-derived height should follow one definition and not be pooled with the other.
- TPA is PDIs (pedicle_incl_sag, pedicle axis inclination to the sagittal plane): same concept. Panjabi PDIs right / left: T11 12.9 / 13.2, T12 8.0 / 11.5, L1 16.5 / 12.4, L2 17.1 / 11.2 deg; Duman sex-pooled TPA 21.09, 23.40, 25.45, 27.57 deg (Inference, pooled). Duman's are 8 to 15 deg larger and monotonic, and 15 to 28 deg larger than Zindrick's transverse angle (T11 1.2, T12 -4.2, L1 10.9, L2 12.0 deg; this set's Zindrick 1987 note), the definition Duman says it follows. Inference: different axis construction (CT pedicle axis to the body's mid-sagittal line, convergence point anterior) or a living-population difference; the generator should not mix them, and a mask-derived PDIs should be checked against Panjabi and Zindrick before Duman.
- PCH is close to VBHp (h_post): Panjabi VBHp T11 21.3, T12 22.7, L1 23.8, L2 24.3 mm; Duman sex-pooled PCH 22.03, 23.60, 24.84, 25.99 mm (Inference, pooled), about 0.7 to 1.7 mm larger, growing caudally. Inference: CT of living patients against dried cadaver bone, and a population difference.
- Zindrick 1987 (this set's note) sides with Panjabi on pedicle height (falling from T11 to L2) but with Duman, roughly, on pedicle width (rising, with a T12 dip Duman lacks); Inference: Duman's thoracic SPD is the outlier, and a generator built on Panjabi should not be re-centred on it.
- The pedicle shape ratio TPD/SPD (0.53 at T11 to 0.63 at L2, Inference from Table 2) is scale-free and could be added beside PDW and PDH; Panjabi's values would give its cadaver counterpart.
- ACH and MCH are not in the generator; ACH, or the ratio ACH/PCH, could be added as a size-free wedge cue (0.93 at T11 to 0.97 at L2; Inference, computed from Table 1). The generator's EPIt (wedge angle) is the angular counterpart.
- PL (screw-path chord) has no generator counterpart and is reported only at T11.
- Nothing in this paper on end-plate widths or depths, canal, facets or processes.

(c) Measurement error: intra-observer ICC 0.912 to 0.965 (linear) and 0.898 (TPA), one observer, 50 scans (p. 6); Inference: SEM about 0.2 to 0.55 mm (pedicle diameters), 0.3 to 0.65 mm (heights), 0.7 to 1.8 deg (TPA). No inter-observer error, so the true reader error is larger. Within-patient right-left TPD difference SD about 1.3 to 1.9 mm (Inference from Table 3's intervals), so right and left should be drawn as two correlated sides, not as one value. Mask-derived values add partial-volume error at 0.625 mm slices.

(d) Covariates: sex is a strong covariate for body heights (men 9.1% to 11.9% larger, p. 7; Inference, computed from Table 1: Cohen's d about 1.1 to 1.4), a weak one for pedicle width and height (1% to 7%, none at T12) and none for TPA. Age (18 to 35, 36 to 55, > 55) has no effect on TPD (Table 4). No body height, weight or BMI were recorded, so no size factor can be fitted from this paper; Inference: the sex shift stands in for size here. Population: Turkish adults at a chest hospital, healthy spines only.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Level identification is never described, although the paper's purpose includes preventing wrong-level surgery; no counting rule, no criterion separating T12 from L1, no handling of numeric variants.
- Laterality of rib anomalies promised (p. 5) but never reported; bifid and foramen ribs recorded but never counted; rib lengths recorded but never reported.
- Table 5 categories sum exactly to the 96 patients, yet Figure 4A to B shows one patient with two anomaly types (hypoplastic right and absent left 12th rib, p. 9); the filing rule is not given.
- Figure 2E against the text (p. 4 to 5): the text puts TPD between X and Y and SPD between P and Q, but the figure puts P and Q across the pedicle on the axial drawing and X and Y at the notches on the sagittal drawing (Inference: letters swapped).
- Figure 2D (p. 5): the caption says lines a and c are the pedicle axes and b the mid-sagittal line, but in the image "b" labels the anterior convergence point and "c" the middle line (Inference on the reading). The example angles (15.12 and 14.50 deg) are well below the table means at any level (21 to 28 deg), and the level is not given.
- Figure 2B (p. 5): the line the caption calls the right pedicle length is labelled "PL-L", while the TPD on the same side is labelled "TPD-R". The example PL (56 to 59 mm) is on an unnamed lumbar level and far above the T11 mean (41 mm).
- Middle height: in Table 1 MCH lies between ACH and PCH at every level (MCH exceeds ACH by 0.24 to 0.81 mm), whereas biconcave endplates normally make the middle height the smallest, as the paper's own Figure 2A example shows (27.0 / 24.2 / 27.3 mm) (Inference: MCH may have been taken between endplate rims rather than at the central concavity, or the table is in error).
- Table 3 (p. 8): the PL difference of 0.17 mm is printed with a 95% interval of -0.13 to 0.19, which is not centred on it; the T11 TPD difference of 0.09 (interval -0.02 to 0.20) would give p near 0.11 (Inference, computed), not the printed 0.31; L2 similarly (implied about 0.62, printed 0.78).
- Table 4 (p. 8): the "Mean Diff" column is not defined; it matches group 2 minus group 1 in most rows, not oldest minus youngest (Inference). The limitations (p. 14) say the age analysis lacks confidence intervals and effect sizes, although Table 4 prints intervals. The claim that age-group differences are all below 0.2 mm (p. 14) fails narrowly for men at L2 (10.18 - 9.98 = 0.20 mm).
- Simple Summary (p. 1) says T12 dimensions become similar between sexes; only the pedicle dimensions do, the body heights still differ by about 12% (Table 1).
- The abstract's conclusion (p. 2) and the conclusions (p. 15) speak of bone volume or volumetric mass, which was never measured.
- The abstract (p. 1) says thoracic CT; the methods (p. 3) and Figure 1 say thoracic or abdominal CT; which scan covered which levels is not given, and every patient has all four levels in Table 1.
- Citation mismatch: the text credits L1 virtual morphometry for sex estimation to "Ekizoglu et al. [10]" (p. 13), but reference 10 is Bozdag and Karaman 2021, Cureus (p. 15).
- Pedicle length is listed among the measured parameters (p. 1, p. 4) but reported only at T11, by side, for the whole cohort.
- The PST threshold (6 mm against a 6.5 mm screw) is proposed without outcome data; the authors cite literature but give no breach rates.
- Ethics approval (20 May 2026) postdates the study period (2021 to 2025), consistent with a retrospective review; not a conflict.
- Table 5 statistics do not reproduce from its counts (Inference, recomputed; see Anomalies): the hypoplastic-rib chi-square of 0.982 matches neither Pearson (1.63) nor Yates (1.29); the cervical rib comparison, with expected cells under 5, was not run as Fisher's exact test despite the methods (p. 6), and Fisher gives p about 0.053, so the female excess of cervical ribs is not established.
- Table 3's intervals imply a within-patient side-difference SD of 1.3 to 1.9 mm (Inference), which the text's "tight" intervals and "high morphometric concordance" (p. 7) do not convey; the paired p-values imply larger SDs still, so intervals and p-values disagree with each other.
- Methods name "numerical" rib anomalies (p. 5), yet no rib-pair or thoracic-vertebra count is reported.
- Against other papers in this set (Inference, from their notes, not from memory): TPA 15 to 28 deg above Zindrick 1987's transverse angle at T11 to L2, although Duman cites Zindrick for the definition; SPD rising T11 to L2 where Zindrick and Panjabi fall; TPD at L1 and L2 about 1.7 mm above Bonczar 2024's pooled pedicle width; PCH at L1 and L2 1 to 1.7 mm below Bonczar's pooled posterior height. PL at T11 (41 mm) agrees with Zindrick's pedicle-axis depth (41.8 mm).
- Against Panjabi (bordercall's data file, not memory): Duman's SPD rises from T11 to L2 while Panjabi's PDH falls; Duman's TPD rises while Panjabi's PDW is flat or falls; Duman's TPA is 8 to 15 deg larger than Panjabi's PDIs, and Panjabi's right-side PDIs has its smallest value at T12 (8.0 deg) where Duman's TPA shows no dip. Inference: definitions (CT axial reformat, isthmus and axis placement), living patients against cadaver bone, sample size (12 specimens) and population may all contribute; the paper does not discuss Panjabi.
