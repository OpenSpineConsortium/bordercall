## What the paper is

- Design: a normative MRI series. The abstract calls the 604 subjects "prospectively enrolled" (p. 1, PDF 1), while the methods say their scans were "selected" from the whole adult population referred to the department for lumbosacral MRI between January 2007 and July 2012 (p. 2, PDF 2). Inference: in practice a selection of referred patients' studies, not volunteers.
- Population: 604 living adults, 302 men and 302 women (the authors call the even split a coincidence), aged 20 to 45, all from Central and Southern Italy and all Caucasian, p. 2 (PDF 2). Weight and height were recorded for every subject to test correlation, but their values (means, ranges) are never reported. Mean age and the age distribution are not reported.
- Why 20 to 45: to have a completed spinal development and no significant pathological condition such as degenerative arthrosis, p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Exclusion criteria (Table 1, p. 2, PDF 2): age under 20 or over 45; neurogenic claudication; a history of typical lumbosacral pain that is severe, constant and radiates to the legs; congenital diseases; oncological or haematological disease; previous surgery or trauma of the spine; a lumbosacral transitional vertebra or any other spinal abnormality on MRI; scalloping of a vertebral body; degenerative arthrosis on MRI; inflammatory, immunological or connective tissue disorders; chronic drug therapy; early menopause with risk of osteopenia; and any qualitative suspicion of stenosis or dural ectasia on the sagittal T1 and T2 images read by a radiologist experienced in neuroradiology. The number screened and the number excluded under each criterion are not given.
- Modality: 1.5 T MRI (GE Signa Excite HD), sagittal T1-weighted (TR/TE 420/8 ms) and T2-weighted (TR/TE 2700/102 ms) fast spin echo; slice thickness 4 mm, spacing 0.4 mm, matrix 384 x 224, field of view 320 x 320 mm, 4 excitations, p. 2 (PDF 2). No axial images were used for the measures. Inference: the 0.4 mm "spacing" is the interslice gap as GE uses the word, so slice centres lie 4.4 mm apart; the acquired in-plane pixel is about 0.83 x 1.43 mm (320/384 and 320/224).
- Readers: three radiologists (initials AP, EC, MC), each with more than 10 years in musculoskeletal radiology and neuroradiology; T1 and T2 were read together to delineate the anterior epidural fat and the ventral surface of the dural sac, p. 2 (PDF 2). Whether each subject was measured by one reader or by all three, and how readings were combined, is not said.
- Levels: L1 to the first sacral vertebra, six levels, each measured at the vertebral body (the mid-portion of the posterior wall), not at the disc, p. 2 (PDF 2); Figures 1 and 2 (p. 3, PDF 3). T12/L1 and above are not measured.
- How levels were identified and counted: not stated. Spines with a lumbosacral transitional vertebra were excluded (Table 1), so every included spine is taken to have five lumbar vertebrae above a normal S1; the counting method (from the sacrum up, from the last rib, or from a whole-spine localiser) is not given. Inference: a 320 mm sagittal field usually shows the lower thoracic spine to the sacrum, so the readers could count up from the sacrum, but the paper does not say what they did.
- Statistics: per level and per sex mean +/- SD; what the authors call the "95% CI" is mean +/- 1.96 SD (p. 2, PDF 2), which is a reference range, not a confidence interval of the mean. The lower limit is the cutoff for a normal canal (SCD, below it means stenosis); the upper limit is the cutoff for a normal dural sac ratio (DSR, above it means dural ectasia). Nonpaired Student's t test between sexes (P < 0.05), a normality test of each distribution (test and result not reported), Pearson correlation with weight and height, SPSS 11.5, p. 2-3 (PDF 2-3). Counted data are given as percentages (p. 2-3).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- Lumbosacral transitional vertebra: an exclusion criterion, worded "lumbosacral transitional vertebra or other abnormality of the spine on MRI" (Table 1, p. 2, PDF 2). No prevalence, no denominator, no definition (no Castellvi type, no counting rule), and no statement of how it was recognised on a lumbar sagittal MRI. The paper therefore gives no prior for L6, T13, sacralisation or lumbarisation.
- Other anomalies excluded without counts: vertebral body scalloping, congenital diseases, any other spinal abnormality on MRI (Table 1, p. 2, PDF 2).
- Congenital stenosis is described as a variant of canal shape, not counted: thick, short pedicles reduce the sagittal canal (p. 1, PDF 1, ref 7); thick laminae and articular processes, and sometimes short pedicles, give a normal transverse but a reduced sagittal diameter (ref 11, p. 4-5, PDF 4-5). One example patient (32 years, sex not stated) with congenital stenosis and early degenerative change is shown in Figure 5c-d (p. 5, PDF 5).
- Consequence for the numbers: the normal limits hold only for five-lumbar spines with a normal S1. Inference: applied to a spine with an unrecognised LSTV, the "S1" values (VBD 22.4, SCD 14.9, DSD 9.9 mm) would be compared against a level of different shape, and a lumbarised S1 or an L6 would be misjudged.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### Sagittal spinal canal diameter (SCD)

- Definition: the anteroposterior diameter of the bony canal, taken at right angles to the spinal canal's long axis, from the posterior wall of the vertebral body at its "midportion" to the front edge of the lamina, p. 2 (PDF 2). The Figure 1 caption (p. 3, PDF 3) draws it from the middle third of the posterior body wall to "the front edge of the base of the spinous process", that is, the spinolaminar junction in the midline.
- Plane and instrument: sagittal MRI (the slice is not named; the landmarks are midline), T1 and T2 read together; Figure 2b draws it on T1 (p. 3, PDF 3). The measuring tool is not named; Figure 2 prints values to 0.01 mm with decimal commas. Inference: on-screen electronic calipers.
- Levels: L1 to S1. At S1 the line is oblique in the image, perpendicular to the inclined sacral canal (Figure 2b, p. 3, PDF 3).
- Values (Table 2, p. 4, PDF 4), mean +/- SD in mm, all / men / women, P, then the lower cutoff men / women:
  - L1 18.7 +/- 2.0 / 18.6 +/- 2.1 / 18.8 +/- 1.9; P = 0.194; cutoff 14.5 / 15.0
  - L2 17.9 +/- 2.0 / 17.7 +/- 1.9 / 18.0 +/- 2.0; P = 0.108; cutoff 13.9 / 14.1
  - L3 16.9 +/- 2.0 / 16.8 +/- 2.0 / 17.0 +/- 2.0; P = 0.108; cutoff 13.0 / 13.2
  - L4 16.9 +/- 2.1 / 16.8 +/- 2.1 / 16.9 +/- 2.1; P = 0.900; cutoff 12.7 / 12.8
  - L5 17.3 +/- 2.4 / 17.5 +/- 2.5 / 17.1 +/- 2.2; P = 0.031; cutoff 12.6 / 12.8
  - S1 14.9 +/- 2.5 / 15.5 +/- 2.6 / 14.2 +/- 2.2; P < 0.001; cutoff 10.4 / 9.9
  - Inference (checked): each cutoff equals the sex mean minus 1.96 SD within 0.1 mm.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Build the vertebra's local frame (cranio-caudal axis from the end-plate normals or the canal centreline, AP axis in the midsagittal plane through the spinous process). In the midsagittal plane, at the middle third of the body's height, take the posterior cortex of the body subregion and the anterior cortex of the spinolaminar junction (where the laminae meet the base of the spinous process) in the posterior-elements subregion; SCD is the length of the empty gap (the vertebral foramen) between them along the direction perpendicular to the local canal axis. At S1 tilt the measuring direction with the sacral canal.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference (the paper uses MRI only and mentions X-ray studies only as literature, p. 5, PDF 5). The lateral radiograph gives it as the distance from the posterior body wall at mid-height to the spinolaminar line, corrected for magnification (or expressed as a ratio to VBD, which shares the magnification). Not possible on an AP radiograph.
- Across levels: in both sexes it decreases from L1 to L4, rises at L5 and falls sharply at S1 (p. 3, PDF 3); pooled minimum at L3 and L4 (16.9 each), L5 17.3, S1 14.9 (Table 2). Inference: L5 to S1 (2.4 mm) is the only step about one population SD (d about 1.0); L1 to L2 (0.8 mm) and L2 to L3 (1.0 mm) are about 0.4 to 0.5 SD; L3 to L4 (0) and L4 to L5 (0.4 mm, about 0.2 SD) do not separate.
- Sex, age, size, population: no sex difference at L1 to L4 (P > 0.05); men larger at L5 (0.4 mm, P = 0.031) and at S1 (1.3 mm, P < 0.001), p. 3 (PDF 3), Table 2. Not correlated with weight or height at any level (R squared < 0.1), p. 3 (PDF 3). Ages 20 to 45 only; no age analysis. Central and Southern Italian Caucasians only; the authors warn the limits may not hold for other populations, citing pedicle differences in Indian and Nepalese series, p. 6 (PDF 6).
- Error: not reported (no intra- or inter-observer statistic). Inference: a pixel of 0.83 x 1.43 mm and 4 mm slices 4.4 mm apart put the floor of precision near 1 mm, about half the population SD.

### Sagittal vertebral body diameter (VBD)

- Definition: the anteroposterior diameter of the body ("soma") of the same vertebra, taken "at the same level" as the SCD, p. 2 (PDF 2), that is at the mid-portion of the body. Figure 1C (p. 3, PDF 3) draws a line across the middle third of the body from the anterior to the posterior cortex, parallel to the end plates. So VBD is a mid-body depth, not an end-plate depth.
- Plane: sagittal MRI; Figure 2a draws it on T1, the lines tilting with each body's lordotic inclination (p. 3, PDF 3). At S1 the line is oblique, across the S1 body along the sacral inclination (Figure 2a).
- Values (Table 3, p. 4, PDF 4), mean +/- SD in mm, all / men / women; P < 0.001 at every level:
  - L1 26.5 +/- 3.2 / 28.3 +/- 2.8 / 24.7 +/- 2.5
  - L2 27.6 +/- 3.3 / 29.5 +/- 2.7 / 25.6 +/- 2.5
  - L3 28.9 +/- 3.3 / 30.8 +/- 2.8 / 27.0 +/- 2.5
  - L4 29.6 +/- 3.1 / 31.4 +/- 2.6 / 27.8 +/- 2.4
  - L5 29.6 +/- 3.1 / 31.4 +/- 2.8 / 27.9 +/- 2.4
  - S1 22.4 +/- 3.4 / 24.4 +/- 2.8 / 20.3 +/- 2.7
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Body subregion only (cut the pedicles off at the posterior wall); in the midsagittal plane, the AP extent of the body at mid-height, along the axis parallel to the end plates (perpendicular to the body's cranio-caudal axis). Record the end-plate depths separately; they are not this measure.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Lateral radiograph, anterior to posterior cortex at mid-height, corrected for magnification; not on an AP radiograph.
- Across levels: the text says it increases from L1 to L5 and then drops sharply at S1 (p. 3, PDF 3). Table 3: pooled steps L1 to L2 +1.1, L2 to L3 +1.3, L3 to L4 +0.7, L4 to L5 0.0 (equal, 29.6), L5 to S1 -7.2 mm; in men L4 and L5 are equal (31.4), in women L5 exceeds L4 by 0.1 mm. Inference: the S1 drop is more than 2 SD and is the strongest L5/S1 marker in the paper; L4 and L5 cannot be told apart by VBD; the L1-L2 and L2-L3 steps are about 0.35 to 0.4 pooled SD.
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger at every level by 3.5 to 4.1 mm (L1 3.6, L2 3.9, L3 3.8, L4 3.6, L5 3.5, S1 4.1), P < 0.001, p. 3 (PDF 3), Table 3. Inference: that is 13 to 15 percent of the female value at L1 to L5 and 20 percent at S1. The authors report no correlation of "all obtained metrics" with weight or height (R squared < 0.1), p. 3 (PDF 3); the sentence that names measures one by one names SCD, DSD and DSR, not VBD. Inference: given a 3.5 to 4 mm sex difference, "no correlation" here means R squared below 0.1 (r up to about 0.3), not a zero slope.
- Error: not reported.

### Sagittal dural sac diameter (DSD)

- Definition: the anteroposterior diameter of the dural sac, at the same level as the SCD, p. 2 (PDF 2); Figure 1B draws it parallel to the SCD line (p. 3, PDF 3). The ventral surface of the sac is separated from the anterior epidural fat by reading T1 and T2 together, p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Plane: sagittal MRI, in the direction of the SCD line; Figure 2c draws it on T1 (p. 3, PDF 3).
- Values (Table 4, p. 4, PDF 4), mean +/- SD in mm, all / men / women, P:
  - L1 15.3 +/- 1.7 / 15.1 +/- 1.7 / 15.6 +/- 1.6; < 0.001
  - L2 14.4 +/- 1.8 / 14.1 +/- 1.7 / 14.8 +/- 1.7; < 0.001
  - L3 13.6 +/- 1.9 / 13.1 +/- 1.9 / 14.0 +/- 1.8; < 0.001
  - L4 13.3 +/- 2.1 / 13.0 +/- 2.2 / 13.6 +/- 2.0; < 0.001
  - L5 12.9 +/- 2.4 / 12.9 +/- 2.6 / 12.9 +/- 2.2; 0.719
  - S1 9.9 +/- 3.0 / 10.3 +/- 3.2 / 9.5 +/- 2.7; 0.001
- From a 3D CT instance mask: not possible; the dural sac is soft tissue (it needs MRI or CT myelography). Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible without myelography. Inference.
- Across levels: a monotonic decrease from L1 to S1 in both sexes, p. 3 (PDF 3), Table 4; the largest step is L5 to S1 (3.0 mm pooled).
- Sex: women larger at L1 to L4 (by 0.5 to 0.9 mm, P < 0.001); equal at L5 (P = 0.719); men larger at S1 (by 0.8 mm, P = 0.001), Table 4. The text says women are larger "except at L5 and S1", p. 3 (PDF 3), without saying that S1 is reversed.
- Error: not reported. The SD widens caudally (1.7 mm at L1 to 3.0 mm at S1, pooled), Table 4.

### Dural sac ratio (DSR)

- Definition: DSD divided by VBD at each level, p. 2 (PDF 2). The upper limit (mean + 1.96 SD) is the cutoff for a normal sac; above it is taken as dural ectasia, p. 2 (PDF 2). Inference: a per-subject ratio, then averaged (the table SDs are of ratios).
- Values (Table 5, p. 5, PDF 5), mean +/- SD, all / men / women, then upper cutoff men / women; P < 0.001 at every level:
  - L1 0.59 +/- 0.09 / 0.54 +/- 0.08 / 0.64 +/- 0.09; cutoff 0.70 / 0.82
  - L2 0.53 +/- 0.08 / 0.48 +/- 0.07 / 0.58 +/- 0.09; cutoff 0.62 / 0.76
  - L3 0.48 +/- 0.08 / 0.43 +/- 0.07 / 0.52 +/- 0.09; cutoff 0.57 / 0.70
  - L4 0.45 +/- 0.08 / 0.42 +/- 0.08 / 0.49 +/- 0.08; cutoff 0.56 / 0.65
  - L5 0.44 +/- 0.09 / 0.41 +/- 0.09 / 0.46 +/- 0.09; cutoff 0.59 / 0.63
  - S1 0.44 +/- 0.14 / 0.41 +/- 0.14 / 0.46 +/- 0.15; cutoff 0.70 / 0.75
- From a 3D CT mask or a radiograph: not possible (it needs the sac). Inference.
- Across levels: the text calls the decrease monotonic from L1 to S1 (p. 3, PDF 3); Table 5 shows L5 and S1 equal in all three columns (0.44, 0.41, 0.46).
- Sex: higher in women at every level (P < 0.001), p. 3 (PDF 3). Inference: the smaller female VBD drives it at every level, helped at L1 to L4 by the larger female DSD. Figure 4 (p. 4, PDF 4) shows the cumulative distributions at L1, the female (dotted) curve to the right of the male (solid), with vertical lines at the two L1 cutoffs, 0.70 and 0.82.
- Error: not reported.

### S1/L4 dural sac diameter ratio

- Definition: DSD at S1 divided by DSD at L4, a test of whether the sac tapers, p. 2 (PDF 2); a sac wider at S1 than at L4 is a major criterion of dural ectasia (ref 28, Habermann), p. 2 and p. 6 (PDF 2, 6).
- Result: the ratio exceeds 1 in 11 percent of men and 4 percent of women (of 302 each), p. 3 (PDF 3), restated p. 6 (PDF 6); the text calls the sex difference significant but gives no test or P. Normal limit (their "95% CI"): below 1 in women, below 1.2 in men, p. 3 and p. 6 (PDF 3, 6). Lundby (ref 25) found the maximum S1 DSD exceeding L4 in 5 percent of controls, p. 6 (PDF 6).
- Figure 3 (p. 4, PDF 4) plots the cumulative distributions; as drawn, its line styles contradict the text (see Gaps and conflicts).
- Not obtainable from a CT bone mask or a plain radiograph. Inference.

### Not measured but discussed

- Dural sac cross-sectional area (DSCA): preferred by other clinicians for stenosis, but significantly affected by slice orientation, p. 5 (PDF 5); that sentence carries no citation. Inference: ref 20 (Henderson, on whether stenosis assessment depends on slice orientation) is the likely source.
- Qualitative morphological grading of stenosis (refs 20, 21; ref 21 is Schizas): based on the dural sac's shape on axial T2 images and the CSF to rootlet ratio, less dependent on slice orientation than DSCA; the authors find it useful for suspicion but subjective, and prefer the quantitative AP diameter of the bony canal, p. 5 (PDF 5).
- Transverse canal diameter: can be normal in congenital stenosis while the sagittal diameter is reduced, p. 4-5 (PDF 4-5). Not measured.
- Canal shape: not constant along the lumbar spine, circular or rounded in the upper lumbar vertebrae, triangular in the mid lumbar and trefoil in the lower lumbar (ref 22, Aly and Amin, an Egyptian population), p. 5 (PDF 5).
- The AP canal diameter correlates statistically with the canal's cross-sectional area (ref 23, Gepstein), which the authors take to justify it as the measure of canal size, p. 5 (PDF 5).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1: widest canal (18.7 mm), widest sac (15.3 mm) and highest DSR (0.59); the smallest lumbar VBD (26.5 mm), Tables 2-5 (p. 4-5, PDF 4-5).
- L2 and L3: the canal narrows by 0.8 then 1.0 mm a level; VBD grows by 1.1 then 1.3 mm a level (Tables 2-3).
- L3 and L4: the canal minimum (16.9 mm both, pooled), Table 2. In the symptomatic Figure 5 patient the stenosis lay from L1 to L4 (p. 3-4, PDF 3-4).
- L4 and L5: VBD identical (29.6 mm pooled); the canal rises 0.4 mm at L5; L5 is the first level with a sex difference in SCD (men 0.4 mm larger), Tables 2-3. DSD has no sex difference at L5 (P = 0.719), Table 4.
- L5/S1 junction: every measure but DSR drops at S1: VBD by 7.2 mm (29.6 to 22.4), SCD by 2.4 mm (17.3 to 14.9), DSD by 3.0 mm (12.9 to 9.9); DSR does not change (0.44 to 0.44), Tables 2-5. S1 has the largest pooled SDs of all four measures (VBD 3.4, SCD 2.5, DSD 3.0 mm, DSR 0.14). At S1 men exceed women in SCD by 1.3 mm and in DSD by 0.8 mm, the reverse of the L1 to L4 DSD pattern. The S1 lines are drawn obliquely, following the sacral inclination (Figure 2, p. 3, PDF 3).
- T12/L1, C2/C3, C7/T1: not covered.
- Worked example (Figure 2, p. 3, PDF 3), a 30-year-old asymptomatic man, T1, L1 to S1 in mm: VBD 31.32, 33.45, 35.37, 32.71, 33.99, 24.68 (the last digit of the S1 label is partly hidden by the line's end marker); SCD 20.36, 19.34, 17.70, 20.21, 20.58, 18.35; DSD 16.79, 14.84, 15.26, 16.33, 13.96, 11.21. Inference: this man is above the male mean at every level; within him VBD dips at L4 below L3 and L5, SCD dips at L3 and rises again at L4 and L5, and DSD rises from L2 to L4, so none of the population's smooth level trends need hold within one person; only the S1 drop is clear in all three.
- Worked example (Figure 5, p. 5, PDF 5), two 32-year-old patients (sex not stated), T2 (a, c) and T1 (b, d): the normal one (panel b) reads 15.34, 14.79, 13.54, 13.72, 14.46, 11.04 mm from L1 to S1; the one with chronic low back pain, neurogenic claudication, early degenerative change and congenital stenosis (panel d) reads 12.21, 9.52, 9.77, 9.28, 12.67, 10.38 mm. The text gives L1 12.1, L2 9.5, L3 9.7, L4 9.2 mm (below cutoff) and L5 12.7, S1 10.4 mm (within normal), and concludes that cutoffs must be defined level by level ("metameric"), p. 4 (PDF 4). Inference: the panel b values, read as SCD, sit within about 1 mm of the sex-specific cutoffs at L1 to L4 although the patient is called normal; and with the panel d label values, whichever the sex, one of L5 (12.67 against the female cutoff 12.8) or S1 (10.38 against the male cutoff 10.4) is marginally below its cutoff; with the text's rounded values a man passes (S1 equal to the cutoff) and a woman fails at L5.

## Measurement error and reliability

- No intra-observer or inter-observer statistic, no repeat measurement, no ICC, no Bland-Altman analysis anywhere in the paper (p. 1-6, PDF 1-6).
- Three experienced readers; whether readings were shared, split or duplicated is not said, p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Instrument: 4 mm sagittal slices with 0.4 mm spacing, matrix 384 x 224 over 320 mm, p. 2 (PDF 2). Inference: in-plane pixels of about 0.83 x 1.43 mm; a midline structure may fall up to about 2 mm off the nearest slice centre; partial volume blurs cortical edges.
- Values on the figures are printed to 0.01 mm (Figures 2 and 5, p. 3 and 5), a false precision given the pixel size. Inference.
- The authors note that DSCA depends on slice orientation (p. 5, PDF 5); they do not test whether their sagittal diameters do. Inference.
- Normality was tested but the result was not reported; the cutoffs assume normality (mean +/- 1.96 SD), p. 2-3 (PDF 2-3).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- MRI is called the best imaging for back pain; causes include disc degeneration, central canal stenosis, metastases and compression fractures, p. 1 (PDF 1).
- Lumbar stenosis narrows the canal and foramina and compresses roots or the cauda equina; neurogenic claudication and radiculopathy (with or without intermittent radicular pain) are the main symptoms; it is primary (congenital) or secondary (acquired: degeneration, trauma, infection, surgery, bony overgrowth, metabolic or endocrine disease), p. 1 (PDF 1).
- Secondary stenosis is easy to recognise on MRI; stenosis without an acquired cause is hard to define, which is why level-specific numbers are needed, p. 1 (PDF 1). A small canal predisposes to back pain and stenosis, and published thresholds vary widely between populations (ref 10), p. 4 (PDF 4).
- Congenital stenosis: thick, short pedicles (ref 7, p. 1, PDF 1); thick laminae and articular processes, sometimes short pedicles, with a normal transverse diameter and a reduced sagittal one (ref 11, Verbiest), p. 4-5 (PDF 4-5).
- The Delphi survey (ref 12, Mamisch) recommends the AP canal diameter for practice and research, p. 5 (PDF 5). A generic sagittal diameter below 12 mm is the accepted definition of congenital stenosis (ref 12); the minimum sagittal canal diameter in anatomical, radiographic, CT and MRI studies ranges from 8 to 15 mm (refs 12-19), p. 5 (PDF 5). The authors argue for their level- and sex-specific cutoffs instead (Table 2 and Figure 5c-d).
- Stenosis may be segmental: in the Figure 5 patient only L1 to L4 were narrow while L5 and S1 were within range, so each level must be judged against its own norm, p. 3-4 (PDF 3-4).
- Dural ectasia is a major criterion of Marfan syndrome (ref 9, De Paepe 1996, the revised diagnostic criteria), p. 2 and p. 5 (PDF 2, 5). DSR is useful because its pathological increase implies ectasia, p. 5 (PDF 5).
  - Oosterhof (ref 8; Table 6 lists 44 subjects, 29 men and 15 women) set normal DSR cutoffs on lumbosacral MRI and concluded that DSR above 0.57 at S1 or above 0.47 at L3 was almost sufficient for Marfan syndrome in young adults, p. 6 (PDF 6). Applying Oosterhof's cutoffs to this normal cohort would call 30, 36, 49, 34, 30 and 18 percent of its subjects ectatic at L1, L2, L3, L4, L5 and S1, p. 6 (PDF 6).
  - Lundby (ref 25; Table 6 lists 101, 64 men and 37 women) found the criteria met by 12 percent of subjects at L1 and 19 percent at L5, and proposed an S1 cutoff of 0.59, p. 6 (PDF 6).
  - Weigang (ref 26), using Oosterhof's method, found the criteria met by 94 percent of Marfan patients and 44 percent of non-Marfan patients, which the authors read as Oosterhof's cutoffs being too small, p. 6 (PDF 6).
  - Table 6 (p. 6, PDF 6) sets the DSR cutoffs side by side: this study men / women L1 0.70 / 0.82, L2 0.62 / 0.76, L3 0.57 / 0.70, L4 0.56 / 0.65, L5 0.59 / 0.63, S1 0.70 / 0.75; Oosterhof (sexes pooled) L1 0.64, L2 0.55, L3 0.47, L4 0.48, L5 0.48, S1 0.57; Lundby S1 only 0.59.
  - The S1-wider-than-L4 criterion (ref 28) is met by 11 percent of normal men and 4 percent of normal women, so the authors take S1/L4 below 1.2 as normal in men and below 1 in women, p. 6 (PDF 6).
- DSR norms are for adults only: DSR is higher in healthy children and falls by about 10 percent every 3 years during growth (ref 27, Knirsch), p. 6 (PDF 6).
- Epidural fat: stenosis may go with less epidural fat around the narrowed segment, while excess epidural fat may itself compress the sac (ref 29, Reina). No subject had a condition that alters epidural fat (kyphoscoliosis, lumbar stenosis, long-term steroid therapy, endogenous steroid excess), so epidural fat was assumed normal and not treated as a variable, p. 6 (PDF 6).
- Women's canals: the authors call the female SCD cutoffs "large" compared with men's and read a wider female canal as an adaptation to pregnancy, citing Hay's lordosis study (ref 24), p. 5 (PDF 5). Inference: Table 2 supports only a 0.1 to 0.5 mm excess at L1 to L5 (see Gaps).
- Population pitfall: the limits come from one homogeneous Central and Southern Italian Caucasian population and may not transfer; Indian and Nepalese pedicles differ from Caucasian ones (refs 30, 31), p. 6 (PDF 6).
- Conclusion: a quantitative normal range may give more accurate diagnoses, avoid repeated tests and speed diagnosis, p. 6 (PDF 6).
- Pitfall (Inference): the "95% CI" cutoffs flag 2.5 percent of normal people at each level by construction; across six levels a normal person will often cross at least one cutoff.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Measure the bony sagittal canal in the midsagittal plane, perpendicular to the local canal axis, from the middle third of the posterior body wall to the anterior edge of the spinolaminar junction (base of the spinous process); at S1 tilt the line with the sacral canal (p. 2-3, PDF 2-3; Figures 1-2).
2. Measure the body's AP diameter at the same height as the canal line (mid-body), cortex to cortex, parallel to the end plates; do not substitute an end-plate depth (p. 2, PDF 2; Figure 1C, p. 3, PDF 3).
3. Use the L5/S1 drop as a junction marker: expect VBD to fall by about 7 mm (29.6 to 22.4), SCD by about 2.4 mm (17.3 to 14.9) and DSD by about 3 mm (12.9 to 9.9) from L5 to S1 (Tables 2-4, p. 4, PDF 4); expect DSR not to change across it (Table 5, p. 5, PDF 5).
4. Do not use VBD to separate L4 from L5 (equal means, 29.6 mm), nor SCD to separate L3 from L4 (16.9 mm each) (Tables 2-3, p. 4, PDF 4).
5. Expect the canal to be widest at L1, narrowest at L3-L4 and slightly wider at L5; a canal that widens steadily caudally from L1 is not this pattern (Table 2, p. 4, PDF 4; text p. 3, PDF 3).
6. Condition body size on sex: male VBD exceeds female by 3.5 to 4.1 mm at every level; do not condition the canal on sex above L5 (Tables 2-3, p. 4, PDF 4).
7. Do not scale canal or sac dimensions with height or weight; none correlated (R squared < 0.1, p. 3, PDF 3).
8. Before applying these norms, establish that the spine has five lumbar vertebrae and a normal S1; this cohort excluded transitional vertebrae, so its S1 values do not describe a transitional segment (Table 1, p. 2, PDF 2).
9. Do not expect the population trends level by level inside one spine: in Figure 2's normal man VBD, SCD and DSD each reverse between L2 and L5; trust the S1 drop, and weigh the smaller steps over several levels (Figure 2, p. 3, PDF 3).
10. To flag stenosis, compare each level to its sex-specific cutoff (Table 2, p. 4, PDF 4), not to a single 12 mm threshold; judge level by level, since stenosis can spare L5 and S1 (p. 3-5, PDF 3-5).
11. To flag dural ectasia, compare DSR to the sex-specific upper cutoffs (Table 5, p. 5, PDF 5) and the S1/L4 DSD ratio to 1.2 in men and 1 in women (p. 6, PDF 6); do not use Oosterhof's cutoffs on normal adults, which they flag 18 to 49 percent of (p. 6, PDF 6); never apply adult DSR norms to children (p. 6, PDF 6).
12. Treat these numbers as Italian Caucasian adults aged 20 to 45; widen the prior for other populations or ages (p. 2 and p. 6, PDF 2, 6).
13. On MRI, read T1 and T2 together to place the ventral sac surface against the anterior epidural fat (p. 2, PDF 2).
14. When reading Figure 3, trust the text and Table 4 over the line legend for which sex has more S1/L4 ratios above 1 (p. 3-4, PDF 3-4; Inference, see Gaps).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. LSTV was an exclusion criterion with no count and no denominator (Table 1, p. 2, PDF 2). The paper gives nothing on L6, T13, rib counts or block vertebrae, and does not say how levels were counted.

(b) Measures:
- Canal depth: this paper's SCD runs from the posterior body wall at its middle third to the spinolaminar junction, perpendicular to the canal axis, on sagittal MRI (p. 2-3). Panjabi 1992 (lumbar) draws SCD on its top view from the posterior body wall to the anterior surface of the arch at the base of the spinous process (from this set's panjabi1992 note), the same two landmarks; the height along the body is not fixed in either. Inference: a mask-derived SCD at mid-body height on the midsagittal plane matches this paper; a minimum over the arch's height (as Eisenstein's caliper took it, per this set's eisenstein1977 note) reads smaller.
- Vertebral body AP diameter here is a mid-body ("soma") depth (Figure 1C), not Panjabi's upper or lower end-plate depth (EPDu, EPDl). Inference: a mid-body depth is smaller than the end-plate depths because of the anterior waist and the rims; Panjabi's EPDu is 34.1 to 35.5 mm at L1 to L5 (panjabi1992 note) against this paper's 26.5 to 29.6 mm pooled (28.3 to 31.4 mm in men), so comparing a mask's end-plate depth with this paper's VBD biases it upward by several millimetres. If the generator draws end-plate depths, add a mid-body depth or do not calibrate end-plate depths with this table.
- S1 is outside the generator's C3 to L5 range; this paper gives S1 values (VBD 22.4, SCD 14.9 mm pooled; by sex in Tables 2-3) that could anchor an S1 state, measured obliquely along the sacral inclination.
- DSD, DSR and the S1/L4 sac ratio are soft-tissue measures, not obtainable from a CT bone mask; useful only for an MRI pathway.
- Inference: a canal-to-body ratio SCD/VBD, free of radiographic magnification, computed from the table means (a ratio of means, not a mean of ratios), is men 0.66, 0.60, 0.55, 0.54, 0.56, 0.64 and women 0.76, 0.70, 0.63, 0.61, 0.61, 0.70 at L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, S1: a U shape with its bottom at L4 in men and L4-L5 in women, which a lateral radiograph could give.

(c) Measurement error: the paper reports none. Inference: take about 1 mm (one in-plane pixel) as a floor on SCD and VBD error for 1.5 T sagittal MRI of this protocol; the within-sex SDs (SCD 1.9 to 2.6 mm, VBD 2.4 to 2.8 mm, DSD 1.6 to 3.2 mm) are between-subject spread including any reader error, not error alone.

(d) Covariates:
- Sex: VBD men larger by 3.5 to 4.1 mm at every level (P < 0.001); SCD equal at L1 to L4, men larger at L5 (0.4 mm) and S1 (1.3 mm); DSD women larger at L1 to L4 by 0.5 to 0.9 mm, equal at L5, men larger at S1 by 0.8 mm; DSR women higher everywhere (Tables 2-5, p. 4-5).
- Size: no correlation of any measure with height or weight (R squared < 0.1), p. 3 (PDF 3). Inference: the canal should be coupled weakly or not at all to the patient size factor that scales the body; body depth differs by sex by 13 to 20 percent while the canal does not above L5.
- Age: 20 to 45 only; no age analysis.
- Population: Central and Southern Italian Caucasians only.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Not stated: how levels were counted; how LSTV was recognised and how many were excluded; the number screened and excluded under each criterion; mean age, height and weight; which sagittal slice was used; which sequence was used for which measure (Figure 2 is T1, the text says both were read); whether each subject had one reader or three; any reliability statistic; the normality test's result; the sex of the Figure 5 patients; any transverse dimension; anything above L1.
- Figure 3 against the text and Table 4 (p. 3-4, PDF 3-4): the caption gives men the solid line and women the dotted, but as drawn the solid curve crosses ratio 1 at about 0.96 and the dotted at about 0.89, which would put about 4 percent of men and 11 percent of women above 1, the reverse of the text. The medians also fit reversed labels: solid about 0.70, dotted about 0.80, while the ratios of Table 4's means are 0.79 in men (10.3/13.0) and 0.70 in women (9.5/13.6). Inference: the caption's line styles are swapped; the text and Table 4 agree with each other. The dotted curve also stays just under 1 out to 1.4, so about 1 percent of that group exceed 1.4 (read from the figure).
- Abstract against Table 2: the abstract gives the male SCD cutoff range as 14.5 to 10.1 mm (p. 1, PDF 1), but Table 2 gives the male S1 cutoff as 10.4 mm (p. 4, PDF 4); 15.5 - 1.96 x 2.6 = 10.4, so the abstract's 10.1 is the error.
- The abstract says the mean DSR ranges from 0.70 to 0.56 in men and from 0.82 to 0.63 in women (p. 1, PDF 1); those are the cutoffs of Table 5 (L1 to the lowest level), not the means (0.54 to 0.41 men, 0.64 to 0.46 women).
- The text says DSD is higher in women "except at L5 and S1" (p. 3, PDF 3); Table 4 shows S1 significantly different (P = 0.001) in the opposite direction, men larger.
- The text calls the DSR decrease monotonic from L1 to S1 (p. 3, PDF 3); Table 5 shows L5 and S1 equal. The text says VBD increases from L1 to L5 (p. 3); in men L4 and L5 are equal (Table 3).
- The text calls the S1/L4 sex difference significant (p. 3, PDF 3) but gives no test or P value.
- The Discussion speaks of "large" female SCD cutoffs relative to males (p. 5, PDF 5); Table 2's female cutoffs exceed the male ones by only 0.1 to 0.5 mm at L1 to L5 and are smaller at S1, and the means do not differ at L1 to L4.
- The Discussion quotes "our cutoff of normality" at S1 as 0.72 (p. 6, PDF 6); no table gives 0.72 (Tables 5 and 6 give 0.70 men, 0.75 women). Inference: a pooled value, 0.44 + 1.96 x 0.14 = 0.71.
- The S1/L4 limit of "below 1" for women (p. 3, p. 6) sits awkwardly with 4 percent of normal women exceeding 1; a 97.5th percentile would lie above 1. Inference.
- Table 2 prints the same P (0.108) at L2 and L3. Inference: possibly a copying error; the rounded means do not allow a check.
- Text against Figure 5: the text gives L1 12.1 mm for the stenotic patient (p. 4, PDF 4) where the label reads 12.21 (p. 5, PDF 5); L3 and L4 labels 9.77 and 9.28 are given as 9.7 and 9.2 (truncated), while L2, L5 and S1 are rounded (9.5, 12.7, 10.4).
- Design wording: "prospectively enrolled" (abstract) against scans selected from 2007 to 2012 referrals (methods), p. 1-2 (PDF 1-2). The subjects were referred for lumbosacral MRI, so "nonsymptomatic" means free of the excluded symptoms, not free of any complaint. Inference.
- Circularity (Inference): subjects with any qualitative suspicion of stenosis or ectasia on the same images were excluded before measuring (Table 1), which trims both tails, so the cutoffs are likely tighter than in an unselected population.
- Figure 4's y axis reads "Relative frequency (%)" on a 0 to 1 scale, and it is a cumulative plot (p. 4, PDF 4); Figure 3's reads "Cumulative relative frequency (%)", also 0 to 1.
- Other papers in this set (from their notes, Inference until checked against the papers): Panjabi 1992's cadaveric SCD at L1 to L5 is 19.0, 18.2, 17.5, 18.6, 19.7 mm with its minimum at L3, against this paper's pooled 18.7, 17.9, 16.9, 16.9, 17.3: the upper levels agree within 0.6 mm, but this paper's rise to L5 (0.4 mm) is far smaller than Panjabi's (2.2 mm from L3). Eisenstein 1977 (ref 13 here) measured Caucasoid South African dry bone at 18, 17, 16, 16, 18 mm (men) with a caliper minimum over the arch, close to this paper's men at L1 to L4 and 0.5 mm higher at L5. MRI values here are not directly comparable with dry-bone or radiographic ones (no magnification here; partial volume at cortical edges).
