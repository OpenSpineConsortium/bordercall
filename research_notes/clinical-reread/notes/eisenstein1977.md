# Eisenstein 1977: Lumbar morphometry and pathological anatomy in South African skeletons, with reference to spinal stenosis

Source: thread "Reread every paper for clinical detail", project symphony_4, 2026-10-28.

Citation as printed on p. 173 (PDF 1): Steve Eisenstein, Johannesburg, South Africa (from the Department of
Orthopaedic Surgery, University of Witwatersrand, Johannesburg). The morphometry and pathological anatomy of the
lumbar spine in South African negroes and caucasoids with specific reference to spinal stenosis. The Journal of
Bone and Joint Surgery, Vol. 59-B, No. 2, May 1977, pp. 173-180. (The journal title is printed in the running
footer of the even pages; volume, issue, month and page on p. 173.)

## What the paper is

- Aims, as the introduction lists them (p. 173, PDF 1): what counts as spinal stenosis and whether developmental
  stenosis is as common as claimed; how to find the canal's boundaries on radiographs and the lower limit of
  normal; where the lumbar canal is narrowest; why the stenosis syndrome is rare in black South Africans and
  whether spinal dimensions differ by group; and where compression occurs in degenerative stenosis.
- Design: an osteometric study of dry, macerated skeletons, with a small radiographic calibration substudy and a
  survey of degenerative, variant and transitional anatomy. p. 173-174 (PDF 1-2). The author calls it a
  "preliminary survey" (Conclusion 7, p. 180, PDF 8) and the sample "a cadaver population" (p. 175, PDF 3).
- Population: 443 adult skeletons of the Raymond Dart Collection, Department of Anatomy (the paper does not name
  the university; the author's own department is at the University of Witwatersrand, Johannesburg): 113 "white"
  Caucasoid (78 male, 35 female), 162 South African Zulu (108 male, 54 female), 168 South African Sotho (106 male,
  62 female). Age at death 16 to 96 years, mean 50. p. 173 (PDF 1). Inference: "adult" includes skeletons from
  16 years, some of which may not be skeletally mature.
- Group mean ages, Table IV, p. 176 (PDF 4): Caucasoid male 66, Zulu male 49, Sotho male 41, Caucasoid female 65,
  Zulu female 47, Sotho female 40 years. Inference: the n-weighted mean of these is 49.8, matching the stated 50;
  the Caucasoid groups are 17 to 25 years older than the African groups, so ethnicity and age are confounded in
  every comparison.
- Denominator for the measures: 49 vertebrae missing or damaged, leaving 2,166 complete lumbar vertebrae.
  p. 173 (PDF 1). Inference: 443 x 5 = 2,215 and 2,215 - 49 = 2,166 exactly, so five lumbar vertebrae were counted
  per skeleton and the two sixth lumbar vertebrae were not added. The n per group per level is never printed.
  Inference: pooled over groups, each level holds between 394 (443 - 49) and 443 vertebrae; a group cell holds at
  most its group's skeleton count.
- Denominator for the anatomy survey: 443 skeletons; every printed percentage in the survey equals n/443 to the
  printed decimal. The stenosis percentages of Tables II and III use each group's skeleton count instead (see
  Range of normal).
- Instrument: vernier caliper; the canal and the body of each vertebra measured across and front to back, by the
  method of Jones and Thomson (1968). p. 173 (PDF 1). Inference: the vertebrae were measured as single bones (the
  paper says "each vertebra"; it mentions the articulated skeleton only for the facet-joint observations,
  p. 176, PDF 4).
- Radiographic substudy: 55 vertebrae of 11 skeletons radiographed in AP and lateral, suspended in sponge blocks
  to mimic a patient's position, tube 110 cm from the plate "by universal convention"; radio-opaque metal markers
  placed inside the canal at the mid-sagittal posterior limit; the caliper measurements repeated on the films;
  magnification factors calculated for each view. p. 174 (PDF 2). Fig. 1 (p. 176, PDF 4) is a lateral radiograph
  of a macerated spine with the markers; Fig. 2 is a tracing of it.
- Levels: L1 to L5 only; nothing above L1 or in the sacrum was measured.
- Statistics: "subjected to statistical analysis", with no method named; no SD, no test statistic, no p value is
  printed anywhere, though the abstract and Conclusion 1 call the mid-sagittal diameter "significantly" reduced and
  p. 179 calls the group and sex differences "insignificant". p. 173-174, p. 179-180 (PDF 1-2, 7-8).
- Inclusion and exclusion: adult skeletons; missing or damaged vertebrae left out. Nothing else is stated.
- How levels were identified and numbered, and whether a lumbarised S1, a sacralised L5 or an L6 was measured,
  renumbered or excluded: not stated (see Gaps).
- Prior work named: the largest earlier study of its kind had 51 skeletons, all Caucasoid (Huizinga, Heiden and
  Vinken 1952). p. 173 (PDF 1).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

All counts are skeletons out of 443, recognised by direct inspection of the dry skeletons (no projection
involved). p. 177 (PDF 5) unless stated. None of the categories is defined beyond its name.

- Lumbarisation of S1: 9 skeletons (2%), both races and both sexes.
- Persistent zygapophysial joints between S1 and S2: 16 skeletons (3.6%), both races and sexes; the author
  suggests this may be the least form of lumbarisation.
- True sixth lumbar vertebra: 2 skeletons, both Sotho (Inference: 0.45% of 443; 1.2% of the 168 Sotho).
- True transitional lumbosacral vertebra: 1 skeleton, a Zulu (Inference: 0.23% of 443; 0.6% of the 162 Zulu).
- Sacralisation of L5: 8 skeletons (1.8%); none among the 35 Caucasoid females.
- All transitional variation together (lumbarisation, sacralisation, transitional and sixth lumbar vertebrae,
  persistent sacral zygapophysial joints): 36 skeletons (8%). p. 180 (PDF 8). Inference: 9 + 16 + 2 + 1 + 8 = 36,
  so the 36 is the plain sum of the five counts; either no skeleton had two of them or the author added counts
  without removing overlaps. It leaves out the sacro-transverse joints below.
- Sacro-transverse joint, patent or ankylosed: 10 skeletons (2.3%). In most of them the lumbosacral apophysial
  facets on the side of the joint or ankylosis were notably smaller. Inference: this is the transverse process
  to sacrum articulation or fusion that later schemes (Castellvi) grade; the paper gives no side or type.
- Sacro-iliac ankylosis: 10 skeletons (2.3%), both races, all male.
- Spondylolysis (pars stress fracture): 10 skeletons (2.3%), both races and sexes; L5 in nine, L4 in one. The L4
  was a left-sided ununited fracture only, with an enlarged left inferior facet (Fig. 7, p. 178, PDF 6); the nine
  L5 defects were bilateral, and one of those vertebrae had a completely bifid spinous process.
- Spina bifida, a complete separation of the left and right laminae, in the lumbar vertebrae of 7 skeletons
  (1.6%); level not given. Called spina bifida occulta on p. 180 (PDF 8).
- Anterior wedge compression of a lumbar body: 17 skeletons (3.8%); none among Caucasoid females; level not given.
- Trefoil ("maple-leaf") canal: 66 skeletons (15%), both races and sexes, "always" at L5 and occasionally also at
  L4 (p. 177, PDF 5; "almost invariably" at L5 on p. 179, PDF 7). It distorts the canal's usual triangular outline
  and is caused by a localised thickening of the lamina running its full vertical height ("laminar bars",
  Fig. 6, p. 178, PDF 6, an inferior view of an L5). Mean age of these skeletons 46 years; 15% of them had canal
  stenosis against 6.3% of the whole sample (Inference: about 10 of the 66). p. 177 (PDF 5).
- Facet orientation variants: the superior articular surface is normally curved from medial to lateral and faces
  posteromedially; 6 skeletons had flat coronal surfaces and 8 flat sagittal surfaces (level not given); only one
  of these, a coronal one, had facet osteophytosis. p. 176-177 (PDF 4-5).
- Not reported at all: thoracic or rib counts, T13, 11 or 13 rib pairs, cervical ribs, block vertebrae.
- Numbering: the paper never says how L1 or L5 was identified, nor how a true L6 was told from a lumbarised S1,
  nor what separates a "true transitional" vertebra from sacralisation or lumbarisation. Inference: on a whole
  skeleton this would be done by counting down from the last rib-bearing vertebra and by the number of fused
  sacral segments, but the paper does not say so.
- Effect on the measures: none reported, except the smaller lumbosacral facets beside a sacro-transverse joint.
- The author's reading (p. 180, PDF 8): these are developmental variations of normal anatomy at "a crucial spinal
  junction", probably of no consequence alone, yet seen incidentally strikingly often in patients, especially
  younger adults, presenting with backache. With spina bifida occulta, abnormal facet direction and
  sacro-transverse joints they may mark a "congenitally predisposed" lumbar spine, the backache arising from some
  overall mechanical instability and not from the variant itself.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### For spine-program PR 239: how Eisenstein's five values were measured

- Every value in Table I (p. 174, PDF 2) is a group mean, by level L1 to L5, of a direct vernier caliper reading on
  a dry, macerated vertebra; skeletal (true-size) values, not radiographic, so no magnification is in them.
- Six groups (Caucasoid, Zulu, Sotho, each by sex); four rows per group: canal A.P. (mid-sagittal), canal I.P.
  (interpedicular), body A.P. (mid-sagittal at mid-height), body T.V. (transverse at the waist). Whole millimetres,
  no SD, no n per cell, no range per level.
- Canal A.P.: one reading per vertebra, taken at the single point where the antero-posterior dimension is least,
  near the canal's upper (cranial) limit, facing a small forward bulge of the canal's posterior wall in
  the midline. p. 173 (PDF 1). So it is a MINIMUM, not a mid-height or average chord.
- Canal transverse: "the greatest interpedicular distance". p. 173 (PDF 1). So it is a MAXIMUM.
- Body A.P.: mid-sagittal, at mid-height, chosen to exclude discrepancies from border osteophytes. Body transverse:
  "the greatest mid-waist distance". p. 173 (PDF 1).
- The method is credited to Jones and Thomson (1968), which this paper does not restate further.
- The radiographic numbers (1.23, 1.14, and the 15 mm and 20 mm film limits) are not measurements of the 2,166
  vertebrae on film: they come from 55 vertebrae of 11 skeletons, whose caliper readings were repeated on films
  with a marker at the posterior canal limit, and the film limits are the skeletal limits times the factors.
  p. 174-175 (PDF 2-3).
- The "range of normal" and "lower limit of normal" in Tables II and III (p. 175, PDF 3) are per group and pooled
  over the five levels; the way they were derived is not stated (see Error). The text and Conclusion 3 call the
  overall limit the "average lower limit" (p. 174, PDF 2; p. 180, PDF 8).

### Canal mid-sagittal (antero-posterior) diameter, "Canal A.P."

- Landmarks: anterior, the posterior surface of the body in the midline (Inference: implied by "mid-sagittal" and
  by the radiographic "anterior mid-sagittal boundary of the canal", p. 178; not stated for the caliper);
  posterior, the inner surface of the posterior arch in the midline, at the point where the AP dimension is
  least, near the canal's cranial limit, opposite a slight anterior bulge of the posterior wall. p. 173 (PDF 1).
  Inference: the bulge is probably the midline ridge where the laminae join the base of the spinous process; the
  paper does not name it.
- Plane: mid-sagittal. Instrument: vernier caliper. Levels L1 to L5.
- Values, Table I, p. 174 (PDF 2), mm, L1 L2 L3 L4 L5:
  - Caucasoid male 18 17 16 16 18; Caucasoid female 18 17 17 16 18.
  - Zulu male 16 15 15 15 16; Zulu female 17 16 15 16 16.
  - Sotho male 16 15 14 15 16; Sotho female 16 16 15 15 16.
- Group means pooled over levels, Table III, p. 175 (PDF 3): Caucasoid male 17, Zulu male 16, Sotho male 15,
  Caucasoid female 17, Zulu female 16, Sotho female 15. Overall average 16 mm (p. 174, PDF 2).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference throughout. Build the vertebra's own frame (z along the column, normal to
  the end-plates; y antero-posterior through the body centre and the spinous process; x across). Take the
  mid-sagittal band of the canal (x within about one voxel of the canal midline). For every z slab along the
  posterior arch where the arch closes behind the canal, read the distance along y from the posterior wall of the
  body subregion to the anterior surface of the arch (lamina or spinous base) at the midline. Eisenstein's value
  is the MINIMUM of that profile, which he places near the cranial end of the canal; a median or a mid-height
  slab reads larger. Use only the vertebra's own bone, as the caliper did: ligamentum flavum and disc are not in
  a dry vertebra.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral view. Anterior limit, the posterior cortex of the body in the midline, which is
  well demarcated (p. 178, PDF 6). Posterior limit at L1 to L4: a point opposite the cephalad half of the body, on
  the line joining the apex of the superior articular process and "the inverted apex of the inferior facet" of
  the same vertebra (p. 174-175, PDF 2-3; restated with "apophysial joint" on p. 178, PDF 6). At L5: just anterior
  to the well-demarcated anterior limit of a radiolucent "lake" in the spinous process (p. 175, PDF 3; p. 178,
  PDF 6). Fig. 2 (p. 176, PDF 4) draws a dotted line down the articular pillar from facet apex to facet apex, the
  markers on it, and a dotted line from each marker forward across the body; at L5 the marker sits just in front
  of a stippled oval, the lucent lake. Inference from Fig. 2: those forward lines cross the upper half of each
  body and tilt with its upper end-plate, so the chord runs roughly parallel to the superior end-plate. Lateral
  magnification at 110 cm tube to plate was 1.23 (p. 175, PDF 3): divide a film chord by 1.23 to compare with dry
  bone or CT. Not obtainable from an AP view.
- Across levels: a "venturi tube", with a slightly narrow waist at L2, L3 and L4 and flared at each end, the same
  in every race and sex; the canal is "not simply a truncated cone". p. 179 (PDF 7). The minimum is at L3 or L4
  depending on group, tied with L2 in Zulu males (Table I); L1 and L5 are equal or within 1 mm in every group, so
  this measure cannot tell L1 from L5, nor L2, L3 and L4 from one another (Inference from Table I). The narrowest
  levels are L2 to L4 (Conclusion 4, p. 180, PDF 8).
- Sex, age, size, population: Inference from Table I: males and females differ by 0 to 1 mm, females equal or
  larger, so the canal depth does not follow body size; the African groups are 0 to 2 mm (mostly 1 to 2 mm)
  shallower than the Caucasoid. The author calls the negroid canal "marginally less capacious" (p. 179, PDF 7;
  Conclusion 6) and the metrical group and sex differences insignificant (p. 179, PDF 7), with no test shown. Age
  was not analysed.
- Error: none reported (see Measurement error and reliability).

### Canal transverse (interpedicular) diameter, "Canal I.P."

- Landmarks: the medial surfaces of the two pedicles; "the greatest interpedicular distance". p. 173 (PDF 1).
- Plane: transverse. Instrument: vernier caliper. Levels L1 to L5.
- Values, Table I, p. 174 (PDF 2), mm, L1 L2 L3 L4 L5:
  - Caucasoid male 23 24 23 24 26; Caucasoid female 22 22 23 23 25.
  - Zulu male 21 22 22 23 26; Zulu female 20 21 21 22 24.
  - Sotho male 21 21 22 23 25; Sotho female 20 20 21 22 24 (this row is mislabelled "T.V." in the table; it is
    the canal's second row, where every other group has I.P.).
- Group means pooled over levels, Table II, p. 175 (PDF 3): Caucasoid male 24, Zulu male 23, Sotho male 22,
  Caucasoid female 23, Zulu female 22, Sotho female 21. Overall average 23 mm (p. 174, PDF 2).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In axial slabs through the pedicles (the slabs where the pedicle
  subregions, or the arch, close the canal laterally), the left-right distance between the medial pedicle
  surfaces; take the greatest over those slabs, as "greatest" says. A width measured through the canal's middle
  at levels above or below the pedicles, where facets or laminae bound the canal, reads smaller, most of all at a
  trefoil L5.
- From a radiograph alone: AP view; the interpedicular limits are well demarcated radiographically (p. 178,
  PDF 6). AP magnification at 110 cm was 1.14 (p. 175, PDF 3): divide by 1.14. Not obtainable from a lateral.
  Inference: on an AP film the gap between the medial pedicle contours is the narrowest projected gap, while the
  caliper took the greatest distance; the empirical factor 1.14 absorbs any such difference for this geometry
  only.
- Across levels: wider at L5 than at L1 in every group (by 3 to 5 mm), nearly flat from L1 to L4 (steps of -1 to
  +1 mm, 0 to 2 mm in all) and then 2 to 3 mm wider at L5 than at L4 (Table I). The author says both that it was
  "remarkably constant" from L1 to L5 (p. 174, PDF 2) and that it shows a "steady increase" from L1 to L5
  (p. 179, PDF 7); the table supports the increase. Inference: the L4 to L5 jump is the best canal cue for L5.
- Sex, age, size, population: Inference from Table I: males 0 to 2 mm (mostly 1 mm) wider than females of the
  same group, equal at L3 in the Caucasoids; the African groups 1 to 3 mm narrower than the Caucasoid at L1 to L4
  and 0 to 1 mm at L5.
- Error: none reported.

### Body mid-sagittal antero-posterior diameter, "Body A.P."

- Landmarks: anterior and posterior cortices of the body in the mid-sagittal plane, at mid-height, chosen to
  exclude border osteophytes. p. 173 (PDF 1). Caliper. L1 to L5.
- Values, Table I, p. 174 (PDF 2), range over the three groups of each sex, mm: males L1 27 to 31, L2 29 to 32,
  L3 31 to 33, L4 32 to 34, L5 32 to 33; females L1 25 to 27, L2 26 to 27, L3 28 to 29, L4 29 to 30, L5 30 to 31.
  By row: Caucasoid male 31 32 33 34 33; Zulu male 28 30 32 33 32; Sotho male 27 29 31 32 33; Caucasoid female
  27 27 29 29 30; Zulu female 25 26 28 30 31; Sotho female 25 26 28 30 31 (the two African female rows are
  identical).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the vertebra's frame, the slab midway between the superior and
  inferior end-plates; within the mid-sagittal band, the y extent of the body subregion from anterior to
  posterior cortex. This is not Panjabi's end-plate depth (EPDu, EPDl), which is taken at the end-plates: the
  anterior surface is concave at the waist and the rims carry osteophytes, so the mid-height depth reads smaller
  than the end-plate depth.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral view, at mid-height; divide by 1.23 at 110 cm (Inference: the paper reports
  no body measure on film, only the magnification of the view).
- Across levels: rises from L1 to L4 by 0 to 2 mm a level, then 1 mm smaller at L5 in Caucasoid and Zulu males,
  still rising (by 1 mm) at L5 in the other four groups (Table I; Inference).
- Sex, age, size, population: Inference from Table I: males 1 to 5 mm deeper than females of the same group;
  Caucasoid males 2 to 4 mm deeper than African males at L1 and L2, converging by L5 (0 to 1 mm).
- Error: none reported.

### Body transverse diameter, "Body T.V."

- Landmarks: the lateral cortices of the body; "the greatest mid-waist distance". p. 173 (PDF 1). Caliper.
- Values, Table I, p. 174 (PDF 2), range over the three groups of each sex, mm: males L1 38 to 39, L2 39 to 40,
  L3 41 to 43, L4 43 to 44, L5 44 to 46; females L1 34 to 35, L2 35 to 37, L3 37 to 38, L4 39 to 41, L5 42 to 43.
  By row: Caucasoid male 39 40 43 44 46; Zulu male 39 40 42 44 45; Sotho male 38 39 41 43 44; Caucasoid female
  34 35 37 39 42; Zulu female 35 37 38 41 43; Sotho female 34 36 38 40 42.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the mid-height slab, the greatest x extent of the body subregion.
  Not Panjabi's end-plate width (EPWu, EPWl), which is taken at the end-plates and is wider than the waist.
- From a radiograph alone: AP view, at mid-height; divide by 1.14 at 110 cm (Inference, as above).
- Across levels: rises in every group at every step from L1 to L5, by 1 to 3 mm a level (Table I). Inference:
  the only one of the four measures that is monotonic in every group, so the best single level cue here, though
  adjacent steps are small.
- Sex, age, size, population: Inference from Table I: males 2 to 6 mm wider than females of the same group; the
  three ethnic groups within 0 to 2 mm of one another.
- Error: none reported.

### Range of normal, lower limit of normal and stenosis (Tables II and III)

- Per group, pooled over L1 to L5, Table II (interpedicular) and Table III (mid-sagittal), p. 175 (PDF 3):
  - Interpedicular range of normal, mm: Caucasoid male 19 to 29, Zulu male 17 to 28, Sotho male 19 to 26,
    Caucasoid female 18 to 28, Zulu female 18 to 26, Sotho female 18 to 25.
  - Mid-sagittal range of normal, mm: Caucasoid male 14 to 20, Zulu male 12 to 20, Sotho male 12 to 19,
    Caucasoid female 13 to 21, Zulu female 13 to 19, Sotho female 12 to 19.
- Overall lower limit of normal: skeletal 18 mm interpedicular and 13 mm mid-sagittal; radiological 20 mm and
  15 mm. p. 175 (PDF 3), Tables II and III; abstract p. 173; p. 179 (PDF 7); Conclusion 3, p. 180 (PDF 8).
- The radiological limits come from the skeletal limits and the view magnifications, 1.23 lateral and 1.14 AP.
  p. 175 (PDF 3). Inference: the overall skeletal limits are the means of the six group lower limits (12.7 and
  18.2 mm), as "average lower limit" suggests; 12.7 x 1.23 = 15.6 and 18.2 x 1.14 = 20.7, so the printed 15 and
  20 are truncated, not rounded (13 x 1.23 = 16.0).
- Stenotic vertebrae, interpedicular (Table II): 18 in 9 skeletons (2.0% of 443 skeletons; 0.8% of 2,166
  vertebrae): Caucasoid male 1 (L1; 1.3% of 78); Sotho male 6 in 3 skeletons (L1 2, L3 3, L5 1; 2.8% of 106); Zulu
  female 2 in 2 (L1 2; 3.7% of 54); Sotho female 9 in 3 (L1 2, L2 2, L3 1, L4 2, L5 2; 4.8% of 62); none in Zulu
  males or Caucasoid females. No interpedicular diameter was below 16 mm. p. 174-175 (PDF 2-3).
- Stenotic vertebrae, mid-sagittal (Table III): 28 in 21 skeletons (4.7% of skeletons; 1.3% of vertebrae):
  Caucasoid male 10 in 6 (L1 2, L3 3, L4 4, L5 1; 7.7% of 78); Zulu male 9 in 7 (L3 4, L4 4, L5 1; 6.5% of 108);
  Sotho male 2 in 2 (L2 1, L4 1; 1.9% of 106); Caucasoid female 1 (L4; 2.9% of 35); Zulu female 3 in 2 (L4 2,
  L5 1; 3.7% of 54); Sotho female 3 in 3 (L1 1, L2 1, L4 1; 4.8% of 62). No mid-sagittal diameter was below
  11 mm. p. 174-175 (PDF 2-3). The column "percentage incidence in skeletal population" is stenotic skeletons
  over the group's skeleton count (every cell checks to the printed decimal).
- Inference: the Caucasoid males, with the deepest mean canal (17 mm), have the highest mid-sagittal stenosis
  rate (7.7%), which fits stenosis being called against each group's own lower limit (14 mm for them, 12 mm for
  three African groups) better than against the pooled 13 mm; the paper does not say which limit was applied.
- By level, summed over groups (Inference): mid-sagittal L1 3, L2 2, L3 7, L4 13, L5 3; interpedicular L1 7, L2 2,
  L3 4, L4 2, L5 3. The AP stenoses gather at the narrow waist (L3, L4) and the interpedicular ones at L1, the
  narrowest level for that diameter.
- Either dimension: 28 of 443 skeletons (6.3%), 46 vertebrae; 2 skeletons stenotic in both diameters; mid-sagittal
  stenosis more than twice as frequent as transverse; one stenotic vertebra implies a 60% chance of a second in
  the same spine (derivation not given). p. 174 (PDF 2); restated p. 178 (PDF 6).
- Structural cause of mid-sagittal stenosis: an increased interlaminar angle (shortened laminae), not shortened
  pedicles. p. 174 (PDF 2). No angle is measured or defined.

### Spinal Index of Jones and Thomson (tested and rejected)

- The product of the two canal diameters expressed as a ratio of the product of the two body diameters; Jones and
  Thomson's lower limit of normal, a ratio of 1:4.5. p. 178 (PDF 6).
- Applied here, it would call 11% of vertebrae stenotic, and many such vertebrae had obviously capacious canals;
  the SD about the mean was so wide that a new normal range was meaningless. Not valid for screening; use absolute
  dimensions, above all the mid-sagittal diameter. p. 178 (PDF 6); Conclusion 2, p. 180 (PDF 8).

### Osteophytosis grade

- Mild: minor projections from the sharp body edges and curling at the facet edges, no real change in the size or
  shape of the articular surfaces. Moderate: major projections from the body edges up to "parrot-beaking",
  normal body contour at mid-height kept; facet edges overgrown and scalloped but joint lines still visible in
  the articulated skeleton. Severe: new bone so gross it enlarges the body's girth, osteophytes fused between
  bodies; facets like "lettuce leaves", joint lines obscured, overlapping and interlocking; articular surfaces
  enlarged onto the upper surface of the laminae ("faceto-laminar" joint); with rare exceptions, curled
  projections into the nerve root tunnel from the posterolateral body corners. p. 175-176 (PDF 3-4); Figs. 3 to
  5, p. 177 (PDF 5), Fig. 3 at the L4-L5 facet joints.
- Incidence by group, Table IV, p. 176 (PDF 4): Caucasoid male 50%, Zulu male 25.2%, Sotho male 43.4%, Caucasoid
  female 51.4%, Zulu female 55.6%, Sotho female 43.5%. Mean age with osteophytosis 70, 58, 53, 70, 58, 52 years.
  Site: facet predominance (Caucasoid and Zulu males); even between bodies alone and facet with body (Sotho
  males); facet with body predominance (Caucasoid and Sotho females); even between facets alone and facet with
  body (Zulu females). The Zulu males had about half the rate of the other five groups (p. 176, PDF 4).
- Worse consistently from L1 to L5. p. 175 (PDF 3).
- From a mask or radiograph: not defined for either (Inference: grades are visual on dry bone).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1: canal AP equal to L5's or 1 mm more, one flared end of the venturi; interpedicular distance and body
  dimensions the smallest or tied smallest of the five (Table I). 7 of the 18 interpedicular stenoses (Inference
  from Table II). Least osteophytosis (Inference from p. 175, PDF 3, which says it worsens from L1 to L5).
- L2 to L4: the canal's narrow waist in AP, the narrowest lumbar levels (p. 179, PDF 7; Conclusion 4, p. 180).
  Posterior canal limit on a lateral film at L1 to L4: opposite the cephalad half of the body on the facet-apex
  line (p. 174-175, PDF 2-3; p. 178, PDF 6).
- L4: most frequent level of mid-sagittal stenosis, 13 of 28 (Inference from Table III); trefoil canal
  occasionally (p. 177, PDF 5); one unilateral left pars defect with an enlarged left inferior facet (p. 177;
  Fig. 7, p. 178).
- L4-L5: Fig. 3 shows severe facet osteophytosis at this joint (p. 177, PDF 5).
- L5: canal AP flares again; interpedicular 2 to 3 mm wider than L4; trefoil canal always or almost always at this
  level when present, 15% of skeletons (p. 177, PDF 5; p. 179-180, PDF 7-8); the L5 canal holds only the L5 and
  sacral roots (p. 180, PDF 8); nine of ten pars defects, all bilateral, one with a bifid spinous process
  (p. 177); posterior canal limit on a lateral film just anterior to the radiolucent lake of the spinous process,
  easier to find than at L1 to L4 and still further back than usually estimated (p. 175, PDF 3; p. 178, PDF 6);
  most osteophytosis.
- L5/S1 junction: lumbarisation 2%, persistent S1-S2 zygapophysial joints 3.6%, true L6 2 skeletons, a true
  transitional vertebra 1, sacralisation 1.8%, all together 8%; sacro-transverse joint 2.3% with smaller
  ipsilateral lumbosacral facets; sacro-iliac ankylosis 2.3%, all male (p. 177, PDF 5; p. 180, PDF 8).
- C2/C3, C7/T1 and T12/L1: nothing. One sentence says the first cervical vertebrae were "impressively unaffected"
  by osteophytosis even in the worst cases (p. 175, PDF 3); the study measured no cervical level (see Gaps).

## Measurement error and reliability

- No intra- or inter-observer error, no repeat measurement, no SD and no confidence interval anywhere; the canal
  AP was a single reading at one point per vertebra (p. 173, PDF 1).
- Instrument: vernier caliper; Table I is printed to whole millimetres, so each mean carries up to +/-0.5 mm of
  rounding (Inference).
- Radiographic markers: the posterior canal positions they showed were more posterior than most clinicians would
  estimate, and were "reproduced consistently" in the 11 skeletons radiographed; no number is given. p. 174
  (PDF 2). The facet-apex construction can be learnt "with very little practice" (p. 174, PDF 2), and the author
  says it applies without difficulty to clinical films of good average quality (p. 178, PDF 6), but it was shown
  only on macerated bone in sponge, with no soft tissue and no clinical film.
- Magnification: 1.23 lateral and 1.14 AP at 110 cm tube to plate, from 55 vertebrae of 11 skeletons; no spread
  or method of calculation given. p. 175 (PDF 3). Inference: if purely geometric, M = FFD / (FFD - OFD) puts the
  canal about 20.6 cm from the film in the lateral set-up and about 13.5 cm in the AP set-up; any other geometry
  (a C-arm, a 100 cm or 180 cm film) needs its own factor.
- Ranges of normal: method not stated. Inference: each is nearly symmetric about its group mean (half-widths 3 to
  4 mm for the mid-sagittal and 3.5 to 5.5 mm for the interpedicular diameter); if they are mean +/- 2 SD the
  pooled SD would be about 1.5 to 2 mm (AP) and 1.75 to 2.75 mm (interpedicular), but they pool five levels, so
  they include the level-to-level spread and overstate the within-level SD.
- Inference: the Table II and III means agree with the means over L1 to L5 of the Table I rows (exactly so for the
  interpedicular rows), except Zulu male AP (15.4 from Table I, printed 16) and Sotho female AP (15.6, printed 15);
  since the Table I cells are themselves rounded and per-level n differ, these two may be rounding, not errors.
- Osteophytosis grading is admitted to be subjective ("eye of the beholder"); three grades simplify Nathan's
  (1962) four; only the severe grade matters clinically. p. 179 (PDF 7).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Spinal stenosis syndrome (p. 173, PDF 1): low back pain in an adult nearing middle age with lower limb
  "claudication"; pain, weakness and numbness on walking, not eased by merely standing and resting; the patient
  must undo the lumbar lordosis by bending or crouching; there may be a lost tendon jerk or sensory change; more
  suggestive when the peripheral circulation is normal. Narrowing may be developmental, or degenerative after
  ageing, injury, disease or spinal operations. Bony stenosis as a cause was first proposed by Sarpyener (1945)
  and Verbiest (1954, 1955).
- The deficient dimension in stenosis is the mid-sagittal one, not the interpedicular, agreeing with Verbiest,
  Teng and Papatheodorou, Schatzker and Pennal and Dommisse. p. 177-178 (PDF 5-6); Conclusion 1.
- Thresholds: dry bone or true size, mid-sagittal below 13 mm and interpedicular below 18 mm; standard films at
  110 cm, 15 mm on the lateral and 20 mm on the AP. p. 175 (PDF 3); p. 179 (PDF 7). The 15 mm agrees with
  myelographic work (Epstein, Epstein and Lavine 1962). p. 179 (PDF 7).
- Standard radiographs can serve as a primary screen for developmental stenosis once the posterior limit is found
  by the facet-apex construction and the 1.23 factor applied. p. 178-179 (PDF 6-7).
- The "stenotic" vertebrae were only just subnormal: least mid-sagittal 11 mm against a 13 mm limit, least
  interpedicular 16 mm against 18 mm; no dramatic stenosis in 443 skeletons. An incidence over 6% looks high only
  until this is seen. Skeletal narrowing as the sole cause of the syndrome may have been exaggerated; marginal
  stenosis may predispose to cauda equina compression by minor degeneration of bone and soft tissue that is
  overlooked clinically (Clark 1969). p. 178 (PDF 6); Conclusion 7.
- Why black South African patients rarely have the syndrome: no case is in the literature and colleagues knew of
  only a handful, unconfirmed; their bony canal is marginally smaller, not larger, so the Caucasoid preponderance
  should be sought in the soft tissues bordering the canal. Zulu (a Natal Nguni subgroup) and Sotho differ
  ethnically and linguistically but not racially; South African studies of teeth, skulls and sacra found no
  difference between them, nor did this study, so they may be pooled. p. 179 (PDF 7).
- Degenerative stenosis: the facet excrescences seen at decompression do not, on the skeletons, encroach on the
  canal; body osteophytes rarely do, at most "sharpening" its rounded lateral corners; lipping begins laterally,
  at the nerve root tunnel (Figs. 4 and 5; Macnab 1975). Of 169 skeletons with osteophytosis, 6 had canal
  encroachment by facet osteophytes and 3 by body osteophytes; posterior lipping was rare; 2 skeletons had facet
  osteophytes on one side only of all five lumbar vertebrae. p. 176 (PDF 4); p. 179 (PDF 7).
- Compression is more likely in the nerve root tunnel than in the canal; a wide or laterally extensive posterior
  decompression succeeds because it partly decompresses the root tunnels, not because it opens the canal.
  p. 179 (PDF 7); Conclusion 5.
- Osteophytosis incidence follows the groups' ages, not race or sex, and rises with age; its site distribution
  follows no pattern of race, sex or age; the low Zulu male incidence is unexplained; it is worse in the lower
  lumbar spine, consistent with upright load-bearing and lordosis (Nathan 1962). The defence-mechanism theory is
  called teleological; the author favours age-related failure of the soft tissue supports, instability and
  repeated periosteal and capsular stripping. p. 179 (PDF 7).
- Trefoil canal: a normal variant, almost always at L5, not a degenerative compressor; low mean age (46) and little
  osteoarthritis favour development; it doubles the rate of stenosis (15% against 6.3%) but is probably harmless
  without additional compressive agents; laminar thickening might still impinge on sacral roots, since only two
  dimensions were measured. p. 177 (PDF 5); p. 179-180 (PDF 7-8).
- Pitfall, lateral film: no single radiographic landmark marks the posterior canal; the transverse processes
  obscure it, and the beam crosses up to 8 cm of bone in this plane; the anterior limit and the interpedicular
  limits are easy. p. 178 (PDF 6). The true posterior limit lies further back than clinicians usually estimate,
  at L5 still more so. p. 174-175 (PDF 2-3).
- Pitfall, magnification: lateral and AP factors differ (1.23 and 1.14 at 110 cm); comparing a film value with a
  dry-bone limit without them overstates the canal by 14 to 23%. p. 175 (PDF 3).
- Pitfall, ratios: the Spinal Index misclassifies; use absolute diameters. p. 178 (PDF 6).
- Pitfall, body measures: take the body AP at mid-height to keep border osteophytes out. p. 173 (PDF 1).
- Pitfall, statistics: "significant" and "insignificant" are asserted without any test or SD (p. 173, p. 179-180);
  the group comparisons are also confounded by age (Inference from Table IV).
- Indications: the paper concerns developmental and degenerative lumbar stenosis and its screening; no screw,
  safe-zone or syndrome (Marfan, achondroplasia) content.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. On a lateral radiograph, fluoroscopy frame or lateral DRR of L1 to L4, place the posterior canal limit at the
   point opposite the cephalad half of the body on the line joining the apex of the superior articular process
   and the inverted apex of the inferior articular process of the same vertebra; measure from the posterior body
   cortex in the midline to that point. p. 174-175, p. 178; Fig. 2, p. 176. (Inference from Fig. 2: draw the chord
   roughly parallel to the superior end-plate.)
2. At L5 place it just anterior to the anterior edge of the radiolucent lake of the spinous process. p. 175,
   p. 178.
3. Before comparing a film chord with dry-bone or CT values, divide by 1.23 (lateral) or 1.14 (AP) when the tube
   was 110 cm from the plate; for any other geometry recompute the magnification (Inference: the factors hold
   only for this set-up). p. 175.
4. When the posterior landmark is hidden by the transverse processes, use the facet-apex construction; do not
   guess, and expect a guessed limit to fall too far anterior, shrinking the canal. p. 174, p. 178.
5. On a CT mask, take the canal AP as the minimum midline chord along the arch, expected near the cranial end of
   the canal, from the vertebra's own bone; do not take a median or mid-height slab when matching Eisenstein.
   p. 173 (Inference for the mask steps).
6. Take the interpedicular distance as the greatest gap between the medial pedicle surfaces, in the pedicle slabs
   (CT) or between the medial pedicle contours on an AP view. p. 173, p. 178.
7. Take body depth and width at mid-height (waist), not at the end-plates, when matching Eisenstein. p. 173.
   (Inference: expect them smaller than end-plate chords.)
8. Read the canal AP as U-shaped (L1 and L5 widest, waist L2 to L4); never use it alone to tell L1 from L5.
   Table I, p. 174; p. 179.
9. Read the interpedicular distance as nearly flat L1 to L4 and 2 to 3 mm wider at L5. Table I, p. 174.
   (Inference: use that jump as an L5 cue.)
10. Read the body waist width as rising 1 to 3 mm at every step from L1 to L5 in both sexes and all three groups;
    use it as the monotonic cue, but expect adjacent levels to overlap. Table I, p. 174.
11. Expect males' bodies to be 1 to 6 mm larger than females' while the canal AP is the same in both sexes
    (females equal or 1 mm larger). Table I, p. 174. (Inference: do not scale the canal AP with a body-size
    factor.)
12. Flag a canal AP below 13 mm (true size) or 15 mm (lateral film at 110 cm), or an interpedicular distance below
    18 mm (true size) or 20 mm (AP film at 110 cm). Tables II and III, p. 175; p. 179. Values below 11 mm AP or
    16 mm interpedicular (true size) lie outside this whole series (p. 174, p. 178). (Inference: check such a value
    for a measuring or segmentation error before calling pathology.)
13. Expect a trefoil canal (laminar bars) at L5 in about 15% of adults, occasionally also at L4: the canal's usual
    triangular outline is pinched by a thickened lamina. p. 177; Fig. 6, p. 178; p. 179. (Inference: on axial CT
    this shape is a weak L5 cue and is anatomy, not a segmentation error.)
14. At the lumbosacral junction, look for a lumbarised S1 (2%), persistent S1-S2 facet joints (3.6%), a sixth
    lumbar vertebra (0.45%), a sacralised L5 (1.8%), a transitional vertebra (0.23%) and a sacro-transverse joint
    (2.3%); expect a smaller L5-S1 facet on the side of a sacro-transverse joint. p. 177, p. 180. (Inference: an
    S1 with free facets to S2 may be split off by a segmenter and miscounted as an L6.)
15. Expect a pars defect most often at L5 and bilateral; a defect at L4 may be one-sided with an enlarged
    inferior facet on that side. p. 177; Fig. 7, p. 178.
16. Expect anterior wedge compression of a lumbar body in about 3.8% of adults (none in Caucasoid women in this
    series, level not given). p. 177. (Inference: a wedged body distorts any height-based level cue.)
17. Expect osteophytes to grow worse from L1 to L5 and with age, and severe facet osteophytes to enlarge the
    posterior elements ("lettuce leaves", faceto-laminar joints); keep body chords at mid-height to avoid rim
    osteophytes. p. 173, p. 175-177.
18. Do not use the Spinal Index (canal product over body product) to call stenosis. p. 178.

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder (skeletons, n/443, adults aged 16 to 96 of the Raymond Dart
Collection, South Africa, 113 Caucasoid, 162 Zulu, 168 Sotho; p. 173, p. 177 and p. 180):
- L6 (true sixth lumbar): 2/443 = 0.45% (both Sotho, 2/168 = 1.2%). Lumbarised S1: 9/443 = 2.0%. Sacralised L5:
  8/443 = 1.8% (0/35 Caucasoid females). True transitional lumbosacral vertebra: 1/443 = 0.23% (a Zulu).
  Persistent S1-S2 facets: 16/443 = 3.6%. Any of these: 36/443 = 8.1%.
- Sacro-transverse joint 10/443 = 2.3%. No T13, rib count or cervical prior: the paper gives none.
- Inference: a sacralised L5 would leave four free lumbar bodies in the count; for a five-to-six lumbar
  transition, L6 plus lumbarised S1 is 11/443 = 2.5%; whether the author's "lumbarisation" yields six free
  lumbar-type vertebrae is not stated, nor how the categories were told apart.

(b) Measures:
- Canal depth (SCD, canal_ap): Eisenstein takes the MINIMUM midline chord near the cranial end of the canal;
  bordercall's scanimport/geometry.py takes the median over slabs across the middle half of the body's height.
  Inference: a mask value by bordercall's recipe should read above Eisenstein's. Inference, from bordercall's
  archive/bordercall/reference/panjabi_full_spine.csv (not this paper): Panjabi 1992 SCD L1 to L5 is 19.0, 18.2,
  17.5, 18.6, 19.7 mm against Eisenstein's Caucasoid male 18, 17, 16, 16, 18, 1.0 to 2.6 mm less at every
  level with a similar mid-lumbar waist, as a minimum chord would be.
- Canal width (SCW, canal_w): Eisenstein's greatest interpedicular distance; bordercall's width through the
  canal's middle, median over the mid-body slabs. Inference: the same at the pedicles, smaller where a slab
  falls above or below them. Panjabi's SCW L1 to L5 in the same archive table, 23.7, 23.8, 24.3, 25.4, 27.1 mm,
  lies 0.2 mm below to 1.4 mm above Eisenstein's Caucasoid male 23, 24, 23, 24, 26 (larger at four of five
  levels).
- Body depth at mid-height and body width at the waist are measures the generator could add (dry-bone means by
  level, sex and group in Table I). They are not Panjabi's EPD and EPW: end-plate chords are larger, so do not
  mix them.
- Magnification for radiograph inputs: 1.23 lateral, 1.14 AP at 110 cm tube to plate (p. 175); not transferable
  to a C-arm without its own geometry (Inference).
- The four dimensions in this paper are integer means with no SD and no n per cell; they cannot set a spread.
  Inference: the pooled n per level is 394 to 443 vertebrae, far more than the 12 that bordercall's
  research_notes/cadaver-power/README.md (line 276) assigns to a series that records no count.

(c) Measurement error: none reported. Use at least +/-0.5 mm rounding on every Table I mean. Inference: an upper
bound on the within-level SD of about 2 mm (canal AP) and 2.75 mm (interpedicular) from the half-ranges of
normal, if those are +/- 2 SD.

(d) Covariates (Table I; Inference unless cited):
- Sex: body depth and width 1 to 6 mm larger in males; interpedicular 0 to 2 mm (mostly 1 mm) larger in males;
  canal AP no sex difference (0 to 1 mm, females equal or larger). The author calls the sex differences
  insignificant (p. 179).
- Population: African groups' canals 0 to 3 mm (mostly 1 to 2 mm) smaller in both diameters than Caucasoid;
  bodies similar in width, Caucasoid males deeper at L1 and L2. Zulu and Sotho may be pooled (p. 179).
- Age: not analysed for any dimension; Caucasoid groups average 65 to 66 years against 40 to 49 for the African
  groups (Table IV), so the population effect is confounded with age.
- Size: no stature, weight or skeletal size recorded.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Abstract against text: 433 skeletons in the abstract, 443 in the methods, results and conclusions (443 matches
  78 + 35 + 108 + 54 + 106 + 62 and 443 x 5 - 49 = 2,166); 45 stenotic vertebrae in 27 skeletons in the abstract,
  46 in 28 in the results and discussion (p. 173 against p. 174 and p. 178). Inference: the tables give
  18 + 28 = 46 vertebrae and 9 + 21 - 2 = 28 skeletons, so 46 and 28 are right. The abstract and conclusions
  speak of "two races" or "two race groups" for three groups.
- Interpedicular profile: "remarkably constant" from L1 to L5 (p. 174) against "a steady increase" (p. 179);
  Table I rises 3 to 5 mm.
- Trefoil canal "always" at L5 (p. 177) against "almost invariably" at L5 (p. 179).
- Table I mislabels the Sotho female canal second row "T.V." for I.P.; the Zulu female and Sotho female body AP
  rows are identical (25 26 28 30 31), possibly a typesetting repeat (Inference).
- Osteophytosis count: the text says 169 skeletons had osteophytosis (p. 176), but Table IV's percentages times the
  group sizes give about 187 (39 + 27 + 46 + 18 + 30 + 27). Inference: the Zulu male 25.2% fits no whole number of
  108 (27/107 = 25.2%), so a denominator other than the group size may have been used somewhere.
- "The first cervical vertebrae were impressively unaffected" (p. 175): no cervical level was studied. Inference:
  either a misprint for the first lumbar vertebra or an aside on the whole skeleton.
- Radiological limits: 13 x 1.23 = 16.0, not the printed 15 mm; the printed values look truncated from the mean of
  the group limits (Inference, see Range of normal).
- Not stated: how levels were numbered; how an L6 or lumbarised S1 was measured or excluded, and how the transition
  categories were defined; the n per level; any SD; how the range and lower limit of normal were derived, and
  whether stenosis was called against the group or the pooled limit; the statistical method; the exact caliper
  direction for the canal AP chord; the level of the facet-orientation variants, the wedge compressions and the
  spina bifida; how magnification factors were computed; which side the sacro-transverse joints were on; whether a
  vertebra stenotic in both diameters counts once or twice among the 46; the derivation of the 60% second-stenosis
  figure.
- Two dimensions only: the author grants that laminar thickening could impinge on sacral roots in ways a two-
  diameter caliper reading cannot show (p. 180).
- The radiographic method was validated only on 11 macerated spines without soft tissue (p. 174, p. 178); no
  clinical film was measured.
- bordercall's research_notes/cadaver-power/README.md (line 251) lists Eisenstein's canal width as "dry bone with no
  count", and its verify/count_series.log names the cohort "Caucasoid and Zulu"; the paper does give counts (2,166
  vertebrae of 443 skeletons; 78, 35, 108, 54, 106 and 62 skeletons per group) and a third group, Sotho. It gives
  no n per level.
- Inference, from other papers in this set as recalled and from the hinck1966 note: Hinck, Hopkins and Clark
  (1965), cited here (p. 178), and Hinck, Clark and Hopkins (1966) measured canal diameters on clinical films with
  magnification left in, and Hinck took the shortest interpedicular distance where Eisenstein took the greatest,
  so their values are not directly comparable with these dry-bone values without a magnification factor and a
  definition check.
