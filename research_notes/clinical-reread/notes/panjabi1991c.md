## What the paper is

- Design: cadaveric morphometry of fresh autopsy vertebrae cleaned of all soft tissue (the paper does not say they were dried), the second paper of Panjabi's series; the methods are given in brief and referred to the thoracic paper "published in this issue (p. 888)" (ref 12), p. 861 (PDF 1).
- Purpose: to quantify clinically important parts of the middle and lower cervical vertebrae in three dimensions, document their variation along the spine, and test whether simple shapes (ellipse, triangle) approximate them, p. 861 (PDF 1).
- Why cervical data were thin: the authors say earlier cervical studies used simple techniques or covered a small part of the vertebra, while thoracolumbar morphometry was more common because surgeons needed pedicle size and orientation, p. 861 (PDF 1).
- Comparator studies the paper leans on, p. 861 (PDF 1) and Table 7, p. 868 (PDF 8): Francis (ref 5), 284 cervical vertebrae of men and women, white and black, aged 25 to 36, measured with a caliper (lateral widths and anteroposterior depths of the vertebral foramen and body, largest facet diameters in planes parallel to the sagittal and frontal planes); Nissan and Gilad (ref 10), 157 living subjects, calipers applied to lateral radiographs, sagittal-plane dimensions only; Liu et al (ref 9), digitised surface points on the cervical vertebrae of six cadavers (the reference title says young men).
- Specimens: 12 fresh autopsy spines, radiographed "to identify any significant abnormalities" (criteria and findings not given); identified by age, sex and height; mean age 46.3 years (19 to 59), mean weight 67.8 kg (54 to 85), mean height 167.8 cm (157 to 178), 8 men and 4 women; individual details in the thoracic paper, p. 861 (PDF 1). Country, ethnicity and causes of death are not given here. Inference: the first affiliation is Yale (New Haven, Connecticut) with co-authors in Iowa and Tokushima, so a United States autopsy source is likely; the thoracic and lumbar papers of the series describe the same twelve spines (by this set's notes on those papers, not checked in this reading, each spine ran from C2 to L5).
- Denominator: 72 vertebrae, C2 to C7 of the 12 spines (12 x 6), p. 863 (PDF 3); every mean is over the 12 spines at one level, dispersion printed as the standard error of the mean (SEM), table notes pp. 863 to 865 (PDF 3 to 5). n per cell is never stated. Inference: SD = SEM x sqrt(12) = 3.46 x SEM if no cell is missing.
- Sex: the 8 male and 4 female specimens were pooled; the authors acknowledge sex differences (citing Francis) but did not study them, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Preparation: complete removal of all soft tissue; two pointed threaded rods printed as "2-mm diameter and 2.5-mm long" screwed into each body, the two ends of one and the end of the other giving three pin markers; AP and lateral radiographs located the pins in the body frame, p. 861 (PDF 1).
- Instrument: a purpose-built morphometer of one linear (LVDT) and two rotational (RVDT) displacement transducers whose axes meet at one point, giving spherical coordinates (r, theta, phi); the tip was placed on each point and on the three pin markers and coordinates went straight into a computer, p. 861 (PDF 1) to p. 862 (PDF 2).
- Digitising: each part was a series of points spanning or circumventing its surface: each uncovertebral facet by up to 20 points spanning the whole surface; each end-plate, spinous process, transverse process and pedicle by points circumventing it, enough that straight lines between neighbouring points describe the shape, p. 861 (PDF 1).
- Local frame: four extra points, the left and right, upper and lower edges of the posterior wall of the body, define by least squares the body's frontal (xy), transverse (xz) and sagittal (yz) planes; the centre of the upper end-plate was digitised as the origin, p. 862 (PDF 2); Figure 1 (p. 862, PDF 2) draws x lateral, y cranial, z anterior.
- Parameters, p. 862 (PDF 2): linear ("major and minor" dimensions of each part, plus gross body dimensions such as body height and the distance between transverse processes); areas (projections of the digitised part on the three anatomic planes, the total being the square root of the sum of the squares of the three projected areas); angles (a least-squares plane fitted to each part's points, and its angles to each anatomic plane).
- Statistics: single-factor ANOVA with Scheffe's F test for pairwise comparison of levels; the authors note that repeated Student t tests between more than two groups are not valid, p. 862 (PDF 2). In the tables, a level in parentheses is one the printed level differs from at P < 0.05, table notes pp. 863 to 865 (PDF 3 to 5).
- Accuracy: a Plexiglas calibration piece cut at 30, 45 and 60 deg, with millimetre graph paper and shapes drawn on its faces and three pin markers, digitised by the same protocol; linear, angular and area errors under 5% in all cases, p. 862 (PDF 2) to p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Levels covered: C2 to C7 (Tables 2 to 6). C1 is not studied. C2 lacks every upper end-plate value, VBHp, all pedicle angles and the upper uncovertebral values (dashes), Tables 2, 4 and 6 (pp. 863 to 865, PDF 3 to 5).
- How levels were identified and counted: not described. No vertebra, specimen or transitional segment is reported excluded.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper reports no anomaly of any kind: no cervical rib, no block or fused vertebra, no C2-C3 fusion, no count anomaly and no transitional segment; all 12 spines contribute exactly C2 to C7 (72 vertebrae, p. 863, PDF 3). How levels were numbered is not stated. Inference: C2 names itself by its dens and C7 follows by counting down five, but the paper says neither; with n = 12 and no anomaly seen, the series bounds nothing useful about prevalence (a rule-of-three upper bound near 25%). The screening radiographs for "significant abnormalities" (p. 861, PDF 1) may have removed anomalous spines silently.

What bears on naming without being an anomaly:
- C2 has a lower uncovertebral articulation (UJArl 19.8, UJAll 17.7 mm2) but no upper one (dashes), Table 6, p. 865 (PDF 5). Inference: the C2/C3 uncovertebral joint is C3's uncinate process against C2's inferolateral body; the paper does not name the partners of either surface.
- C7 has both an upper and a lower uncovertebral surface (upper 43.1 right, 41.2 left; lower 23.4 right, 19.0 left mm2), Table 6. Inference: the C7 lower surface articulates with T1, so T1 carries uncinate processes in these spines; the paper does not say so.
- C7's transverse processes are far wider than any above (TPW 66.6 mm against 46.4 to 52.6), Table 5, p. 864 (PDF 4). Inference: a cervical rib or an elongated C7 transverse process would sit exactly on this measure; the paper never mentions either.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### The frame and conventions (apply to every measure below)

- Frame: plane through the four posterior-wall corner points (least squares) as the frontal plane; x lateral, y cranial, z anterior; origin at the centre of the upper end-plate, p. 862 (PDF 2); Figure 1 (p. 862, PDF 2).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: build the same frame per instance: the posterior surface of the body subregion (or its four corners) gives the frontal plane, its normal pointing anterior is z, the line joining the two superior posterior corners is x, y = z x x is cranial, and the origin is the centroid of the superior end-plate. Inference: every width is then an extent along x, depth along z, height along y. For C2 the paper does not say where the origin went, since C2 has no upper end-plate (gap).
- Areas are vector areas of a digitised contour (the Euclidean norm of the three projected areas), p. 862 (PDF 2). Inference: on a mask, the area of a planar section or of the projection on the best-fit plane matches better than a voxel surface count of a curved surface.
- Specimens are cleaned bone, so a CT bone mask (not soft tissue) is the analogue. Inference.
- The nomenclature is three upper-case letters (part, part, dimension) plus suffixes: a anterior, p posterior, s sagittal plane, t transverse plane, f frontal plane, u upper, l lower or left by context, r right, Table 1, p. 863 (PDF 3).

### EPWu, EPWl: upper and lower end-plate width

- Landmarks: Figure 1's front view (p. 862, PDF 2) draws EPWu between two vertical lines at the medial bases of the two uncinate processes, so the upper width is the flat end-plate between the unci and excludes them; EPWl spans the whole lower end-plate between its lateral corners. Inference from the drawing; the text gives no words for it.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: EPWl is the x-extent of the inferior end-plate rim of the body subregion. EPWu is the x-extent of the superior end-plate between the points where the surface turns up into each uncinate process (Inference); a plain x-extent of the superior surface includes the unci and will read larger than this paper.
- From a radiograph alone: AP radiograph. EPWl between the inferior body corners; EPWu between the bases of the unci, not their tips. Magnification is not addressed by the paper and must be corrected.
- Across levels (Table 2, p. 863, PDF 3; Figure 2A, p. 865, PDF 5): EPWu 15.8 (C3), 17.2, 17.5, 18.5, 21.8 (C7); EPWl 17.5 (C2), 17.2, 17.0, 19.4, 22.0, 23.4 (C7). EPWl is flat C2 to C4 and rises from C5; EPWu climbs gradually C3 to C6 (+1.4 at C3 to C4, then +0.3 and +1.0). The steepest step is C6 to C7 for the upper (+3.3) and C5 to C6 for the lower (+2.6), the text placing the greatest lower-width increase "at C6", p. 863 (PDF 3). Significance: EPWu C7 differs from C3 to C6 (an adjacent-level separation of C7 from C6), C6 from C3; EPWl C6 from C2 to C4, C7 from C2 to C5. EPWl exceeds EPWu at every level but C4 (17.0 against 17.2).
- Width always exceeds depth, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: SEM 0.29 to 0.98 mm (Inference: SD about 1.0 to 3.4 mm).

### EPDu, EPDl: upper and lower end-plate depth

- Landmarks: posterior wall to anterior edge of the upper (lower) end-plate along z, Figure 1 side view (p. 862, PDF 2).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: z-extent of the superior (inferior) end-plate rim of the body subregion, at the end-plate itself (Inference). Cervical bodies carry an anteroinferior lip, drawn in Figure 1, so the lower depth must be taken at the end-plate rim and not at mid-body.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, posterior to anterior corner of each end-plate; this is how Nissan and Gilad measured 157 subjects, and their lower depths are within 2 mm of this study's, p. 861 (PDF 1), p. 868 (PDF 8), Table 7. Magnification not addressed.
- Across levels: EPDu 15.0 (C3), 15.3, 15.2, 16.4, 18.1 (C7); EPDl 15.6 (C2), 15.6, 15.9, 17.9, 18.5 (C6), 16.8 (C7), Table 2. For the upper end-plate the significant rise is at C7 (C7 differs from C3 to C5); for the lower the largest step is at C5 and depth falls at C7, p. 863 (PDF 3). EPDl C6 differs from C2 to C4; the C7 drop is not marked significant. The authors read the C7 drop of lower depth as a distinct transition to the thoracic region, p. 867 (PDF 7).
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: SEM 0.32 to 0.75 mm (SD about 1.1 to 2.6 mm).
- Pitfall: Liu et al's lower depths run 1.2 to 4.1 mm below this study's (my arithmetic from Table 7, p. 868, PDF 8); the authors think Liu measured at mid-height of the centrum instead of at the end-plate, p. 868 (PDF 8).

### EPWu/EPDu, EPWl/EPDl: end-plate width to depth (derived)

- Table 2; Figure 2B (p. 865, PDF 5). Upper 1.05 (C3), 1.12, 1.15, 1.13, 1.20 (C7); lower 1.12 (C2), 1.10, 1.07, 1.08, 1.19, 1.39 (C7). The upper ratio varies little C3 to C6 and rises at C7; the lower is flat C2 to C5 and rises at C6 and C7, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- From a radiograph alone: needs both an AP (width) and a lateral (depth) of the same level.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the ratio of the two extents above, both taken in the instance's own frame. Inference.
- Use: size-free; the lower ratio of 1.39 at C7 against 1.07 to 1.12 at C2 to C5 is a C7 cue. Inference.
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: no dispersion is printed for the ratios, Table 2.

### EPAu, EPAl: end-plate area

- Definition: area of the digitised end-plate contour (Euclidean sum of projected areas), p. 862 (PDF 2). The areas exclude the uncovertebral facet surfaces, p. 868 (PDF 8).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: vector area of the superior (inferior) end-plate rim contour of the body subregion, the upper one stopping at the bases of the unci. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: not directly. The ellipse pi x W x D / 4 overestimates by about 10% at every level; the triangle W x D / 2 underestimates (the paper says by about 40%), pp. 864 and 868 (PDF 4 and 8); Figure 2C (p. 865, PDF 5). By my arithmetic from Table 2 the measured area is 0.69 to 0.74 of W x D at every level, so ellipse/measured is 1.07 to 1.14 and triangle/measured 0.68 to 0.73 (a 27 to 32% shortfall; the "40%" is the measured area's excess over the triangle). So an AP width and a lateral depth give the area within about 10% with the ellipse.
- Across levels: EPAu 169.4 (C3), 183.0, 182.9, 221.2, 278.3 (C7); EPAl 194.4 (C2), 190.7, 199.2, 246.2, 289.9 (C6), 280.3 (C7), Table 2. The total rises C2 to C7 except the lower end-plate from C2 to C3 and from C6 to C7; the significant rise is at C7 for the upper (C7 differs from C3 to C6, adjacent-level) and at C6 for the lower (C6 and C7 differ from C2 to C4), p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: SEM 5.74 to 16.54 mm2 (SD about 20 to 57 mm2).

### EPItu, EPItl: end-plate inclination to the transverse plane

- Definition: angle between the least-squares plane of the upper (lower) end-plate and the transverse plane (xz, perpendicular to the posterior wall), seen in the sagittal view, Figure 1 (p. 862, PDF 2); p. 862 (PDF 2).
- Values: EPItu 2.7 (C3), 3.5, 1.7, 4.7, 2.2 (C7); EPItl 4.2 (C2), 4.0, 2.1, 2.7, 2.7, 1.8 (C7), Table 2, p. 863 (PDF 3). The text gives 2 to 5 deg for the upper and 2 to 4 deg for the lower, C3 to C7, and an angular difference between upper and lower end-plates under 2 deg at all levels with no significant trend, p. 864 (PDF 4); Table 2 has the upper at 1.7 (C5) and the lower at 1.8 (C7), both just under the stated ranges. No level differs significantly.
- Inference from Figure 1's side view: both end-plate lines are drawn sloping down anteriorly from the z axis, so the two inclinations are in the same sense and the vertebral body is close to a parallelogram whose end-plates are about 0 to 2 deg apart; this is why the text speaks of their difference. This is a different convention from the thoracic paper's wedge (the sum). By my arithmetic the difference is -1.3 (C3), 1.4, -1.0, 2.0 (C6), 0.4 (C7) deg.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: fit planes to the superior and inferior end-plate surfaces of the body; take each one's angle to the plane perpendicular to the posterior-wall plane, in the y-z plane. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph (angles need no magnification correction); the paper made no radiographic check for the cervical spine.
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: SEM 0.4 to 1.5 deg (SD about 1.4 to 5.2 deg), so the per-level SD exceeds the mean tilt at several levels.

### VBHp: posterior vertebral body height

- Definition: height of the posterior wall of the body in the midsagittal plane, p. 864 (PDF 4) and p. 868 (PDF 8); Figure 1 side view.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: y-extent of the posterior wall of the body subregion in the midsagittal slice of the instance. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: a lateral view reads higher. The authors explain that their heights run 2 to 2.5 mm below Nissan and Gilad's (calipers on lateral radiographs) and Francis's (caliper on dry bone), because both took the posterior wall height as seen from the side, and with the uncovertebral joints the lateral projection of the height appears greater than the midsagittal height, p. 868 (PDF 8). By my arithmetic from Table 7 the excess is 2.1 to 3.0 mm against Nissan and 1.8 to 2.7 mm against Francis. Inference: Nissan's radiographs may also carry uncorrected magnification, but Francis's dry-bone values show nearly the same excess, so projection, not magnification, carries most of it. On a lateral radiograph, measure the posterior wall at the midline, not the top of the uncinate silhouette, which Figure 1's side view draws above the upper end-plate.
- Across levels: 11.6 (C3), 11.4, 11.4, 10.9 (C6), 12.8 (C7) mm, Table 2; relatively constant C3 to C7, range 10.9 to 12.8, p. 864 (PDF 4). C7 differs from C6 (an adjacent-level separation); C6 is the shortest. C2 not measured.
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: SEM 0.26 to 0.46 mm (SD about 0.9 to 1.6 mm).

### SCW: spinal canal width

- Definition: lateral width of the canal, Figure 1 top view (p. 862, PDF 2), drawn across the widest part of the canal next to the posterior body wall. The axial level of the section is not stated.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: in an axial section through the vertebral foramen (the hole bounded by the posterior body wall, pedicles and laminae), the x-extent of the foramen. Inference; choose the section through the pedicles if matching an interpedicular measure.
- From a radiograph alone: AP radiograph, interpedicular distance between the medial pedicle cortices (Inference; the paper takes no radiographs of the canal). Magnification must be corrected.
- Across levels: 24.5 (C2), 22.9 (C3), 24.7, 24.9, 25.8 (C6), 24.5 (C7), Table 3, p. 864 (PDF 4); constant C2 to C7 by the text, p. 865 (PDF 5); no level differs significantly. Width exceeds depth at every level, p. 865 (PDF 5) (the paper calls this significant but reports no test of it).
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: SEM 0.52 to 1.15 mm (SD about 1.8 to 4.0 mm).

### SCD: spinal canal depth

- Definition: anteroposterior depth of the canal, Figure 1 top view, drawn in the midline from the posterior body wall to the inner arch.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: z-extent of the vertebral foramen in the midsagittal line of an axial section. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, posterior body wall to the spinolaminar line (Inference; classic but not in the paper). Magnification must be corrected; Inference: a ratio to body depth would cancel it, but the paper does not give one.
- Across levels: 21.0 (C2), 16.2 (C3), 17.7, 17.4, 18.1 (C6), 15.2 (C7) mm, Table 3. Greatest at C2, dropping at C3, roughly constant to C6, with a decrease at C7 (the text says "slight"; it is 2.9 mm), p. 865 (PDF 5). Significance: C2 differs from C3 and C7 only.
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: SEM 0.35 to 1.33 mm (SD about 1.2 to 4.6 mm; the C3 and C7 SDs, 4.6 and 4.0 mm, are a quarter of the mean).

### SCW/SCD: canal width to depth (derived)

- 1.17 (C2), 1.41, 1.40, 1.43, 1.42, 1.61 (C7), Table 3; lowest at C2, constant C3 to C6, maximum at C7, p. 865 (PDF 5); Figure 3B (p. 866, PDF 6). The jump from about 1.4 to 1.6 at C7 is one of the authors' bony indicators of the cervicothoracic transition, p. 867 (PDF 7).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the ratio of the two foramen extents above. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: size-free and magnification-free; needs both an AP and a lateral view of the same level (Inference: with equal magnification in both).
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: no dispersion is printed for the ratio, Table 3.

### SCA: spinal canal area

- Definition: cross-sectional area of the canal, p. 865 (PDF 5); Figure 1 top view.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: area of the vertebral foramen in an axial section of the instance (filled hole). Inference; the section level is not stated.
- From a radiograph alone: not directly. The ellipse pi x W x D / 4 was best at C2 and C3, the triangle W x D / 2 better at C4 to C7; the ellipse always overestimates and the triangle always underestimates, p. 866 (PDF 6), p. 868 (PDF 8); Figure 3C (p. 866, PDF 6). By my arithmetic SCA/(SCW x SCD) is 0.73 (C2), 0.67, 0.62, 0.58, 0.57, 0.60 (C7): the canal is oval at C2 and C3 and triangular from C4 down (a triangle is 0.50, an ellipse 0.79).
- Across levels: 374.5 (C2, largest), 248.7, 272.0, 249.5, 266.5, 223.8 mm2 (C7, smallest), Table 3; no significant change C3 to C6, p. 865 (PDF 5); only C2 against C7 is significant. The C6 to C7 fall (266.5 to 223.8) is the authors' point of clinical interest, p. 867 (PDF 7).
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: SEM 13.08 to 36.26 mm2. Inference: SD about 45 to 126 mm2, i.e. a coefficient of variation up to 56% at C7, far larger than SCW and SCD imply; treat SCA's printed dispersion with suspicion.

### PDWr, PDWl, PDHr, PDHl: pedicle width and height

- Definition: width and height of the pedicle's section, Figure 1 top view inset "Pedicle Section" (PDW across, PDH up and down), p. 862 (PDF 2). The section is perpendicular to the pedicle axis (stated for PDA, p. 866, PDF 6). Its position along the pedicle is not stated; the paper does not say "narrowest".
- From a 3D CT instance mask: find the pedicle as the bridge of the posterior-elements subregion between the body and the articular pillar on each side; fit its axis; in the section perpendicular to the axis take the extent across (width, mostly in the transverse plane) and up and down (height). Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: not reliably. Inference: the cervical pedicle axis is 30 to 45 deg from the sagittal plane, so an AP view sees it obliquely and a lateral view superimposes it on the lateral mass; an oblique view near the pedicle angle or CT is needed.
- Across levels: right width 7.7 (C2), 5.8, 5.7, 6.1, 6.3, 6.6 (C7); left width 8.3, 5.4, 5.1, 5.1, 5.6, 6.5; right height 9.4, 7.6, 7.4, 6.7, 7.1, 7.5; left height 11.1, 7.2, 7.3, 7.3, 7.5, 7.5 mm, Table 4, p. 864 (PDF 4); Figure 4A (p. 867, PDF 7). Largest at C2, small at C3 to C5, rising slightly to C7, p. 866 (PDF 6). Significance: C2 differs from C3 to C7 in right height, left width and left height (not right width); left width at C7 differs from C4 and C5. Height exceeds width on both sides at every level; the text calls this significant, but no test of height against width is described (the ANOVA compares levels), p. 866 (PDF 6).
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: SEM 0.28 to 0.64 mm at C3 to C7 (SD about 1.0 to 2.2 mm); the C2 left SEMs are 0.07 (width) and 0.14 (height), implausibly small beside the right side's 0.35 and 0.46.

### PDH/PDW: pedicle height to width (derived)

- Right 1.22 (C2), 1.3, 1.3, 1.1, 1.1, 1.1 (C7); left 1.34, 1.3, 1.43, 1.43, 1.34, 1.15, Table 4; Figure 4B (p. 867, PDF 7). The cervical pedicle is a slightly tall oval (1.1 to 1.43), much rounder than the thoracic (about 1.9 mid-thoracic, by the thoracic paper of the series, not this one).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the ratio of the section's two extents. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible; size-free; CT only (Inference).
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: no dispersion is printed for the ratios, Table 4.

### PDAr, PDAl: pedicle cross-sectional area

- Definition: area of the section perpendicular to the pedicle axis, p. 866 (PDF 6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: area of the pedicle section perpendicular to its fitted axis. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible. Of the shapes, the ellipse overestimates and the triangle underestimates, but the triangle (W x H / 2) comes closest, p. 866 (PDF 6), p. 868 (PDF 8); Figure 4C (p. 867, PDF 7). By my arithmetic triangle/measured is 0.75 to 0.91 at C3 to C7 and 0.89 at C2 left, but 1.12 at C2 right (7.7 x 9.4 / 2 = 36.2 against 32.3 measured), so at C2 right the triangle overestimates, against the text; Figure 4C plots the C2 right triangle above the measured area too. Ellipse/measured is 1.18 to 1.76.
- Across levels: right 32.3 (C2), 24.2, 24.7, 23.8, 24.5, 30.4; left 51.8 (C2), 21.4, 24.0, 23.9, 27.9, 27.8 mm2, Table 4. Greater at C2 than at all lower levels on both sides (significant on the left only), p. 866 (PDF 6). At C2 the left area was 60% greater than the right, which the authors call large variation, p. 866 (PDF 6).
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).
- Error: SEM 2.02 to 3.08 mm2 at C3 to C7 (SD about 7 to 11 mm2); at C2 the left SEM is 0.28 against 2.44 on the right, again implausibly small.

### PDIsr, PDIsl, PDItr, PDItl: pedicle axis inclination

- Definition: PDIs, the angle between the pedicle axis and the sagittal plane, drawn in the top view; PDIt, the angle between the axis and the transverse plane, drawn in the side view, Figure 1 (p. 862, PDF 2). Negative PDIt is "below" the transverse plane by the text, p. 866 (PDF 6); which end of the axis is below is not defined in words. - From a radiograph alone: PDIs needs an axial view (CT). PDIt could in principle be read on a lateral radiograph from the pedicle's long axis, but the pedicle overlaps the lateral mass (Inference).
- Across levels (Table 4, p. 864, PDF 4; Figure 4D, p. 867, PDF 7): PDIs right 41.6 (C3), 44.6, 39.3, 29.6, 33.1 (C7); left 42.9, 43.9, 41.2, 34.1, 26.7 deg; side means (my arithmetic) 42.2, 44.2, 40.2, 31.9, 29.9. - Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).

### SPL: spinous process length

- Definition: three-dimensional length from the lower posterior tip of the spinous process to the vertebral body centroid, p. 866 (PDF 6). Figure 1's side view draws the line from the spinous tip to about the origin at the centre of the upper end-plate (p. 862, PDF 2), and the thoracic and lumbar papers of the series start it at the upper end-plate centre. Inference: the two endpoints differ by about half a body height (about 5 to 6 mm vertically), which would change SPL by up to a few mm.

Inference. C2 to C6 spinous processes are often bifid (Inference, textbook anatomy; the text does not say, though Figure 1's front view draws a two-pronged spinous tip below the body, p. 862, PDF 2); take the most posterior-inferior point of either limb.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph; the spinous process lies near the midsagittal plane, so its projection on a true lateral approximates the 3D length, corrected for magnification (Inference).
- Across levels: 33.7 (C2), 29.6, 30.3, 28.5, 34.2 (C6), 45.7 (C7) mm, Table 5, p. 864 (PDF 4); Figure 5 (p. 867, PDF 7). Slight fall C2 to C3, constant through C5, significant rise at C7 (C7 differs from C2 to C6), p. 866 (PDF 6). C7 is the longest by 11.5 mm over C6.
- Sex, age, size, population: the 8 men and 4 women (19 to 59 years) were pooled and no sex, age or size effect is reported, p. 863 (PDF 3).

### UJIfu, UJIsu, UJIfl, UJIsl: uncovertebral joint inclination

- Definition: angles of the least-squares plane of each uncovertebral surface to the frontal (f) and sagittal (s) planes, Figure 6 caption (p. 867, PDF 7); Figure 1 draws UJIs in the front view at the uncus and UJIf in the top view (p. 862, PDF 2). ## Level by level and at the junctions

The paper names two transition regions, abstract p. 861 (PDF 1) and pp. 866 to 868 (PDF 6 to 8): toward the thoracic spine at C7 (a wider vertebra with a narrower canal) and toward the upper cervical region at C2 (larger pedicles and spinous process).

- C2 (and the C2/C3 junction): no upper end-plate, VBHp, pedicle angles or upper uncovertebral surface measured (dashes, Tables 2, 4 and 6). Deepest and largest canal (SCD 21.0, SCA 374.5) and the lowest width/depth ratio (1.17, a round canal), Table 3. Pedicles much larger than C3 to C7 (width 7.7 right, 8.3 left; height 9.4 and 11.1; area 32.3 and 51.8), Table 4, with the left area 60% larger than the right, p. 866 (PDF 6). Lower end-plate 17.5 x 15.6 mm, area 194.4, inclination 4.2 deg (the largest lower tilt), Table 2. Lower uncovertebral surfaces 19.8 and 17.7 mm2, with the steepest sagittal-plane angle (63.7 deg), Table 6. SPL 33.7, TPW 52.6, Table 5. The authors' reasons, p. 868 (PDF 8): C0-C1-C2 carries the head in a highly flexible region around vulnerable neural structures; the deep canal allows motion without encroaching on the cord; with a deep canal the load-bearing facets sit further back, so the pedicles need a larger section to bridge facets and body; and the muscles on C2's large spinous process load its laminae and pedicles. C2 to C3: canal depth falls 4.8 mm (significant), pedicle size falls (significant for three of four dimensions), canal area falls 126 mm2 (not significant by Scheffe), SPL falls 4.1 mm (not significant).
- C3: the smallest vertebra of the series in most body measures and in canal width: EPWu 15.8, EPDu 15.0, EPAu 169.4, EPAl 190.7 (slightly below C2's), SCW 22.9, Tables 2 and 3. Its canal depth (16.2) is the smallest above C7 but not overall (C7 15.2), and its lower end-plate width (17.2) is not the smallest (C4 17.0). Pedicles smallest at C3 by the text, p. 866 (PDF 6), though by Table 4 the smallest single values are at C4 and C5 (gaps). Pedicle axes about 42 deg to the sagittal plane and about 8 deg below the transverse plane.
- C3 to C5 (middle): a plateau. No measure in Tables 2 to 6 separates any two of C3, C4 and C5 significantly (my reading of every parenthesis entry). Pedicle angles about 40 to 44 deg medial and 6 to 9 deg below; uncovertebral frontal angles 76 to 85 deg; canal triangular from C4. Inference: C3, C4 and C5 cannot be named from their own measures; the sequence and a count from C2 or C7 must carry them.
- C4 to C5: the lower end-plate grows (EPWl 17.0 to 19.4, EPDl 15.9 to 17.9, EPAl 199.2 to 246.2), the largest lower-depth step, p. 863 (PDF 3), but not significant against C4.
- C5 to C6: the orientation break of the region. The pedicle axis crosses the transverse plane (PDIt about -6 to about +6.5 deg, significant), the pedicle sagittal angle falls (40 to 32 deg; right side significant), and the uncovertebral frontal-plane angle crosses 90 deg (82.7 to 104.0 upper, 84.9 to 106.2 lower, significant), Tables 4 and 6. Lower end-plate widest step (EPWl 19.4 to 22.0), lower area peaks at C6 (289.9), canal widest at C6 (SCW 25.8). Of clinical note, a high incidence of cord injury at C6 (Fife and Kraus), p. 868 (PDF 8).
- C6 to C7 (toward C7/T1): the cervicothoracic transition. Upper end-plate jumps (EPWu 18.5 to 21.8 and EPAu 221.2 to 278.3, both significant against C6), EPDu 16.4 to 18.1; lower end-plate depth falls (18.5 to 16.8, a "distinct transition", p. 867, PDF 7) and width/depth rises to 1.39; VBHp rises 10.9 to 12.8 (significant against C6); canal depth falls 18.1 to 15.2, canal area falls 266.5 to 223.8 (the smallest), width/depth jumps to 1.61; SPL 34.2 to 45.7 and TPW 49.5 to 66.6 (both significant against C2 to C6); uncovertebral frontal angle 104.0 to 115.6 (significant against C6); pedicle sagittal angle about 30 deg and transverse angle about +11 deg. The authors attribute the larger C6 and C7 upper end-plates to greater load bearing, p. 867 (PDF 7). The paper has no T1 data (T1 is in the thoracic paper).
- Left against right: the authors say the data give the left-right variation, p. 868 (PDF 8), but test no side difference. Beyond C2's pedicle area (left 51.8 against right 32.3), the larger printed gaps are PDIs at C7 (right 33.1, left 26.7), PDIt at C7 (13.4, 9.2) and the upper uncovertebral area at C6 (right 48.8, left 58.1), Tables 4 and 6. Inference: with SDs of this size, these may be noise; average the sides.
- T12/L1 and L5/S1: not covered.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Method accuracy: under 5% on the Plexiglas pseudo-vertebra for linear, angular and area parameters, p. 863 (PDF 3). The point-location error of the morphometer is not restated here (the thoracic paper gives +/-0.5 mm).
- No intra- or inter-observer study, no repeated digitising, number of operators not stated.
- Dispersion: SEM over 12 spines. Inference, SD = 3.46 x SEM: body lengths 0.9 to 3.4 mm; canal lengths 1.2 to 4.6 mm; canal area 45 to 126 mm2; pedicle lengths 1.0 to 2.2 mm (C3 to C7); pedicle area 7 to 11 mm2 (C3 to C7); pedicle angles 2.3 to 16.5 deg; end-plate tilts 1.4 to 5.2 deg; SPL 2.7 to 6.5 mm; TPW 3.9 to 10.3 mm; uncovertebral areas 6 to 26 mm2 and angles 3.8 to 17.5 deg.
- Doubtful SEMs: C2 left pedicle width 0.07, height 0.14 and area 0.28 (against 0.35, 0.46 and 2.44 on the right, at the level the text calls highly variable), Table 4; canal areas (CV up to 56%), Table 3.
- Shape models as estimators (pp. 864, 866 and 868; PDF 4, 6 and 8): end-plate ellipse +10% at every level, triangle well under; canal ellipse best at C2 and C3, triangle at C4 to C7, ellipse always over and triangle always under; pedicle triangle closest.
- Between studies (p. 868, PDF 8; Table 7): lower end-plate depth within 2 mm of Nissan and Gilad (radiographs) and Francis (caliper) with the same trend; Liu's depths smaller (probably mid-centrum); Liu's end-plate areas much larger (309.7 to 440.5 against 190.7 to 289.9), probably because they included the uncovertebral facets; adding the uncovertebral areas to this study's would bring the totals "very close" to Francis's; canal dimensions consistent with Francis except that Francis saw a relatively small canal throughout, with no widening at C5 and C6 and no narrowing at C7; body heights 2 to 2.5 mm below the radiographic and caliper studies because of where height was taken. By my arithmetic Francis's lower end-plate widths run 2.0 to 5.9 mm above this study's at every level (most at C7), which the paper does not discuss.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indications for the data: mathematical (kinetic and kinematic) models of the cervical spine for injury mechanisms, kinematics and instability; clinical diagnosis of disease or injury; design of surgical implants and procedures; instability determinations, p. 861 (PDF 1), p. 868 (PDF 8). The introduction ties the earlier thoracolumbar morphometry to the need for pedicle size and orientation, p. 861 (PDF 1); this paper adds the cervical pedicle's section and axis but no pedicle length.
- Cord at risk near C6 to C7: the canal area falls from C6 to C7 while the cord stays about the same size, so a smaller canal around the cord gives more potential for mechanical impingement in trauma; Fife and Kraus observed a high incidence of cord injury at C6, pp. 867 to 868 (PDF 7 to 8). No stenosis threshold is given.
- C2's deep canal allows motion without cord encroachment; C2's posteriorly placed load-bearing facets and heavy muscle attachments explain its large pedicles, p. 868 (PDF 8).
- Pedicle axes converge, crossing in the anterior part of the body; medial angulation about 40 to 44 deg at C3 to C5 and about 30 deg at C6 to C7, and the craniocaudal tilt changes sign between C5 and C6, p. 866 (PDF 6); Table 4. Inference (the paper gives no screw guidance): C3 to C5 pedicles are about 5 to 6 mm wide with SD about 1 to 2 mm, so a share of them is narrower than common screw diameters, and the trajectory must change between C5 and C6.
- Large right-left asymmetry of the C2 pedicle (left area 60% above right), p. 866 (PDF 6). Inference: do not assume symmetry at C2.
- Pitfall, height on a lateral projection: posterior body height from a lateral projection reads about 2 to 3 mm more than the midsagittal posterior-wall height because the uncovertebral region projects, p. 868 (PDF 8).
- Pitfall, depth at mid-centrum: smaller than at the end-plate (Liu against this study), p. 868 (PDF 8).
- Pitfall, end-plate area with or without the uncovertebral facets: including them inflates the area greatly (Liu), p. 868 (PDF 8).
- Pitfall, statistics: repeated t tests among more than two levels are invalid; use ANOVA with a multiple-comparison test, p. 862 (PDF 2).
- Pitfall, frame: every angle is to planes built from the posterior wall of the body, not to the scanner, the end-plates or the neighbouring vertebra, p. 862 (PDF 2).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. For every cervical instance build Panjabi's frame first: least-squares frontal plane through the four posterior-wall corners, z anterior, x lateral, y cranial, origin at the centre of the upper end-plate; measure widths along x, depths along z, heights along y, and angles against these planes. p. 862 (PDF 2), Figure 1.
2. Identify C2 by its dens (Inference: the paper never mentions the dens; it only leaves C2's upper end-plate values blank) and confirm it by the deepest canal (about 21 mm, width/depth about 1.2, area about 375 mm2), the largest pedicles (width about 8, height 9 to 11 mm) and a lower end-plate about 17.5 x 15.6 mm. Do not expect symmetric C2 pedicles. Tables 2 to 4, pp. 863 to 864 (PDF 3 to 4); p. 866 (PDF 6).
3. At C2 to C3 expect the canal depth to drop by about 5 mm and pedicle size by about 2 to 4 mm; a C3 candidate with a C2-like canal or pedicles is a misnamed C2 or a C2-C3 block (Inference; the paper reports no fusion). Tables 3 and 4.
4. Identify C7 from below or above by its combination: transverse processes about 67 mm tip to tip (about 17 mm more than C6, on an AP view or CT), spinous process about 46 mm from the body (about 11 mm more than C6, on a lateral view or CT), upper end-plate about 22 x 18 mm with area about 280 mm2, posterior height about 12.8 mm (taller than C6), the smallest canal (area about 224 mm2, depth about 15 mm, width/depth about 1.6), and lower end-plate width/depth about 1.4. Tables 2, 3 and 5; p. 867 (PDF 7).
5. Before naming C7 by TPW, check for a cervical rib or long C7 transverse process on CT (Inference: the paper never mentions them, and they would inflate TPW). Table 5, p. 864 (PDF 4).
6. Separate C6 from C5 on CT by orientation, not size: the pedicle axis turns from about 6 deg below to about 6 deg above the transverse plane, the pedicle sagittal angle falls from about 40 to about 32 deg, and the uncovertebral facets' frontal-plane angle passes 90 deg (about 83 to about 105). Expect also the widest lower end-plate step (EPWl 19.4 to 22.0). Tables 2, 4 and 6; p. 866 (PDF 6).
7. Do not name C3, C4 or C5 from their own measures: no measure in this paper separates any two of them. Count from C2 down and from C7 up, and let the sequence decide. Tables 2 to 6 (parenthesis entries). Inference: Scheffe's test on 12 spines is conservative, so a missing mark is weak evidence of sameness; small graded differences (for example EPWu 15.8, 17.2, 17.5) may still help a likelihood, but not alone.
8. Measure the cervical upper end-plate width between the bases of the uncinate processes, not across them (Inference from Figure 1's front view, p. 862, PDF 2; the text gives no words for it), and leave the uncovertebral facets out of end-plate areas (stated, p. 868, PDF 8); otherwise widths and areas read high against this paper.
9. Take end-plate depth at the end-plate rim, not at mid-body: Liu's mid-centrum depths run 1.2 to 4.1 mm smaller, p. 868 (PDF 8), Table 7. Inference: count the anteroinferior lip as part of the lower end-plate rim, as Figure 1's side view draws EPDl to the lip's front.
10. On a lateral radiograph, expect the posterior body height to read 2 to 3 mm above the CT midsagittal VBHp (about 11 to 13 mm); compare like with like. p. 868 (PDF 8), Table 7.
11. Use size-free ratios where magnification is unknown: end-plate width/depth (upper 1.05 to 1.20, lower 1.07 to 1.39), canal width/depth (1.17 at C2, about 1.4 at C3 to C6, 1.61 at C7), pedicle height/width (1.1 to 1.43). Tables 2 to 4. Inference: the ratios cancel magnification only when both views share it.
12. When estimating an area from a width and a depth, use the ellipse for end-plates (about 10% high), the ellipse for the canal at C2 and C3 and the triangle at C4 to C7, and the triangle for pedicles. pp. 864, 866, 868 (PDF 4, 6, 8).
13. Expect the end-plate tilts to be small (about 2 to 5 deg) and nearly equal above and below at every level; do not use them to name a level (no level differs). Table 2, p. 864 (PDF 4).
14. Treat any single pedicle angle with a wide tolerance (SD up to about 16 deg at C5) and use the mean of the two sides. Table 4.
15. Flag a cervical canal depth near or below C7's 15 mm at C4 to C6 as a narrow canal for that level (Inference: SD about 1.6 to 2.5 mm at C4 to C6); the paper gives no stenosis threshold. Table 3.

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. No cervical rib, block vertebra, count anomaly or transitional segment is reported in 12 spines, and the numbering method is not stated; take every prior from other papers. What the paper gives the decoder is emission structure: C2 is distinct (pedicles, canal depth; plus, by Inference, the dens, which the paper does not mention), C7 is distinct from C6 in six measures (EPWu, EPAu, VBHp, SPL, TPW, UJIfu, all significant against C6), C6 is distinct from C5 only in orientation (pedicle angles, uncovertebral frontal angles), and C3, C4, C5 are not distinguishable by any measure. Transitions for a missing or extra cervical level must therefore rest on the C2 and C7 anchors and on T1's thoracic features.

(b) Measures and definitions against schwingcheck/mnemonics.py:
- SPL: this paper defines it from the lower posterior spinous tip to the vertebral body centroid (p. 866, PDF 6), Figure 1 drawing it to about the upper end-plate centre; mnemonics.py says "from the posterior wall of the canal to the tip". A mask extractor following mnemonics.py would read shorter than Table 5 by roughly the canal depth plus about half the body depth (Inference: of the order of 20 to 25 mm at C3 to C6). Fix the definition before using SPL (the thoracic and lumbar notes found the same).
- EPWu (the archive's "endplate"; mnemonics.py: "the distance between the lateral ends of the superior end-plate"): for cervical levels the paper's upper width excludes the uncinate processes (Figure 1, p. 862, PDF 2); a mask's superior-surface extent includes them and will bias EPWu upward at C3 to C7. EPAu likewise excludes the uncovertebral facets (p. 868, PDF 8). The extractor needs an uncus-aware rule for the cervical superior end-plate.
- EPIt (the thoracic wedge, a sum): the cervical paper prints no wedge and speaks of the difference of the two inclinations (under 2 deg, p. 864, PDF 4); Figure 1 draws both end-plates tilting the same way. Do not build a cervical wedge as EPItu + EPItl (Inference).
- PDW, PDH, PDA: mnemonics.py says "smallest" and "at its narrowest"; the paper says only the section perpendicular to the pedicle axis, position unstated (p. 866, PDF 6; Figure 1).
- SCW: mnemonics.py says "at the level of the pedicles"; the paper does not state the level. SCD "in the midline" agrees with Figure 1.
- VBHp: the paper's midsagittal posterior-wall height (p. 864, PDF 4) agrees with mnemonics.py's "between the end-plates at the posterior wall" in landmark, but mnemonics.py names no plane and marks VBHp as readable on a lateral radiograph; a lateral-view value reads 2 to 3 mm higher (p. 868, PDF 8; Table 7), so an extractor must take the midline of the posterior wall, not its lateral silhouette.
- TPW: 3D tip-to-tip (p. 866, PDF 6) agrees; which tubercle is the tip is unstated.
- UJA and UJI (CT only in mnemonics.py, agreed): C2 has no upper surface and C7 has a lower one; model C2's upper uncovertebral values as structurally absent, not missing at random. UJI is printed once per level and surface (sides combined), so do not split it by side.
- C2 dashes (EPDu, EPWu, EPAu, EPItu, VBHp, PDIs, PDIt, UJAru, UJAlu, UJIfu, UJIsu) are structural or unmeasured; the generator draws C3 to L5, but these C2 values are the only C2 source in Panjabi's series and could anchor the decoder's first state (Inference).
- Measures not yet held that this paper offers: the canal shape factor SCA/(SCW x SCD) (0.73 at C2 falling to 0.57 at C6; my arithmetic), the end-plate and canal width/depth ratios, PDH/PDW, and the presence of uncovertebral surfaces (upper absent at C2) as a size-free feature.
- Facet measures for C2 to C7 are not in this paper; they are in the facets paper of the series (bordercall's facets tables).

(c) Measurement error to use: phantom error under 5% for lengths, angles and areas; no observer error. Between-person spread: SD = SEM x sqrt(12) per cell (Inference), with wide or replaced values for the doubtful cells: the C2 left pedicle (PDWl SEM 0.07, PDHl 0.14, PDAl 0.28), SCA at every level (SD 45 to 126 mm2), and the pedicle sagittal angle at C5 (SD about 15 to 16.5 deg).

(d) Covariates: none analysed. 8 men and 4 women pooled, 19 to 59 years, 54 to 85 kg, 157 to 178 cm, nationality and ethnicity not stated, the same twelve spines as the thoracic and lumbar papers (Inference from the series). Sex differences are acknowledged from Francis (284 cervical vertebrae, men and women, white and black, aged 25 to 36, caliper; p. 861, PDF 1) but not quantified. The size factor and any sex or population effect must come from elsewhere.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Not stated: how levels were identified and counted; whether any spine was excluded after the screening radiographs; n per cell; where the C2 frame's origin was (no upper end-plate); the axial level of the canal measures; the position of the pedicle section along the axis; which transverse-process tubercle is the tip; whether SPL ends at the body centroid (text) or the upper end-plate centre (Figure 1); the sign convention of PDIt in words; how left and right were combined for UJI; why C2's pedicle angles and VBHp were not measured.
- Not measured at all: C1, lateral masses, laminae, facets (in the facets paper), foramen transversarium, intervertebral foramina, disc heights, anterior body height, body width and depth at mid-height, pedicle length or chord length (needed for screw length).
- Text against table (all read on the page images):
  - p. 866 (PDF 6): pedicle widths and heights "smallest at C3"; Table 4 has the smallest right width at C4 (5.7), right height at C5 (6.7) and left width at C4 and C5 (5.1); only left height is smallest at C3 (7.2).
