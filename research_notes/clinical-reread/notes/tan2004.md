## What the paper is

- Design: dry-bone cadaveric morphometry with a direct-contact 3D digitiser, one series, compared against published white (Caucasian) series: Panjabi et al. for all levels (refs 13, 14, 15: cervical 1991, lumbar 1992, thoracic 1991), McLain et al. 2002 (ref 11) for upper-thoracic pedicle length, Zindrick et al. 1987 (ref 16) for thoracolumbar pedicle length and pedicle angle. p. 137 (PDF 1), p. 138 (PDF 2).
- Population: 10 Chinese cadavers "of Singaporean origin"; mean age 65.7 years (range 56 to 77), mean weight 61.6 kg (50 to 70), mean height 1.66 m (1.59 to 1.72). Sex is not reported anywhere. p. 137 (PDF 1).
- Denominator: 220 vertebrae from 10 cadavers, C3 to L5 (5 cervical + 12 thoracic + 5 lumbar = 22 levels per cadaver). p. 137 (PDF 1). Inference: 220 = 10 x 22 means every cadaver gave exactly one vertebra per named level, so n = 10 per level; the paper never states n per level or per cell, and nothing says how levels were identified or whether any spine had a numerical variant.
- Preparation: soft tissue trimmed, 30 min in a solution the paper calls "sodium hydrochloride" (Inference: almost certainly sodium hypochlorite), rinsed 20 min, air dried, stored at constant temperature and humidity. p. 137 (PDF 1).
- Fixation: a 5 mm hole drilled about three quarters of the way into the body from anterior; a screw in it, clamped in a vice, clay to stop motion; all coordinates taken without repositioning bone or instrument (Fig. 2). p. 138 (PDF 2).
- Instrument: direct-contact 3D digitiser, stated accuracy 0.01 mm, calibrated per the maker; connected to a computer. Its make and model are not named. p. 138 (PDF 2).
- Fig. 1 (the parameter drawings for a cervical, a thoracic and a lumbar vertebra) is reproduced with permission from Panjabi et al., so the drawn landmarks are Panjabi's (caption, p. 138, PDF 2).
- Levels covered: C3 to L5 only. No C1, C2, S1. Pedicle length only T1 to L5.
- Inclusion and exclusion criteria: none stated beyond Chinese Singaporean origin. Nothing is said about excluding degenerative, osteoporotic, fractured or deformed spines. Inference: the authors chose the posterior body wall for the frame because osteophytes disturb other surfaces (p. 139, PDF 3), which suggests osteophytic specimens were kept.
- Output: Table 1 gives mean +/- "standard error of mean" for 26 parameters at 22 levels (pp. 140-141, PDF 4-5); Table 2 gives the percent difference from Panjabi for 16 parameters (pp. 142-143, PDF 6-7); Table 3 pedicle sagittal inclination against Panjabi and Zindrick; Table 4 pedicle length against McLain and Zindrick (pp. 144-145, PDF 8-9). Figs. 5 to 12 plot the trends.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about transitional vertebrae, rib counts, lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, L6, T13, cervical ribs, block vertebrae or any anomalous specimen; it does not say how levels were numbered or counted (whole paper read, pp. 137-146, PDF 1-10). Inference: the 220 = 10 x 22 count implies no specimen contributed a missing or extra segment, but whether such spines were screened out is unknown.

## Measures, each as a recipe

Common frame (p. 139, PDF 3, Fig. 3; Fig. 1 p. 138, PDF 2). The definitions follow Panjabi et al. (refs 13 to 15), with pedicle length from McLain and Zindrick and pedicle sagittal inclination from Zindrick (p. 138, PDF 2). The vertebral coordinate system has its origin at the centroid of the upper end-plate. The posterior wall of the body is the reference plane, chosen because it was "fairly consistent" and showed least variation with osteophytes (p. 139, PDF 3). That plane is set by three points: digitised points a and b, and the average of points c and d (Fig. 3). The x-axis runs from point b to point a; digitising in right-hand-thumb order fixes the z-axis. Each digitised point is the average of ten hits. The end-plate centroid is found by polygonising the digitised end-plate, extruding it 0.1 mm into a thin volume and taking that volume's centroid. From Figs. 1 and 3: Y is cranial, Z is antero-posterior (pointing anterior, normal to the posterior-wall plane) and X is lateral. Once the frame is set, the profile is traced with the probe and turned into a 3D surface. A best-fit plane can then be generated in any orientation (Fig. 4: plan view for linear and area measures, side view for angles). A linear measure needs two marked points. An area is the best-fit plane polygonised into a triangle mesh. An angle is the angle between two chosen planes. Each parameter is the average of three readings (p. 139, PDF 3).

The paper discusses no radiograph and no CT. Every "From a 3D CT instance mask:" and "From a radiograph alone:" line below is Inference, and so is every computed ratio, unless a page is cited for it. The landmark descriptions come from the paper's Fig. 1 drawings (Panjabi's), since the text defines the parameters only by name.

Inference for CT masks, common to all recipes below: build the same frame per instance. (1) Take the dorsal surface of the body subregion (the voxels of the body that face the canal, between the pedicle roots); fit a plane; its normal pointing ventrally is +Z. (2) X is the left-right direction within that plane (the principal in-plane axis perpendicular to the body's cranio-caudal axis); Y = Z x X, pointing cranially. (3) The origin is the centroid of the cranial end-plate surface voxels. Measure everything in this frame, not in scanner axes, so that lordosis, kyphosis and scoliosis do not leak into widths and depths.

### Upper and lower end-plate width (EPWu, EPWl), mm
- Landmarks: lateral extent of the upper or lower end-plate, in the frontal view along X (Fig. 1, front views). p. 138 (PDF 2). In the cervical front view (Fig. 1a) EPWu is drawn between two lines that sit inside the uncinate processes, so the cervical upper width is the end-plate between the uncinates, not the full body; EPWl is drawn across the whole lower body. In the lumbar front view (Fig. 1c) EPWu spans the whole upper end-plate. p. 138 (PDF 2), Fig. 1a and 1c.
- Plane: coronal, measured on the plan view of the end-plate best-fit plane (Fig. 4, p. 139, PDF 3). Instrument: digitiser.
- Levels: C3 to L5. Table 1, pp. 140-141 (PDF 4-5).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: take the cranial (or caudal) end-plate surface of the body subregion (outermost 1 to 2 mm of the body along Y, or the end-plate subregion where the segmenter gives one). Project it onto its best-fit plane and take the extent along X. Inference: at C3 to C7 the upper width must stop at the bases of the uncinate processes, as Fig. 1a draws it; a naive extent of the cranial surface would include the uncinates and overstate EPWu.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: AP radiograph, the width of the end-plate line, between the uncinate bases at C3 to C7. Magnification applies (dry bone, none here). On AP the upper and lower end-plates superimpose unless the beam is parallel to the disc.
- Across levels (Table 1): EPWu C3 13.8, C4 14.7, C5 14.9, C6 15.8, C7 19.0, T1 24.7, then it falls to T4 22.5 and rises again: T5 23.3, T6 23.7, T7 24.6, T8 25.9, T9 27.0, T10 28.8, T11 31.6, T12 34.5, L1 36.3, L2 38.2, L3 39.9, L4 42.0 (maximum), L5 41.6 (dip). EPWl C3 14.3, C4 15.0, C5 15.9, C6 19.5, C7 20.3, T1 27.1, T2 25.3, T3 24.4, T4 25.0, T5 23.8, T6 24.8, T7 26.8, T8 27.9, T9 29.2, T10 31.9, T11 35.3, T12 36.4, L1 39.2, L2 41.4, L3 43.5, L4 45.3 (maximum), L5 43.7. The paper's own reading: C3 to C5 fairly constant; the steepest rise into T1; a slight fall from T1 to T4, then a rise to T12; a steady rise from L1 to L4 and a dip at L5; the lower width is larger than the upper (p. 139, PDF 3). Inference: EPW separates C6/C7 (EPWu +3.2 mm) and C7/T1 (+5.7) clearly. It does not order T1 to T6, where it is non-monotone. It does not separate L4 from L5.
- Sex, age, size, population: against Panjabi, average -10.2% (upper) and -10.4% (lower). Regional means computed from Table 2: upper C -13.9, T -8.3, L -10.7%; lower C -14.3, T -9.0, L -9.6%. Largest at C5 (mean of upper and lower -16.5%, p. 144, PDF 8). Table 2, pp. 142-143 (PDF 6-7).
- Error: SE 0.0 to 0.5 mm (n = 10 per level, Inference). Digitiser linear error 1.28% at 20 mm and 0.44% at 10 mm (p. 138, PDF 2).

### Anterior and posterior vertebral body height (VBHa, VBHp), mm
- Landmarks: distance between upper and lower end-plates at the anterior and posterior body margins along Y (Fig. 1, side views). p. 138 (PDF 2).
- Plane: sagittal. Levels C3 to L5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: in the mid-sagittal plane of the frame, the Y-distance between the cranial and caudal body surfaces at the anterior and posterior cortex. Inference: the paper does not say whether the rim or the end-plate centre at the margin is used; take the cortical margin.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: lateral radiograph, anterior and posterior body heights; the ratio VBHa/VBHp is magnification-free.
- Across levels (Table 1): VBHa C3 10.0, C4 9.9, C5 9.6, C6 10.4, C7 11.2, T1 12.8, T2 14.2, T3 14.5, T4 14.7, T5 15.2, T6 15.5, T7 15.1, T8 16.1, T9 16.5, T10 17.5, T11 17.5, T12 18.7, L1 20.2, L2 20.8, L3 21.4, L4 21.6, L5 22.0. VBHp C3 11.2, C4 11.3, C5 11.3, C6 11.3, C7 11.8, T1 14.0, T2 15.2, T3 15.3, T4 15.8, T5 16.4, T6 17.0, T7 17.4, T8 17.8, T9 18.0, T10 19.1, T11 20.4, T12 21.5, L1 22.4, L2 23.1 (maximum), L3 22.1, L4 21.6, L5 20.0. The posterior height exceeds the anterior in the cervical spine, through the thoracic spine (the paper ties this wedge to the thoracic kyphosis) and in the lumbar spine except at L5 (p. 139, PDF 3); in the table L4 is equal (21.6 and 21.6). Inference, computed: VBHa/VBHp is 0.85 to 0.95 from C3 to L2, 0.97 at L3, 1.00 at L4, 1.10 at L5, so L5 is the only level here with a taller anterior body. - Sex, age, size, population: VBHp averages -4.0% against Panjabi (C -1.8, T -3.1, L -8.2 computed; -12.7 at L5). VBHa is not compared (Table 2).
- Error: SE 0.1 to 0.7 mm.

### Spinal canal width (SCW), mm
- Landmarks: transverse width of the vertebral foramen in the top view (Fig. 1). p. 138 (PDF 2). Fig. 1a and 1c draw it as the widest transverse span of the canal, between the pedicles; the text gives no landmark or height. Inference: this is the inter-pedicular distance.
- Plane: axial (top view). Levels C3 to L5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: in an axial slab of the frame at pedicle height, fill the hole enclosed by the posterior body wall and the neural arch (the instance mask's foramen, or the segmenter's canal label inside it); take the maximum extent along X. Inference throughout.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: AP radiograph, inter-pedicular distance between the medial cortices of the pedicle ovals. Magnification applies.
- Across levels (Table 1): C3 19.2, C4 19.3, C5 20.3, C6 20.6 (cervical maximum), C7 19.7, T1 17.7, T2 15.2, T3 14.2, T4 13.5 (minimum), T5 13.6, T6 13.8, T7 13.9, T8 14.1, T9 14.2, T10 14.2, T11 15.3, T12 17.9, L1 19.4, L2 19.5, L3 19.4, L4 20.2, L5 23.4. Text: about 5 mm fall from T1 to T4, flat to T10, rise to T12; flat L1 to L3, maximum at L5 (text says 23.5, table 23.4) (p. 142, PDF 6). Inference: SCW separates T11/T12 (+2.6 mm), L4/L5 (+3.2 mm) and T1/T2 (-2.5 mm); it does not separate T3 to T10.
- Sex, age, size, population: average -19.8% against Panjabi (C -19.4, T -20.7, L -18.1 computed); Fig. 10 overlays both series (p. 144, PDF 8).
- Error: SE 0.2 to 0.6 mm.

### Spinal canal depth (SCD), mm
- Landmarks: antero-posterior depth of the vertebral foramen in the top view (Fig. 1). p. 138 (PDF 2). Fig. 1a and 1c draw it in the midline, from the posterior body wall to the inner surface of the arch; the text gives no landmark.
- Plane: axial. Levels C3 to L5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: along Z at x = 0, distance from the dorsal surface of the body to the ventral surface of the lamina junction, in the same slab as SCW. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, posterior body wall to the spinolaminar line (Inference: the paper does not discuss radiographs; this is the usual radiographic surrogate of the bony mid-sagittal canal). Magnification applies; the ratio SCD/EPD cancels it.
- Across levels (Table 1): C3 to C6 10.3 at every level (the smallest of the column), C7 11.0, T1 11.6, T2 11.7, T3 12.0, T4 11.8, T5 11.5, T6 11.6, T7 11.9, T8 11.9, T9 11.8, T10 11.9, T11 11.8, T12 12.4, L1 12.5 (maximum), L2 11.7, L3 11.2, L4 11.2, L5 11.4. The text calls it almost constant through the thoracic spine and falling from L1 to L5 (p. 142, PDF 6). Inference: SCD is the flattest measure in the paper (10.3 to 12.5 mm, C3 to L5) and carries almost no level information.
- Sex, age, size, population: the largest group difference in the paper, average -31.8% against Panjabi (C -37.9, T -26.7, L -37.6 computed; -43.1 at C6, -42.1 at L5).
- Error: SE 0.1 to 0.4 mm.

### Spinal canal area (SCA), mm2
- Landmarks: area of the vertebral foramen in the top view (Fig. 1). p. 138 (PDF 2). Instrument: polygonised best-fit plane, triangle-mesh area (p. 139, PDF 3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: pixel area of the filled foramen in the axial slab of the frame (Inference).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: not measurable directly. ### Pedicle height (PDHl, PDHr) and width (PDWl, PDWr), mm
- Landmarks: outer height and width of the "pedicle section" (Fig. 1, top views: PDW horizontal, PDH vertical on the section drawing). p. 138 (PDF 2). Inference: Panjabi took the narrowest (isthmus) section; the paper does not state where along the pedicle.
- Plane: a section across the pedicle. Levels C3 to L5, left and right separately.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: fit the pedicle axis (the line of centroids of cross-sections through the pedicle between body and arch). Step along it, cutting sections perpendicular to it. Find the section of least area (isthmus). PDH is its extent along the frame's Y; PDW is its extent across the axis in the axial plane. Inference throughout. Outer cortical dimensions, not the cancellous channel.

### Pedicle length (PL), mm
- Definition (p. 145, PDF 9): distance from the facet cortex to the anterior cortex of the body, along the line of the pedicle sagittal inclination PDIs (Fig. 1c shows PL along the pedicle axis in the top view). Follows McLain (ref 11) and Zindrick (ref 16). Inference: this is the screw-path chord (the length a transpedicular screw would traverse), not the short anatomical pedicle.
- Plane: axial (the line lies in the plane of PDIs). Levels T1 to L5 only; no cervical value.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: along the pedicle axis line projected in the axial plane at angle PDIs, the chord length through the instance mask from the dorsal cortex at the facet to the ventral cortex of the body (Inference).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: not directly. A lateral radiograph shows at most the projection of PL onto the sagittal plane (shortened by the axial angle PDIs), and the facet entry point is obscured; an axial image is needed.
- Across levels (Table 1): T1 30.4, T2 27.5 (minimum), T3 28.6, T4 27.6, T5 31.2, T6 30.3, T7 35.0, T8 34.3, T9 32.7, T10 34.6, T11 32.8, T12 31.5, L1 41.4, L2 43.8 (maximum), L3 42.2, L4 39.7, L5 39.2. Inference: the T12/L1 step (+9.9 mm) is the largest adjacent change in PL; the thoracic values are noisy (up and down by 1 to 5 mm).
- Population: shorter than Zindrick's at every level by 15.6 to 28.3%, mean -22.1%. Shorter than McLain's at T1 to T6 by 2.9 to 26.2% (Table 4, p. 145, PDF 9). McLain's cadavers were 11 women and 7 men aged 62 to 85, which the authors offer as why the McLain gap is smaller (p. 145, PDF 9).
- Error: SE 0.3 to 0.8 mm.

### Spinous process length (SPL), mm

## Measurement error and reliability

- Digitiser: stated accuracy 0.01 mm. Checked against a micrometer screw gauge: 1.28% error at 20 mm and 0.44% at 10 mm. Mean angle error 0.68% on 15 and 30 deg gauge blocks. Mean area error 1.71% on two bars of about 2,250 mm2. The authors judge this accuracy high and acceptable. p. 138 (PDF 2). Inference: these are instrument checks on gauges, not repeat measurements of bone, so they bound only the instrument's share of the error.
- Each frame point is the average of ten hits (p. 139, PDF 3); each parameter is the average of three readings (p. 139, PDF 3). No intra- or inter-observer statistics, no ICC, no repeat on a second day, and the number of observers is not stated.
- Table 1's "+/-" is the standard error of the mean, which the paper defines as the sample standard deviation divided by "the sample size" (p. 139, PDF 3). Read literally, SD = 10 x SE. Read in the standard way, SD = sqrt(10) x SE = 3.16 x SE. Inference, computed: the standard reading gives between-specimen CVs of about 3 to 5% for end-plate widths and depths, 5 to 7% for canal width and depth, 7 to 9% for pedicle height, 12 to 13% for pedicle width, about 9.5% for end-plate and canal areas and about 14% for pedicle areas. These are plausible for a homogeneous group of 10. The literal reading would put pedicle-width CVs near 40%. So the methods sentence is probably a slip; treat the spread as uncertain.
- Left-right agreement: within 0.8 mm for PDH and PDW at every level (largest PDH at L3); PDIs magnitudes differ by up to 8.2 deg (C5). Table 1.
- No significance tests: "significant" in the discussion is used without a test (pp. 144-145, PDF 8-9).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indications named: modelling of the spine for analysis and for designing implants and instruments; more precise diagnosis and management of the spine in Chinese Singaporeans (abstract, p. 137, PDF 1; conclusion, p. 145, PDF 9).
- Implants: most spinal implants were designed from Caucasian measurements, and Chinese pedicles are about 26% narrower; this matters for implant fit (p. 144, PDF 8).
- Transpedicular screws: the outer pedicle width is too small for a 5 mm screw at most levels. The text names T1 and T10 to L5 as the exceptions (p. 144, PDF 8); Table 1 also gives more than 5 mm at C6, C7 and T2. Together with the shorter upper-thoracic pedicle length and the T8 to T12 divergence, the authors conclude a transpedicular screw "may not be feasible" (abstract p. 137; p. 145, PDF 9).
- Thoracic pedicle direction: at T8 to T12 the paper finds a slight divergence (about 7 deg) instead of the convergence seen in white spines, which it calls a significant difference that may affect pedicle screw placement (abstract p. 137, PDF 1; p. 145, PDF 9). Inference: read literally, the pedicle axes there run slightly anterolaterally, so a screw would aim outward; see Gaps on whether the sign is real.
- Canal: SCD and SCA are about 32% smaller than in Panjabi's specimens, which the authors say may affect surgical management, especially of low back pain (p. 144, PDF 8; p. 145, PDF 9). The mid-thoracic minimum at T4 is the critical vascular zone (ref 2) (p. 144, PDF 8).
- Measuring pitfall named by the authors: end-plate-based frames are disturbed by osteophytes, so they used the posterior body wall (p. 139, PDF 3).
- Measuring pitfall named by the authors: comparing pedicle length across studies needs the same definition (facet cortex to anterior cortex along the pedicle axis), and the other series' cadavers differ in age and sex (p. 145, PDF 9).
- Inference, pitfall: the paper's naming is easy to invert. "Sagittal inclination" (PDIs) is the axial-plane medial angle; "transverse inclination" (PDIt) is the sagittal-plane tilt.
- Inference, pitfall: each vertebra was held by a screw in a 5 mm hole drilled three quarters of the way into the body from the front (p. 138, PDF 2, Fig. 2). The hole sits where the anterior cortex, the end point of pedicle length, and the anterior body height are taken; the paper does not say how the hole was kept out of those landmarks.
- Inference, pitfall: the cervical and L1 to L3 bodies here are taller behind than in front (VBHa/VBHp 0.85 to 0.97), so a body-wedge prior should not assume lordotic wedging above L5; L5 alone is taller in front (1.10). Table 1; p. 139 (PDF 3).
- Inference, pitfall: the junction markers that hold in both this series and the comparison series (TPW at C7/T1 and T12/L1, PL at T12/L1, the L5 pedicle jump) are safer than ones seen only here (the PDW drop at T12/L1, the SCA maximum at L1, the T8 to T12 sign of PDIs). Computed from Tables 1, 2 and 4.
- Inference, pitfall: a 10.3 mm bony mid-sagittal canal at C3 to C6 sits at the commonly used absolute stenosis threshold of about 10 mm. A whole series of unselected cadavers at that value suggests a landmark difference (for example measuring to a different arch point) as much as anatomy; Panjabi's implied cervical SCD is 15 to 18 mm (Table 2 arithmetic).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Build each vertebra's frame on the posterior body wall (normal = antero-posterior axis), with the origin at the centroid of the upper end-plate. The posterior wall is the most consistent and least osteophyte-affected surface. p. 139 (PDF 3), Fig. 3.
2. Measure bilateral quantities (PDH, PDW, PDA, PDIs, PDIt) on both sides and use the mean of the magnitudes. The paper prints left and right PDIs with opposite signs (Table 1, pp. 140-141, PDF 4-5). Inference: the left-right mean cancels a small rotation of the frame, and a left-right PDIs gap well above the largest gap between this paper's level means, 8.2 deg at C5, suggests the frame is wrong.
3. To place T1, look for the step from the level above on many measures at once (Table 1, pp. 140-141, PDF 4-5): TPW should rise about 10 mm, to the thoracic maximum; EPWu about 5.7 mm; the mean pedicle width about 1.8 mm, to a local maximum; the pedicle area to a local maximum; the lower end-plate inclination should flip from positive to negative. Inference: the size of the EPWu jump differs between series (Panjabi's implied step is 2.7 mm), so weight TPW and the pedicle more; then confirm by counting ribs, which the paper does not cover.
4. To place T12/L1: TPW has its minimum at T12 (41.0) and rises about 12.6 mm at L1 (Table 1). Inference: on an AP image use TPW / EPWu, which falls to about 1.2 at T12 and is about 1.5 at L1 and 1.6 to 1.8 from L2 to L5 (computed from Table 1). Confirm with the pedicle-axis chord length (+10 mm at L1, Table 1; the same step is in Zindrick's series, Fig. 12, p. 145, PDF 9) and the sign change of the pedicle's sagittal tilt from cephalad to caudal (p. 143, PDF 7). Treat the drop in pedicle width (7.55 to 5.55) and the canal-area maximum at L1 as weak cues: neither appears in the Panjabi values implied by Table 2.
5. To place L5: it is the only level with VBHa > VBHp (about 1.10 on a lateral image), with a lower end-plate inclination of about 10 deg, pedicle height about 17 mm and width about 11.5 mm. The canal widens about 3 mm and end-plate width dips against L4. Table 1; p. 139, 142 (PDF 3, 6). Inference: these are the traits of the last of five lumbar vertebrae in spines with no reported transitional segment; with a lumbosacral transitional vertebra or an L6 they may sit one level off, so decode them with the sequence and a sacrum check, not alone.
6. Do not use canal depth to tell levels apart: it stays between 10.3 and 12.5 mm from C3 to L5. Do not use canal width from T3 to T10 (13.5 to 14.2 mm). Table 1; p. 142 (PDF 6).
7. Do not use end-plate width to order T1 to T6: it falls from T1 to T4 or T5 and only then rises. Use end-plate depth there, which rises nearly linearly from T1 to T12. p. 139 (PDF 3); Table 1.
8. In the cervical spine, C3 to C5 bodies are fairly constant (p. 139, PDF 3). Order them by spinous process length (25.6, 30.3, 33.6) and transverse process width (41.4, 44.9, 47.6), each rising about 3 to 5 mm per level, and by the sign change of the pedicle's sagittal tilt between C4 and C5 (Table 1; p. 143, PDF 7). Inference: the between-specimen SD (about 1.3 to 3 mm for SPL and TPW, and 2 to 3.5 deg for the tilt) is close to the step, so use them jointly and with the sequence, never one level alone.
9. Inference: do not use the sense of the pedicle's axial angle at T8 to T12 as a level marker until it is confirmed on CT. The abrupt reversal at T7/T8 in this paper may be a convention error (see Gaps). p. 143, 145 (PDF 7, 9); Fig. 11.
10. Read "pedicle length" in this paper as the screw-path chord from the facet cortex to the anterior body cortex along the pedicle axis, never as the short pedicle. p. 145 (PDF 9).
11. Inference: as a check on mask-derived end-plate planes, verify that the lower minus upper end-plate inclination roughly equals the sagittal wedge from the heights, atan((VBHa - VBHp)/EPD). In this paper's table they agree within about 2 deg from T5 to L5. Computed from Table 1.
12. Inference: when estimating areas from two radiographs, use end-plate area of about 0.85 to 0.9 x AP width x lateral depth, and canal area of about 0.7 to 0.9 (0.78 typical) x SCW x SCD. Never apply this to the cervical lower end-plate areas of this paper, which exceed width x depth. Computed from Table 1.
13. Inference: work in ratios on radiographs (TPW/EPW, SCW/EPW, VBHa/VBHp, SCD/EPD) because the paper gives dry-bone values and no magnification.
14. For a Chinese patient compared with a Panjabi-based prior, expect smaller values: body widths about 10% smaller, depths about 15%, pedicle heights about 16%, pedicle widths about 26% and transverse processes about 16%; canal width about 20% and canal depth about 32% (p. 144, PDF 8; Table 2, pp. 142-143, PDF 6-7). Inference: treat the canal depth figure with caution (definition), and remember the cervical lower end-plate areas and some cervical spinous processes are larger, not smaller.
15. Flag for the surgeon any T3 to T9 pedicle with a width under 5 mm, the usual finding in this population (T3 to T9 means 4.0 to 4.95 mm). p. 144 (PDF 8); Table 1.
16. Measure the cervical upper end-plate width between the bases of the uncinate processes, not across them, as Fig. 1a (Panjabi's drawing) shows; measure the lower width across the whole body. p. 138 (PDF 2), Fig. 1a.
17. Inference, from Fig. 1c: take spinous process length from the upper end-plate centroid (the frame origin) to the spinous tip, so that thoracic and lumbar values near 48 to 58 mm are reproduced; a free-process length will be far shorter. p. 138 (PDF 2); Table 1.
18. Do not expect anomalies from this paper: it reports none and gives no numbering method, so take every transitional prior from another source. p. 137 (PDF 1).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper reports no transitional or anomalous segment and no level-identification method. 220 vertebrae = 10 cadavers x 22 levels (C3 to L5), so no L6, T13, missing segment or block vertebra appears in the sample, but whether any was screened out is unknown. p. 137 (PDF 1).

(b) Measures the generator holds or could add, and definition differences:
- Held and directly given here (C3 to L5, mean +/- SE, n = 10): EPWu, EPWl, EPDu, EPDl, VBHa, VBHp, SCW, SCD, PDH (left, right), PDW (left, right). Table 1.
- Could be added from this paper: SCA, PDA, EPA (upper, lower), TPW, SPL, PL (T1 to L5, screw-path chord), EPIt (upper, lower) and PDIs and PDIt angles.
- TPW and PL are the strongest junction separators here; the paper's TPW at T12 and L1 alone separates the two by about 3.6 pooled SDs (computed, SD = 3.16 x SE).
- Facet dimensions and angles: not measured in this paper.
- Definition differences that would bias a mask-extracted value:
  - The frame is built on the posterior wall with the origin at the upper end-plate centroid, as Panjabi's is described. A mask pipeline in scanner axes would mix sagittal alignment into depths and heights.
  - PDIs here is the axial-plane medial angle, printed with opposite signs for left and right. Use magnitudes.
  - PL is the facet-to-anterior-cortex chord (McLain/Zindrick), not a short pedicle length.
  - SPL's anterior landmark is not given in the text; Fig. 1c draws it at the upper end-plate centroid (Inference from the drawing). A mask SPL measured as the free process would be biased far low.
  - Cervical EPWu is drawn between the uncinate processes (Fig. 1a); a mask extent across the cranial body surface would include them and be biased high at C3 to C7.
  - The cervical EPAl values exceed EPWl x EPDl (ratio 1.00 to 1.19), which a planar outline bounded by those extents cannot do; do not fit a mask's planar area to them.
  - SCD and SCA are about 30 to 40% below Panjabi. Inference: part of this is probably landmark or method, so do not copy the population ratio for canal depth into the generator without a second source.
  - L3 EPAl is a misprint (printed 172.0; Inference: about 1,172, a dropped leading digit); L4 PDHr is printed without its +/- sign (13.4, SE 0.3).

(c) Measurement error to use:
- Instrument calibration: about 1.3% of a 20 mm length (about 0.26 mm), 0.68% of an angle, 1.7% of an area. p. 138 (PDF 2).
- Between-specimen SD: take SD = 3.16 x SE for this sample (n = 10 per level), with the caveat that the paper's own definition would give 10 x SE. p. 139 (PDF 3).
- Computed median CVs under the standard reading: EPW 3%, EPD 4 to 5%, VBH 4.5 (posterior) to 6% (anterior), SCW 7%, SCD 6%, PDH 7 to 9%, PDW 12 to 13%, PL 6%, SPL 5.5%, TPW 6%, EPA 9.5%, SCA 9.5%, PDA 14%. Angle SDs are about 1.3 to 1.7 deg (end-plate) and 2.4 to 4.1 deg (pedicle).
- Observer reliability: none reported.

(d) Size, sex and population covariates:
- Population: Chinese Singaporean against Panjabi's white specimens. Multiplicative factors, computed as 1 + regional mean difference / 100 from Table 2 (C3 to C7 / T1 to T12 / L1 to L5):

  - SCW 0.81 / 0.79 / 0.82.
  - SCD 0.62 / 0.73 / 0.62.
  - PDH 0.87 / 0.82 / 0.86.
  - PDW 0.85 / 0.72 / 0.68.

  - SCA 0.64 / 0.73 / 0.60.
- PL against Zindrick: 0.78 (mean -22.1%). PDIs against Panjabi: much smaller in the thoracic spine (T4 to T7 about half).
- Inference: the canal and pedicle factors are far below the body-size factors (0.83 to 0.91). One patient size factor cannot reproduce them, so a population term would have to be measure-specific. These factors also mix in age (mean 65.7), unreported sex, unreported Panjabi demographics and a different instrument.

## Gaps and conflicts

- The abstract says the study covers C3 to T12 (p. 137, PDF 1), while the title, methods and tables cover C3 to L5. The introduction says the purpose is to report the thoracic vertebrae (p. 137, PDF 1).
- Sex of the 10 cadavers is never given; neither are Panjabi's or Zindrick's demographics. Only McLain's (11 women, 7 men, 62 to 85 years) are given (p. 145, PDF 9).
- How levels were identified and counted, and whether any specimen had a transitional or numerical variant, is not stated.
- The SE is defined as the SD divided by the sample size (p. 139, PDF 3), which is not the standard error.
- "Sodium hydrochloride" (p. 137, PDF 1): Inference, sodium hypochlorite.
- The text says the smallest SCW, 10.3 mm, is at C3 to C6 (p. 142, PDF 6). In Table 1 that is SCD; SCW there is 19.2 to 20.6.
- The text says the upper end-plate area is always larger than the lower in the cervical spine (p. 143, PDF 7). Table 1 has the lower larger at every level, as the text itself says for the thoracic spine.
- Table 1 L3 EPAl printed 172.0 +/- 20.6 (p. 141, PDF 5). Fig. 8 draws L3 between L2 (1,090.9) and L4 (1,199.3), and Table 2's -9.2% at L3 is in line with its neighbours, so the true value lies near 1,100 to 1,200; Inference: 1,172.0 with a dropped leading digit is the likeliest reading.
- Table 1 L4 PDHr printed "13.40.3" (p. 141, PDF 5).
- SCW at L5: 23.5 in the text (p. 142, PDF 6), 23.4 in Table 1.
- "TPL" in the text (pp. 139, 142) is TPW in the table and figure.
- The 5 mm screw statement names T1 and T10 to L5 as the only wide-enough levels (p. 144, PDF 8). Table 1 also gives C6 (5.1/5.4), C7 (5.6/5.7) and T2 (6.1/5.8) above 5 mm.
- The maximum upper-thoracic pedicle length is given as 30.4 mm (p. 145, PDF 9); Table 1 has T5 at 31.2.
- The greatest pedicle-length differences are placed at T6 (-25.7% McLain, 28% Zindrick, p. 145, PDF 9). Table 4 has T4 larger for both (-26.2 and -28.3).
- A sentence on pedicle length begins "In the cervical spine" (p. 145, PDF 9), but no cervical pedicle length was measured; McLain covered only the upper thoracic spine.
- The conclusion places the pedicle divergence in the "upper thoracic spine" (p. 145, PDF 9); the results place it at T8 to T12 (p. 143, PDF 7).
- The words "divergence" and "convergence" are used inconsistently: "divergence" for the cervical angle of about 40 deg, for T1, for the reversed T8 to T12 angle and for the lumbar angles, "converge" for the fall from C6 and through T1 to T7 (p. 143, PDF 7).
- Fig. 11 and Table 3 use the right side only (Inference from arithmetic), while the text quotes left-right means.
- The text says Zindrick also found a divergence at T4 (pp. 144-145, PDF 8-9); Table 3's -28.6% at T4 implies, and Fig. 11 draws, Zindrick at about 12.6 deg there, not reversed (computed).
- Inference: the PDIs reversal is abrupt and uniform (+8.6 at T7 to -7.6 at T8 on the right; -5.2 to +9.6 on the left), and both sides flip back together at L1. With the sign flipped, T8 to T12 would read 3.2 to 9.6 deg on the left and 5.3 to 7.6 on the right and join T7 and L1 smoothly. That suggests a handedness or sign error for those five levels rather than anatomy. Against that, Table 3's +26.2% at T12 against Zindrick implies Zindrick's own T12 value was also slightly negative (about -4 deg, computed), so a small reversed angle at T12 may be real. Check against a CT series.
- Table 2's printed Average for SPL is -5.5%, but the 22 printed level values average -5.0%; EPWl prints -10.4 against -10.3 computed.
- The lumbar lower end-plate inclination is said to increase caudally from L1 to L2 (p. 143, PDF 7); the table goes from -2.6 to -1.1, a smaller caudal tilt.
- The text says posterior body height exceeds anterior except at L5 (p. 139, PDF 3); at L4 they are equal (21.6 each).
- The cervical lower end-plate area exceeds the bounding product EPWl x EPDl (computed ratio 1.00 to 1.19 at C3 to C7). That is impossible for a planar outline whose maximum width and depth are EPWl and EPDl, so either the width or depth is not the maximum extent, or the area was taken on another outline or a curved surface (Inference).
- The anterior landmark for SPL and the section position for PDH and PDW are not stated in the text, only drawn in Fig. 1 (from Panjabi); the SPL line in Fig. 1c ends at the upper end-plate centroid.
- The abstract says all the dimensions were smaller than in the Caucasian data (p. 137, PDF 1), but Table 2 has positive differences: EPAl +9.1 to +21.2% at C3 to C7, SPL +17.8 and +18.4% at C5 and C6 and small positives at C7, T7 and T10 to T12, EPWu +0.8% at T1, VBHp +3.7% at C6, +1.2% at T5 and +0.9% at T12, EPAu +2.5% at C5, +1.6% at T1 and +4.4% at T2, TPW +2.6% at C5 (pp. 142-143, PDF 6-7). The discussion itself notes the larger cervical EPAl (p. 144, PDF 8).
- The paper never gives the number of observers, or on which side of the specimen the digitised points b and a lie, so which side the X axis points to is unknown; the left and right labels cannot be tied to the frame's axes from the text (p. 139, PDF 3).
- Inference, computed: Panjabi's thoracic SCA minimum implied by Table 2 is at T2 (about 176) rather than T3 (about 180) as the paper says, a near tie that rounding may explain.
- Inference, from memory, to verify against the Panjabi notes in this set: Panjabi's series were about a dozen white cadaveric spines per region, younger on average. If so, part of the "ethnic" difference is age, sex and stature, which this paper cannot separate.
