## What the paper is

- Design: an anatomic-radiologic study of dry bone. Its stated aim is to define pedicle size and shape in the coaxial projection (the view along the pedicle) of L1 to S1, and to define how the radiographic pedicle image relates to the true cortical bone, as guidance for fluoroscopy-assisted pedicle screw placement. p. 709 (PDF 1).
- Population: 10 dry-bone lumbosacral spine specimens, giving 100 lumbar pedicles and 20 S1 pedicles. p. 709 (PDF 1). Inference (arithmetic): 10 spines x 5 lumbar vertebrae x 2 sides, and 10 sacra x 2 sides. No age, sex, ethnicity, stature, source collection or pathology of the specimens is given anywhere. The authors work in Auckland, New Zealand (p. 709, PDF 1); the specimens' origin is not stated.
- Specimen handling: the abstract says lumbar vertebrae and sacra were marked (p. 709, PDF 1), and every figure shows a single vertebra or a sacrum on its own (Figures 1 to 4, p. 710, PDF 2; Figure 8, p. 712, PDF 4). Inference: the spines were disarticulated and each bone imaged alone, so no image has neighbouring vertebrae, ribs or pelvis superimposed, unlike a clinical film.
- Marking: the pedicle isthmus, which the authors define as its "narrowest circumference" (p. 709, PDF 1), was encircled at each lumbar pedicle with 0.4 mm tinned copper wire drawn tight so that wire and cortex touched at every point (text p. 709, PDF 1; Figure 1, p. 710, PDF 2). Initial films showed the fine wire did not show the S1 pedicle border, so S1 was ringed with 0.8 mm enamelled copper wire (text p. 709, PDF 1; Figure 2, p. 710, PDF 2).
- Imaging: plain radiographs only. Each vertebra was mounted on plate glass with malleable adhesive so it could be placed at any angle in the beam (p. 709, PDF 1); the opaque blob over the spinous process in the figures is this adhesive (Figure 3 caption, p. 710, PDF 2). A direct posteroanterior (PA) film was taken first (Figure 3, an L4 with the wire on), then the vertebra was rotated about its own longitudinal (craniocaudal) axis, with repeated films, until the pedicle image was seen at its maximum transverse dimension with the whole pedicle superimposed within the vertebral body: this is the coaxial image (Figure 4, p. 710, PDF 2; definition p. 709, PDF 1). Both pedicles of each vertebra were imaged coaxially in turn (p. 709, PDF 1). No CT, MRI or lateral radiograph was used, although the title says "radiologic"; the introduction cites CT-based pedicle studies (its references 2 and 7) but this study makes no CT measurement (p. 709, PDF 1). The PA films were not measured; only the coaxial films were.
- Measurements: taken directly from the coaxial films and corrected to a known standard dimension to remove magnification (p. 710, PDF 2). Results are given as mean and standard deviation per level and side (Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3); the n per cell is not printed (Inference: 10 per cell).
- Levels: L1 to L5 measured; S1 described qualitatively only, with no dimensions. No thoracic level was studied.
- Level identification and counting: not described. Transitional segments are not mentioned, neither as an inclusion nor an exclusion criterion. Inference: the count of 100 lumbar pedicles means five lumbar vertebrae were analysed per spine, so either all ten spines had five lumbar vertebrae or extra segments were silently left out.
- No inclusion or exclusion criteria are stated.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts or block vertebrae, and gives no prevalence of any anomaly. How the bones were assigned to levels is not stated. The anatomical variants it does report, none with a count: cortical defects at vascular foramina, which stopped the wire from lying flat on bone, most evident along the superior border of the L5 pedicle (p. 710, PDF 2); a transverse process whose craniocaudal direction varies, so that on some occasions the midpoint of its base did not match the pedicle's craniocaudal centre (p. 712, PDF 4; Figure 7); a wide spread of L4 ellipse tilt (SD 17.1 and 20.8 deg, Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3); and, citing the first author's earlier work (their reference 36), anatomic variation that makes L5 entry-point landmarks inaccurate (p. 713, PDF 5). Inference: the shape contrasts it describes (an oblique elliptical L5 pedicle with a distinct lateral border, against a triangular S1 pedicle whose lateral side merges into the ala) are the morphology a transitional L5 or S1 would blur, but the paper did not study that.

## Measures, each as a recipe

Common frame for all four lumbar measures (p. 710, PDF 2): the pedicle is viewed perpendicular to the vertebral body's longitudinal axis, that is, the beam lies in the vertebra's axial plane and the image plane is parallel to the body's long axis; the authors say this excludes the effect of lumbar lordosis (stated with the height definition, p. 710, PDF 2). The vertebra is turned about its long axis only, until the pedicle looks widest (p. 709, PDF 1). Inference: the beam therefore follows the pedicle's transverse (axial-plane) angle but not its sagittal angle, so the "coaxial" view here is coaxial in the axial plane only; and "vertical" on these films is the body's long axis. The rotation reached for each pedicle (in effect its transverse angle) was not recorded.

### Pedicle height (coaxial)

- Definition: the distance between the upper and lower extent of the pedicle cortex, taken at the inner margin of the encircling wire (that is, at the outer bone surface), on the coaxial view, measured along the vertebral body's long axis. p. 710 (PDF 2).
- Instrument: direct measurement on plain film, magnification corrected to a known standard. Levels L1 to L5.
- Values (Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3), mean (SD) mm, left / right: L1 18.6 (3.2) / 19.9 (3.9); L2 18.6 (3.0) / 18.7 (3.2); L3 18.6 (13.0, see Gaps) / 18.7 (2.5); L4 17.8 (3.0) / 17.4 (2.5); L5 17.6 (2.9) / 19.5 (3.3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: set the vertebral frame (craniocaudal axis normal to the end plates); find each pedicle in the arch between the posterior body wall and the pars; find its isthmus, the cross-section of least perimeter or area along the pedicle's axial-plane trajectory; project that isthmus outline along the trajectory direction (kept in the axial plane, no sagittal tilt) onto the plane perpendicular to it; height is the extent of that outline along the craniocaudal axis. The mask is the outer bone surface, which matches the wire-defined outer cortex used here. SPINEPS gives no separate pedicle label as far as I recall (Inference from memory); the pedicle has to be cut out of the arch label.
- From a radiograph alone: on a coaxial (en face, rotated AP) film or fluoroscopy shot, the vertical extent of the pedicle ring, corrected for magnification. The dense ring seen on film is the inner cortex, 1 to 3 mm inside the true outer border (p. 711, PDF 3), so a film value taken on the dense ring would read short of these wire values. Inference: by up to about 2 to 6 mm in total over the two borders. The paper does not use a lateral radiograph.
- Across levels: flat. 17.4 to 19.9 mm at every level, no rise to L5, slightly lowest at L4. The authors explain the missing rise at L5 by the obliquity of the L5 ellipse (p. 713, PDF 5). Height separates no adjacent pair of levels.
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported. The authors' left L5 values (17.6 height, 21.6 width) agree with Olsewski's mean male L5 values of 17.4 mm and 21.1 mm, which Olsewski took by direct measurement of cadaveric specimens (p. 713, PDF 5). The authors call their dimensions in general consistent with earlier studies, among them Krag 1988, Misenhimer 1989, Olsewski 1990, Panjabi 1992 and Zindrick 1987 (their references 25, 31, 33, 34, 46; p. 713, PDF 5), but give no numeric comparison except Olsewski's.
- Error: no intra- or inter-observer data. The SD across specimens is 2.5 to 3.9 mm at every cell except the L3 left 13.0. Wire 0.4 mm thick; height read at its inner margin.

### Pedicle width (coaxial)

- Definition: the most medial to most lateral extent of the pedicle outlined by the encircling wire, on the coaxial view. p. 710 (PDF 2). Which margin of the wire is used is not said for width (it is for height).
- Values (Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3), mean (SD) mm, left / right: L1 10.3 (2.5) / 9.5 (2.5); L2 10.3 (3.0) / 10.0 (2.9); L3 13.0 (2.6) / 12.7 (2.9); L4 16.3 (3.7) / 16.5 (3.8); L5 21.6 (3.9) / 21.5 (4.5).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: the same projected isthmus outline as for height; width is its extent along the in-plane axis perpendicular to the craniocaudal axis (a bounding-box width). At L4 and L5 this is NOT the isthmus width that an axial CT cut gives, because the outline is an oblique ellipse (see the isthmus diameter and the bordercall section).
- From a radiograph alone: coaxial film, the transverse extent of the pedicle ring, magnification corrected. At L5 the lateral pedicle border lies on the lateral border of the L5 body image (p. 711, PDF 3), so the lateral end of the width has to be read from the body edge.
- Across levels: rises steadily caudally, which the authors call a dramatic increase from L1 to L5 (p. 711, PDF 3). Steps (mean of the two sides, Inference: my arithmetic): L1 9.9, L2 10.2, L3 12.9, L4 16.4, L5 21.6 mm. L1 and L2 cannot be told apart; L2 to L3 is +2.7 mm, L3 to L4 +3.5 mm and L4 to L5 +5.2 mm, against SDs of 2.5 to 4.5 mm. Inference: one pedicle's width separates L5 from L1 to L3 well, L4 from L5 only moderately (about 1.3 SD), and adjacent upper lumbar levels hardly at all. The width/height ratio goes from about 0.5 at L1 and L2 to about 0.7 at L3, 0.9 at L4 and 1.1 to 1.2 at L5 (Inference: my arithmetic), crossing 1 between L4 and L5.
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported; agrees with Olsewski's male L5 values (p. 713, PDF 5).
- Error: as for height; SD 2.5 to 4.5 mm.

### Pedicle elliptical axis (tilt of the coaxial ellipse)

- Definition: the long axis of the coaxial pedicle image, approximated as an ellipse, given as an angle in degrees from the vertical (the body's long axis). p. 710 (PDF 2); obliquity from the vertical p. 711 (PDF 3). The sign convention is not stated (L1 left is -2.4 deg).
- Values (Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3), mean (SD) deg, left / right: L1 -2.4 (5.3) / 1.7 (3.1); L2 0.8 (5.6) / 4.0 (6.1); L3 3.5 (4.6) / 7.5 (6.6); L4 31.0 (17.1) / 33.5 (20.8); L5 55.0 (8.8) / 50.3 (7.4).
- Direction of the tilt: Figure 5 (p. 711, PDF 3) draws the L4 and L5 ellipses with the upper pole leaning toward the midline line of the drawing on both sides, and the text puts the long boundary of the L5 pedicle on its inferomedial side, next to the L5 root (p. 713, PDF 5; p. 714, PDF 6). Inference: the long axis runs from superomedial to inferolateral, and the two sides mirror each other, so both sides' angles are positive.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: fit an ellipse (second moments) to the projected isthmus outline; the tilt is the angle between its major axis and the craniocaudal axis, signed with medial-upward positive on each side so that left and right mirror.
- From a radiograph alone: coaxial view of each pedicle. Inference: a plain AP film shows the pedicle foreshortened by its medial angulation, so the tilt read on an AP film would differ from these coaxial values, most at L4 and L5; the paper gives no AP values.
- Across levels: about vertical at L1 to L3 (all means within 8 deg), then a jump at L4 (about 31 to 34 deg, with a very wide SD of 17 to 21 deg) and at L5 (50 to 55 deg, SD 7 to 9 deg). The authors' Figure 5 shows the shape change. Inference: this is the strongest single pedicle cue for L5 against L1 to L3 (difference about 50 deg against SDs under 9 deg), and a fair cue for L4 against L3; the L4 SD suggests a mix of near-vertical and oblique L4 pedicles.
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported.
- Error: no reliability data; SDs as above.

### Pedicle isthmus diameter (minor axis of the ellipse)

- Definition: the short axis of the pedicle ellipse at the isthmus; the authors take it as the narrowest diameter a screw can fit within and, citing Banta, the key parameter for screw fit. p. 710 (PDF 2); p. 711 (PDF 3).
- Values (Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3), mean (SD) mm, left / right: L1 9.9 (2.5) / 8.7 (2.4); L2 10.0 (3.0) / 9.6 (2.8); L3 12.3 (2.1) / 12.0 (2.9); L4 13.4 (2.8) / 12.2 (2.1); L5 13.2 (2.4) / 14.0 (2.9).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: the minor axis length of the ellipse fitted to the isthmus cross-section, perpendicular to the pedicle trajectory. The paper does not say whether the minor axis was read from the wire outline or from the radiographic ring; with height and width wire-based, it is most likely the outer cortical (wire) value, so it is an outer, not an endosteal, diameter.
- From a radiograph alone: coaxial view, the short axis of the oblique ellipse, magnification corrected; the visible dense ring is the inner cortex (p. 711, PDF 3).
- Across levels: rises from about 9 to 10 mm at L1 and L2 to about 12 mm at L3, then plateaus at 12 to 14 mm through L4 and L5. Isthmus/width ratio (Inference: my arithmetic) is 0.92 to 0.97 at L1 to L3, 0.74 to 0.82 at L4 and 0.61 to 0.65 at L5. The authors stress that the extra width of the distal lumbar pedicles is not matched by as great a rise in isthmus diameter, because of the ellipse's obliquity (p. 711, PDF 3; Figure 5 caption), and the abstract states that the minor diameter was considerably less than the width at L5 (p. 709, PDF 1). The discussion says the L5 isthmus is larger than at the levels above (p. 713, PDF 5); Table 1 bears that out on the right (14.0 against 12.2 at L4) but not on the left (13.2 against 13.4 at L4).
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported.
- Error: none reported beyond the SDs (2.1 to 3.0 mm).

### Internal consistency of Table 1 (Inference: my geometry)

Treating each coaxial outline as an ellipse with minor axis m, tilt t and bounding width W, the major axis follows from W, m and t, and the predicted bounding height can be checked against the printed height. At L5 the check holds within 0.2 mm (left: major axis about 24.7 mm, predicted height 17.8 against 17.6 printed; right: about 25.4 mm, predicted 19.5 against 19.5). At L4 it does not (predicted about 20.5 mm against 17.4 to 17.8 printed), as expected when mean tilts are averaged over a mixed set of shapes (SD 17 to 21 deg). The same L5 ellipse cut horizontally through its centre (what an axial CT slice at mid-pedicle height measures) gives about 18.3 mm, and cut vertically through its centre (a sagittal height) about 15 to 16.5 mm.

### Radiographic-cortical relation (qualitative measure)

- L1 to L4: in every case the cortical image of the pedicle lay wholly inside the wire. p. 711 (PDF 3).
- L5: superior, medial and inferior borders seen; the lateral border lay on the image of the lateral border of the L5 body. p. 711 (PDF 3).
- The densest part of the pedicle image lay 1 to 3 mm inside the wire image, so the authors conclude that the inner cortex, not the outer, is what the film shows (Figure 6, p. 712, PDF 4). At times the wire made a "halo" around the pedicle image even though the wire was confirmed in contact with bone. p. 711 (PDF 3); p. 713 (PDF 5). The abstract says the image lay inside the true cortex "by up to 3 mm" (p. 709, PDF 1).
- No image misrepresented the true bony contour of the lumbosacral pedicles. p. 713 (PDF 5).

### S1 pedicle (qualitative)

- Shape: unlike the lumbar pedicles, the lateral side of S1 merges with the sacral ala. Seen from above and on axial projection the medial border is lumbar-like, the lateral aspect joins the ala. p. 712 (PDF 4).
- Projection: with the wire on, the lateral border showed only with marked rotation of the sacrum out of the PA plane, judged clinically inappropriate; so only 10 to 15 deg of obliquity was used to "widen" the S1 pedicle image (Figure 8, p. 712, PDF 4).
- Image: each S1 pedicle looked triangular, with superior, medial and lateral boundaries. Superior and medial cortices were well seen, the lateral cortex was not. The superior cortical image lay close against the wire. The medial cortical image lay lateral to the wire, inside the pedicle, in all cases, often by 3 to 4 mm, and the gap grew toward the caudal end of the pedicle (Figure 9, p. 712-713, PDF 4-5). That medial image is a "trabecular line" which sweeps laterally and caudally to the sacroiliac joint (p. 712, PDF 4; Figure 9 caption, p. 713, PDF 5).
- Inferior limit: no inferior curvature marks the inferior angle of the S1 pedicle, but the S1 foramen is clearly seen and its upper margin defines the inferior extent of the pedicle. p. 712 (PDF 4).
- Lateral limit: not seen with modest rotation. The S1-S2 intervertebral foramen lay medial to the lateral border of the S1 pedicle in every case and is offered as the landmark for that border. p. 712-713 (PDF 4-5); Figure 9 caption p. 713 (PDF 5); p. 714 (PDF 6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: the S1 pedicle is the bone between the S1 body, the S1 superior articular process and the ala, above the first dorsal sacral foramen; it has no free lateral cortex, so a width measured like the lumbar width has no defined lateral end.

### Direct PA view (qualitative, not measured)

- Figure 3 (p. 710, PDF 2) is the direct PA film of an L4 with the wire on: both pedicles show as wire-ringed ovals at the upper lateral corners of the body image, with the adhesive over the spinous process. No dimension was measured on PA films, and the paper does not compare PA with coaxial pedicle size.
- What changes from PA to coaxial (Figure 4 caption, p. 710, PDF 2): the imaged pedicle widens to its widest projection, the spinous process turns away from it, and the opposite pedicle narrows. Inference: on a clinical AP film or fluoroscopy shot, an off-centre spinous process with one wide and one narrow pedicle means the beam is closer to coaxial with the wide pedicle, which is a cue to vertebral rotation relative to the beam.
- The transverse process base usually looks the same on the PA and the coaxial film (p. 712, PDF 4), so it is one landmark that does not move with the rotation.

### Transverse process base as a landmark

- The base of the transverse process where it meets the pedicle usually looks the same on AP and coaxial films. Its craniocaudal direction varies, and in some specimens the midpoint of its base did not match the pedicle's craniocaudal centre (Figure 7: base at the lower part of the pedicle, so a screw aimed at the TP midpoint would sit low). p. 712 (PDF 4); p. 714 (PDF 6), citing Ebraheim.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1: coaxial ellipse upright (tilt -2.4 / 1.7 deg), width 10.3 / 9.5 mm, isthmus 9.9 / 8.7 mm, height 18.6 / 19.9 mm (Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3). The narrowest isthmus of the series (right 8.7 mm).
- L2: practically the same as L1 (width 10.3 / 10.0, isthmus 10.0 / 9.6, tilt 0.8 / 4.0) (Table 1).
- L3: still upright (3.5 / 7.5 deg), width up to 13.0 / 12.7, isthmus 12.3 / 12.0 (Table 1).
- L4: the change begins; tilt 31.0 / 33.5 deg with very wide spread (SD 17.1 / 20.8), width 16.3 / 16.5, isthmus 13.4 / 12.2 (Table 1; p. 711, PDF 3).
- L5: the widest pedicle (21.6 / 21.5 mm) but an oblique ellipse (55.0 / 50.3 deg), so the isthmus is only 13.2 / 14.0 mm, little more than L3 and L4 (Table 1). Height does not rise (p. 713, PDF 5). Its lateral border projects onto the lateral border of the L5 body (p. 711, PDF 3). Its long inferomedial wall lies against the L5 nerve root (p. 713, PDF 5; p. 714, PDF 6). Vascular-foramen defects are most marked along its superior border (p. 710, PDF 2). The authors observe, without data, that misplaced screws are often reported at L5, which surprises them since the accepted L5 pedicle dimensions are the largest in the thoracic and lumbar spine (p. 713, PDF 5). Their summary: the L5 pedicle is "less capacious" than published measures suggest (p. 714, PDF 6; Key Points).
- L5/S1 junction: L5 is an oblique ellipse with a lateral border on the body edge; S1 is a triangle whose lateral side merges into the ala and is never seen coaxially (p. 713, PDF 5). Since the L5 and S1 pedicles run obliquely, on coaxial views the pedicle's lateral border is sometimes missing at L5 and is never seen at S1 (p. 713, PDF 5).
- S1: superior border true to the cortex; medial radiographic border a trabecular line 3 to 4 mm inside the true border, more so caudally; lower limit the upper margin of the S1 foramen; lateral limit lateral to the S1-S2 foramen (p. 712-714, PDF 4-6).
- Thoracic and T12/L1: not studied. The authors state, as the received view, that the coaxial pedicle is an ellipse with an upright long axis through most of the thoracic and lumbar spine (p. 711, PDF 3); their own data cover L1 to L5 only.
- C2/C3, C7/T1: not covered.

## Measurement error and reliability

- No intra- or inter-observer reliability, no repeat measurement, no statistical test of left against right or level against level.
- Magnification corrected to a known standard dimension (p. 710, PDF 2); the correction's accuracy is not given.
- Wire: 0.4 mm (lumbar), 0.8 mm (S1); height read at the wire's inner margin (p. 709-710, PDF 1-2). Wire contact failed where vascular foramina left cortical defects, most along the superior L5 border (p. 710, PDF 2).
- The coaxial position was found by repeated films until the pedicle looked widest (p. 709, PDF 1); the rotation angle reached is not reported.
- The film shows the inner cortex 1 to 3 mm inside the outer surface at L1 to L5 (p. 711, PDF 3) and 3 to 4 mm inside at the medial S1 border (p. 712, PDF 4): a systematic offset for anyone measuring on the visible ring.
- Between-specimen SD: 2.1 to 4.5 mm for linear measures (13.0 at L3 left height, probably a misprint), 3.1 to 20.8 deg for the tilt (Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3).
- Figure 5 (p. 711, PDF 3) is a drawn representation of size and shape with a 1 cm scale bar, not a traced image; it shows the mean shapes only.
- Sides: the figures name left and right as viewed by the reader (Figure 3 caption, p. 710, PDF 2; Figure 4 caption); Table 1 does not say whether its left and right are the reader's or the specimen's.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication: fluoroscopy-assisted lumbar and S1 pedicle screw placement; freehand placement risks misplacement, and routine PA and lateral films are unreliable for judging screw position (p. 709, PDF 1, citing others). Coaxial fluoroscopy is accurate in cadavers when the pedicle is large enough for a screw (p. 709, PDF 1, citing Steinmann).
- Pearl: the coaxial pedicle image is a reliable, safe guide to the true lumbar pedicle; a screw kept within the radiographic pedicle image has a margin of safety against wall breach and neural injury, because the image lies inside the real cortex (p. 713, PDF 5; Key Points p. 714, PDF 6).
- Pearl: the coaxial pedicle image sits within the vertebral body image, below the superior end plate and medial to the lateral body wall; at the coaxial angle the spinous process turns away from the imaged pedicle and the opposite pedicle looks narrow (Figure 4 caption, p. 710, PDF 2).
- Pitfall: at L5 the pedicle is wide but oblique, so its effective screw diameter (isthmus, about 13 to 14 mm) is much less than its mediolateral width (about 21.5 mm) (p. 711, PDF 3; Figure 5 caption; p. 713-714, PDF 5-6).
- Pitfall: the long inferomedial wall of the L5 pedicle lies against the L5 root, which is at risk from a breach of that wall (p. 713, PDF 5; p. 714, PDF 6).
- Pitfall: reasons the authors give for frequent L5 misplacement: the oblique elliptical shape not being recognised, a smaller effective internal diameter than expected, the greater obliquity needed to reach the L5 pedicle axis (more soft-tissue retraction), and anatomical variation making entry-point landmarks inaccurate (p. 713, PDF 5).
- Pitfall: the transverse process base midpoint is not a reliable guide to the pedicle's vertical midpoint (p. 712, PDF 4; p. 714, PDF 6).
- Landmark: at L5 the lateral border of the body image guides the lateral pedicle border (p. 714, PDF 6).
- Landmark: at S1 the medial radiographic border is a trabecular line, not the cortex, but it always lies inside the pedicle and so is a safe medial guide; the lateral pedicle border is always lateral to the S1 foramen, which is a safe lateral guide; the upper margin of the S1 foramen is the inferior pedicle limit (p. 712-714, PDF 4-6).
- Pitfall: marked sacral rotation to show the S1 lateral border is not practical clinically; 10 to 15 deg is (p. 712, PDF 4).
- S1 in summary: the S1 pedicle image matches its cortical borders less well than the lumbar ones, only the superior and medial borders being seen, but other reliable landmarks exist (abstract, p. 709, PDF 1); the S1 pedicle is placed radiographically by its cortical images together with those landmarks (Key Points, p. 714, PDF 6).
- Context the authors give: concern over misplaced screws and over lost fixation when the pedicle wall is broken; electrophysiological monitoring and computer-assisted guidance are alternatives, the latter needing special equipment at extra cost (p. 709, PDF 1, citing others).
- Halo: the wire, though touching bone, often formed a halo around the pedicle image; the authors suggest the denser inner wall shows, separated from the marker by a less dense outer wall, as others have seen (p. 713, PDF 5).
- No stenosis thresholds, syndromes (Marfan, achondroplasia) or screw diameters are given.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. On a coaxial (en face, rotated AP) view of a lumbar vertebra, find each pedicle as an ellipse inside the vertebral body image, below the superior end plate and medial to the lateral body wall; expect the spinous process to have turned away from the pedicle in view (Figure 4, p. 710, PDF 2).
2. Read the dense pedicle ring as the inner cortex: place the true outer bone border 1 to 3 mm outside it before comparing with any outer-cortex (CT mask or caliper) value (p. 711, PDF 3).
3. Use the tilt of the coaxial pedicle ellipse as a level cue: near vertical (within about 8 deg) at L1 to L3, about 30 deg at L4 with wide spread, about 50 to 55 deg at L5, upper pole leaning medially (Table 1 and Figure 5, p. 711, PDF 3). A strongly oblique, wide pedicle points to L5; do not use tilt to separate L1, L2 and L3.
4. Use coaxial pedicle width as an ordering cue, rising about 10, 10, 13, 16, 21.5 mm from L1 to L5; L1 and L2 have the same width, so never split them by width (Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3).
5. Do not use pedicle height to tell lumbar levels apart; it is 17.4 to 19.9 mm at every level (Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3).
6. At L4 and L5, report the ellipse's minor axis (isthmus diameter) separately from the bounding width; at L5 the minor axis is about 13 to 14 mm against a width of about 21.5 mm (Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3).
7. At L5, take the lateral pedicle border from the lateral border of the L5 body image, since the two coincide on the coaxial view (p. 711, PDF 3; p. 714, PDF 6).
8. Expect cortical gaps at vascular foramina along the superior border of the L5 pedicle; Inference: do not read them as a fracture or a mask error (p. 710, PDF 2).
9. Never locate the craniocaudal centre of a pedicle from the midpoint of the transverse process base; it may sit at the lower part of the pedicle (Figure 7, p. 712, PDF 4; p. 714, PDF 6).
10. Recognise S1 by a triangular pedicle image with clear superior and medial borders and no lateral cortex, the lateral side merging into the ala, on a view rotated only 10 to 15 deg from PA (p. 712, PDF 4; Figure 8).
11. At S1, put the true medial pedicle wall medial to the visible medial line (a trabecular line running laterally and caudally to the SI joint), always on that side and often by 3 to 4 mm, more so caudally; take the inferior limit at the upper margin of the first sacral foramen and the lateral wall lateral to that foramen (p. 712-713, PDF 4-5; Figure 9).

14. Correct every radiographic length for magnification against a known dimension before comparing it with these values (p. 710, PDF 2).
15. Do not lean on L4 ellipse tilt alone: its spread (SD 17 to 21 deg) means a single L4 can look upright like L3 or oblique like L5; combine it with width and the neighbouring levels (Table 1, p. 711, PDF 3).

17. Do not compare these values with measures from a plain AP or lateral film: every number here is from the coaxial view, and the paper reports no AP, lateral or CT dimension (p. 710, PDF 2).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of any transitional or numbering anomaly, and its denominator (10 spines with 5 lumbar vertebrae analysed each) carries no anomaly information.

(b) Measures and definition differences:
- The generator's pedicle width and pedicle height (Panjabi) correspond only loosely to Robertson's. Inference from memory, to be checked against the Panjabi notes: Panjabi measures on the anatomical planes (width in the transverse plane at the isthmus, height in the sagittal plane). The authors themselves call their dimensions consistent with Panjabi 1992 and Zindrick 1987 among others, without a numeric comparison (p. 713, PDF 5). Robertson measures the bounding extent of the isthmus outline seen along the pedicle in the axial plane. At L1 to L3, where the outline is upright, they should be close. At L4 and L5, where it is tilted 30 to 55 deg, they differ. Inference (my geometry on Table 1): at L5, Robertson's bounding width (about 21.5 mm) exceeds a horizontal chord through the ellipse centre (about 18.3 mm, which is what an axial CT slice would give) by about 3 mm, and the minor axis (about 13.5 mm) is about 8 mm less than the bounding width; a sagittal chord through the centre (about 15 to 16.5 mm) is 2 to 3 mm less than the bounding height.
- A mask-derived pedicle width must therefore state which of four quantities it is: bounding width in the coaxial projection (Robertson's width), horizontal chord at mid-height (Panjabi-like), minor axis of the fitted ellipse (Robertson's isthmus diameter), or the narrowest width along the pedicle in an axial slice. Mixing them would bias L4 and L5 most, by 3 to 8 mm.
- Candidate new generator measures: pedicle ellipse tilt (strong L5 and L4 marker, near zero at L1 to L3) and isthmus minor axis; both computable from a mask. Their per-level means and SDs are in Table 1 (p. 711, PDF 3). No covariance between measures is given.
- A CT mask is the outer bone surface, which matches the wire definitions here; radiographic measurements on the visible ring are smaller by 1 to 3 mm per border at lumbar levels (p. 711, PDF 3) and 3 to 4 mm at the medial S1 border (p. 712, PDF 4).
- S1: no dimensions; the S1 pedicle has no lateral cortex, so a lumbar-style width is undefined at S1 (p. 712, PDF 4).

(c) Measurement error: no reliability data. Use the between-specimen SDs of Table 1 as the spread (2.1 to 4.5 mm linear; tilt 3 to 7 deg at L1 to L3, 17 to 21 deg at L4, 7 to 9 deg at L5), and for a radiograph-based value add a systematic inward offset of 1 to 3 mm per border (lumbar) and 3 to 4 mm at the S1 medial border.

(d) Covariates: none. No sex, age, stature or ethnicity of the 10 specimens is given. The only external comparison is to Olsewski's male L5 height and width (17.4 and 21.1 mm) (p. 713, PDF 5). The introduction notes that other studies took age and racial variation into account (p. 709, PDF 1), without data.

## Gaps and conflicts

- No demographics of the 10 specimens, no source collection, no pathology screen; n per cell not printed.
- No CT, no lateral film, no AP-film dimensions: only the coaxial view is quantified, although the abstract and title speak of radiologic anatomy generally.
- Level identification and transitional segments are never discussed.
- Table 1, L3 left height SD 13.0 mm is out of line with every other SD (2.5 to 3.9 mm); Inference: probably a misprint for 3.0.
- The tilt's sign convention is not stated; Figure 5 shows mirror-image tilts on the two sides while Table 1 gives positive values on both, so the angle is side-relative (Inference).
- Height is defined at the wire's inner margin, width only "as defined by" the wire, and the isthmus diameter does not say whether it was read from the wire or the radiographic ring.
- The text on p. 711 (PDF 3) gives the L4 and L5 axes as 31.0 and 55.0 deg, both left-side values (the right are 33.5 and 50.3, Table 1).
- The discussion says the L5 isthmus is larger than at the levels above (p. 713, PDF 5); on the left side Table 1 has L5 13.2 mm below L4 13.4 mm (p. 711, PDF 3). The statement holds for the right side and for the mean of both sides.
- The results say the L5 lateral border lay on the image of the L5 body's lateral border (p. 711, PDF 3), the discussion that the L5 lateral border is occasionally not seen coaxially (p. 713, PDF 5); no count of the L5 pedicles whose lateral border was unseen is given.
- Table 1 does not say whether left and right are the specimen's sides or the reader's, as the figures use (Figure 3 caption, p. 710, PDF 2).
- The rotation needed to reach each coaxial view, which is in effect the pedicle's transverse angle, was not recorded, so the paper gives no pedicle angle.
- The authors call their dimensions consistent with Panjabi 1992 and Zindrick 1987 (p. 713, PDF 5) while defining them in a different projection; see the last item of this section.
- Inference (my geometry): the L5 row of Table 1 is consistent with a single ellipse, the L4 row is not (bounding height predicted about 20.5 mm against 17.4 to 17.8 printed), fitting a mixed set of L4 shapes.
- The abstract's "up to 3 mm" offset applies to the lumbar pedicles; at the S1 medial border the offset is often 3 to 4 mm (p. 712, PDF 4).
- The "S1 foramen" (p. 712) and the "S1-S2 intervertebral foramen" (p. 712-714) appear to be the same first sacral foramen (Inference).
- The authors' claim of frequent L5 misplacement is their observation, not data (p. 713, PDF 5).
- Against other papers (Inference from memory, to be checked against those notes): the commonly cited axial-plane L5 pedicle widths of Zindrick 1987 and Panjabi 1992 are, as I recall, a few millimetres smaller than Robertson's coaxial 21.5 mm; the horizontal chord of Robertson's L5 ellipse (about 18 mm) would reconcile them. Robertson's coaxial heights (17 to 20 mm) also look larger than the sagittal pedicle heights in those papers for the same reason.
