## What the paper is

- Design: retrospective cohort, consecutive sampling, one tertiary centre in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia (p. 312 (PDF 1) abstract; p. 314 (PDF 3); p. 316 (PDF 5) Discussion). IRB no. 19-563 (p. 313 (PDF 2)).
- Aims: a sex-specific database of L1 to L5 pedicle morphometry for Saudi adults, to propose screw trajectories, screw dimensions and safe bony corridors, and a comparison with CT series of other populations (p. 313 (PDF 2)).
- Population: 300 adult Saudi living patients, 150 men and 150 women, aged 18 to 60 years (mean 42.0); age by sex not given (p. 314 (PDF 3) Results). Scans of the abdomen or lumbar spine done 2015 to 2020 for suspected abdominal pathology or low back ache (p. 313 (PDF 2)). The authors call the centre representative of the Saudi population (p. 316 (PDF 5)) and say it serves patients from almost all of the country (p. 318 (PDF 7)); no regional, tribal or ancestry breakdown is given.
- Denominators: 300 patients x 5 lumbar levels (L1 to L5) = 1,500 vertebrae; every measure was taken on both sides (Table 1 reports left and right separately, p. 315 (PDF 4)), so Inference: 3,000 pedicles.
- Exclusions: congenital or acquired deformities, fractures, spondylolysis or spondylolisthesis, any previous spine surgery, instrumented or not (p. 313 (PDF 2)). Nothing else (no BMI, height, osteoporosis or degenerative grade).
- Sample size: from Dzierzanowski's TPD of 6.1 +/- 1.5 mm in 100 subjects, a precision of 0.4 and 95% confidence gave a minimum of 55; 150 per sex were taken because sex differences were also studied (p. 314 (PDF 3)). Inference: (1.96 x 1.5 / 0.4)^2 = 54, consistent.
- Modality: non-contrast CT, 2.5 mm contiguous axial sections from the first lumbar to "the fifth sacral" vertebra; 140 kV, 500 mA, 0.7 s rotation, 200 images, 5 s total exposure, DLP 700 to 900 mGy cm (p. 314 (PDF 3)). Read on a GE AW 5 workstation with GE Centricity Enterprise zero-footprint software version 6.0 SP4 (p. 314 (PDF 3)). Pixel size, field of view, kernel, window and reformat thickness are not given. Inference: 200 images x 2.5 mm = 500 mm of coverage.
- Measures: transverse pedicle diameter (TPD), pedicle length (PL), pedicle height (PH), chord length (CL), pedicle axis angle, all "as described by Vaccaro and Garfin" (ref. 12) (p. 313 (PDF 2), Figures 1 to 3).
- Observers: two radiologists "with equal experience" measured every parameter independently, each three times, and the average was taken (p. 314 (PDF 3)).
- Statistics: results stratified by side and by sex (p. 313 (PDF 2)); SPSS v22; Student t-test between sexes and between this study and published CT studies; P < 0.05 significant; an inter-observer reliability test is mentioned but its statistic is not named (p. 314 (PDF 3)). Inference: the comparison with published series can only have used their printed means and SDs. The abstract instead says the Mann-Whitney test compared the sexes (p. 312 (PDF 1)); see Gaps.
- Level identification and counting: not described. The scan field began at L1 (p. 314 (PDF 3)). No word on how L1 was recognised, nor on transitional segments.
- Comparison set (Table 3, p. 317 (PDF 6); Figure 4, p. 314 (PDF 3)): CT-only studies that presented adequate data; case reports, plain-radiograph measurements, manual measurements on anatomical specimens, intraoperative measurements and studies of children excluded (p. 314 (PDF 3)). What each comparison series' n counts (patients, vertebrae or pedicles) is not stated.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper takes the lumbar spine as five vertebrae (p. 312 (PDF 1), Introduction) and says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib count or any other numbering anomaly; no prevalence is given. Inference: "congenital deformities" were excluded (p. 313 (PDF 2)), which may or may not have removed transitional spines; with the scan starting at L1 (p. 314 (PDF 3)) the lowest rib was not always in view, so the levels were presumably counted up from the sacrum, and any L6 or sacralised L5 would have shifted labels silently. The paper's level values therefore carry an unknown, probably small, numbering contamination.

## Measures, each as a recipe

Instrument for every measure: the GE AW 5 workstation and Centricity software (p. 314 (PDF 3)); the measuring tool itself (distance or angle caliper) is not named. Levels for every measure: L1 to L5, left and right (Table 1, p. 315 (PDF 4)). Pooled values below are mean +/- SD of 300 patients from Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5); how a patient's left and right were combined is not stated. Sex differences are male minus female computed from Table 2 (Inference: simple arithmetic). The text's "ranges" are all exactly mean +/- 2 SD, not observed minima and maxima (Inference: checked for TPD, PL, PH, CL and angle; see Gaps).

### Transverse pedicle diameter (TPD)

- Definition: distance between the medial and lateral outer cortex of the pedicle at the isthmus (p. 313 (PDF 2), Figure 1 label b). Outer-cortical width, i.e. the bony envelope, not the cancellous channel.
- Plane: axial CT section (Figure 1B, p. 313 (PDF 2)), where b is a short arrow across one pedicle from its canal-side cortex to its lateral cortex; whether the line was drawn perpendicular to the pedicle axis or along the scanner's left-right axis is not stated. Inference: the arrow runs nearly left-right, roughly across the pedicle's own axis.
- Levels: L1 to L5, both sides.
- Values: L1 5.76 +/- 1.02, L2 6.34 +/- 0.96, L3 8.11 +/- 1.1, L4 9.82 +/- 1.2, L5 13.62 +/- 1.48 mm (Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5)).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Find the pedicle as the narrowest bony bridge on each side between the body and the posterior arch (SPINEPS, as far as known, gives no pedicle label of its own; it lies inside the arch subregion). Fit the pedicle axis (posterior entry point through the isthmus centre into the body); in the axial plane, or better in the plane perpendicular to that axis, take the minimum mediolateral extent of the mask's outer boundary along the axis. Report left and right. A mask with a filled cortex gives outer width directly; a mask that excludes cortex underestimates.
- From a radiograph alone: not addressed by the paper (plain-radiograph studies were excluded, p. 314 (PDF 3)). Inference: the AP radiograph shows the pedicle oval, whose width approximates TPD after magnification correction; a lateral cannot give it.
- Across levels: rises monotonically L1 to L5; step sizes L1-L2 +0.58, L2-L3 +1.77, L3-L4 +1.71, L4-L5 +3.80 mm (Inference from Table 2). L1 and L2 barely separate (step about 0.6 SD); L4 to L5 separates well (about 2.8 pooled SD).
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger at every level, P < 0.001 (Table 2; text p. 316 (PDF 5)); male minus female +0.86, +0.77, +0.99, +0.97, +0.64 mm L1 to L5 (Inference arithmetic), a relative difference of about 16% at L1 falling to 5% at L5. No age or body-size analysis. Saudi TPD is among the smallest reported: at L1 to L4 it exceeds only Makino et al.'s Asian series of degenerative spines (ref. 24; L1 5.46, L5 12.86 mm), and at L5 it also exceeds Dzierzanowski's European series (13.2 +/- 2.5 mm); the Indian (Acharya), Eastern Anatolian (Kadioglu), North American (Zindrick, Olsewski), Mexican (Urrutia) and Burkinabe (Tall) series are wider at every level (Table 3, p. 317 (PDF 6)). The authors tie smaller Asian pedicles to shorter stature (ref. 27; p. 317 (PDF 6)).
- Error: see Measurement error below; no per-measure reliability given.

### Pedicle height (PH)

- Definition: distance between the superior and inferior cortex of the pedicle in the sagittal plane at the isthmus (p. 313 (PDF 2), Figure 3 label e).
- Plane: sagittal (p. 313 (PDF 2)). Figure 3A puts arrow e at the narrowest part of the pedicle, between the superior and inferior vertebral notches just behind the body; Figure 3B is a parasagittal CT image through the pedicles and facet joints with a short arrow on the lowest vertebra in view (p. 313 (PDF 2)). Inference: 3B is a reformat of the 2.5 mm axial series.
- Levels: L1 to L5, both sides.
- Values: L1 9.75 +/- 1.2, L2 9.32 +/- 1.12, L3 9.11 +/- 1.1, L4 8.71 +/- 1.03, L5 8.3 +/- 0.99 mm (Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5)).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the plane perpendicular to the pedicle axis at the isthmus, or in a parasagittal slice through the pedicle centre, take the craniocaudal extent of the mask between the superior and inferior vertebral notches. The craniocaudal axis is the direction of the 2.5 mm slice spacing in this paper, so the paper's PH has the coarsest resolution of its measures.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. A lateral radiograph shows the pedicle between the superior and inferior notches and gives its height after magnification correction; the AP pedicle oval gives it too.
- Across levels: falls slowly from L1 to L5 by about 0.2 to 0.4 mm a level (Inference arithmetic: -0.43, -0.21, -0.40, -0.41), 0.2 to 0.4 SD a step: no use for telling adjacent levels apart.
- Sex, age, size, population: men taller, P < 0.001 at every level (Table 2; text p. 316 (PDF 5)); male minus female +0.82, +0.61, +0.59, +0.50, +0.50 mm (Inference arithmetic). The authors state Saudi PH is the smallest and that of "Caucasians" the greatest (p. 318 (PDF 7)).
- Error: none per measure. Inference: the values sit 4.5 to 7.6 mm below every European and North American series at the same level (Dzierzanowski, Kadioglu, Zindrick, Olsewski: 13.4 to 17.3 mm) and 0.65 to 2.1 mm below the Asian degenerative series of ref. 24 (Takahiro, i.e. Makino: 11.89 at L1 to 8.95 at L5) (Table 3, p. 317 (PDF 6)), although the caudal fall matches theirs. The uniform 5 mm gap to the Western series suggests a definitional difference (perhaps the narrowest point of the isthmus on a coarse sagittal reformat) or a reformat artefact rather than a pure population difference; the Asian series of ref. 24 also runs low, so population cannot be excluded. Do not use these PH values as absolute anatomy (Inference).

### Pedicle length (PL)

- Definition: from the posterior cortex of the pedicle to the posterior longitudinal ligament, along the pedicle's mid-axis (p. 313 (PDF 2), Figure 2 label c). Figure 2B draws two lines parallel to the image's left-right axis, one through the posterior entry point and one along the posterior wall of the body, with the pedicle axis crossing both (p. 313 (PDF 2)). Inference: PL is the part of the axis between those two lines; the PLL is not seen on bone CT, so the end point is in practice the posterior wall of the body, and PL is the posterior entry-to-body length of the screw path.
- Plane: axial (Figure 2B, p. 313 (PDF 2)).
- Levels: L1 to L5, both sides.
- Values: L1 16.01 +/- 1.27, L2 14.63 +/- 1.16, L3 13.42 +/- 1.17, L4 12.18 +/- 1.12, L5 9.93 +/- 1.31 mm (Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5)).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Along the fitted pedicle axis, from where the axis enters the posterior bone surface of the mask (the entry point at the base of the transverse process, lateral to the superior articular process, as Figure 2B places it) to where it crosses the posterior wall of the body subregion. Inference: if the end point is taken as a line parallel to the scanner's left-right axis, as drawn, PL depends on the vertebra's axial rotation.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Not directly; the lateral projection foreshortens a path angled in the axial plane by cos(axis angle), about 0.88 at L5 (PL x cos gives about 8.7 mm at L5 and 15.6 mm at L1).
- Across levels: falls steadily, -1.38, -1.21, -1.24, then -2.25 mm L4 to L5 (Inference arithmetic); each step is about 1 SD, the L4-L5 step about 1.8 SD.
- Sex, age, size, population: sex difference significant only at L1 (P < 0.001; 15.69 F vs 16.33 M); L2 to L5 P 0.138, 0.509, 0.407, 0.145 (Table 2; text p. 316 (PDF 5)). Population values diverge widely, probably by definition: Urrutia (Mexican, n = 60) 16.67 to 20.92 mm, Tall (Burkinabe, n = 70) 9.5 at L1 to 6.45 mm at L5 (Table 3, p. 317 (PDF 6); populations from the reference titles, p. 319 (PDF 8)).
- Error: none per measure.

### Chord length (CL)

- Definition: the length along the pedicle's mid-axis, starting at the pedicle's posterior cortical entry and ending at the front cortex of the body (p. 313 (PDF 2), Figure 1 label a). In Figure 1B the arrow runs from the entry point at the base of the transverse process through the pedicle to the anterior cortex near the midline (p. 313 (PDF 2)). Inference: it is the full bony length of a unicortical pedicle screw path, not a bicortical exit.
- Plane: axial (Figure 1B).
- Levels: L1 to L5, both sides.
- Values: L1 49.18 +/- 3.29, L2 50.39 +/- 3.09, L3 50.92 +/- 2.86, L4 50.15 +/- 3.31, L5 47.63 +/- 3.45 mm (Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5)).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Ray-cast along the pedicle axis from the posterior entry point to the last voxel of the body's anterior cortex.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Not directly; its sagittal projection is CL x cos(axis angle), about 48 mm at L1 and 42 mm at L5, and a lateral film also adds magnification.
- Across levels: an inverted-V, which the authors call a "Caret" pattern: rises L1 to L3 (49.18 to 50.92) and falls to L5 (47.63) (p. 316 (PDF 5)). Peak at L3. Inference: adjacent steps are 0.2 to 0.75 SD; only L5 against L3 or L4 separates at all.
- Sex, age, size, population: women shorter at every level (Table 2 P < 0.001; text says P = 0.001, p. 316 (PDF 5)); male minus female +2.99, +2.78, +2.21, +2.17, +2.51 mm (Inference arithmetic). Among other series: Dzierzanowski (Europe, n = 100) about 52 mm, the highest; Matsukawa (Japanese, n = 100) 36.8 to 39.8 mm (average about 38.5 mm, p. 318 (PDF 7)); Zindrick 33.7 +/- 5.6 mm at L5, the shortest single value (p. 318 (PDF 7); Table 3). The authors call the Saudi CL (about 50 mm) one of the highest (p. 318 (PDF 7)).
- Error: none per measure.

### Pedicle axis angle (transverse angle)

- Definition: angle between the pedicle mid-axis and the midline, i.e. medial convergence in the axial plane (p. 313 (PDF 2), Figure 2 label d). Figure 2B draws two lines parallel to the image's vertical axis, one through the midline of the body and spinous process and one through the posterior entry point, and marks d between the pedicle axis and each (p. 313 (PDF 2)). Whether the midline was corrected for axial rotation is not stated.
- Plane: axial.
- Levels: L1 to L5, both sides.
- Values: L1 12.68 +/- 2.54, L2 13.26 +/- 2.3, L3 15.47 +/- 2.37, L4 17.47 +/- 3.3, L5 28.23 +/- 3.87 deg (Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5)).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Use the midsagittal plane of the vertebra itself (fitted to the mask's left-right symmetry, or the line from the body centroid to the spinous process tip), not the scanner's y axis; take the angle between the pedicle axis, projected onto the vertebra's own axial (end-plate-parallel) plane, and that midsagittal plane.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Not measurable on a lateral; on an AP only indirectly (pedicle oval position and shape), not as an angle.
- Across levels: rises L1 to L5; the authors call it about 2 deg per level from L1 to L4 and a sudden 11 deg jump from L4 to L5 (p. 316 (PDF 5); p. 318 (PDF 7)). From Table 2 the steps are +0.58, +2.21, +2.00, +10.76 deg (Inference arithmetic): L1 and L2 do not separate; L4 to L5 separates by about 3 pooled SD, the strongest single level marker in the paper.
- Sex, age, size, population: Table 2 shows a significant sex difference only at L1 (P = 0.009) and L2 (P = 0.042); L3 to L5 P 0.131, 0.322, 0.57. The text's sentence states this back to front (see Gaps). Male minus female +0.76 at L1 down to +0.25 deg at L5 (Inference arithmetic). Other series: Olsewski 6 deg at L1 (least), Dzierzanowski 20.6 at L1 and 32.1 at L5 (most) (Table 3; p. 318 (PDF 7)). Acharya's Indian L3 value (15.4 +/- 3.6) is the only value with an SD in Table 3 that did not differ from the Saudi one (p. 316 (PDF 5); Table 3).
- Error: see below. Side: left exceeds right at every level by 1.1 to 2.4 deg (Table 1, p. 315 (PDF 4); Inference arithmetic), though the text reports no side difference (p. 314 (PDF 3)).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1: smallest TPD (5.76 mm), longest PL (16.01 mm), tallest PH (9.75 mm), least medial angle (12.68 deg) (p. 316 (PDF 5)). The authors judge L1 able to take a 4.0 to 5.0 mm screw (p. 317 (PDF 6)) and a 10 deg medial angulation safe (p. 316 (PDF 5)). The sex difference in PL and in angle is significant here (Table 2).
- L2: close to L1 in TPD (6.34) and angle (13.26); PL 14.63. Inference: L1 and L2 are the hardest adjacent pair to tell apart by pedicle measures.
- L3: longest CL (50.92 +/- 2.86 mm) (p. 316 (PDF 5)); the only level where a comparison series with an SD (Acharya, angle) matched the Saudi value (Table 3, p. 317 (PDF 6)).
- L4: TPD 9.82, angle 17.47, CL 50.15, PL 12.18 (Table 2). Inference: the TPD to PH ratio passes 1 between L3 (0.89) and L4 (1.13) with this paper's PH; given the doubts about PH, use with caution.
- L5: widest pedicle (13.62 mm), shortest PL (9.93), shortest PH (8.3), shortest CL (47.63), steepest medial angle (28.23 deg); a 25 deg medial angulation is called safe from medial breach (p. 316 (PDF 5); p. 318 (PDF 7)). The sudden L4-L5 jump in angle is reported also in North American and North Indian series (refs 9, 11, 25; p. 318 (PDF 7)).
- T12/L1 and L5/S1 junctions: nothing measured; S1 was in the field (p. 314 (PDF 3)) but not measured. Cervical and thoracic junctions: outside the paper.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Two radiologists of equal experience, each measuring three times, values averaged (p. 314 (PDF 3)).
- An inter-rater analysis found "no interobserver bias (P > 0.05)" (p. 314 (PDF 3); abstract p. 312 (PDF 1)). No ICC, kappa, standard error of measurement, Bland-Altman limits or intra-observer statistic is given, nor the observers' separate values.
- Table 1 (p. 315 (PDF 4)) carries an unexplained "Correlation" column with values from -0.018 to 0.143. Inference: if this were inter-observer correlation it would mean almost no agreement, which contradicts the text; more likely it is something else (perhaps side or sex against value), but the paper does not say.
- Instrument resolution: 2.5 mm contiguous axial slices (p. 314 (PDF 3)); in-plane pixel size not given. Inference: PH is measured across the slice direction and carries up to about +/- 1.25 mm of partial-volume uncertainty; TPD, PL, CL and angle are in-plane.
- Inference: pooled coefficients of variation (biological plus measurement) from Table 2 are TPD 11 to 18%, PH 12%, PL 8 to 13%, CL 6 to 7%, angle 14 to 20%; measurement error can be no larger than these.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indications for lumbar pedicle screws: fractures, spondylolisthesis, tumours, scoliosis, kyphosis and degenerative disease, the last often fused with uni- or bilateral screws (p. 312 (PDF 1)). Structures at risk: lumbar nerve roots, meninges, aorta, inferior vena cava, abdominal viscera (pp. 312-313 (PDF 1-2)).
- TPD sets the screw diameter that avoids a mediolateral breach (pp. 316-317 (PDF 5-6)). The authors: L1 takes 4.0 to 5.0 mm screws, the other lumbar levels "easily" 6.0 or 6.5 mm (p. 317 (PDF 6)). Pedicle fracture or cut-out becomes possible once more than 80% of the outer pedicle diameter is violated (refs 15, 16; p. 317 (PDF 6)). Inference: by the paper's own numbers L2 (0.8 x 6.34 = 5.1 mm overall, 0.8 x 5.95 = 4.8 mm in women) cannot "easily" take a 6.0 to 6.5 mm screw within the 80% rule, and L3 takes 6.0 mm but not 6.5 mm in women (0.8 x 7.61 = 6.1 mm); the advice fits L4 and L5 fully.
- Screw trajectory: 10 deg medial at L1 and 25 deg at L5 avoid medial breach (pp. 316, 318 (PDF 5, 7)); the angle rises sharply at L5.
- Screw length and purchase: deeper insertion raised fixation, reduced loosening and raised pull-out strength under cyclic loading only with mid-body and bicortical purchase (ref. 28); a purchase of at least 50% to 80% of the body makes the construct sound (p. 318 (PDF 7)). CL governs body purchase and, if exceeded, anterior breach with risk to the great vessels; it affects resistance to lateral bending and axial rotation and the stress at the bone-screw interface (P < 0.01, ref. 30; p. 318 (PDF 7)). Bone mineral density, insertion depth and insertion angle bear on pull-out strength (ref. 14; p. 316 (PDF 5)).
- Population: Asians, Saudis included, have smaller TPD than Western series; the authors tie this to stature, taller individuals taking larger screws (ref. 27; p. 317 (PDF 6)). Every Saudi value differed significantly from every comparison value that carries an SD, except Acharya's L3 angle (p. 316 (PDF 5); Table 3); Tall's and Matsukawa's rows carry no SD and no significance mark.
- Pitfall: PL is defined to a soft-tissue landmark (PLL) that bone-window CT does not show (p. 313 (PDF 2)).
- Pitfall: comparing across series mixes definitions: PL at L1 runs from 9.5 (Tall) to 20.92 mm (Urrutia) and at L5 from 6.45 to 17.23 mm; PH from 8.3 to 17.3 mm; so cross-population "significance" partly measures method (Inference from Table 3, p. 317 (PDF 6)). Matsukawa's chord lengths come from a cortical bone trajectory study (ref. 29 title, p. 319 (PDF 8)), so Inference: they are not comparable to a traditional-trajectory CL.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Measure pedicle width at the isthmus as outer cortex to outer cortex, the narrowest mediolateral span between body and arch, on both sides; expect it to rise from about 5.8 mm at L1 to about 13.6 mm at L5 in Saudi adults (p. 313 (PDF 2) Fig. 1; Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5)).
2. Use the L4 to L5 step to anchor the caudal end: TPD jumps by about 3.8 mm and the transverse angle by about 11 deg (17.5 to 28.2 deg) between L4 and L5, both large against their SDs (Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5); Inference arithmetic). A vertebra with a wide, steeply converging pedicle is L5-like; if one more such vertebra lies below, suspect L6 or lumbarised S1 (Inference: the paper gives no transitional data).
3. Do not try to tell L1 from L2 by pedicle measures: TPD differs by 0.6 mm and angle by 0.6 deg, under 1 SD (Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5)); anchor L1 by the T12 rib instead (Inference).
4. Measure the transverse angle against the vertebra's own midsagittal line, not the scanner axis, and compare left with right; a consistent one-sided excess (the paper's left exceeds right by 1.1 to 2.4 deg, Table 1, p. 315 (PDF 4)) can mean axial rotation or a misplaced midline (Inference; the paper's Figure 2B draws the midline parallel to the image axis, p. 313 (PDF 2)).
5. Measure pedicle height on a reformat perpendicular to the pedicle axis at the isthmus, but do not compare it with this paper's absolute PH (8.3 to 9.75 mm), which sits 4.5 to 7.6 mm below the European and North American CT series (Table 3, p. 317 (PDF 6)); use its trend only (slight caudal fall, p. 316 (PDF 5)) (Inference).
6. Measure chord length along the pedicle axis from the posterior entry to the anterior body cortex; expect a peak at L3 (about 51 mm) and the shortest at L5 (about 47.6 mm) (p. 316 (PDF 5)); treat it as a weak level cue.
7. Measure pedicle length along the pedicle axis from the posterior pedicle cortex to the posterior body wall, which stands in for the paper's PLL end point (p. 313 (PDF 2), Figure 2; Inference); expect a steady fall of about 1.2 to 1.4 mm a level and 2.25 mm from L4 to L5 (Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5)).
8. Adjust for sex: men have wider (+0.6 to 1.0 mm), taller (+0.5 to 0.8 mm) pedicles and longer chords (+2.2 to 3.0 mm); pedicle length and angle differ significantly only at L1 (length) and L1 to L2 (angle) (Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5); Inference arithmetic).
9. Adjust for population: Saudi pedicles are narrower than the Indian, Eastern Anatolian, North American, Mexican and Burkinabe CT series at every level, and than Dzierzanowski's European series at L1 to L4; they are wider only than Makino's Asian degenerative-spine series, and than Dzierzanowski's at L5 (Table 3, p. 317 (PDF 6)); use a population-specific prior when the population is known.
10. On radiographs: take pedicle width from the AP oval and height from the lateral or AP, after magnification correction; the transverse angle, PL and CL cannot be read from a lateral, whose projected path is foreshortened by cos(angle) (Inference; the paper excluded radiographic studies, p. 314 (PDF 3)).
11. When a scan starts at L1, do not count down from the top edge; count from a confirmed anchor and flag numbering uncertainty, because this paper's own scans began at L1 with no stated counting method (p. 314 (PDF 3); Inference).
12. Read a printed "range" in this paper as mean +/- 2 SD, not the observed spread (p. 316 (PDF 5); Inference from arithmetic).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper assumes five lumbar vertebrae (p. 312 (PDF 1)), reports no transitional vertebra, L6, T13 or rib-count anomaly and gives no denominator for one; its series may carry an unknown contamination of misnumbered levels (Inference).

(b) Measures:
- Pedicle width: the generator's pedicle width corresponds to TPD (outer-cortical width at the isthmus). Inference: if the mask code measures along the scanner's left-right axis rather than perpendicular to the pedicle axis, it overestimates width by about 1/cos(angle), about 13% at L5 (28 deg) and 2.5% at L1; measure perpendicular to the fitted axis. A mask whose boundary excludes the cortex underestimates.
- Pedicle height: the generator holds it, but this paper's PH (8.3 to 9.75 mm) should not be used to set or validate its means; its definition or resolution appears to differ from the other CT series (Table 3, p. 317 (PDF 6)) and, Inference from memory, from Panjabi's.
- Transverse angle: Inference (from memory): Panjabi's transverse pedicle inclination is the same idea, relative to the midsagittal plane. From a mask, reference the vertebra's own midsagittal plane, or rotation inflates one side.
- Could add: chord length (posterior entry to anterior cortex, along the pedicle axis) and pedicle length (posterior cortex to posterior body wall, along the axis); both need the fitted pedicle axis and the body subregion. CL is a weak level discriminator (peak at L3); PL is a moderate one (steady caudal fall).
- Sources of bias from the mask: SPINEPS masks may give the pedicle only as part of the arch (Inference); the isthmus must be found as the narrowest cross-section; partial volume at 2.5 mm slices hits PH most.

(c) Measurement error: the paper gives none usable (no ICC or SEM; "no interobserver bias, P > 0.05", p. 314 (PDF 3)). Use outside values for error; the pooled SDs of Table 2 bound the total (TPD 0.96 to 1.48 mm, PH 0.99 to 1.2 mm, PL 1.12 to 1.31 mm, CL 2.86 to 3.45 mm, angle 2.3 to 3.87 deg). Inference: add about +/- 1.25 mm resolution error to any PH from 2.5 mm slices.

(d) Covariates: sex effects per level as above (Table 2, p. 316 (PDF 5)); relative TPD sex effect shrinks caudally (about 16% at L1, 5% at L5; Inference arithmetic), which a single global size factor would not reproduce. Age: range 18 to 60, mean 42, not analysed. Body size: no height, weight or BMI. Population: Saudi Arabian adults at one Riyadh centre; Table 3 supplies L1 to L5 values for Indian (Acharya), European (Dzierzanowski, country not stated in this paper; Kadioglu, Eastern Anatolian Turkish), North American (Zindrick, Olsewski), Mexican (Urrutia), Asia Pacific (Makino, degenerative spines; Matsukawa, Japanese, cortical trajectory) and Burkinabe (Tall) series for a population prior (p. 317 (PDF 6); populations from the text p. 318 (PDF 7) and reference titles, p. 319 (PDF 8)).

## Gaps and conflicts

- Statistical test: the abstract says Mann-Whitney (p. 312 (PDF 1)); Methods say Student t-test (pp. 313-314 (PDF 2-3)).
- Axis-angle sex sentence: the text says there was "no statistically significant difference" only at L1 and L2 (p. 316 (PDF 5); p. 318 (PDF 7)), but Table 2 shows the opposite: significant at L1 (P = 0.009) and L2 (P = 0.042), not at L3 to L5.
- Side test: the text and abstract report no side difference (p. 312 (PDF 1); p. 314 (PDF 3)), but Table 1's P column compares sexes within a side and no side-versus-side statistic is shown; the angle is larger on the left at every level by 1.1 to 2.4 deg (Table 1, p. 315 (PDF 4)).
- Table 1's "Correlation" column is never explained (p. 315 (PDF 4)).
- CL sex P: text P = 0.001 (p. 316 (PDF 5)); Table 2 P < 0.001 at every level.
- "Ranges" are mean +/- 2 SD (Inference). The L1 angle range 7.6 to 17.06 deg (p. 316 (PDF 5)) does not fit: mean + 2 SD is 17.76, so 17.06 is probably a typographical error.
- The text puts the angle rise at 2 deg a level from L1 to L4 (p. 316 (PDF 5)); Table 2 gives +0.58 deg for L1 to L2.
- Olsewski's L5 angle: 20 deg in the text (p. 318 (PDF 7)) against 25.6 +/- 5.5 deg in Table 3 (p. 317 (PDF 6)).
- Largest TPD at L1: the text names Olsewski and Zindrick (about 8 mm, p. 317 (PDF 6)), but Table 3 gives Kadioglu 8.8 mm at L1, more than Zindrick's 8.7 and Olsewski's 8.2.
- Smallest CL: the text says Matsukawa's was the smallest at all lumbar levels (p. 318 (PDF 7)), but Zindrick's L5 value (33.7 mm) is below Matsukawa's (38.3 mm) (Table 3, p. 317 (PDF 6)).
- PH trend sources: the text cites Urrutia and Tall for the caudal fall in pedicle height (p. 318 (PDF 7)), but Table 3 has no PH row for either; their rows are PL (and TPD and angle for Tall).
- PL by population: the text says "Caucasians" had the longest PL (p. 318 (PDF 7)), but Table 3's only other PL series are Mexican (Urrutia) and Burkinabe (Tall).
- The text claims significance against all continents and regions (p. 317 (PDF 6)), yet Tall's rows and Matsukawa's CL row carry no SD and no asterisk in Table 3 (p. 317 (PDF 6)); Inference: they could not be tested.
- Citation errors in Table 3 (p. 317 (PDF 6)): the current study is cited as ref. 23, which is Tall et al.; Tall's angle row is cited as ref. 11 (Olsewski); the TPD row's "Makino Takahiro et al." and the PH row's "Takahiro et al." are both ref. 24, Makino T. et al., a series of degenerative spines (ref. list, p. 319 (PDF 8)); Inference: Takahiro is the first author's given name used as a surname. Tall's study is listed as n = 70 while its reference title says 170 cases (p. 319 (PDF 8)). What any n in Table 3 counts is not stated.
- Ref. 26 (Tse et al.), cited for Asian versus Western lumbar pedicle size (p. 317 (PDF 6)), is a study of C7 in southern Chinese (p. 319 (PDF 8)).
- The paper's inclusion rule for comparison studies excludes anatomical specimens (p. 314 (PDF 3)). Inference (from memory, unverified): Zindrick 1987 and Olsewski 1990 drew at least partly on cadaveric material.
- PH values far below the Western series, with no discussion of definition (Table 3, p. 317 (PDF 6)); the authors attribute it to population (p. 318 (PDF 7)). Inference from memory: Panjabi's and Zindrick's lumbar pedicle heights are in the mid-teens of millimetres, consistent with the other series, not with this paper.
- Not given: how levels were counted, any transitional or rib anomaly, pixel size, reconstruction kernel, window, reformat thickness, the inter-observer statistic, per-observer values, intra-observer error, how left and right were combined in Table 2, age or body-size effects, sagittal pedicle angle, the cancellous (inner) pedicle width, S1 values.
- Inference (from memory, unverified): Panjabi's lumbar transverse inclinations rise steeply to L5 as here, and his cadaveric pedicle heights are in the mid-teens of millimetres; a prior taken from this paper should be scaled to Panjabi's definitions, not mixed with them, and its PH left out.
- Inference: every other series in Table 3 also shows PH falling from L1 to L5 (p. 317 (PDF 6)), so the trend is shared and only the absolute level is suspect.
