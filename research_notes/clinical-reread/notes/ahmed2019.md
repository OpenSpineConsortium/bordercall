# Ahmed 2019: Radiological anatomy of the lumbar canal, an MRI study

Source: thread "Reread every paper for clinical detail", project symphony_4, 2026-10-28.

Citation as printed: Mohammed Awais Ahmed, Aditya M, Anitha T, Sailaja TK. "Radiological Anatomy of the Lumbar
Canal: A MRI study." Journal of Chalmeda Anand Rao Institute of Medical Sciences, Vol 17, Issue 1, January - June
2019, pp. 21-26 (ISSN (Print) 2278-5310). Original Article.

Copy read: publisher's free copy (J Chalmeda Anand Rao Inst Med Sci), project files
literature/synthetic_spine/ahmed2019.pdf; sha256 begins bd1788c145fc8000. Six PDF pages = printed pp. 21-26
(PDF 1 = p. 21 ... PDF 6 = p. 26, references only).

## What the paper is

- Design: cross-sectional study, Department of Radiology, Chalmeda Anand Rao Institute of Medical Sciences,
  Karimnagar, Telangana, India (p. 22, PDF 2). Two aims: lumbar canal dimensions by sex in a symptomatic Telangana
  population compared with other populations, and whether the mid-sagittal and transverse diameters differ by sex
  (p. 21, PDF 1).
- Population and denominators: 80 symptomatic adults, 36 men and 44 women, aged 20-70 years, living patients
  referred for lumbar MRI between June 2017 and October 2018 with low back pain with or without radiculopathy;
  all consented (p. 22, PDF 2). Indian (Telangana state); ethnicity beyond that not stated. No mean age, no age
  distribution by sex, no height, weight or BMI.
- Exclusions: age under 20; previous lumbar spine surgery; vertebral trauma; tumours; infections; "congenital
  anomalies" (p. 21, PDF 1 abstract; p. 22, PDF 2).
- Modality and instrument: GE Signa HDxt 1.5 T, "sense spine" coil, 4 mm slice thickness. Sequences: T2W fast
  spin echo (FSE) sagittal, T1W FSE sagittal, STIR coronal, T1W FSE axial, T2W FSE axial, and a high-resolution
  T2 FSE "drive" axial with a small field of view to reduce CSF flow artefact (p. 22, PDF 2). Measured on the
  INSTARAD viewer version 1.7, sequential images, in millimetres (p. 22, PDF 2). The example screens (Figs. 1-2,
  p. 23, PDF 3) show a 512 x 512 display at zoom 1.69 and readouts to 0.01 cm (0.1 mm). Their overlays give
  the sagittal T2 as TR 2780 ms, TE 119.2 ms, echo train 25, slice 4.3 mm, and the axial T2 as TR 2680 ms,
  TE 93.3 ms, echo train 15, slice 4 mm; both screens are of one subject, female per the overlay (Inference:
  the two overlays carry the same subject identifier and study date).
- Why MRI and why this study (p. 22, PDF 2): earlier lumbar canal norms leaned on Ullrich et al.'s CT range
  (normal adult sagittal diameter 14-15 mm) or on Hinck, Eisenstein and Amonoo-Kuofi, who worked on plain
  radiographs or cadaveric osteological specimens; North Indian data exist but symptomatic Telangana data are
  sparse, and the authors call Indian norms hard to set because the Indian population is heterogeneous. MRI was
  chosen as non-invasive, free of radiation and showing the soft tissues.
- Levels: L1 to L5 only, two measures per level, 80 x 5 = 400 level-measurements of each measure (Inference:
  arithmetic; no missing data are reported).
- Level identification: not described. Only Fig. 2 carries a level label (L1). No statement on how L1 or L5 were
  identified, on whether the whole spine was imaged for counting, or on transitional segments.
- Statistics: descriptive (percentage, mean, SD); Student's t-test between sexes; p < 0.05 significant (p. 22,
  PDF 2). No observer repeatability, no confidence intervals, no correlation with age or body size.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- The paper reports no anomaly prevalence. "Congenital anomalies" were an exclusion criterion (p. 21, PDF 1;
  p. 22, PDF 2), with no definition, no count of how many were excluded and no projection named. Inference: a
  lumbosacral transitional vertebra may or may not have been counted as a congenital anomaly; the paper does not
  say, and it does not say how levels were numbered.
- Inference: because the norms come from spines with congenital anomalies removed and an unstated numbering
  rule, they should not be applied to a spine with 4 or 6 lumbar-type vertebrae without renumbering first.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### 1. Mid-sagittal (antero-posterior) diameter of the canal (MS)

- Definition: on the T2-weighted mid-sagittal image, measured from the body's posterior wall at its midpoint,
  located by where the basivertebral vein leaves the body, back to the most anterior part of the
  spinous process (p. 22, PDF 2; Fig. 1, p. 23, PDF 3). Inference: the posterior landmark is the spinolaminar
  junction in the midline, i.e. the bony posterior wall of the canal, not the dural sac.
- Plane: mid-sagittal, T2W FSE, 4 mm slice. Instrument: software calliper in the viewer. Levels: L1-L5.
- Fig. 1 (p. 23, PDF 3) shows one subject with five lines drawn at the mid-height of the bodies, each roughly
  perpendicular to the local canal axis (oblique in the lordotic upper lumbar spine, horizontal at the lowest),
  reading top to bottom 1.63, 1.50, 1.33, 1.20, 1.29 cm. Inference: these are L1 to L5, and in this subject the
  minimum is at L4, not L3; the mid-body height and the line orientation are read from the figure, not stated in
  the text. Each line runs from the posterior body wall across the bright CSF to the dark posterior boundary of
  the canal; no level labels are printed on Fig. 1.
- Values, Table 1, p. 22 (PDF 2), mean +/- SD mm (men n = 36; women n = 44), p value, total population mean:
  - L1: 14.28 +/- 1.16; 14.38 +/- 1.38; p 0.398; 14.34
  - L2: 13.33 +/- 1.24; 13.51 +/- 1.27; p 0.521; 13.43
  - L3: 12.63 +/- 1.47; 12.12 +/- 1.35; p 0.110; 12.35
  - L4: 12.88 +/- 1.44; 12.54 +/- 1.17; p 0.262; 12.7
  - L5: 13.91 +/- 2.06; 13.4 +/- 1.68; p 0.233; 13.63
  - Fig. 3 (p. 23, PDF 3) plots these means. Inference: the total column is the sample-weighted mean of the two
    sexes (checked at every level).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference throughout; the paper is MRI): build the vertebra's own frame (cranio-
  caudal axis normal to the end plates, AP axis through the body centre and spinous process, left-right axis
  orthogonal). Take the mid-sagittal plane of the instance (its left-right symmetry plane). In that plane, the
  anterior point is the posterior cortex of the body subregion at half the body's height (between the superior
  and inferior end plates; the basivertebral foramen sits there). The posterior point is the anterior (canal-
  facing) surface of the posterior-element subregion at the base of the spinous process (spinolaminar junction).
  MS is the straight distance between the two points; the paper's line need not lie in one axial plane. Use bone
  only; do not use SPINEPS's spinal-canal label, which marks the canal contents.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral view (Inference; the paper uses no radiograph). Anterior point at the mid-
  height of the posterior body line, posterior point at the spinolaminar line; divide by the lateral
  magnification before comparing with these MRI values. Not obtainable from an AP view.
- Across levels: largest at L1, falling to a minimum at L3 in both sexes, then rising at L4 and L5; the authors
  call this the "hour-glass" shape, wider at the cephalic than the caudal end with a mid-lumbar narrowing (p. 22,
  PDF 2; p. 23, PDF 3; Fig. 3). Steps (Inference, arithmetic from Table 1): men -0.95, -0.70, +0.25, +1.03 mm;
  women -0.87, -1.39, +0.42, +0.86 mm (L1->L2, L2->L3, L3->L4, L4->L5). Standardised separation of adjacent
  levels (difference / root-mean-square of the two SDs, Inference): men 0.79, 0.51, 0.17, 0.58; women 0.66,
  1.06, 0.33, 0.59. MS alone separates no adjacent pair reliably; L3 and L4 are nearly identical; the U shape
  over several consecutive vertebrae is the cue. Inference: these separations use between-person SDs; within
  one spine adjacent levels are correlated, so the step inside one person is less noisy than these figures
  suggest, but the paper gives no within-person data to say by how much. The authors report the same
  hour-glass in Nigerian, Pakistani and Iraqi series (p. 23, PDF 3; but see Gaps).
- Sex, age, size, population: no significant sex difference at any level (p. 22, PDF 2; Table 1). Women's mean
  is slightly larger at L1 and L2 (14.38 vs 14.28; 13.51 vs 13.33), which the authors put down, tentatively, to
  "greater differences in general somatic size in women" (p. 24, PDF 4) and liken to Eisenstein's South
  African and Malas et al.'s Turkish (MRI, per reference 16) findings of wider female canals (p. 24, PDF 4).
  They cite Amonoo-Kuofi, Al-Anazi, Shukri et al. and Varol et al. as also finding no sex difference in MS
  (p. 24, PDF 4; but see Gaps). Age and body size not analysed. Population: slightly smaller than previous
  studies (p. 25, PDF 5; Table 3), see Level by level.
- Error: none reported. Biological SD 1.16-2.06 mm, CV 8-15% (Inference: arithmetic), largest at L5 in both
  sexes. Instrument readout 0.1 mm (Fig. 1). Slice 4 mm; how the mid-sagittal slice was chosen is not stated.

### 2. Transverse diameter, interpedicular distance (TD, IPD)

- Definition: on the T2-weighted axial image at the mid-pedicular level, the distance between the inner
  (medial) borders of the two pedicles (p. 22, PDF 2; Fig. 2, p. 23, PDF 3).
- Plane: axial, T2W FSE, 4 mm slice; slice angulation not stated. Levels: L1-L5. Fig. 2 shows L1, one line
  reading 2.20 cm, drawn slightly oblique to the image horizontal (Inference: from the figure). Inference, from
  the figure at high zoom: the slice cuts the body and the canal, the line runs across the canal just behind
  the body, and its ends sit on the dark lateral margins of the canal's bright contents, where separate pedicle
  cortices are hard to make out on T2; an MRI IPD may therefore track the canal's inner margin rather than a
  crisp bony cortex.
- Values, Table 2, p. 24 (PDF 4), mean +/- SD mm (men; women), p value, total:
  - L1: 23.32 +/- 2.00; 21.69 +/- 1.38; p < 0.0001; 22.42
  - L2: 23.41 +/- 2.16; 22.00 +/- 1.29; p < 0.001; 22.64
  - L3: 24.30 +/- 1.99; 22.76 +/- 1.36; p < 0.0002; 23.45
  - L4: 25.23 +/- 2.37; 23.93 +/- 1.53; p < 0.006; 24.52
  - L5: 27.89 +/- 2.76; 27.50 +/- 2.50; p 0.520; 27.68
  - Fig. 4 (p. 24, PDF 4) plots these means. The total column matches the sample-weighted mean of the sexes
    within 0.01 mm (L2: 22.63 computed, 22.64 printed; Inference: arithmetic).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): in the vertebra's frame, take the axial section through the middle of
  the pedicles' cranio-caudal extent; IPD is the left-right distance between the medial cortices of the two
  pedicles (the bony canal's width at the pedicles). SPINEPS gives the arch as one subregion, so the pedicles are
  the arch voxels bridging the posterior body wall to the articular pillars. A minimum over all pedicle sections
  (Hinck's film definition) or a greatest gap (Eisenstein's) will differ slightly from a single mid-pedicle
  section.
- From a radiograph alone: AP view, distance between the medial cortices of the pedicle rings, over the AP
  magnification (Inference; the paper compares with radiographic series in Table 4 but takes no radiograph).
  Not from a lateral view.
- Across levels: widens from L1 to L5 in both sexes (p. 23, PDF 3; Fig. 4). Steps (Inference, arithmetic):
  men +0.09, +0.89, +0.93, +2.66 mm; women +0.31, +0.76, +1.17, +3.57 mm. Standardised separation of adjacent
  levels (Inference): men 0.04, 0.43, 0.42, 1.03; women 0.23, 0.57, 0.81, 1.72. IPD cannot tell L1 from L2,
  especially in men; the L4->L5 jump is the strongest single-level cue in the paper. The authors attribute the
  axial "trefoil" shape of the canal to this caudal widening (p. 23, PDF 3), and explain the widening by the
  cauda equina roots below L1-L2 running laterally to their foramina (p. 24, PDF 4). They cite Kapoor et al.'s
  South Indian cadavers as showing the same steady L1-L5 rise (p. 24, PDF 4).
- Sex, age, size, population: men wider at every level; significant at L1-L4 (difference 1-1.5 mm per the
  text; computed 1.63, 1.41, 1.54, 1.30 mm), not at L5 (0.39 mm, under 0.5 mm) (p. 23, PDF 3; Table 2). Matches
  Tacar et al.'s Turkish 1-1.5 mm male excess; Sethi et al. (North India) found no sex difference and women
  larger (p. 24, PDF 4). Earlier work tied IPD to stature, growth and build (pp. 24-25, PDF 4-5); not tested
  here. Population: similar to Nigerian and Pakistani values (p. 25, PDF 5).
- Error: none reported. Biological SD 1.29-2.76 mm; CV about 8-10% in men, 6% in women at L1-L4, 9% at L5
  (Inference: arithmetic). Women markedly less variable than men at L1-L4 (Inference: from the SDs; the
  authors do not comment).

### 3. Derived: TD/MS ratio (Inference, not in the paper)

- Ratio of means, Tables 1-2: men 1.63, 1.76, 1.92, 1.96, 2.01; women 1.51, 1.63, 1.88, 1.91, 2.05; total 1.56,
  1.69, 1.90, 1.93, 2.03 (L1-L5). A size-free shape cue that rises monotonically, steepest L1-L3. It is a ratio
  of group means, not a mean of individual ratios.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- T12/L1 (L1): widest MS in both sexes (14.28 men, 14.38 women) and narrowest TD (23.32, 21.69) (p. 22, PDF 2;
  p. 23, PDF 3). The authors cite Davis: L1 usually lies at the functional transition between the stiff thoracic
  and mobile lumbar spine, houses the lower end of the conus medullaris, and sits where the curve turns from
  thoracic convexity to lumbar concavity; the lower cord is displaced dorsally when erect, so the sagittal
  diameter must be capacious there; the canal's width at L1 may reflect both the size of its contents and an
  adaptation that protects this transitional region (p. 24, PDF 4). Fig. 2's axial L1 slice shows both
  kidneys beside the vertebra (p. 23, PDF 3; Inference: read from the image, not stated). They state the
  widest MS at L1 holds "as in the other populations" (p. 24, PDF 4; but see Gaps).
- L1-L2: women's MS slightly exceeds men's (p. 24, PDF 4). The IPD increase caudal to L1-L2 is explained by the
  cauda equina roots running laterally to their foramina (p. 24, PDF 4).
- L3: narrowest MS in both sexes (12.63 men, 12.12 women); Kapoor et al. (South Indian cadavers, MS 13.06-14.75
  mm over L1-L5) held L3 to be the pivot of the level-to-level change in size and so the level likelier to
  suffer stenosis and nerve compression; the authors agree and suggest L3 narrowing could underlie their
  patients' symptoms (pp. 23-24, PDF 3-4; p. 25, PDF 5 conclusion).
- L4: MS only slightly above L3 (men 12.88, women 12.54; Table 1, p. 22, PDF 2); TD 25.23 and 23.93, still
  significantly different by sex (Table 2, p. 24, PDF 4).
- L5 and L5/S1: widest TD (27.89, 27.50) with no sex difference; MS rises again (13.91, 13.40) and is most
  variable (SD 2.06, 1.68). Amonoo-Kuofi's reading, cited: the L5 increase accommodates the sacral roots, which
  would otherwise bowstring during angular movement between the mobile lumbar spine and the fixed sacrum at the
  lumbosacral junction (p. 24, PDF 4).

## Measurement error and reliability

- No intra- or inter-observer data, no repeat measurements, no statement of who measured (p. 22, PDF 2).
- Instrument: viewer readout 0.1 mm (Figs. 1-2, p. 23, PDF 3); slice thickness 4 mm (p. 22, PDF 2); field of
  view and pixel size not stated. Inference: a 4 mm axial slice places "mid-pedicle" only to within a few mm of
  pedicle height, and a 4 mm sagittal slice that misses the midline shortens MS; neither is quantified.
- Inference: MRI shows cortical bone dark, next to dark ligamentum flavum and epidural structures, so the bony
  spinolaminar landmark is less sharp on T2 than on CT. From Table 3 (p. 25, PDF 5), this study's MS runs
  below every other series except El-Rakhawy's Egyptian cadavers: by about 1.2-2.7 mm against Amonoo-Kuofi's
  dry bone, 3.8-4.4 mm against Al-Anazi's CT, 0.4-3.4 mm against Alam's CT and 0.8-2.6 mm against Varol's CT,
  and also 1.9-2.8 mm against Shukri's MRI (Inference: arithmetic). Since another MRI series is also higher,
  modality alone does not explain the gap; population, symptomatic sampling and landmark choice may all add.
- Fig. 1's overlay gives a 4.3 mm sagittal slice against the 4 mm in the methods (p. 23, PDF 3; p. 22, PDF 2).
- The only spread available is the between-subject SD (Tables 1-2).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Context: lumbar spinal stenosis matters through compression of the cauda equina by narrowing of the bony
  ring, as opposed to soft-tissue impingement; reduced interpedicular distance has been proposed as one cause of
  primary narrowing (Nelson); stenosis from a reduced mid-sagittal diameter is reported in the cervical as well
  as the lumbar spine (p. 21, PDF 1).
- Prevalence cited: Shobeiri et al., LSS in 37% of patients with sciatica and 11% of patients with low back pain
  (p < 0.001); the North American Spine Society, about 20% of adults (5% central, 15% lateral stenosis) (p. 21,
  PDF 1). Denominators of those studies are not given here.
- Thresholds cited: Verbiest, from his CT work on developmental (idiopathic) narrowing of the bony lumbar
  canal, AP diameter < 12 mm relative stenosis and < 10 mm absolute stenosis (p. 23, PDF 3); Inference:
  these are bony-canal thresholds, so applying them to a T2 MRI value that may sit inside the bony wall flags
  more canals; Eisenstein and Verbiest, midline sagittal diameter < 12 mm pathological, <= 10 mm can compress the
  cauda equina without any additional compressive agent (p. 24, PDF 4). Ullrich et al. (CT): normal adult
  sagittal diameter 14-15 mm (p. 22, PDF 2).
- Inference (normal approximation from Table 1): in this symptomatic Indian sample about 33% of men and 46% of
  women fall below 12 mm at L3, and about 4% and 6% below 10 mm; a 12 mm cut-off drawn from other populations
  would flag a large share of these patients at L3, so the threshold is population-dependent.
- The authors call MS the clinically most significant canal dimension, reasoning from TD being the larger one
  (p. 21, PDF 1; p. 25, PDF 5); they also say knowledge of both diameters helps detect LSS (p. 25, PDF 5).
- The "hour-glass" sagittal profile (L3 narrowest) is offered as the anatomical reason L3 is prone to stenosis
  and low back pain (p. 23, PDF 3; p. 24, PDF 4; p. 25, PDF 5).
- Canal growth reflects postural, mechanical and genetic factors, and canal studies show ethnic and racial as
  well as age and sex differences; the canal's room lets its contents move without harm from tension and
  pressure, so any reduction of the normal size could cause low back pain (p. 23, PDF 3); differences between
  populations are attributed to racial, ethnic and environmental factors (p. 25, PDF 5).
- Pitfall: TD shows sexual dimorphism and MS does not (p. 25, PDF 5); Inference: so sex-specific norms are
  needed for TD only, and at L5 not even for TD (Table 2, p. 24, PDF 4).
- Pitfall (Inference): radiographic IPD series in Table 4 (Hinck, Piera, Tacar, Nirvan) are film values with
  magnification; comparing them with MRI or CT true-size values overstates the population differences.
- No content on screw sizing, safe zones, Marfan syndrome or achondroplasia.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. On a mid-sagittal T2 MRI or CT reformat, measure MS at each lumbar level from the mid-height point of the
   posterior body wall (where the basivertebral vein exits) to the most anterior part of the spinous process,
   that is the spinolaminar junction (p. 22, PDF 2; Fig. 1, p. 23, PDF 3); draw it roughly perpendicular to
   the local canal axis (Inference: read from Fig. 1, not stated).
2. On an axial image through the middle of the pedicles, measure IPD between the medial pedicle cortices
   (p. 22, PDF 2; Fig. 2, p. 23, PDF 3).
3. Expect MS to be U-shaped over L1-L5 with its minimum at L3 (L4 nearly equal) and maximum at L1; use the
   turning point across three or more consecutive vertebrae, never one value, since adjacent steps (0.25-1.4 mm)
   are smaller than the between-person SDs (1.2-2.1 mm) (Table 1, p. 22, PDF 2; Fig. 3, p. 23, PDF 3; the
   steps and the rule are Inference). Expect individuals to depart from the group shape: the example subject of
   Fig. 1 bottoms out at the fourth line, not the third (Inference from Fig. 1).
4. Expect IPD to rise monotonically from L1 to L5 with the biggest step at L4->L5 (about +2.7 mm in men, +3.6 mm
   in women); use that jump as a caudal anchor for L5, and do not use IPD to separate L1 from L2 (Table 2,
   p. 24, PDF 4; the anchor rule is Inference).
5. Use the rising TD/MS ratio (about 1.5-1.6 at L1 to 2.0 at L5) as a size-free ordering check (Inference from
   Tables 1-2).
6. Condition IPD on sex at L1-L4 (men about 1.3-1.6 mm wider), not at L5; do not condition MS on sex (Tables 1-2,
   pp. 22, 24, PDF 2, 4).
7. Before comparing an IPD with radiographic norms (Table 4, p. 25, PDF 5), correct for AP magnification; before
   comparing an MS with other norms (Table 3, p. 25, PDF 5), expect this study's MS to run about 0.4-4.4 mm
   lower than the CT, dry-bone and other MRI series, El-Rakhawy's Egyptian cadavers excepted (Inference:
   arithmetic from Table 3).
8. Before applying these norms, check the lumbar count: the series excluded congenital anomalies and states no
   numbering rule (p. 22, PDF 2); renumber a transitional spine first (Inference: the paper gives no rule).
9. Flag MS < 12 mm as relative and < 10 mm as absolute stenosis (Verbiest, p. 23, PDF 3; p. 24, PDF 4), but
   expect many symptomatic Indian patients near 12 mm at L3 (Inference from Table 1).
10. Do not trust the printed L1 MS p value (0.398); recomputed from Table 1 it is about 0.73 (Inference). The
    conclusion of no sex difference is unaffected.
11. Inference throughout, from Fig. 2 (p. 23, PDF 3) and the Panjabi comparison below: on an axial T2 MRI,
    expect the IPD's ends to sit on the canal's soft-tissue margin where pedicle cortex is faint; on CT or a
    bone mask, measure between the medial pedicle cortices, and expect the two to differ by about a millimetre,
    not more, since this study's MRI IPD lies within 1.3 mm of Panjabi's dry-bone canal width.

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13, rib
anomalies or any other variant; congenital anomalies were excluded uncounted (p. 22, PDF 2).

(b) Measures:
- Canal depth (Panjabi SCD) corresponds to MS but is not the same quantity. Here MS is MRI T2, at mid-body
  height, body to spinolaminar junction. Values 14.34, 13.43, 12.35, 12.70, 13.63 mm (L1-L5) against Panjabi
  1992's SCD 19.0, 18.2, 17.5, 18.6, 19.7 mm (from the panjabi1992 note in this set): 4.7-6.1 mm lower, with the
  same L3 minimum. Inference: modality (MRI cortex and ligamentum flavum), landmark height (mid-body rather than
  the arch's inner surface), population (Indian, symptomatic, smaller build) and degeneration all push MS down;
  a value from a CT bone mask will read near Panjabi, not near this paper. Do not pool without a definitional
  offset; use the shape (L3 minimum, L1 and L5 higher), not the level of the values.
- Canal width (Panjabi SCW) corresponds to TD/IPD: here 22.42, 22.64, 23.45, 24.52, 27.68 mm against Panjabi
  SCW 23.7 at L1 to 27.1 at L5 (from the panjabi1992 note): close, so MRI IPD at mid-pedicle and dry-bone SCW
  agree within about 1.3 mm (1.3 mm below Panjabi at L1, 0.6 mm above at L5; Inference: arithmetic). A
  mask-derived width must be taken between the medial pedicle walls at mid-pedicle height, not at the lateral
  recess or mid-body (Inference: to match this paper's definition, p. 22, PDF 2).
- Candidate additions: a sex term on canal width (L1-L4 only); the TD/MS ratio as a size-free shape feature;
  population offsets from Tables 3-4 (with radiographic magnification removed first).

(c) Measurement error: none reported. Use at least the viewer's 0.1 mm resolution plus a slice-placement term
(4 mm slices; Inference: on the order of 0.5-1 mm for MS and IPD, unmeasured). Between-subject SDs for the
generator's spread: MS 1.16-2.06 mm, TD 1.29-2.76 mm (Tables 1-2).

## Gaps and conflicts

- Unstated: level identification and numbering; transitional segments; how many patients were excluded for
  congenital anomalies; who measured; repeatability; axial slice angulation; FOV and pixel size; mean age; any
  body-size measure; any disc-level or foraminal measure; anything above L1 or below L5; how the mid-sagittal
  slice and the mid-pedicle axial slice were chosen.
- Methods against figure: slice thickness 4 mm in the methods (p. 22, PDF 2), 4.3 mm on Fig. 1's sagittal
  overlay (p. 23, PDF 3).
- Text against table: the male-female TD difference at L1-L4 is called 1-1.5 mm (p. 23, PDF 3; p. 24, PDF 4);
  Table 2 gives 1.63 and 1.54 mm at L1 and L3.
- Table p value: L1 MS p 0.398 (Table 1) does not match the t-test recomputed from its own means and SDs (about
  0.73 two-sided, pooled or Welch; the other four MS and five TD p values reproduce within rounding).
  Inference: a typo; a one-sided value would be about 0.36, which does not match either.
- Abstract against results: the abstract says TD is significantly larger in men (p. 21, PDF 1) without noting
  that L5 is not (Table 2).
- Text against Table 3: "as in the other populations" the widest MS is at L1 (p. 24, PDF 4), but El-Rakhawy's
  widest is L2 and Al-Anazi and Varol give no L1; the "hour-glass" is said to occur in the Pakistani population
  (p. 23, PDF 3), but Alam's values in Table 3 fall to L4 in men and L5 in women, and in the Nigerian series
  (also cited for the hour-glass) the female minimum is at L4 (14.10 against 14.20 at L3). MS is said to be
  smaller than in previous studies (p. 25, PDF 5), but El-Rakhawy's Egyptian values are smaller at L3-L5.
- Text against Table 3: Amonoo-Kuofi is cited as supporting no sex difference in MS (p. 24, PDF 4), yet his
  values in Table 3 have women 0.7-1.5 mm narrower at every level (Inference: arithmetic; the amonookuofi1985
  note in this set says that paper reports females narrower at every level and ran no significance test).
- Text against Table 4: IPD is said to rise steadily L1-L5 in all populations (p. 24, PDF 4), but Alam's
  Pakistani values dip at L3 in men (24.13 after 24.34) and at L2 and L3 in women (23.46, 22.36 after 23.50).
- Reasoning: MS called the most significant dimension because TD is the largest (p. 21, PDF 1; p. 25, PDF 5) is
  not argued.
- Trefoil: the authors attribute the axial trefoil shape to the caudal widening of TD (p. 23, PDF 3). Inference,
  from the eisenstein1977 note in this set: Eisenstein found the trefoil canal in 66 of 443 skeletons, almost
  always at L5, as a normal variant, not as a feature of every caudal lumbar canal; the two readings differ.
- Cross-paper (from the notes in this set): Table 3's Amonoo-Kuofi MS values and Table 4's Amonoo-Kuofi IPD
  values match those papers' own tables (amonookuofi1985, amonookuofi1982 notes); Table 4's Hinck values match
  the hinck1966 note's adult sex means at L1 (25.9/24.3) and L5 (30.7/29.0), the only lumbar levels that note
  lists by sex. Table 3 labels Amonoo-Kuofi 1985 a cadaveric study, which fits that note (dry bone, caliper).
  Amonoo-Kuofi measured MS at the narrowest point near the cranial ring and Eisenstein at a similar point, not
  at mid-body height as here, so the Table 3 comparison mixes definitions as well as modalities.
- Fig. 1's example subject has its MS minimum at L4 (Inference about level labels), a reminder that the group
  L3 minimum does not hold in every person.
