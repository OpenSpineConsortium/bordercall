## What the paper is

- Design: descriptive anatomical study in two parts, dry bone morphometry and cadaver dissection, by authors from the Department of Orthopaedic Surgery (Tri-Service General Hospital) and the Department of Anatomy, National Defense Medical Center, Taipei, Taiwan (p. 1215, PDF 1). The specimens were obtained from that Department of Anatomy (p. 1216, PDF 2).
- Dry bone arm: 15 sets of human vertebrae, 8 male and 7 female; each vertebra from T1 to L5 separated and measured individually (p. 1216, PDF 2). The discussion calls the same material "15 whole dry vertebrae" (p. 1220, PDF 6). Inference: if every set was complete, 15 x 17 = 255 vertebrae and 30 pedicles per level (15 left, 15 right); the paper never states the count per level nor any missing or damaged specimen.
- Cadaver arm: 6 cadavers, 3 male and 3 female, placed prone; the posterior lower thoracic and lumbar spine exposed by removing all soft tissue, then the spinous processes, laminae, superior and inferior facets and transverse processes removed to expose the dura, the nerve roots and the pedicles; T11 to L5 measured, every paired structure on both sides (p. 1216, PDF 2; Fig. 3, p. 1217, PDF 3). Inference: 6 x 7 = 42 levels, 84 sides.
- Population: all cadavers Taiwanese and of "average body size for the population" (p. 1216, PDF 2); the paper says nothing of the dry sets' ethnicity beyond their source in Taipei. No ages, statures or weights are reported for either arm.
- Exclusion: cadavers with gross deformity such as scoliosis or kyphosis were excluded (p. 1216, PDF 2). No exclusion criteria are stated for the dry sets.
- Instruments: Vernier calipers with 0.1 mm resolution and a standard goniometer accurate to 1 deg for the dry vertebrae (p. 1216, PDF 2). The instrument for the cadaver distances and angles is not named.
- Measures: six per dry vertebra (pedicle width, pedicle height, safe zone width, safe zone height, transverse angle, sagittal angle); five per cadaver level (transverse root angle and four distances from the pedicle cortex to the neural structures: superior, inferior and lateral to the nerve root, medial to the dural sac) (p. 1216, PDF 2).
- Statistics: data "analyzed and averaged", presented as mean +/- SD per level and side; paired (left and right) data compared with Student's t test (p. 1216, PDF 2). Whether the test was paired, and the significance level, are not stated. No p values, no counts per cell, no sex-split results are printed.
- Figures 4 to 6 (p. 1219 to 1220, PDF 5 to 6) are bar and line plots by level. Inference from reading the plotted points against the tables: they plot the left-side means and SDs (for example Fig. 6A shows T9 near 10.9 deg, the left value, not the right 14.1; Fig. 5B shows L2 near 15.2 mm, the left value).
- Level identification: not described. The vertebrae came as sets and were separated T1 to L5; how the sets were numbered, and whether any set had a transitional segment, is not said. Inference: the sets were presumably labelled by the anatomy department.
- Purpose (p. 1216, PDF 2): to describe pedicle morphometry, the relation of the pedicles to the adjacent neural structures, and a potential safe distance around the pedicle for "through-pedicle" procedures.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing of transitional vertebrae, rib counts, L6, T13, sacralisation, lumbarisation or block vertebrae, and gives no prevalence of any anomaly. Inference: it treats every set as T1 to L5, i.e. 12 thoracic and 5 lumbar vertebrae (p. 1216, PDF 2). The only selection on anatomy is the exclusion of cadavers with gross deformity such as scoliosis or kyphosis (p. 1216, PDF 2). The regional difference it stresses, that the T11 and T12 roots run nearly parallel to the ribs (Table 4 footnote, p. 1219, PDF 5), is normal anatomy, not a variant.

## Measures, each as a recipe

Side-averaged means below are computed here from the printed left and right means (Inference: arithmetic only); every printed value is in the table cited. "SD separation" between adjacent levels is the difference of side-averaged means over the root mean square of the four SDs (Inference: arithmetic only).

### Pedicle width (PW)

- Definition: the narrowest dimension of the pedicle in the transverse plane (Fig. 1 caption, p. 1217, PDF 3); the methods name it the width of the pedicle isthmus ("anatomic width, height of the pedicle isthmus", p. 1216, PDF 2). Measured on dry bone with calipers, so it is the outer (cortex to cortex) width. Inference from Fig. 1a: the PW bar is drawn across the pedicle roughly perpendicular to its axis; the paper does not say so in words.
- Levels: T1 to L5, both sides (Table 1, p. 1218, PDF 4).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: isolate the pedicle as the part of the posterior arch between the posterior wall of the body and the articular processes and transverse process base; fit the pedicle axis (line through the centroids of successive cross-sections from the body to the posterior arch); in planes perpendicular to that axis, take the mediolateral extent of the outer contour and keep the minimum along the axis. Inference from memory: SPINEPS's subregions put the pedicle inside the vertebral arch label with the laminae, with no pedicle label of its own, so the cut must be made geometrically. At 1 mm voxels the mid-thoracic pedicle is only 3 to 4 voxels wide, so partial volume and a one-voxel erosion or dilation change the value by roughly 25 percent.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: on an AP radiograph or AP fluoroscopy, the transverse diameter of the pedicle oval approximates PW when the beam is parallel to the pedicle axis (tilted by the transverse and sagittal angles below); a straight AP beam sees the pedicle obliquely, least so at T10 to L1 where the transverse angle is smallest (about 8 to 9 deg) and most at T1 to T3 and L5 (23 to 28 deg). Magnification must be removed, or the value used as a ratio to neighbouring levels or to the body width. Not obtainable from a lateral view.
- Across levels (side-averaged, Table 1, p. 1218, PDF 4; text p. 1216 and 1218, PDF 2 and 4; Fig. 4A, p. 1219, PDF 5): T1 7.65, T2 5.45, T3 4.0, T4 3.5 (minimum), T5 3.8, T6 3.95, T7 4.4, T8 4.8, T9 5.25, T10 5.65, T11 7.2, T12 7.55, L1 6.45, L2 7.2, L3 9.15, L4 11.9, L5 17.6 mm (maximum). The text puts the narrowest at T4 (3.5 mm) or T5 (3.8 mm) and the widest at L5 (17.6 mm); the thoracic width falls sharply from T1 to T4, then rises gradually to T12; the lumbar width rises gradually L1 to L4 and sharply at L5 (p. 1216 to 1218, PDF 2 to 4). The abstract names T4 alone as narrowest (p. 1215, PDF 1). L1 is narrower than T11 and T12 (Inference: a dip at the thoracolumbar junction).
- Adjacent-level separation (Inference): T1/T2 2.3 SD, T2/T3 1.9, T10/T11 1.2, T12/L1 0.7 (in the reverse direction), L2/L3 1.1, L3/L4 1.4, L4/L5 2.3; T4 to T10 adjacent pairs 0.2 to 0.45 SD, i.e. not separable.
- Sex, age, size, population: the text says the extremes held "in both males and females" (p. 1218, PDF 4) but gives no sex-split values. Taiwanese specimens. The authors call their general trend consistent with Panjabi (thoracic), Olsewski (lumbar), Zindrick and Hou (thoracolumbar), but their L5 width larger than Hou's and Berry's (p. 1220, PDF 6). Hou's series was itself a Chinese population (reference 14, p. 1221, PDF 7); Inference: the L5 difference is therefore not plainly an ethnic one, and more likely reflects where along the widening L5 pedicle the width was taken.
- Error: SD between specimens 0.6 to 1.5 mm from T1 to T12 and 1.6 to 2.7 mm from L1 to L5 (Table 1, p. 1218, PDF 4); caliper resolution 0.1 mm; no observer reliability reported. Width and height varied between individuals and between levels but did not differ significantly between left and right (p. 1216, PDF 2).

### Pedicle height (PH)

- Definition: the narrowest dimension of the pedicle in the sagittal plane (Fig. 1 caption, p. 1217, PDF 3); outer caliper dimension, craniocaudal. Inference from Fig. 1b: the PH bar is drawn across the pedicle roughly perpendicular to the oblique pedicle axis.
- Levels: T1 to L5, both sides (Table 1, p. 1218, PDF 4).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: along the same pedicle axis, in planes perpendicular to it, take the craniocaudal extent of the outer contour and keep the minimum; or, in a parasagittal plane through the pedicle centre, the minimum craniocaudal extent between the superior and inferior pedicle cortices.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: on a true lateral radiograph the pedicle is the bar between two neural foramina; its craniocaudal height at the narrowest point approximates PH (the two sides superimpose; obliquity blurs it). Magnification as for any lateral film.
- Across levels (side-averaged, Table 1, p. 1218, PDF 4; Fig. 4B, p. 1219, PDF 5): T1 8.65 (minimum), T2 10.3, T3 10.4, T4 10.3, T5 10.55, T6 10.2, T7 10.4, T8 10.85, T9 12.35, T10 13.65, T11 15.1, T12 15.1 (maximum, shared), L1 13.65, L2 14.05, L3 13.9, L4 12.75, L5 12.5 mm. Text: the thoracic height rises gradually T1 to T12; the lumbar height falls gradually L2 to L5, and L1 (13.7 mm) is slightly lower than L2 (14.1 mm); in both sexes the maximum, 15.1 mm, falls at T11 or T12 and the minimum, 8.7 mm, at T1 (p. 1218, PDF 4). Inference: a plateau T2 to T8, a rise T8 to T11, a drop at T12/L1.
- Adjacent-level separation (Inference): T1/T2 1.7 SD, T8/T9 1.2, T9/T10 0.9, T10/T11 1.0, T12/L1 1.0 (falling); all other pairs 0.0 to 0.6 SD.
- Width to height ratio (Inference, from the side-averaged means; magnification-free on an image): T1 0.88, T2 0.53, T3 0.38, T4 0.34, T5 0.36, T6 0.39, T7 0.42, T8 0.44, T9 0.43, T10 0.41, T11 0.48, T12 0.50, L1 0.47, L2 0.51, L3 0.66, L4 0.93, L5 1.41. Only L5 has a pedicle wider than it is tall.
- Sex, age, size, population: as for PW; the authors say their pedicle heights were smaller than those of the series they compare with (p. 1220, PDF 6).
- Error: SD between specimens 0.8 to 1.6 mm T1 to T12, 1.4 to 2.3 mm L1 to L5 (Table 1, p. 1218, PDF 4); no reliability data; no side difference (p. 1216, PDF 2).

### Safe zone width

- Definition: in the top (overhead) view, the distance from the lateral cortex of the vertebral body to the medial cortex of the ipsilateral pedicle (p. 1216, PDF 2). The authors call the safe zone the "far-out boundary of peri-pedicle area" that can be identified on clinical imaging (p. 1220, PDF 6). Fig. 2a and 2c (p. 1217, PDF 3) draw two lines parallel to the midline, one on the lateral body border and one on the lateral pedicle cortex, so the distance is mediolateral (Inference from the figure).
- The T1 to T10 rule: in the upper thoracic vertebrae the pedicle inclines more cranially and laterally, so that its cortex lies outside the upper end plate and the lateral border of the body (Fig. 2a and 2b caption, p. 1217, PDF 3); the measured safe zone width and height were then smaller than the pedicle dimensions in T1 to T10, and the authors set the true safe zone equal to the pedicle dimensions there (p. 1216, PDF 2). In the lower thoracic and lumbar vertebrae the pedicle cortex lies within the borders of the body (Fig. 2c and 2d). The caption labels the difference between pedicle cortex and body border as a (top views, mediolateral) and b (side views, craniocaudal) in all four panels; Inference from the figure: a and b are what the pedicle overhangs the body in T1 to T10 and what the body margin adds to the pedicle in T11 to L5. Table 1's T1 to T10 safe zone columns are copies of the pedicle columns, value for value, not independent measurements. Inference: the rule is applied by level range, not specimen by specimen, so the reported value is not the larger of the two dimensions per pedicle.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: in the vertebra's own axial frame (perpendicular to the end plates), on each side take the mediolateral coordinate of the most medial point of the pedicle isthmus and of the most lateral point of the body; the difference is the measured safe zone width; apply the T1 to T10 rule above to match the paper, or better keep the raw value.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: an AP view preserves mediolateral distances as a top view does, so on a true AP radiograph or fluoroscopic image the distance from the medial edge of the pedicle oval to the lateral edge of the body on the same side approximates it (with magnification and rotation errors). The authors call these preliminary data practicable and useful in fluoroscopy-guided spinal surgery (p. 1220, PDF 6).
- Across levels (side-averaged, Table 1, p. 1218, PDF 4; Fig. 5A, p. 1219, PDF 5): T11 7.4, T12 8.35, L1 8.4, L2 9.9, L3 10.15, L4 12.5, L5 17.7 mm; T1 to T10 equal to PW. Printed ranges: 3.4 to 7.7 mm T1 to T10, 7.2 to 10.0 mm T11 to L2, 10.1 to 17.8 mm L3 to L5 (p. 1218, PDF 4); abstract 7.2 to 17.8 mm T11 to L5 (p. 1215, PDF 1). Inference: the excess over PW is 0.2 (T11), 0.8 (T12), 1.95 (L1), 2.7 (L2), 1.0 (L3), 0.6 (L4), 0.1 (L5) mm, largest at L1 and L2.
- Sex, age, size, population: none given.
- Error: SD 1.1 to 2.7 mm T11 to L5 (Table 1); no reliability; no side comparison reported.

### Safe zone height

- Definition: in the lateral (side) view, the distance measured from the body's upper end plate down to the inferior cortex of the pedicle on the same side (p. 1216, PDF 2). Same T1 to T10 rule as the width: set equal to PH there.
- The discussion adds that the measured safe zone height was below PH at T1 to T6, did not differ from PH at T7 to T10, and exceeded PH at T11 to L5; the safe zone width was below PW at T1 to T10 and above it at T11 to L5; the true safe zone in T1 to T10 is therefore the pedicle itself, because the pedicle angles incline more superiorly and laterally there (p. 1220, PDF 6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: in the vertebra's own midsagittal frame, take the craniocaudal distance from the superior end plate (top of the body subregion, at its posterior part) to the lowest point of the pedicle isthmus on the same side.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: on a true lateral view, from the superior end plate to the inferior border of the pedicle (the roof of the neural foramen below it).
- Across levels (side-averaged, Table 1, p. 1218, PDF 4; Fig. 5B, p. 1219, PDF 5): T11 15.85, T12 16.65, L1 16.0, L2 15.7, L3 16.15, L4 14.4, L5 15.05 mm; T1 to T10 equal to PH. Printed ranges: 8.6 to 13.7 mm T1 to T10, 13.9 to 16.7 mm T11 to L5 (p. 1218, PDF 4). Inference: the excess over PH is 0.75 to 2.55 mm from T11 to L5. It varies only between 14.4 and 16.65 mm from T11 to L5, so it separates no lumbar level well.
- Sex, age, size, population: none given.
- Error: SD 1.3 to 2.3 mm T11 to L5 (Table 1); no reliability; no side comparison reported.

### Transverse angle of the pedicle (TA)

- Definition: the angle the pedicle axis makes with a line drawn parallel to the vertebral midline, in the transverse plane (p. 1216, PDF 2; Fig. 1a, p. 1217, PDF 3). Goniometer, 1 deg. Inference from Fig. 1a: the axis converges toward the midline anteriorly (medial angulation); no sign convention or axis construction is printed.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: in the vertebra's own axial plane (end-plate based, not the scanner axial plane), the angle between the fitted pedicle axis projected onto that plane and the midsagittal plane of the instance (from the body and spinous process centroids or a left-right symmetry fit).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: not measurable on a single lateral or AP film; on AP fluoroscopy it is the oblique tilt that brings the pedicle end-on. Axial CT is the natural source.
- Across levels (side-averaged, Table 2, p. 1218, PDF 4; Fig. 6A, p. 1220, PDF 6): T1 28.2 (maximum), T2 26.35, T3 23.3, T4 22.85, T5 18.2, T6 15.4, T7 12.8, T8 13.8, T9 12.5, T10 9.0, T11 8.0, T12 7.9 (minimum), L1 8.45, L2 12.35, L3 15.25, L4 18.9, L5 24.35 deg. The text gives the largest at T1 (mean 28.2 deg) and the shallowest at T12 (mean 7.9 deg), decreasing T1 to T12 and increasing L1 to L5 (p. 1218 to 1219, PDF 4 to 5); the discussion places the smallest angles at T10 to L1, increasing above and below (p. 1220, PDF 6). Inference: T11, T12 and L1 are indistinguishable by TA (0.1 to 0.3 SD); the clearest steps are T9/T10 (2.0 SD), T4/T5 (1.6), L1/L2 and L4/L5 (1.4 each), T6/T7 (1.2).
- Sex, age, size, population: none given.
- Error: SD 1.2 to 4.5 deg thoracic, 2.5 to 4.7 deg lumbar (Table 2). Left and right means differ by up to 3.2 deg at T9 (10.9 left vs 14.1 right), and by 1.6 to 2.3 deg at T3, T5, L2, L3, L4 and L5 (Table 2); the paper reports no side comparison for the angles, though the methods say all paired data were t tested (p. 1216, PDF 2).

### Sagittal angle of the pedicle (SA)

- Definition: the angle between the pedicle axis and the superior border of the vertebral body, in the sagittal plane (p. 1216, PDF 2; Fig. 1b, p. 1217, PDF 3). Inference from Fig. 1b: the axis runs from posterosuperior to anteroinferior relative to the superior end plate line; no sign is printed.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: in a parasagittal plane through the pedicle, the angle between the fitted pedicle axis and the superior end plate line of the same instance (not the scanner axis and not the disc).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: on a true lateral radiograph, possible in principle as the angle between the superimposed pedicle bar's axis and the superior end plate; the paper does not do it.
- Across levels (side-averaged, Table 2, p. 1218, PDF 4; Fig. 6B, p. 1220, PDF 6): T1 11.6, T2 18.9 (maximum), T3 16.6, T4 15.0, T5 17.85, T6 16.5, T7 13.4, T8 14.15, T9 13.05, T10 13.45, T11 12.15, T12 10.7, L1 6.1, L2 5.45, L3 5.0, L4 3.35, L5 2.9 deg (minimum). Printed: largest T2 (18.9 deg), smallest L5 (2.9 deg) (p. 1219, PDF 5), a decreasing trend from T2 to L5 (p. 1220, PDF 6). Inference: the clearest junction cue in the paper is the drop T12/L1 (10.7 to 6.1 deg, 2.8 SD); also T1/T2 (2.6 SD) and L3/L4 (2.3 SD). In the lumbar spine the pedicle axis is within about 3 to 6 deg of the upper end plate, in the thoracic spine 11 to 19 deg.
- Sex, age, size, population: none given.
- Error: SD 1.4 to 3.5 deg thoracic, 0.6 to 2.1 deg lumbar (Table 2); goniometer 1 deg; side differences up to 1.8 deg (T7) (Table 2).

### Transverse root angle (TRA), cadavers

- Definition: the angle between the midline of the dura and the axis of the spinal nerve root (p. 1216, PDF 2; Fig. 3a caption, p. 1217, PDF 3), after removal of the spinous processes, laminae, facets and transverse processes; the caption says no mobilisation of the neural structures during dissection was evident.
- Values (Table 3, p. 1218, PDF 4): T11 70.6 left, 75.2 right; T12 58.6, 60.0; L1 40.2, 40.0; L2 38.0, 38.0; L3 35.4, 37.8; L4 37.2, 40.0; L5 28.6, 29.0 deg. Ranges 58.6 to 75.2 deg at T11 and T12, 28.6 to 40.2 deg in the lumbar spine; no significant side difference; the lower thoracic roots are positioned more cranially, nearly parallel to the rib cage (p. 1219, PDF 5). Inference: a larger angle to the dural midline is a more horizontal course; the lumbar roots run steeply caudal, most of all at L5.
- From CT or radiograph: Inference: not obtainable from a bony mask or a plain film; MRI or CT myelography would be needed.
- Error: SD 3.4 to 9.4 deg (Table 3).

### Distances from the pedicle cortex to the neural structures, cadavers

- Definition: four linear distances from the pedicle cortex: superior, inferior and lateral to the nerve root, medial to the dural sac (p. 1216, PDF 2; Fig. 3b caption, p. 1217, PDF 3; Table 4, p. 1219, PDF 5). Which root (the one above or below the pedicle) is not named. Inference: superior is the root of the level above, in the foramen above the pedicle; inferior the root exiting beneath the pedicle; lateral the root of the level above after it leaves its foramen and runs caudally past the pedicle.
- Values (Table 4, p. 1219, PDF 5; text p. 1219 to 1220, PDF 5 to 6): superior 4.5 to 5.3 mm at T11 and T12, 4.1 to 6.1 mm lumbar (largest L3, 6.1 both sides); inferior 3.0 to 3.8 mm at T11 and T12, 1.9 to 2.6 mm lumbar (smallest L4 and L5, about 2.0); medial to dura 1.0 to 1.5 mm at T11 and T12, 1.7 to 2.1 mm lumbar in the table (the text says 1.9 to 2.1); lateral more than 10 mm at T11 and T12 (not given as a number, Table 4 footnote), 9.4 and 9.6 mm at L1 falling to 4.5 and 4.7 (L2), 4.2 and 4.3 (L3), 3.5 and 3.5 (L4), 2.4 and 2.6 mm (L5). In the lower thoracic spine the superior distances were slightly greater than the inferior; in the lumbar spine much greater (p. 1219, PDF 5). The lateral distance is greatest at L1 (9.5 mm) and smallest at L5 (2.5 mm), with no significant side difference (p. 1220, PDF 6), and smaller in the lower lumbar spine (p. 1221, PDF 7).
- Abstract summary: mean distances greater superiorly and laterally than inferiorly and medially; lumbar lateral distance 2.4 to 9.6 mm (p. 1215, PDF 1).
- Error: SD 0.1 to 2.5 mm (Table 4); measured in 2D, not as the shortest distance in 3D; roots may be retracted or shrunk post mortem; the authors conclude the true distances are shorter than measured (p. 1221, PDF 7).
- From CT or radiograph: Inference: only the bony end (pedicle cortex) is visible; the dura and roots need MRI or myelography.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- T1: widest thoracic pedicle (7.65 mm, just above T12's 7.55), lowest pedicle height (8.65 mm), largest transverse angle (28.2 deg), sagittal angle 11.6 deg, much lower than T2's 18.9 (Tables 1 and 2, p. 1218, PDF 4). Inference: at the cervicothoracic junction T1 is set apart from T2 by PW (2.3 SD), PH (1.7 SD) and SA (2.6 SD). The paper has no cervical data.
- T2: maximum sagittal angle (18.9 deg) (p. 1219, PDF 5).
- T3 to T6: narrowest pedicles, T4 3.5 mm and T5 3.8 mm the minimum (p. 1218, PDF 4); in the upper thoracic vertebrae the pedicle cortex lies outside the body outline in the top and side views (Fig. 2a, 2b, p. 1217, PDF 3); the measured safe zone height fell below PH at T1 to T6 (p. 1220, PDF 6).
- T7 to T10: measured safe zone height did not differ from PH; safe zone width still below PW (p. 1220, PDF 6). PH rises from T8 to T10 (Table 1).
- T10 to L1: smallest transverse angles (p. 1220, PDF 6).
- T11 and T12: largest pedicle heights (15.1 mm each); roots nearly parallel to the ribs, transverse root angles 58.6 to 75.2 deg; lateral distance to the root more than 10 mm; medial distance to the dura the smallest of all levels measured (1.0 to 1.5 mm); superior distances slightly greater than inferior (4.5 to 5.3 vs 3.0 to 3.8 mm) (Tables 1, 3 and 4, p. 1218 to 1219, PDF 4 to 5); the discussion calls them similar (p. 1220, PDF 6).
- T12/L1 junction: PW falls from 7.55 to 6.45 mm, PH from 15.1 to 13.65 mm, SA from 10.7 to 6.1 deg; the safe zone width hardly changes (8.35 to 8.4 mm) (Tables 1 and 2, p. 1218, PDF 4). Transverse root angle falls from about 59 to 40 deg; the lateral root distance is first given as a number at L1 (9.5 mm) (Tables 3 and 4, p. 1218 to 1219, PDF 4 to 5).
- L1: pedicle narrower and shorter than T12 and L2; with L2, the largest safe zone width excess over PW (Inference from Table 1).
- L2 to L4: PW rises 7.2, 9.15, 11.9 mm; TA rises 12.35, 15.25, 18.9 deg; SA falls 5.45, 5.0, 3.35 deg; lateral root distance 4.6, 4.25, 3.5 mm (Tables 1, 2, 4).
- L5 (lumbosacral side): widest pedicle (17.6 mm), the only pedicle wider than tall (Inference: ratio 1.41), transverse angle 24.35 deg, smallest sagittal angle (2.9 deg), smallest lateral distance to the root (2.5 mm) and among the smallest inferior distances (about 2.0 mm), smallest transverse root angle (28.6 to 29.0 deg) (Tables 1 to 4). No S1 data.
- C2/C3, C7/T1 below T1 and L5/S1 below L5 are not covered.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Instruments: calipers 0.1 mm, goniometer 1 deg (p. 1216, PDF 2); the cadaver instrument is not named. No intra- or inter-observer study, no repeat measurements, no observer count.
- Spread between specimens (Tables 1 to 4) is the only variability given: for pedicle width about 14 to 26 percent of the mean (Inference: T2 0.7 to 0.8 on 5.45 mm, 14 percent; T7 and L1 about 24 to 26 percent; L5 2.6 to 2.7 on 17.6 mm, 15 percent), for pedicle height 8 to 18 percent.
- Table 4 SDs of 0.1 to 0.2 mm (for example medial distance T11 1.0 +/- 0.1) equal the dry-bone caliper's resolution; Inference: implausibly tight for 6 cadavers, suggesting averaging or rounding.
- Cadaver distances are 2D, not the shortest 3D distance; post-mortem root retraction or shrinkage; roots are relatively mobile outside the foramen (p. 1221, PDF 7).
- Left and right compared by t test: no significant difference in pedicle width and height (p. 1216, PDF 2), root angles (p. 1219, PDF 5) and neural distances (p. 1220, PDF 6); no p values; no comparison reported for the safe zones or the pedicle angles.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indications named: posterior pedicle screw instrumentation (Roy-Camille's pedicle screw plating for the lumbar spine, put in 1985); percutaneous vertebroplasty (first by Galibert in 1987, for a C2 haemangioma) and kyphoplasty with an inflatable bone tamp for osteoporotic compression fracture, through a transpedicular or extrapedicular route, which strengthen and stiffen the body and reduce pain (p. 1215, PDF 1); transpedicularly implanted anterior support; revision of pedicle screws, bone biopsy, bone grafting and restoration of the body (p. 1216, PDF 2).
- Complications cited: screw failure and damage to adjacent neurovascular structures; about 10 percent of pedicle screws misplaced (Roy-Camille, put in 1988) and an overall failure rate of 21 percent in cadaveric studies (Weinstein) (p. 1216, PDF 2).
- Earlier work used plain films, CT and cadaver dissection to find the screw entry point (p. 1216, PDF 2); Ebraheim (1997) showed true safe distances exist between pedicles and neural structures (p. 1216, PDF 2), and Louis described where the cord and roots lie relative to the column (p. 1220, PDF 6).
- The "in-out-in" technique enters the body extrapedicularly and is used in the upper and mid-thoracic spine because of its pedicle morphometry (p. 1216, PDF 2; p. 1221, PDF 7). The "all-through-pedicle" concept carries a true safe zone to biopsy, grafting, screws, vertebroplasty and kyphoplasty, even when the upper and lateral pedicle walls are penetrated; the safe zone data are meant to let the surgeon reach the body through the pedicle with more confidence (p. 1221, PDF 7).
- The safe zone is decided by the transverse and sagittal angles as well as the pedicle dimensions, because the pedicle axis is oblique (p. 1220, PDF 6; p. 1221, PDF 7).
- The true safe zone should include the pedicle plus the extrapedicular distance to the neural structures; the authors claim to be first to measure the safe zone width and height and the lateral mean distances (p. 1220 to 1221, PDF 6 to 7), and offer the distances as a safe range if the pedicle is disrupted during surgery (p. 1221, PDF 7).
- Neural risk: medial (to the dura, 1.0 to 2.1 mm) and inferior (to the root, 1.9 to 3.8 mm) are the closest (Table 4, p. 1219, PDF 5); in the lumbar spine superior distances are larger because the roots run caudally; lateral is generous at T11 and T12 (more than 10 mm, roots parallel to the ribs) and at L1 (9.5 mm) and tight at L5 (2.5 mm) (p. 1220 to 1221, PDF 6 to 7). Ebraheim judged superior and lateral sides safer than inferior and medial in the lumbar spine, and lateral, superior and inferior equally safe in the thoracic spine; the authors found their relations no different in substance (p. 1220, PDF 6; p. 1221, PDF 7).
- Ebraheim's transverse root angles for comparison: 57.1 to 84.0 deg at T10 to T12, 33.7 to 39.2 deg lumbar (p. 1220, PDF 6).
- Pitfall of anatomy: in the upper thoracic vertebrae the pedicle projects outside the lateral border of the body (T1 to T10 by the width) and above the upper end plate (T1 to T6 by the height), so a safe zone drawn from the body outline is smaller than the pedicle itself; in T11 to L5 the pedicle sits inside the body outline (Fig. 2 caption, p. 1217, PDF 3; p. 1220, PDF 6).
- Pitfall of measuring: cadaver distances overstate the in-vivo margin (2D measurement, post-mortem change) (p. 1221, PDF 7).
- Screw sizing: no thresholds are given; the paper reports the narrowest pedicles at T4 and T5 (3.5 to 3.8 mm) and the safe zone ranges by region (p. 1218, PDF 4).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. In the lumbar spine, look for the abrupt pedicle-width step between L4 and L5 (side-averaged 11.9 to 17.6 mm, the largest absolute step in the spine) and for L5 as the only pedicle wider than tall; use width-to-height ratios, which need no magnification correction (Table 1, p. 1218, PDF 4). Inference: on an AP image the L5 pedicle is seen obliquely (TA about 24 deg), so compare ratios between neighbours rather than absolute widths.
2. On a lateral image or a sagittal CT plane, read the pedicle sagittal angle against the superior end plate: expect about 10 to 12 deg at T11 and T12 and about 5 to 6 deg at L1 and L2; a drop of about 4.6 deg marks T12/L1 (Table 2, p. 1218, PDF 4). Do not rely on it alone: lumbar SDs reach 2.1 deg. (The lateral radiograph route is Inference; the paper measured dry bone.)
3. Also at T12/L1, expect the pedicle height to peak at T11 and T12 (15.1 mm) and fall at L1 (13.65 mm), and the pedicle width to dip at L1 (6.45 mm) below T12 (7.55 mm) (Table 1, p. 1218, PDF 4).
4. At the top of the thoracic spine, expect T1's pedicle to be wider (7.65 mm) and lower (8.65 mm) than T2's (5.45 and 10.3 mm), its sagittal angle smaller (11.6 vs 18.9 deg) and its transverse angle the largest (28.2 deg) (Tables 1 and 2, p. 1218, PDF 4).
5. Do not try to tell T4 to T10 apart by pedicle width or height alone: adjacent differences are 0.2 to 0.45 SD (Inference from Table 1); count from a junction instead.
6. From a CT instance mask, take PW and PH as the narrowest outer dimension of the pedicle in the transverse and sagittal planes respectively (Fig. 1 caption, p. 1217, PDF 3); Inference: compute them perpendicular to the fitted pedicle axis, as the bars in Fig. 1 are drawn.
7. Measure TA against a line parallel to the vertebra's own midline and SA against its own superior end plate (p. 1216, PDF 2); Inference: never against the scanner axes, which mix in the patient's lordosis, kyphosis and rotation.
8. In T1 to T10, expect the pedicle to reach beyond the body's lateral border on an axial or AP view, and in T1 to T6 above the upper end plate on a lateral view; do not clip the pedicle to the body outline there (Fig. 2, p. 1217, PDF 3; p. 1220, PDF 6).
9. In T11 to L5, compute the safe zone width (medial pedicle wall to lateral body wall, axial or AP) and height (upper end plate to inferior pedicle wall, lateral); expect them to exceed PW by about 0.1 to 2.7 mm and PH by about 0.75 to 2.55 mm on side-averaged means (Inference from Table 1, p. 1218, PDF 4).
10. When planning or checking a trajectory, flag medial breaches at T11 and T12 first (1.0 to 1.5 mm to the dura) and inferior and lateral breaches at L4 and L5 (about 2.0 mm inferior, 2.5 to 3.5 mm lateral) (Table 4, p. 1219, PDF 5); treat these cadaver margins as upper bounds (p. 1221, PDF 7).
11. Average left and right before comparing with these values, but tolerate side differences in TA of up to about 3 deg (T9 3.2 deg, several levels 1.6 to 2.3 deg) (Table 2, p. 1218, PDF 4).
12. Treat these absolute values as one Taiwanese sample of 15 dry spines: the authors found a larger L5 pedicle width than Hou's (also a Chinese population) and Berry's series, and smaller pedicle heights than all the series they compared (p. 1220, PDF 6; reference 14, p. 1221, PDF 7); Inference: scale by a patient size factor and prefer ratios between levels before using them elsewhere.
13. Do not take numbering or transitional anatomy from this paper: it reports no anomaly and does not say how levels were identified (p. 1216, PDF 2).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors: none. The paper reports no prevalence of any transitional or numerical anomaly and does not say how levels were counted.

(b) Measures:
- Pedicle width and height, T1 to L5, correspond to the generator's pedicle width and height (Inference from memory: Panjabi 1991 for the thoracic, cited here as reference 22, and 1992 for the lumbar vertebrae measured them with a 3D digitiser on landmarks, not as a caliper minimum, which can differ by tenths of a millimetre to a millimetre). From a CT mask, the minimum over the isthmus of the outer contour matches Lien's definition; a value taken at a fixed plane, or across the pedicle base, will be larger, most of all at L5 where the pedicle widens into the body.
- Transverse and sagittal pedicle angles, T1 to L5. Lien's sagittal angle is referenced to the superior border of the body; a mask-based angle referenced to the scanner axis or to the bisector of the end plates will be biased by the vertebra's wedge and the lordosis or kyphosis. Inference from memory: Panjabi's pedicle inclination angles use the vertebra's own reference frame, close but not identical to Lien's end-plate line.
- Candidates to add: the width-to-height ratio of the pedicle (size-free; L5 1.41, L4 0.93, L3 0.66, thoracic 0.34 to 0.53, T1 0.88), the safe zone width (AP-visible) and the safe zone height (lateral-visible), both computable from a mask and from radiographs, recorded raw rather than with the T1 to T10 rule.
- Lien's T1 to T10 safe zone values are not measurements; do not use them as independent observations.
- Mask bias: at mid-thoracic widths of 3.5 to 4 mm, a one-voxel boundary error at 1 mm voxels is about 25 percent of the value (Inference); smooth or use subvoxel contours.

(c) Measurement error: none reported beyond instrument resolution (0.1 mm, 1 deg). Use the between-specimen SDs of Tables 1 and 2 as the biological spread per level (PW 0.6 to 2.7 mm, PH 0.8 to 2.3 mm, TA 1.2 to 4.7 deg, SA 0.6 to 3.5 deg) and take measurement error from other papers.

(d) Covariates: 8 male and 7 female dry sets, 3 and 3 cadavers; Taiwanese, cadavers of average body size; ages unknown; no sex-split values though the text states the extremes of width and height held for both sexes (p. 1218, PDF 4). Population differences named by the authors: larger L5 pedicle width than Hou's and Berry's series, smaller pedicle heights than the series compared (p. 1220, PDF 6). No size covariate is given.

## Gaps and conflicts

- No ages, statures, specimen counts per level, sex-split values, p values, significance level or reliability data.
- The pedicle axis, used for both angles and for the safe zone, is never defined in words; only Fig. 1 shows it.
- Methods vs discussion: the methods say the measured safe zone width and height were smaller than the pedicle dimensions throughout T1 to T10 (p. 1216, PDF 2), while the discussion says the height was smaller only at T1 to T6 and did not differ at T7 to T10 (p. 1220, PDF 6); the raw T1 to T10 safe zone values are not printed.
- The abstract calls the safe zone ranges those "in individual pedicle" (p. 1215, PDF 1), but they are the ranges of the left and right level means of Table 1, not of individual specimens.
- The methods say 15 sets of vertebrae (p. 1216, PDF 2); the discussion says 15 whole dry vertebrae (p. 1220, PDF 6). Inference: 15 whole dry spines from T1 to L5.
- Text vs table: the lumbar medial distance to the dura is given as 1.9 to 2.1 mm in the text (p. 1219, PDF 5) but Table 4 shows 1.7 to 2.1 mm (L3 1.7 left, L2 1.8 left, L3 1.8 right).
- The abstract and results say the transverse angle decreases progressively T1 to T12 (p. 1215, PDF 1; p. 1218 to 1219, PDF 4 to 5); Table 2 is not monotonic (T4 left 23.1 above T3 left 22.4; T8 above T7; T9 right 14.1 above T8 right 13.4) and T11 (8.0) and T12 (7.9) are equal within 0.1 deg.
- The sagittal angle is called a decreasing trend from T2 to L5 (p. 1220, PDF 6), but T5 (17.85) exceeds T3 and T4 (Table 2).
- Lateral distances at T11 and T12 are given only as more than 10 mm.
- The roots measured superiorly, inferiorly and laterally are not named by level.
- Inference: a distance measured in a plane between the same two points cannot exceed the 3D distance between them; the authors' remark that their 2D distances are not the true shortest distances (p. 1221, PDF 7) must mean they measured along fixed directions rather than to the nearest point of the root.
- Figures 4 to 6 appear to plot the left side only (Inference from the plotted values), though the text quotes side-averaged values.
- Dates of the cited history disagree with the references: Roy-Camille's first use is put in 1985 and the misplacement report in 1988, but the 1985 statement cites Gertzbein 1990 and Roy-Camille 1986 (references 13 and 25, also on p. 1220, PDF 6) and the 1988 statement cites those two plus Boachie-Adjei 2000 (reference 4); no Roy-Camille paper of 1985 or 1988 is listed (p. 1215 to 1216, PDF 1 to 2; p. 1221 to 1222, PDF 7 to 8).
- The byline prints the first author twice (p. 1215, PDF 1).
- Inference (from memory, not checked against the PDFs in this set): Panjabi 1991 also found the narrowest thoracic pedicles in the mid-thoracic spine and pedicle height rising caudally through the thoracic spine, consistent with Lien; Lien's L5 width of 17.6 mm is at the high end of published L5 values, which differ between series partly by where along the pedicle the width is taken. Check the Panjabi, Zindrick and Olsewski notes for the exact values.
