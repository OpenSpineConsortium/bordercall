## What the paper is

- Design: a laboratory morphometric study of the pedicles at every level from T1 to L5, written to guide pedicle screw fixation (choice of screw diameter, trajectory and length); its stated aim is detailed knowledge of pedicle morphology and of the depth to which screws can be inserted safely at every thoracolumbar level. p. 160 (PDF 1).
- Denominator: "a total of 2,905 pedicle measurements" T1 to L5, abstract p. 160 (PDF 1). The 2,905 is the sum of the five tables' "total studies" lines: 547 (transverse width) + 595 (sagittal width) + 628 (transverse angle) + 522 (sagittal angle) + 613 (depth) = 2,905; of these 2,005 came from specimen roentgenograms (387 + 433 + 410 + 372 + 403) and 900 from CT (160 + 162 + 218 + 150 + 210). Footnotes of Tables 1 to 5, pp. 161-165 (PDF 2-6). It is a count of measurements, not of people or vertebrae. Inference (arithmetic): Table 5 counts each depth study once although each gives two depths (pedicle axis and midline), so the tables hold 2,905 + 613 = 3,518 values. Each table, and each level within a table, has its own N (for example L3: 49 transverse widths, 27 sagittal widths, 68 transverse angles, 28 sagittal angles, 64 depth studies), so the five measures were not all taken on the same vertebrae.
- Population: "postmortem adult spine specimens" plus CT examinations. The paper gives no number of specimens or patients, no age, no sex, no race or ethnicity, no body size, and no country of origin (the authors work in Illinois and California, USA; Inference: a US population of unstated make-up). Some specimens were whole spines, others only thoracic or only lumbar segments. p. 160 (PDF 1).
- Modality and instrument, two routes (p. 160, PDF 1):
  1. Postmortem specimens (whether wet or macerated dry bone is not stated): each vertebra was disarticulated and radiographed singly "in the transverse plane" (Inference: an axial, craniocaudal projection of the isolated vertebra). The vertebra was then sectioned sagittally in the midline and a lateral roentgenogram of each hemisected vertebra was taken. Technique was held constant; the authors state no detectable magnification. Measurements were taken directly from the films (the measuring tool, ruler, caliper or protractor, is not named).
  2. CT: computerized axial tomography examinations were reviewed and measured with the scanner's software. The region most often studied was L3 to S1. No scanner, slice thickness, gantry tilt or reformatting method is given, and the paper does not say how the sagittal-plane measures were taken from CT. Inference: the acknowledgement thanks two CT technologists of Multiplanar Diagnostic Imaging (Torrance, California), which is also the affiliation of author Rothman, p. 160 (PDF 1); multiplanar (sagittal) reformats from that centre would explain how sagittal widths and angles came from CT, but the paper does not say so.
- Levels covered: T1 to L5, every level. No cervical level and no sacral data, although S1 lay in the CT region.
- Level identification and counting: not described. Transitional segments: not mentioned, neither excluded nor handled. Inclusion and exclusion criteria: none stated.
- Statistics: per level N, mean, range, SD and SEM (Tables 1 to 5). Comparisons are said to be significant at P < 0.05, with no test named. Figs 1 to 4 are line plots of the mean by level with bars that match mean +/- 1 SD of the tables (Inference, by matching L5 transverse width 18.0 +/- 4.1 to the plotted bar of about 14 to 22 mm in Fig 1, p. 161; L1 transverse angle 10.9 +/- 2.2 to the bar of about 8.7 to 13.1 deg in Fig 3, p. 163; T12 sagittal angle 11.6 +/- 2.6 to the bar of about 9 to 14 deg in Fig 4, p. 164). Fig 5 is a bar chart of the two depth means by level, p. 165 (PDF 6).
- Side: the paper never says whether left and right pedicles were both measured or how a vertebra's two pedicles enter N (Inference: N probably counts pedicles from both sides, pooled).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about transitional vertebrae, sacralization, lumbarization, L6, T13, rib count, block vertebrae or how levels were numbered (pp. 160-166, PDF 1-7). Inference: because some specimens were isolated thoracic or lumbar segments (p. 160, PDF 1), and every vertebra was then disarticulated, the level of each vertebra could only have been assigned by counting within its segment or from its own shape, by a rule the paper does not give; no rib count or whole-spine count is mentioned. The very wide T12 spreads (transverse angle -17.0 to +14.5 deg, depth along the pedicle axis 23 to 61 mm, Tables 3 and 5, pp. 163 and 165) are what one would expect if the thoracolumbar transitional vertebra, or misnumbered specimens, were mixed into "T12".

## Measures, each as a recipe

### 1. Transverse pedicle isthmus width (TW; in modern terms pedicle width)

- Definition: the narrowest dimension of the pedicle in the transverse plane (the authors' words: "The narrowest dimension in both the transverse and sagittal planes" was taken as the isthmus width). p. 160 (PDF 1). The Fig 1 inset marks a chord across the narrowest mediolateral part of one pedicle on an axial outline of a vertebra, p. 161 (PDF 2).
- Plane: transverse (axial). Instrument: axial-projection films of isolated vertebrae, or axial CT with software calipers.
- Levels: T1 to L5. Table 1 and Fig 1, p. 161 (PDF 2). N 24 to 56 per level; 387 films + 160 CT = 547.
- Values, mean (SD) mm, Table 1, p. 161 (PDF 2): L5 18.0 (4.1), L4 12.9 (2.1), L3 10.3 (2.6), L2 8.9 (2.2), L1 8.7 (2.3), T12 7.1 (2.3), T11 7.8 (2.0), T10 6.3 (1.7), T9 6.1 (1.5), T8 5.9 (1.6), T7 5.3 (1.0), T6 5.2 (1.0), T5 4.5 (0.9), T4 4.7 (1.3), T3 5.6 (1.4), T2 7.0 (1.8), T1 7.9 (1.4). Extremes of range: L5 9.1 to 29.0; T5 3.0 to 7.0; smallest single values 2.5 mm at T8 and T4.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: build a vertebra frame (Inference): z normal to the mean end-plate plane, y anteroposterior in the midsagittal plane (body centroid to spinous process), x left-right. Isolate each pedicle as the bony bridge between the posterior body wall and the branching of the transverse and articular processes (Inference: SPINEPS gives no separate pedicle label to my knowledge; take it from the arch/posterior-element part of the instance next to the body). Find the pedicle axis in the axial plane, step along it, and at each step take the chord perpendicular to the axis; TW is the smallest such chord. To mimic the paper's projection film, project the mask along z before measuring (Inference: a projection gives the envelope of all axial slices, so it reads equal to or wider than any single slice); to mimic its CT route, use the axial slice at the pedicle's mid-height. Measure outer cortex to outer cortex (Inference: the paper does not say outer or inner, but film and CT silhouettes are outer margins). Do not measure along the vertebral x axis: where the pedicle is angled (L5 about 30 deg) an x-axis width reads about 1/cos(angle), roughly 15 percent, too wide (Inference).
- From a radiograph alone: the paper used axial projections of disarticulated vertebrae, which no clinical film gives. Inference: an AP radiograph shows each pedicle as an oval whose mediolateral width approximates TW only after magnification correction and only where the pedicle axis lies near the beam; at L4 and L5, where the pedicle runs 18 to 30 deg medially, the AP oval is the projection of an oblique tube and overstates the isthmus. A lateral radiograph cannot give TW.
- Across levels: largest at L5 and falling steeply to L3 (L5 18.0, L4 12.9, L3 10.3), flat from L2 to L1 (8.9, 8.7), a dip at T12 (7.1) below T11 (7.8), then falling through the mid thoracic spine to the minimum at T5 (4.5), and rising again to T1 (7.9). p. 161 (PDF 2), Fig 1, Table 1. Inference, my effect sizes (difference of means over pooled SD): L5 vs L4 1.6, L4 vs L3 1.1, L3 vs L2 0.6, L2 vs L1 0.1, L1 vs T12 0.7, T12 vs T11 -0.3, T11 vs T10 0.8, T10 vs T9 and T9 vs T8 about 0.1, T6 vs T5 0.7, T3 vs T2 -0.9, T2 vs T1 -0.6. So TW separates L5, L4 and L3 well, separates nothing between L2 and L1 or within T6 to T10, and its cranial rise T4 to T1 separates the upper thoracic levels moderately.
- Sex, age, size, population: none reported. The SD is between-specimen spread pooled over unknown sexes and ages and over two modalities. Inference (SD/mean from Table 1): the coefficient of variation is 0.16 to 0.32, largest at T12 (0.32), so TW is a noisy size measure.
- Error: SEM 0.2 to 0.5 mm (Table 1); no intra- or inter-observer study; no instrument resolution; films said to carry no detectable magnification; CT and film said not to differ significantly (no numbers). pp. 160-161 (PDF 1-2).

### 2. Sagittal pedicle isthmus width (SW; in modern terms pedicle height)

- Definition: the narrowest dimension of the pedicle in the sagittal plane, i.e. its craniocaudal height at the isthmus. p. 160 (PDF 1). Fig 2 inset: a chord between the superior and inferior margins of the pedicle on the lateral outline of a hemisected vertebra, p. 162 (PDF 3).
- Plane: sagittal. Instrument: lateral films of midline-hemisected vertebrae (one pedicle per film, so no contralateral superimposition), or CT.
- Levels: T1 to L5. Table 2 and Fig 2, p. 162 (PDF 3). N 21 to 52 per level; 433 films + 162 CT = 595.
- Values, mean (SD) mm, Table 2, p. 162 (PDF 3): L5 14.0 (2.3), L4 14.8 (2.1), L3 14.9 (2.4), L2 15.0 (1.5), L1 15.4 (2.8), T12 15.8 (2.4), T11 17.4 (2.5), T10 15.2 (2.0), T9 13.8 (1.3), T8 12.8 (1.2), T7 12.1 (1.0), T6 12.2 (1.0), T5 11.9 (1.4), T4 12.1 (1.0), T3 12.4 (1.3), T2 12.0 (1.2), T1 9.9 (2.0). Text: widest T11, 17.4 (12.5 to 24.1); narrowest T1, 9.9 (7.0 to 14.5); in the lumbar spine narrowest L5, 14.0 (9.5 to 19.0), widest L1, 15.4 (11.0 to 21.0). p. 161 (PDF 2).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: in the vertebra frame, split the instance at the midsagittal plane (as the authors hemisected the bone), project one half along x to a lateral silhouette, and take the narrowest craniocaudal chord across the pedicle between the superior and inferior vertebral notches, perpendicular to the pedicle's sagittal axis (Inference). Or, slice-wise, in the parasagittal plane through the pedicle axis.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, in principle yes, as the neck between the superior and inferior notches; the authors removed the other pedicle by hemisection and had no magnification. Inference: on a clinical lateral film both pedicles, and in the thoracic spine the rib heads, superimpose, and magnification must be corrected from a known reference.
- Across levels: nearly flat from L5 to T12 (14.0 to 15.8, rising slightly cranially), peaks at T11 (17.4), falls through T10 (15.2), T9 (13.8) and T8 (12.8) to a plateau of 11.9 to 12.4 from T7 to T2, and drops sharply at T1 (9.9). Fig 2, p. 162 (PDF 3). Inference, my effect sizes: T12 vs T11 -0.65, T11 vs T10 0.97, T10 vs T9 0.83, T9 vs T8 0.80, T8 vs T7 0.63, T2 vs T1 1.27; every lumbar pair below 0.4. So SW locates T11 (peak), the T10-T8 descent and T1; it does not separate the lumbar levels from one another.
- Ratio TW/SW (Inference, from the two tables' means): L5 1.29, L4 0.87, L3 0.69, L2 0.59, L1 0.56, T12 and T11 0.45, T10 to T4 0.38 to 0.46, T3 0.45, T2 0.58, T1 0.80. Only L5 has a pedicle wider than tall. Caution: Tables 1 and 2 have different N at every level, so this is a ratio of means over partly different vertebrae, not the mean of per-vertebra ratios. The abstract says the sagittal width was "generally larger" because the pedicle is oval, p. 160 (PDF 1); L5 is the exception the tables show.
- Sex, age, size, population: none reported. Inference (SD/mean from Table 2): coefficient of variation 0.08 to 0.20, smallest (0.08 to 0.12) from T9 to T2 and largest at T1 (0.20) and L1 (0.18); SW varies proportionally less than TW.
- Error: SEM 0.2 to 0.6 mm (Table 2); nothing else.

### 3. Transverse pedicle angle (TA)

- Definition: the angle between a line perpendicular to the transverse isthmus (that is, the pedicle axis in the axial plane) and a line parallel to the vertebral midline, in the transverse plane. p. 160 (PDF 1). Positive = medial (the axis converges toward the midline as it runs anteriorly; Fig 3 inset, p. 163, PDF 4); negative = lateral (T12). The Fig 3 inset (an axial outline, posterior up) draws the pedicle line from the posterior tip of the left articular process outline anteromedially to the anterior cortex, where it meets a line drawn parallel to the midline; the angle is marked at that anterior vertex (reading of the drawing). The abstract calls the usual orientation "posterolateral to anterolateral", which reads as a misprint for posterolateral to anteromedial (see Gaps).
- Plane: transverse (axial). Instrument: axial films of isolated vertebrae or axial CT.
- Levels: T1 to L5. Table 3 and Fig 3, p. 163 (PDF 4). N 20 to 76 per level; 410 films + 218 CT = 628.
- Values, mean (SD) deg, Table 3, p. 163 (PDF 4): L5 29.8 (6.3), L4 17.7 (5.2), L3 14.4 (3.8), L2 12.0 (3.5), L1 10.9 (2.2), T12 -4.2 (9.5), T11 1.2 (4.4), T10 4.6 (4.6), T9 7.6 (2.8), T8 8.1 (5.2), T7 8.7 (4.7), T6 9.6 (4.5), T5 9.4 (4.4), T4 12.6 (4.1), T3 14.6 (4.4), T2 19.1 (7.1), T1 26.6 (5.6). Text: largest L5, 29.8 (19.0 to 44.0); shallowest T12, -4.2 (-17.0 to +14.5); in the lumbar spine shallowest L1, 10.9 (6.5 to 14.5); largest thoracic T1, 26.6 (16.0 to 33.5). pp. 161-162 (PDF 2-3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: per side, in the vertebra's axial plane, fit the pedicle axis (perpendicular to the narrowest transverse chord, or the line through the chord midpoints) and take its angle to the y axis of the midsagittal plane; sign positive when the anterior end lies medial (Inference). The midsagittal plane must come from the vertebra itself (canal centre, spinous process, body symmetry), not from the scanner axes, or rotation (scoliosis, tilt) adds to one side and subtracts from the other.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible from a lateral film. Inference: not measurable on an AP film either; it needs an axial image (CT, or an axial projection of a dry vertebra as here).
- Across levels: a U shape with a reversal. Large and medial at L5, falling to L1 (10.9), reversing to slightly lateral at T12 (-4.2), near neutral at T11 (1.2), then rising steadily through the thoracic spine to 26.6 at T1. Fig 3, p. 163 (PDF 4); Discussion p. 164 (PDF 5). Inference, my effect sizes: L5 vs L4 2.1, L4 vs L3 0.7, L3 vs L2 0.7, L1 vs T12 2.2, T12 vs T11 -0.7, T11 vs T10 -0.8, T10 vs T9 -0.8, T9 to T5 all below 0.2, T5 vs T4 -0.75, T3 vs T2 -0.76, T2 vs T1 -1.2. TA is the best single separator of L5 from L4 and of L1 from T12 in this paper, and it separates the upper thoracic levels; it is useless between T9 and T5.
- Sex, age, size, population: none reported.
- Error: SEM 0.4 to 1.9 deg (Table 3); SD as large as 9.5 deg at T12 and 7.1 at T2.

### 4. Sagittal pedicle angle (SA)

- Definition: measured between a line perpendicular to the sagittal isthmus (the pedicle axis in the sagittal plane) and the posterior vertebral body border, in the sagittal plane. p. 160 (PDF 1). The values are near 0 in the lumbar spine, where the text says the pedicle was "essentially perpendicular to the posterior vertebral margin", p. 162 (PDF 3); so the number reported is the deviation of the pedicle axis from the perpendicular to the posterior body wall, not the literal angle to the wall (which would be near 90 deg). The Fig 4 inset supports this reading (Inference from the drawing): the hemisected vertebra is drawn rotated, posterior elements up and body below, as in the Fig 2 inset, and beta lies between a reference line running in the vertebra's anteroposterior direction (that is, perpendicular to the posterior body wall) and the pedicle axis, the two meeting at the anterior cortex, p. 164 (PDF 5). Positive = "cephalad", negative = "caudal". The paper does not say which end of the axis is cephalad. Inference (from memory of other thoracic studies, unverified here): the anatomic pedicle axis runs from a cephalad posterior end to a caudal anterior end (the "anatomic" screw path aims caudally), so "cephalad" here most likely means the posterior end of the axis lies cephalad; check the sign before using it. Fig 4 inset labels the angle beta on a lateral outline, p. 164 (PDF 5).
- Plane: sagittal. Instrument: lateral films of hemisected vertebrae, or CT.
- Levels: T1 to L5. Table 4 and Fig 4, p. 164 (PDF 5). N 20 to 52 per level; 372 films + 150 CT = 522.
- Values, mean (SD) deg, Table 4, p. 164 (PDF 5): L5 -1.8 (3.5), L4 0.2 (3.9), L3 0.2 (4.7), L2 1.8 (5.5), L1 2.4 (6.3), T12 11.6 (2.6), T11 15.4 (4.9), T10 16.8 (5.3), T9 16.0 (3.8), T8 16.6 (4.1), T7 15.7 (4.2), T6 15.0 (4.2), T5 15.0 (3.4), T4 16.3 (4.4), T3 17.3 (6.0), T2 17.5 (7.7), T1 12.6 (5.8). Text: largest cephalad T2, 17.5 (7.0 to 32.0); L5 caudal -1.8 (-8.0 to +8.0); thoracic pedicles all cephalad. p. 162 (PDF 3). The printed ranges of T12 (-7.0 to 11.0) and T9 (9.0 to 14.0) do not contain their means (see Gaps); Fig 4 plots T12 near 12 with an SD bar of about 9 to 14 and T9 near 16 with a bar of about 12 to 20, so the means and SDs stand and the ranges are misprints.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: in the parasagittal plane through the pedicle, fit a line to the posterior cortex of the body, take its perpendicular, and measure the angle from it to the pedicle's sagittal axis (perpendicular to the narrowest craniocaudal chord) (Inference). The hemisection projection of measure 2 gives the same outline the authors measured.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, in principle (the authors measured lateral films of hemisected bone). Inference: on a clinical lateral the posterior body line is clear but the pedicle outline is blurred by superimposition, and any tilt of the patient changes nothing in this angle since both lines belong to the same vertebra.
- Across levels: a step. Lumbar near 0 (L5 slightly caudal, -1.8; L4 and L3 0.2; L2 1.8; L1 2.4), a jump at T12 (11.6), then a thoracic plateau of 15 to 17.5 from T11 to T2, and a fall at T1 (12.6). Fig 4, p. 164 (PDF 5); Discussion p. 164. Inference, my effect sizes: L1 vs T12 -1.9, T12 vs T11 -1.0, T2 vs T1 0.7, all others below 0.6. SA separates lumbar from thoracic at the T12/L1 junction and little else.
- Sex, age, size, population: none reported.
- Error: SEM 0.5 to 1.5 deg (Table 4); lumbar SDs 3.5 to 6.3 deg are as large as the lumbar means.

### 5. Depth to the anterior cortex along the pedicle axis (Dpa)

- Definition: the distance from the most posterior aspect of the transverse process to the anterior cortex, taken in the line of the transverse pedicle angle. p. 160 (PDF 1). The Fig 5 inset draws it as a line through the pedicle converging medially to the anterior cortex, p. 165 (PDF 6). The y axis of Fig 5 is labelled "cort-cort distance".
- Plane: transverse (axial). Instrument: axial films or CT.
- Levels: T1 to L5. Table 5 and Fig 5, p. 165 (PDF 6). N 22 to 74 per level; 403 films + 210 CT = 613 (the same N for both depth measures).
- Values, mean (SD) mm, Table 5, p. 165 (PDF 6): L5 51.0 (4.7), L4 49.7 (4.4), L3 51.9 (4.6), L2 51.9 (3.7), L1 50.7 (4.3), T12 38.6 (11.0), T11 41.8 (6.5), T10 44.0 (5.0), T9 45.2 (4.9), T8 45.4 (4.8), T7 44.6 (3.7), T6 42.1 (4.7), T5 41.9 (4.7), T4 38.5 (3.9), T3 37.7 (5.1), T2 35.7 (4.7), T1 36.9 (5.8). Text: longest at L2 and L3, 51.9 (45.0 to 58.0 and 42.0 to 62.0); shortest given as T1, 36.9 (26.0 to 52.0), although T2 is shorter in the table and figure. pp. 162-163 (PDF 3-4).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: on each side, find the most posterior voxel of the transverse process (SPINEPS's transverse/costal process subregion where given), cast a ray in the axial plane at the transverse pedicle angle toward anteromedial, and measure to where the ray leaves the anterior cortex of the body mask (Inference). Inference (reading of the drawing): the Fig 5 inset starts both depth lines at the posterior tip of the articular process outline, behind the root of the transverse process, not at the lateral tip of the transverse process; the midline-parallel line then runs down the lateral side of the body and the pedicle-axis line converges medially through the pedicle, p. 165 (PDF 6). So "most posterior aspect of the transverse process" is best read as the most posterior bone at the pedicle entry (the facet/transverse process junction), but the start point is ambiguous; record which start was used.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: not from a lateral film, which foreshortens a line running at the angle TA by cos(TA) and cannot show the transverse process start reliably; it needs an axial image.
- Across levels: a lumbar plateau of 49.7 to 51.9 from L5 to L1, a sharp drop at T12 to a local minimum (38.6, as short as T4, with the largest SD of the paper, 11.0, range 23 to 61), a thoracic hump of 44.0 to 45.4 at T10 to T7, and a decline to 35.7 to 38.5 at T4 to T1, the overall minimum being T2 (35.7). Fig 5, p. 165 (PDF 6). Inference, my effect sizes: L1 vs T12 1.45, T5 vs T4 0.8, T7 vs T6 0.6, all others below 0.5.
- Sex, age, size, population: none reported. Inference: a depth is a size measure and should scale with the patient size factor.
- Error: SEM 0.5 to 2.2 mm (Table 5).

### 6. Depth to the anterior cortex parallel to the midline (Dma)

- Definition: the same start (most posterior aspect of the transverse process) to the anterior cortex, along a line parallel to the midline of the vertebral body. p. 160 (PDF 1); Fig 5 inset, p. 165 (PDF 6).
- Plane: transverse (axial).
- Values, mean (SD) mm, Table 5, p. 165 (PDF 6): L5 33.7 (5.6), L4 40.7 (4.3), L3 44.4 (5.1), L2 45.5 (3.7), L1 44.7 (4.5), T12 43.2 (5.0), T11 41.2 (3.8), T10 40.1 (4.6), T9 41.2 (5.3), T8 40.6 (4.1), T7 39.5 (3.7), T6 36.9 (5.0), T5 35.9 (4.1), T4 31.5 (3.2), T3 31.1 (3.1), T2 27.4 (6.3), T1 26.7 (5.8). Text: shortest T1, 26.7 (17.0 to 43.0); longest L2, 45.5 (37.0 to 52.0). p. 163 (PDF 4).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the same start voxel, a ray parallel to the midsagittal y axis, to the exit through the anterior or anterolateral cortex (Inference). Inference: because this ray starts lateral and runs straight forward, it leaves the body through its rounded anterolateral contour; Dma is therefore much shorter than Dpa x cos(TA) where the body is narrow relative to the start's offset (L5: 33.7 printed against 51.0 x cos 29.8 deg = 44.3), so it must be computed by ray casting on the mask, not derived from Dpa and TA.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: a lateral radiograph shows a line parallel to the midsagittal plane at true length (after magnification correction), so a lateral film could approximate Dma if the start point can be found; the posterior aspect of the transverse process is hard to see on a lateral film.
- Across levels: smallest at T1 (26.7), rising through the thoracic spine to a maximum at L2 (45.5), then falling sharply at L5 (33.7). Inference, my effect sizes: L5 vs L4 -1.4, L4 vs L3 -0.8, T5 vs T4 1.2, T3 vs T2 0.75, the rest below 0.6.
- Difference Dpa minus Dma (Inference, from Table 5 means): L5 17.3, L4 9.0, L3 7.5, L2 6.4, L1 6.0, T12 -4.6, T11 0.6, T10 3.9, T9 4.0, T8 4.8, T7 5.1, T6 5.2, T5 6.0, T4 7.0, T3 6.6, T2 8.3, T1 10.2 mm. It follows the transverse angle: largest where the pedicle converges most (L5, T1), near zero or negative where the pedicle is neutral or lateral (T11, T12). The paper finds Dpa significantly longer at every level except T11 and T12, P < 0.05, test not named. p. 163 (PDF 4); p. 165 (PDF 6). Inference (my arithmetic): an unpaired t from Table 5's means, SDs and N reproduces that pattern, about -1.9 at T12 and 0.4 at T11 against 2.8 or more at every other level.
- Error: SEM 0.5 to 1.3 mm (Table 5).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L5: widest pedicle in the transverse plane, 18.0 mm (9.1 to 29.0), p. 161 (PDF 2); the only level whose transverse width exceeds its sagittal width (18.0 vs 14.0), Tables 1-2; narrowest lumbar sagittal width, 14.0 (9.5 to 19.0), p. 161; largest transverse angle, 29.8 deg (19.0 to 44.0), p. 161; the only level with a caudal sagittal angle, -1.8 (-8.0 to +8.0), p. 162 (PDF 3); the largest gap between the two depths (51.0 vs 33.7 mm), Table 5, p. 165 (PDF 6).
- L4 to L2: transverse width 12.9, 10.3, 8.9; transverse angle 17.7, 14.4, 12.0; sagittal angle about 0 (0.2, 0.2, 1.8), "essentially perpendicular" to the posterior body margin, p. 162 (PDF 3); depth along the pedicle axis 49.7 to 51.9, longest at L2 and L3 (51.9), pp. 162-163.
- L1: narrowest lumbar transverse width, 8.7 mm (4.5 to 13.0), p. 161 (PDF 2); widest lumbar sagittal width, 15.4 (11.0 to 21.0), p. 161; shallowest lumbar transverse angle, 10.9 deg (6.5 to 14.5), p. 162 (PDF 3).
- T12/L1 junction: the sharpest change in the paper. Transverse angle 10.9 to -4.2 deg, sagittal angle 2.4 to 11.6 deg, depth along the pedicle axis 50.7 to 38.6 mm, transverse width 8.7 to 7.1 mm (Tables 1, 3, 4, 5). The Discussion says the transverse angle returns to neutral by L1 and begins to angle laterally at T12, p. 164 (PDF 5) (the L1 mean is in fact 10.9, see Gaps).

- T11: widest pedicle in the sagittal plane, 17.4 mm (12.5 to 24.1), p. 161 (PDF 2); transverse angle near neutral, 1.2 deg; sagittal angle 15.4 deg; transverse width 7.8, a local maximum among T5 to T12.
- T10 to T6: transverse width 6.3 falling to 5.2; sagittal width 15.2 falling to about 12; transverse angle 4.6 rising to 9.6; sagittal angle 15 to 17; depth along the pedicle axis 42 to 45 mm.
- T5: the narrowest pedicle in the transverse plane, 4.5 mm (3.0 to 7.0), p. 161 (PDF 2); T4 is almost the same (4.7, 2.5 to 7.0).
- T4 to T2: transverse width rises (4.7, 5.6, 7.0); transverse angle rises (12.6, 14.6, 19.1); T2 has the largest cephalad sagittal angle, 17.5 (7.0 to 32.0), p. 162 (PDF 3), and the shortest pedicle-axis depth in the table, 35.7 mm.
- T1: the narrowest pedicle in the sagittal plane, 9.9 mm (7.0 to 14.5), p. 161 (PDF 2); transverse width back up to 7.9 (5.0 to 10.0), so the pedicle is nearly round (ratio 0.80, Inference); the largest thoracic transverse angle, 26.6 deg (16.0 to 33.5), p. 162 (PDF 3), with the Discussion calling T1 the maximum medial angulation of the thoracic spine, p. 164 (PDF 5); sagittal angle falls back to 12.6 (T2 17.5); shortest midline depth, 26.7 mm (17.0 to 43.0), and given in the text as the shortest pedicle-axis depth, 36.9 (26.0 to 52.0), pp. 162-163.

## Measurement error and reliability

- No intra- or inter-observer study, no repeat measurement, no instrument resolution. pp. 160-166.
- Films: roentgenographic technique held constant; "no detectable magnification" with this technique, p. 160 (PDF 1). Hemisection removed contralateral superimposition on the lateral films.
- CT against roentgenogram: the only statement is that there was no significant difference between CT and specimen-film measurements, printed with "(P < 0.05)", p. 160 (PDF 1), and repeated in the abstract. No test, no per-measure or per-level comparison, no mean difference and no limits of agreement are given, and the CT and film sets were not the same vertebrae (Inference: the CT were of other spines). CT contributed 27 to 35 percent of each table's measurements (160/547, 162/595, 218/628, 150/522, 210/613), and the CT region was most often L3 to S1, so lumbar levels carry most of the CT data (Inference).
- Per-level SEM: widths 0.2 to 0.6 mm; angles 0.4 to 1.9 deg; depths 0.5 to 2.2 mm (Tables 1-5). The SDs (widths 0.9 to 4.1 mm, angles 2.2 to 9.5 deg, depths 3.1 to 11.0 mm) mix true variation with measurement error and with the two modalities.
- Internal checks (Inference, my arithmetic): SEM equals SD/sqrt(N) to rounding everywhere except Table 4 T6 (printed 0.6, computed 0.66); Table 1 T8 sits on a rounding edge (1.6/sqrt(41) = 0.25, printed 0.3).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication: pedicle fixation (plates and screws, rods and screws, external fixator pins) lets fixation and fusion cover fewer segments than Harrington rods or segmental intralaminar wires, and a laminectomy can be done at the fused levels without lengthening the fusion. p. 160 (PDF 1).
- The pedicle is oval and narrowest in the transverse plane; the transverse width sets the largest screw diameter allowed. A screw larger than the pedicle can break the pedicle or cut out its threads. The sagittal width is much larger and does not limit diameter. p. 164 (PDF 5).
- Methyl methacrylate augmentation can be disastrous if it leaks through a pedicle defect made by the screw (refs 13, 14). p. 164 (PDF 5).
- Follow the angles: medial in the lumbar spine (largest at L5), returning toward neutral by L1 by the Discussion's account (the table puts L1 at 10.9 deg medial and the near-neutral level at T11), lateral at T12, then increasingly medial up to T1; in the sagittal plane, near perpendicular to the posterior body wall in the lumbar spine (slightly caudal at L5) and increasingly cephalad through the thoracic spine. p. 164 (PDF 5).
- Depth: knowing the depth to the anterior cortex prevents anterior perforation and injury to vital structures; the path along the pedicle axis is significantly longer than a path parallel to the midline at all levels except T11 and T12. p. 165 (PDF 6).
- Many authors advised straight-ahead insertion parallel to the midline, but in the laboratory deeper screws survived the most cycles in cephalad-caudad and medial-lateral loading before failure, and screws engaging the anterior cortex pulled out at higher loads than shallower ones (refs 5, 13). p. 165 (PDF 6).
- Fluoroscopy pearl: the "radiographic blind spot" on lateral fluoroscopy during screw insertion shrinks as the screw tip approaches the midline of the body on its way to the anterior cortex; so a screw along the converging pedicle axis is less likely to perforate the anterior cortex unseen than one parallel to the midline, at all levels except T11 and T12. p. 165 (PDF 6). Inference: a laterally placed tip can lie outside the anterolateral cortex while still projecting inside the body outline on a lateral view.
- Screw length chosen from these depths must always be checked intraoperatively under fluoroscopy. p. 166 (PDF 7).
- Summary: knowing these relationships matters to any surgeon using the pedicle for screw purchase, to prevent screw cutout, failure of fixation or neurologic injury. pp. 165-166 (PDF 6-7).
- Pitfall (measuring): a "width" must name its plane; the transverse and sagittal widths differ by up to 2.6 times (T5 4.5 vs 11.9 mm). Tables 1-2.
- Pitfall (anatomy): the mid thoracic pedicles are very narrow (T4 to T8 means 4.5 to 5.9 mm, single values down to 2.5 mm). Table 1, p. 161. Inference: a 5 mm or larger screw will not fit many mid thoracic pedicles; the paper itself states no screw size.
- Pitfall (anatomy): T12 is the most variable level for angle and depth (SD 9.5 deg and 11.0 mm). Tables 3 and 5.
- Prior work: Saillant gave only means (ref 11); Krag et al. measured the lower thoracic and lumbar spine on CT (ref 5); Berry et al. measured selected pedicles directly (ref 1); the present data agree closely with them. p. 164 (PDF 5).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Measure pedicle width (TW) as the narrowest chord across the pedicle in the vertebra's transverse plane (the paper's definition). Inference: take that plane from the vertebra's own end plates, take the chord perpendicular to the pedicle axis, outer cortex to outer cortex, and never along the scanner's x axis. Expect L5 18.0, L4 12.9, L3 10.3, L2 8.9, L1 8.7 mm. p. 160 (PDF 1); Table 1, p. 161 (PDF 2).
2. Measure pedicle height (SW) as the narrowest craniocaudal chord between the superior and inferior notches on a lateral silhouette of one half of the vertebra (split at the midsagittal plane, as the authors hemisected the bone). p. 160 (PDF 1); Table 2, p. 162 (PDF 3).
3. Use TW to tell L5 from L4 and L4 from L3; do not use it to tell L2 from L1 or to order T6 to T10, where adjacent means differ by 0.1 to 0.6 mm against SDs of 1.0 to 2.3 mm. Table 1, p. 161.
4. Flag an instance whose pedicle is wider than tall (TW/SW above 1) as L5-like; the only such level in this paper is L5 (18.0/14.0). Tables 1-2, pp. 161-162. Inference: a sacralized or lumbarized segment was not studied, so do not assume the rule holds for a transitional segment.
5. Locate the thoracolumbar junction by the transverse angle: L1 about 11 deg medial, T12 about 4 deg lateral (wide spread), T11 about 1 deg. Measure it against the vertebra's own midsagittal plane. Table 3, p. 163 (PDF 4).
6. Confirm the junction with the sagittal angle: lumbar near 0, T12 about 12, T11 to T2 about 15 to 17.5 deg. Table 4, p. 164 (PDF 5). Check the sign convention first; the paper does not say which end of the axis is "cephalad".
7. Locate T11 by its tall pedicle (SW 17.4 mm, the maximum) with a near-neutral transverse angle (1.2 deg). Tables 2-3.
8. Recognise T1 by the shortest pedicle height (9.9 mm, about 2 mm below T2), a nearly round pedicle (TW 7.9), the largest thoracic medial angle (26.6 deg, T2 19.1) and a lower sagittal angle than T2 (12.6 vs 17.5). Tables 1-4, pp. 161-164.
9. Recognise the T4-T5 region by the narrowest transverse widths (4.5 to 4.7 mm). Table 1, p. 161.
10. Take depths from the most posterior point of the transverse process (drawn in the Fig 5 inset at the junction of the articular and transverse processes, the usual screw entry; Inference from the drawing), once along the pedicle axis and once parallel to the midline; expect the pedicle-axis depth to be the longer at every level except T11 and T12, and shorter at T12 (38.6 vs 43.2 mm). p. 160 (PDF 1); Table 5, p. 165 (PDF 6).
11. Expect Dpa minus Dma to track the transverse angle (about 17 mm at L5, about 10 mm at T1, about 0 at T11, negative at T12); use the pair as a consistency check on a measured angle. Table 5 (Inference from the table).
12. The sagittal measures were taken on lateral films of hemisected vertebrae with no detectable magnification, p. 160 (PDF 1). Inference: on a clinical lateral radiograph or lateral fluoroscopy take only the sagittal measures (pedicle height, sagittal angle, perhaps a midline-parallel depth if the start point can be seen); correct magnification; expect both pedicles (and in the thoracic spine the rib heads) to superimpose, which the authors avoided by hemisection; do not attempt the transverse width, transverse angle or pedicle-axis depth from a lateral image.
13. When judging a screw tip on lateral fluoroscopy, distrust a tip far from the midline: the blind spot is largest there. p. 165 (PDF 6).
14. Give T12 wide priors for transverse angle and depth (SD 9.5 deg, 11.0 mm); a T12 that looks lumbar in its angles may be a transitional segment or a numbering error (Inference). Tables 3, 5.
15. Do not trust the printed ranges of the sagittal angle at T9 and T12; use the means and SDs. Table 4, p. 164 (PDF 5).
16. Do not split these values by sex, age or size; the paper gives none. pp. 160-166.

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of any transitional or numbering variant and does not say how it numbered isolated segments.

(b) Measures:
- The generator's pedicle width and height correspond to TW and SW here, and pedicle transverse and sagittal angles (if held) to TA and SA. T1 to L5 only; nothing for C3 to C7.
- Definition differences that would bias a value taken from a mask (Inference throughout): (1) TW and TA here come from an axial projection of the whole dry vertebra or an axial CT; a projection gives the envelope of all axial slices, so it reads equal to or wider than a single mid-height slice; (2) both widths are the narrowest chord perpendicular to the pedicle axis; a mask method that measures along the vertebral x or z axes overstates width by about 1/cos(angle) (15 percent at L5, about 12 percent at T1); (3) SA is reported as the deviation from the perpendicular to the posterior body wall, positive "cephalad", sign convention unstated; Panjabi's sagittal pedicle angle may be referenced to a different line (from memory, unverified: check the bordercall Panjabi notes before pooling); (4) the depths start at the posterior aspect of the transverse process (drawn at the facet/transverse process junction, Fig 5), so they are screw-path lengths, not a body depth; whether any Panjabi chord length matches them must be checked against the Panjabi notes; (5) outer versus inner cortex is not stated; (6) the five measures come from partly different vertebrae (different N per table and level), so the paper gives no correlation between measures, which a joint generator needs.
- Measures the generator could add from this paper: TW/SW ratio (L5 above 1, the rest below 0.9); Dpa and Dma with their difference.
- Strongest separators here (Inference, effect sizes from Tables 1-5): L5 vs L4 by TA (2.1) and TW (1.6); L1 vs T12 by TA (2.2), SA (1.9) and Dpa (1.45); T11 vs T10 by SW (1.0); T2 vs T1 by SW (1.3) and TA (1.2).

(c) Measurement error to use: the paper has no reliability data. Use the SEMs only as the error of the means (widths 0.2 to 0.6 mm, angles 0.4 to 1.9 deg, depths 0.5 to 2.2 mm). The SDs are total spread (biology plus measurement plus modality) and can serve as an upper bound on per-level sigma for these measures. Inference (SD/mean): TW varies 16 to 32 percent of its mean within a level and SW 8 to 20 percent, so a multiplicative (log-normal) spread fits TW better than a fixed mm sigma; angle SDs are 2.2 to 9.5 deg and depth SDs 3.1 to 11.0 mm, with T12 the outlier for TA and Dpa.

(d) Covariates: none. No sex, age, body size, race or country is reported; the specimens were adult and postmortem, the CT patients undescribed. The size factor cannot be calibrated from this paper.

## Gaps and conflicts

- No demographics at all: number of specimens and patients, sex, age, race, stature. Side (left/right) not reported; unclear whether N counts pedicles or vertebrae. p. 160 (PDF 1).
- CT method unstated (scanner, slice thickness, gantry angle, reformatting); unclear how sagittal widths and angles were taken from CT. The methods describe reviewed CT examinations (clinical, most often L3 to S1) while every table footnote says "specimen CT scans". p. 160 vs Tables 1-5.
- No level-numbering method and no transitional anomalies; isolated thoracic or lumbar segments were numbered by an unstated rule.
- The CT-versus-film statement reads "no significant difference ... (P < 0.05)", p. 160 (PDF 1); Inference: P > 0.05 was meant. No test or numbers.
- Table 1: the per-level N sum to 551, but the footnote gives 387 + 160 = 547 total studies, p. 161 (PDF 2). Across the five tables the per-level N sum to 2,909 against the 2,905 of the abstract. The other four tables' per-level N sum to their footnote totals (595, 628, 522, 613).
- The split between film and CT is given only per table, never per level, so no level's values can be separated by modality.
- The abstract states the CT-film agreement with no P value; the Results add "(P < 0.05)". p. 160 (PDF 1).
- Table 4: T12 mean 11.6 lies outside its printed range -7.0 to 11.0, and T9 mean 16.0 outside its range 9.0 to 14.0, p. 164 (PDF 5); Fig 4 supports the means and SDs, so the ranges are misprints. T6 SEM 0.6 does not match SD 4.2 / sqrt(40) = 0.66.
- Fig 4 agrees with Table 4: read against the axis alone the thoracic points seem about 0.5 to 1 deg above the table, but the scanned plot is slightly tilted (its drawn zero line rises about 0.7 deg between L5 and T5); measured in pixels from the zero line under each point, every one of the 17 plotted means lies within 0.3 deg of Table 4 (for example T12 11.8, T3 17.5, T2 17.6, T1 12.9 against 11.6, 17.3, 17.5, 12.6). p. 164 (PDF 5). (Corrects an earlier reading of this note.)
- Text against table: the shortest depth along the pedicle axis is given as T1, 36.9 mm (p. 162, PDF 3), but Table 5 and Fig 5 show T2 shorter, 35.7 mm (p. 165, PDF 6).
- Text against table: the Discussion says the transverse angle had "returned to neutral by L1" (p. 164, PDF 5), but L1's mean is 10.9 deg medial; neutral is T11 (1.2).
- Abstract: the usual transverse orientation is printed "posterolateral to anterolateral", which contradicts the medial angles of the tables; Inference: anteromedial was meant. p. 160 (PDF 1).
- Abstract says pedicles angle cephalad "from L3-T1", but L3 and L4 means are 0.2 deg and the Results call the lumbar pedicles other than L5 essentially perpendicular. p. 160 vs p. 162.
- Methods' sagittal angle wording (axis against posterior body border) would give about 90 deg; the reported values are deviations from the perpendicular. p. 160 vs Table 4.
- Fig 5's y axis is labelled cortex to cortex distance while the definition starts at the transverse process. p. 165 vs p. 160.
- Agreement with other papers (Inference, from memory, to verify against their notes): the pattern of widest pedicles at L5, narrowest transverse width in the T4 to T6 region, and pedicle height peaking in the lower thoracic spine agrees with Panjabi's thoracic and lumbar studies and with Krag's and Scoles's measurements; the T12 lateral angle and its large spread are less often reported elsewhere and may reflect this sample's mixing of transitional or misnumbered vertebrae.
