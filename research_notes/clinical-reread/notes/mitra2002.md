## What the paper is

- Design: a cadaver morphometric study of the lumbar pedicles L1 to L5, measured three ways (direct on bone, plain roentgenogram, CT), compared with published white, Chinese and Korean series, written to choose pedicle screw dimensions and entry points for Indian patients. p. 453 (PDF 1), abstract.
- Material: 20 cadavers, 18 male and 2 female, from the Department of Anatomy, Government Medical College and Hospital, Nagpur, India. Male ages 20 to 70 years; the two females were 55 and 60. Cadavers with an obvious spinal deformity or a fractured spine were excluded. p. 453 (PDF 1). No stature, weight, ethnicity within India, or cause of death is given.
- Preparation: a midline posterior approach from T12 to the sacrum; the facet joints and the vertical bony crest just below them were cleared of soft tissue; the segment L1 to sacrum was removed en bloc. p. 453 (PDF 1).
- Sequence of measurement (pp. 453-454, PDF 1-2):
  1. Direct, on the intact column L1 to L5: interpedicular distance, sagittal diameter, sagittal angle (Figure 2).
  2. Lateral roentgenograms of the column: sagittal diameter, sagittal angle, interpedicular distance. The Methods state that the angles were read with the goniometer "on lateral roentgenograms" (p. 453, PDF 1), and p. 458 (PDF 6) blames the radiographic excess of the interpedicular distance on overlap of the left and right pedicles, which fits a lateral view. No AP film was used. Cassette 100 cm from the tube; a metal measuring guide gave a magnifying factor the authors report as "0". Sagittal angle by Olsewski's method: one line along the pedicle axis, one along the superior border of the body.
  3. CT ("computerized roentgenogram"): 5-mm-thick axial slices at the pedicle isthmus (the "midpedicle cut"), the plane chosen on the sagittal (scout) view; distances read from hard-copy films with computer software, angles with a goniometer on the films. CT measures: transverse diameter, transverse angle, chord length, medial and lateral cortical thickness.
  4. Direct after sectioning: each vertebra cut at the midpedicle level with an oscillating fret hacksaw; transverse diameter, transverse angle (Figure 1), chord length, and the pedicle entrance point.
- Instruments: Vernier caliper, resolution 0.1 mm, for "all the diameters and distances"; goniometer, resolution 1 deg, used on the lateral radiographs and directly on the column. p. 453 (PDF 1). The CT distances were nevertheless read from hard-copy films with computer software and the CT angles with a goniometer on the films, p. 454 (PDF 2), so the caliper statement does not cover CT.
- Limitation the authors admit: the abstract calls it a "limited study in a subset of the Indian population", p. 453 (PDF 1).
- Statistics: two-tailed Student t test, P < 0.05 significant. The two female cadavers were not compared with earlier studies. p. 454 (PDF 2).
- Tables give means only, by sex, with n = cadavers (M 18, F 2): no SD, range or per-side value anywhere. Whether left and right were averaged, or one side measured, is not stated. Table 1 (TD, TA, CL) and Table 2 (SD, SA, IPD, cortical thickness), p. 455 (PDF 3).
- Levels: L1 to L5 only. How L1 and L5 were identified (rib count, sacrum) is not described; the specimen was taken from T12 to the sacrum, which suggests counting from the sacrum or from T12 with its rib, but the paper does not say (Inference).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13 or rib-count anomalies, and nothing about how levels were counted. Cadavers with "obvious deformity" or fracture were excluded (p. 453, PDF 1); whether a transitional lumbosacral segment would have counted as a deformity is not said (Inference: unknowable from the paper).

Morphological variants it does report:
- Very narrow pedicles: in two specimens the direct transverse diameter at the upper lumbar levels was under 5 (printed "cm", meaning mm), p. 454 (PDF 2); "as low as 3 mm in a few" specimens, also seen in the Korean series, p. 457 (PDF 5). Cause unknown; the authors suggest short stature or a developmental factor. Denominator not given (18 or 20 cadavers, 5 levels each).
- Caudad-directed L5 pedicles: direct sagittal angle at L5 ranged -8 to +15 deg in the males (negative = caudad), p. 454 (PDF 2); the radiographic range at L5 was -4.5 to 11 deg, p. 456 (PDF 4); the Discussion gives -9 to 11 deg, p. 458 (PDF 6). The count of caudad pedicles is not given; the authors say it was significantly smaller than Zindrick's, p. 456 (PDF 4). Table 2 quotes Olsewski's L5 radiographic sagittal angle as "7(30), -10(12)", i.e. 30 cephalad pedicles averaging 7 deg and 12 caudad averaging -10 deg, 42 pedicles of Olsewski's 21 disarticulated spines, though Mitra labels the row n = 49 (reading confirmed against the olsewski1990 note in this set; Inference), p. 455 (PDF 3).
- Pedicle entrance point low on the transverse process: in specimen 5 at L3 and L4, and in specimen 9 at L1, L2, L3 and L5, the pedicle centre lay at the bottom of the transverse process instead of its midpoint, p. 456 (PDF 4). Inference: 6 of 100 level observations (20 cadavers x 5 levels; sides not stated).

## Measures, each as a recipe

Naming: "transverse diameter" (TD) is pedicle width (mediolateral), "sagittal diameter" (SD) is pedicle height (craniocaudal), "transverse angle" (TA) is the axial-plane convergence, "sagittal angle" (SA) the sagittal-plane tilt, "chord length" (CL) the along-axis depth, "interpedicular distance" (IPD). Units are mm and deg; the text misprints several values as cm (see Gaps).

### TD: transverse diameter (pedicle width)

- Definition: the mediolateral width of the pedicle at its narrowest part (the isthmus) in the axial plane; Figure 1 (p. 454, PDF 2), an axial drawing, puts two opposed horizontal arrows across the narrowest part of the left pedicle, one from the lateral surface and one from inside the canal, so outer surface to outer surface (Inference from the drawing; the text gives no words). The CT cut and the saw cut were both at the isthmus ("midpedicle cut"), p. 454 (PDF 2). Whether the chord is perpendicular to the pedicle axis or to the midsagittal plane is not stated; the arrows are drawn horizontally (mediolateral), not perpendicular to the inclined axis.
- Projection and instrument: direct, caliper on the vertebra sectioned at midpedicle; CT, 5-mm axial slice at the isthmus, software on hard copy. Not measured on radiographs.
- Values, Table 1, p. 455 (PDF 3), mean mm, L1 / L2 / L3 / L4 / L5:
  - Direct M(18): 7.05 / 7.85 / 9.01 / 11.6 / 16.19; F(2): 5.95 / 6.5 / 7.55 / 9.25 / 12.75.
  - CT M(18): 7.5 / 7.59 / 8.65 / 9.81 / 14.64; F(2): 5.85 / 6.15 / 7.25 / 8.8 / 13.1.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): SPINEPS gives no pedicle label. Build a vertebra frame (end-plate normal, midsagittal plane from the canal, spinous process and body symmetry). Take the pedicle as the bony bridge of the posterior-element part between the posterior body wall and the branching of the transverse and articular processes. Step along the pedicle axis and, in each section perpendicular to it, take the mediolateral chord to the mask's outer boundary; TD is the smallest. Record also the chord along the frame's left-right axis: at L5, where the axis turns about 29 deg medially, that chord reads about 1/cos(29 deg) = 1.14 times wider, which is the size of the ambiguity in this paper's definition.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference; not done in this paper): AP radiograph, the width of the pedicle oval divided by the magnification; at L4 and L5 the beam crosses an obliquely running pedicle and the oval overstates the isthmus. A lateral radiograph cannot give it.
- Across levels: rises monotonically L1 to L5 in both sexes, slowly at L1-L3 and steeply at L4-L5. Male direct steps (my subtraction): L1-L2 +0.80, L2-L3 +1.16, L3-L4 +2.59, L4-L5 +4.59 mm. Inference: separates L4 from L5 and L3 from L4 well; L1 from L2 poorly (no SDs are given, so separations cannot be stated as effect sizes). The CT series is flat at the start: L1 7.5 and L2 7.59, only 0.09 mm apart.
- Sex, age, size, population: the two females narrower at every level, direct and CT (Table 1; "significantly lower in the females at all levels" for CT, p. 456, PDF 4). Population: Indian male L5 16.19 against Olsewski's white males 21.1, Kim's Koreans 18.9 and Hou's Chinese 12.8 (p. 457, PDF 5); Table 1 marks Olsewski's male values at L2 to L5 and Hou's male L5 as significantly different. Kim et al M(42): 7, 7.5, 9.9, 12.7, 18.9; F(31): 6.6, 6.9, 8.9, 11.9, 17.6. Hou et al M(25): 7.2, 7.6, 9.4, 10.8, 12.8*; F(15): 6.6, 7.1, 9, 10.2, 13. Olsewski et al M(24): 9.5, 9.6*, 11.7*, 14.7*, 21.1*; F(25): 7.7, 7.9, 9.6, 12.5, 18.4. CT, Zindrick et al (85): 8.7, 8.9, 10.3, 12.9*, 18* (Table 1, p. 455, PDF 3). "All Asian studies" lower than Olsewski's white series, p. 457 (PDF 5); Figure 5 (p. 458, PDF 6) plots the male direct means of the current study, Kim, Hou and Olsewski, with the current curve below Olsewski at every level, below Kim at L3-L5 and above Hou at L4-L5 (the plotted values are those of Table 1). On CT, the current TD is smaller than the white series (Zindrick, Olsewski), with the same trend, significantly at L4 and L5, p. 457 (PDF 5). The authors say TD "showed great variation", p. 458 (PDF 6), but print no SD or range. Short stature is offered as a partial reason for small widths, with a developmental factor; no stature data, p. 457 (PDF 5). No age analysis.
- Error: caliper 0.1 mm; no observer error. CT minus direct, males (my subtraction): +0.45, -0.26, -0.36, -1.79, -1.55 mm L1 to L5; the authors call CT lower at all levels, significant only at L4 and L5, p. 456 (PDF 4), though CT is higher at L1 in males. Inference: 5-mm slices and film-based reading likely blur the isthmus and the cortex.

### SD: sagittal diameter (pedicle height)

- Definition: the craniocaudal height of the pedicle; Figure 2 (p. 454, PDF 2), a lateral outline, draws two opposed vertical arrows across the pedicle between the superior and inferior vertebral notches. Inference: the narrowest craniocaudal chord, as in Olsewski, whose method the paper follows.
- Projection and instrument: direct, caliper on the intact column; lateral roentgenogram of the column (p. 453, PDF 1), read presumably with the caliper, since "all the diameters and distances" were (Inference; the paper does not say how the films were read). Not on CT.
- Values, Table 2, p. 455 (PDF 3), mean mm, L1 / L2 / L3 / L4 / L5:
  - Direct M(18): 15.68 / 15.27 / 15.03 / 14.79 / 15.67; F(2): 15.7 / 15.7 / 15.4 / 14.7 / 17.
  - Roentgenogram M(18): 16.3 / 15.5 / 15.1 / 15.1 / 14.8; F(2): 17.5 / 17 / 16.5 / 17 / 18.5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): in the same pedicle sections as TD, the craniocaudal chord to the outer boundary, its minimum along the pedicle; or the narrowest chord between the superior and inferior notches in the parasagittal plane through the pedicle axis.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, the narrowest vertical dimension of the pedicle between the notches, both sides superimposed (as done here). Divide by the magnification (here reported as none). Inference: an AP view gives the height of the pedicle oval too.
- Across levels: nearly flat, a shallow U in males (L1 15.68, lowest L4 14.79, L5 15.67), p. 454 (PDF 2). On the radiograph it falls steadily L1 to L5 in males (16.3 to 14.8); in the females it rises again at L4 and L5, p. 456 (PDF 4). Inference: SD separates no adjacent lumbar pair; its value is as a denominator for the shape ratio below.
- Sex, age, size, population: the two females larger at most levels, not significant (P > 0.05), p. 454 (PDF 2). Olsewski's white series larger at all levels; current values resemble the Chinese series; Olsewski's significant sex difference was not seen here or in the Chinese study, p. 456 (PDF 4). Roentgenographic SD smaller than Olsewski's but slightly larger than Zindrick's, not significant; Olsewski's radiographic values exceed the Indian ones except at L5 (13.8 against 14.8), p. 457 (PDF 5). Comparators (Table 2): Olsewski direct M(24) 17, 16, 16, 16.4, 17.4, F(25) 15.3, 15.3, 15, 14.9, 16.2; Hou M(25) 15.9, 15.4, 15.3, 15.3, 20.5, F(15) 15.5, 14.7, 14.3, 14.5, 18.7; "B&S" (30) 15.6, 15.4, 14.6, 13, 13.8; roentgenogram Olsewski (49) 18.2, 17.2, 16.9, 15.6, 13.8; Zindrick (85) 15.4, 15, 14.9, 14.8, 14.
- Clinical weight: SD exceeds TD at almost every level, so SD does not limit screw diameter, p. 456 (PDF 4); p. 458 (PDF 6).
- Error: caliper 0.1 mm. Roentgenogram minus direct, males (my subtraction): +0.62, +0.23, +0.07, +0.31, -0.87 mm; higher on film except at L5, not significant, p. 456 (PDF 4); the same pattern as Olsewski's, p. 458 (PDF 6).

### TD/SD: pedicle shape (derived, not in the paper)

- Inference, from the male direct means of Tables 1 and 2: 0.45, 0.51, 0.60, 0.78, 1.03 L1 to L5. The pedicle is tall and narrow at L1-L2 and as wide as tall at L5. The paper's own words: TD "almost always less than" SD, p. 458 (PDF 6). Size-free, monotonic, and computable from any mask; on an AP radiograph or fluoroscopy the pedicle oval gives both dimensions (distorted at L4-L5).

### TA: transverse angle (axial convergence)

- Definition: in the axial plane, the angle between the pedicle axis and the midsagittal line; Figure 1 (p. 454, PDF 2) draws a midline through the body, canal and spinous process and an oblique line through the right pedicle that meets it just in front of the body, with the angle arrow marked between the two lines behind the spinous process. All values positive, meaning convergent (anteromedial) axes; the pedicle axis is "oriented anteromedially", p. 458 (PDF 6).
- Projection and instrument: direct, goniometer on the sectioned vertebra; CT, goniometer on the hard-copy axial film.
- Values, Table 1, p. 455 (PDF 3), mean deg, L1 / L2 / L3 / L4 / L5:
  - Direct M(18): 9 / 10.05 / 12.33 / 14.72 / 29.33; F(2): 5.5 / 10 / 10.5 / 8.5 / 21.
  - CT M(18): 11 / 9.5 / 10.8 / 12.3* / 24.3*; F(2): 3.5 / 8 / 9.5 / 11.5 / 24.5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): fit each pedicle's axis (the line through the centroids of successive pedicle cross-sections, or the normal to the narrowest section), project it on the vertebra's axial plane, and take its angle to the vertebra's own midsagittal plane (from canal centre, spinous process and body symmetry, not the scanner's y axis, or axial rotation adds to one side and subtracts from the other). Average left and right.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible from a lateral or an AP in a living patient (Inference); the paper used sectioned bone and axial CT.
- Across levels: rises L1 to L5, gently L1-L4 (about 1 to 2.4 deg per level in males) and then a jump of about 14.6 deg at L4-L5 (male direct, my subtraction). The L5 pedicle is far more oblique, about 30 deg, p. 458 (PDF 6). Inference: TA is the strongest single L4/L5 discriminator in this paper; it barely separates L1 from L2.
- Sex, age, size, population: the females lower at all levels except L2 (direct), p. 454 (PDF 2); with n = 2 the female series is not monotonic (L4 8.5 below L3 10.5). Population: higher than Olsewski's white series at every level, significantly at L5 (29.3 against 17 deg in white males), p. 456 (PDF 4); "pedicles are more converging in the Indian population"; agrees with Cheung that Asian pedicles have a larger transverse angle at L1 to L4 than whites; Roy-Camille and Saillant found about 30 deg at L5, p. 456 (PDF 4). Comparators (Table 1): Olsewski direct M(24) 7, 7, 8, 11, 17*, F(25) 5, 6, 7, 10, 18; CT-labelled Zindrick (85) 10.9, 12, 14.4, 17.7*, 29.8*; CT-labelled Olsewski (51) 6, 8, 13, 16*, 29*. On CT the current values are said to be smaller than the white series, significantly at L4 and L5, p. 457 (PDF 5); at L1-L2 the table does not bear this out against Olsewski's (51) row (current 11 and 9.5 against 6 and 8). Figure 6 (p. 458, PDF 6) plots the current male direct TA above Olsewski's men and women at every level, the gap widest at L5.
- Error: goniometer 1 deg; no observer error. CT minus direct, males (my subtraction): +2.0, -0.55, -1.53, -2.42, -5.03 deg. The text says CT significantly lower at all levels except L2 in males and L3 in females, p. 456 (PDF 4), which the table does not bear out at male L1 (see Gaps).

### SA: sagittal angle (craniocaudal tilt of the pedicle axis)

- Definition: on a lateral view, the angle between a line along the pedicle axis and a line along the superior border (end plate) of the vertebral body, after Olsewski, p. 453 (PDF 1); Figure 2 (p. 454, PDF 2). Positive = cephalad, negative = caudad, p. 454 (PDF 2). Pedicles are "directed cranially" at all lumbar levels except L5 (abstract, p. 453, PDF 1). Figure 2 (p. 454, PDF 2), a lateral drawing with the body on the left, draws the end-plate line along the superior border of the body and the pedicle-axis line from the anterosuperior corner of the body back and down through the pedicle into the posterior elements, the angle arrow between them behind the pedicle; so cephalad means the axis rises toward the head as it runs anteriorly (reading of the drawing).
- Projection and instrument: direct, goniometer on the intact column; roentgenogram (lateral), goniometer.
- Values, Table 2, p. 455 (PDF 3), mean deg, L1 / L2 / L3 / L4 / L5:
  - Direct M(18): 8.4 / 9.3 / 9.4 / 10.4 / 6.7; F(2): 4 / 6 / 8 / 11 / 7.
  - Roentgenogram M(18): 5 / 5.8 / 5.9 / 5.2 / 0.6; F(2): 1.5 / 0 / 4 / 5.5 / -4.5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): project the fitted pedicle axis on the vertebra's sagittal plane and measure it against a plane fitted to the upper surface of the body subregion (superior end plate); sign positive when the anterior end is cranial. Report L5 cephalad and caudad cases separately, not as one mean.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, the pedicle axis (both pedicles superimposed) against the superior end-plate line, as done here, p. 453 (PDF 1).
- Across levels: male direct rises slightly L1 to L4 (8.4 to 10.4) and falls at L5 (6.7), with L5 widely spread (-8 to +15 deg), p. 454 (PDF 2); radiographic about 5 to 6 deg at L1-L4 and near 0 at L5 (Table 2, p. 455, PDF 3), cephalad at L1-L4 and spread from -4.5 to 11 deg at L5, p. 456 (PDF 4). Inference: SA does not separate L1 to L4; a caudad (negative) pedicle is a weak L5 cue.
- Sex, age, size, population: the females lower at the upper lumbar levels, p. 454 (PDF 2). Close to Olsewski's white values, slightly greater at most levels, significant only at L4, p. 456 (PDF 4); Figure 4 (p. 457, PDF 5) plots current male direct against Olsewski M and F (Olsewski direct M(24) 5, 6, 6, 6, 5 with caudad -8 at L5; F(25) 6, 5, 6, 7, 8, Table 2). Significant difference from Zindrick at L1-L4 on radiographs, due to Zindrick's higher incidence of caudad pedicles, p. 458 (PDF 6); Zindrick (85) 2.4, 1.8*, 0.2*, 0.2*, 1.8 (Table 2). At L5: -10 to 8 deg in Olsewski, -9 to 11 deg here, p. 458 (PDF 6).
- Error: goniometer 1 deg. Roentgenogram minus direct, males (my subtraction): -3.4, -3.5, -3.5, -5.2, -6.1 deg; the authors call the radiographic values significantly lower at all levels, p. 456 (PDF 4).

### CL: chord length (pedicle axis depth)

- Definition: no words; the label in Figure 1 (p. 454, PDF 2) reads "chord length (along pedicle axis)", so a length along the pedicle axis from the posterior surface of the arch to the anterior cortex of the body. But the line the label points to is drawn through the left pedicle almost parallel to the midline (within about 3 deg of the drawn midline, not converging at the 9 to 29 deg of the transverse angle), from the anterior cortex of the body back through the pedicle to the posterior arch below the root of the transverse process (my reading of the drawing). The convergent line in the same figure, through the right pedicle, is the transverse-angle line. So whether CL was taken along the convergent axis or parallel to the midline cannot be settled from the paper (see Gaps). Called "pedicle depth" in the Discussion, p. 457 (PDF 5). The posterior start point is not defined in words.
- Projection and instrument: direct, caliper on the sectioned vertebra; CT, axial slice, software.
- Values, Table 1, p. 455 (PDF 3), mean mm, L1 / L2 / L3 / L4 / L5:
  - Direct M(18): 46.01 / 47.56 / 46.9 / 46.31 / 45.87; F(2): 41.2 / 45.8 / 47.3 / 46.55 / 41.65.
  - CT M(18): 46.8 / 49.7 / 49.35 / 49.83 / 46.87; F(2): 45.8 / 44 / 45.25 / 44.95 / 46.7.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): extend the fitted pedicle axis (axial-plane projection, at the TA) through the whole instance and take the chord from its posterior exit at the arch surface to its anterior exit at the body surface. Compute also the chord through the pedicle centre parallel to the vertebra's own midsagittal plane, since Figure 1 draws that one; keep both and say which is compared with this paper. Record the start point, since this paper's is a drawn point. It crosses pedicle and body, not the canal.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible from a lateral or AP (it needs the axial angle); Inference.
- Across levels: nearly flat, 45.9 to 47.6 mm in males direct, maximum at L2, p. 455 (PDF 3); on CT the male maximum is at L4 (49.83), p. 457 (PDF 5). "Fairly constant between individuals and between vertebral levels", p. 458 (PDF 6). Direct: in all the studies compared CL is smallest at L1 and L5 and largest at the midlumbar levels, except in the Chinese males (Hou), whose CL falls from L1 to L5, p. 457 (PDF 5). CT: in all the studies L5 is said to measure less than the other levels, p. 457 (PDF 5), which the table does not bear out (see Gaps). Inference: CL separates no adjacent lumbar pair here.
- Sex, age, size, population: females lower at all levels except L3 and L4 (direct), p. 455 (PDF 3). Comparators (Table 1): Olsewski direct M(24) 45, 46.8, 49, 50.4, 46.2, F(25) 42.5, 43.5, 44.8, 45.9, 42.3; Hou M(25) 50.7, 50.3, 49.7, 47.9, 43.9, F(15) 47.6, 48.6, 46.3, 46.3, 43.1; CT-labelled Zindrick (85) 44.7, 45.5, 44.4, 40.7, 33.7; CT-labelled Olsewski (51) 45.8, 49.2, 51.7, 55.3, 49.2. Range across studies 41.2 to 50.7 mm, p. 457 (PDF 5). Inference: Hou's and Zindrick's figures quoted here appear to be their depths parallel to the midline, not along the pedicle axis (see Gaps), so the comparison mixes definitions.
- Error: caliper 0.1 mm. CT minus direct, males (my subtraction): +0.79, +2.14, +2.45, +3.52, +1.00 mm; CT greater at all levels except L2-L4 in females, p. 456 (PDF 4), as Olsewski also found, p. 457 (PDF 5).

### IPD: "interpedicular distance" (as used in this paper)

- Definition: not defined in words. Inference, from four statements: it was measured on the intact column and on (lateral) roentgenograms, p. 453 (PDF 1); it is reported for L1 to L4 only, with a dash at L5 (Table 2); its extremes are named "L4-L5" and "L1-L2", p. 456 (PDF 4); the radiographic excess is blamed on overlap of the left and right pedicles, p. 458 (PDF 6); and it is compared with the hole spacing of pedicle plates (Roy-Camille holes every 1.3 cm with screws in alternate holes, 2.6 cm between pedicles; Steffee slots 2.6 cm apart), p. 458 (PDF 6). Together these say this IPD is the craniocaudal distance between the pedicles of adjacent vertebrae (the intersegmental screw spacing along a rod or plate), probably centre to centre, labelled by the upper vertebra (L1 = L1-L2 ... L4 = L4-L5). It is not the classical interpediculate (transverse, AP-film) distance of canal width. Do not mix the two.
- Values, Table 2, p. 455 (PDF 3), mean mm, L1 / L2 / L3 / L4 (L5 not given):
  - Direct M(18): 32.4 / 32.8 / 33.4 / 34.1; F(2): 32.5 / 34.5 / 34 / 35.
  - Roentgenogram M(18): 37.3 / 38.5 / 38 / 37.1; F(2): 37.5 / 38 / 40.5 / 37.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): for consecutive instances i and i+1, the distance between their pedicle centroids (left with left, right with right, then averaged), measured along the local spinal axis or as the straight 3D distance; this equals roughly the body height plus the disc height, so it is a derived segmental measure.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, centre of the superimposed pedicle shadows of one vertebra to the next, divided by the magnification; on an AP view the pedicle ovals of adjacent levels give the same spacing (Inference).
- Across levels: increases slowly L1-L2 to L4-L5 by direct measurement (32.4 to 34.1 in males), p. 454 (PDF 2) and Table 2; on film it peaks at the L2 column in males (38.5) and the L3 column in females (40.5), Table 2, p. 455 (PDF 3). Greatest at L4-L5 and smallest at L1-L2, p. 456 (PDF 4).
- Sex, age, size, population: slightly greater in females, p. 454 (PDF 2). About 10 mm above Roy-Camille's constant 26 mm for all lumbar levels, p. 456 (PDF 4).
- Error: radiographic minus direct, males (my subtraction): +4.9, +5.7, +4.6, +3.0 mm (9 to 17 percent), significantly higher on film at all levels, p. 456 (PDF 4); the authors attribute it to unavoidable overlap of the left and right pedicles, p. 458 (PDF 6). Inference: with a magnification factor reported as zero, this excess is unexplained and may be partly magnification.

### Medial and lateral pedicle cortical thickness

- Definition: the thickness of the medial (canal-side) and lateral walls of the pedicle at the isthmus on the axial CT slice, p. 454 (PDF 2). Units not printed (mm assumed; Inference). Sex not separated.
- Values, Table 2, p. 455 (PDF 3), CT, L1 / L2 / L3 / L4 / L5: lateral 0.82 / 0.55 / 0.87 / 0.6 / 1.25; medial 1.07 / 1.15 / 1.15 / 1.37 / 1.32.

### Pedicle entrance point (Hou's method)

- Definition: the pedicle centre found by visual examination in the lateral plane, then related (a) to the transverse process, as at its top, upper half, midpoint or lower half, and (b) to the superior articular facet, which was divided into vertical segments, with an added segment lateral to it, numbered 1 to 4 (p. 454, PDF 2; Figure 3, p. 457, PDF 5, shows horizontal zones TOP, UPPER, BOTTOM and vertical lines numbered 5, 4, 3, 2, 1). The text's wording of the segment scheme is garbled (see Gaps). Inference from Figure 3, a dorsal-view drawing with the transverse process on the left: the horizontal line runs through the transverse process with the labels TOP, UPPER and BOTTOM beside it; five vertical lines, numbered 1 (medial) to 5 (lateral), bound four segments; lines 1 to 4 span the superior facet, so segments 1 to 3 are its medial, middle and lateral thirds and segment 4 (between lines 4 and 5) lies lateral to the facet, as in Hou's scheme.
- Results: at all levels the entry point lay at the midpoint of the transverse process, except specimen 5 at L3 and L4 and specimen 9 at L1, L2, L3 and L5, where it was at the bottom of the transverse process; L1 to L4 entry point in segment 3, the lateral division of the facet; L5 in segment 4 in all cadavers, p. 456 (PDF 4). The Discussion restates L5 as "well lateral to the facet joint (zone 4)", p. 457 (PDF 5).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): in a posterior (coronal) projection of the instance, take the transverse process base's craniocaudal extent and midline, and the superior articular process's medial and lateral borders; project the pedicle isthmus centroid posteriorly and code its height against the transverse process and its mediolateral position in facet thirds.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): AP radiograph or AP fluoroscopy, the pedicle oval's centre against the transverse process midline and the facet's lateral border; ratios are magnification-free.
- Across levels: lateral migration of the entry point from L1 to L5, most at L5, p. 457 (PDF 5); p. 458 (PDF 6). Agrees with Hou that one universal starting point is not appropriate, p. 457 (PDF 5).
- Comparators described in words, p. 454 (PDF 2) and p. 457 (PDF 5): Roy-Camille, the intersection of a horizontal line through the middle of the transverse process insertion and a vertical line through the facet joint, about 1 mm below the facet joint on a vertical bony crest; Magerl (printed "Magrel", reference 15), where the lateral border of the superior facet meets the mid-transverse process; Weinstein, a lateral approach at the "nape of the neck" of the superior articular facet.
- Error: visual, observer(s) not named; none reported.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Instrument resolution only: caliper 0.1 mm, goniometer 1 deg, p. 453 (PDF 1). No intra- or inter-observer study, no repeat measurements, no SDs or ranges in the tables.
- Radiographic magnification: the cassette was 100 cm from the tube and a metal guide gave a magnifying factor reported as "0", p. 453 (PDF 1). Inference: they mean no measurable magnification (a factor of 1.0), which is implausible for a specimen several cm off the film; radiographic SD ran 0.1 to 0.6 mm above direct at L1-L4, and IPD 3 to 5.7 mm above.
- Modality offsets, male means (my subtraction, Inference): CT TD -1.8 to +0.5 mm (CT smaller at L2-L5); CT TA -5.0 to +2.0 deg (CT smaller at L2-L5); CT CL +0.8 to +3.5 mm (CT larger); radiograph SD -0.9 to +0.6 mm; radiograph SA 3.4 to 6.1 deg lower; radiograph IPD 3.0 to 5.7 mm higher.
- CT technique: 5-mm slices at the isthmus, plane set from the scout; gantry tilt, window, pixel size and reader not given, p. 454 (PDF 2).
- Female series n = 2: not analysed against other studies, p. 454 (PDF 2); its means are not population estimates. Yet the Results report sex differences as significant on these two women (CT TD "significantly lower in the females at all levels", p. 456, PDF 4); Inference: a t test with two subjects has almost no power and such claims should be ignored.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication context: posterior instrumentation with rods, hooks and wires fails without a lamina (trauma, laminectomy); Harrington and Luque systems span many levels and lack a rigid link to the body, reducing mobility; Harrington needs an intact anterior longitudinal ligament; pedicle fixation gives three-dimensional fixation, and, even where bone is osteoporotic, no part of the vertebra is stronger than the pedicle, p. 453 (PDF 1).
- Screw diameter: TD, not SD, limits the screw; a 5-mm screw is safer at L1-L2 and a 6-mm screw at L3-L5; a 7-mm screw should not be used in this population, especially in the upper lumbar spine, p. 457 (PDF 5); p. 458 (PDF 6); abstract p. 453 (PDF 1).
- Screw length: a 40-mm screw appeared safe at all lumbar levels; shorter screws at L1 and L5, p. 457 (PDF 5); p. 458 (PDF 6).
- Trajectory: the pedicle axis runs anteromedially, about 30 deg at L5; keep a larger mediolateral inclination at the lower lumbar levels; the oblique placement gives an interlocking ("toe-nailing") effect between left and right screws, p. 458 (PDF 6).
- Entry point: migrates laterally caudally; at L5 lateral to the facet; no single universal starting point, p. 457 (PDF 5).
- Probing: the medial cortex is consistently thicker than the lateral, so the medial wall can be sounded safely while the pedicle is probed, p. 458 (PDF 6); abstract p. 453 (PDF 1).
- L5 sagittal angle: wide cephalad-to-caudad variation calls for special care at insertion, p. 456 (PDF 4).
- Implants: plates with a fixed 2.6-cm pedicle spacing (Roy-Camille, Steffee) poorly fit the Indian IPD; the hole spacing should be larger, p. 456 (PDF 4); p. 458 (PDF 6).
- Preoperative CT gives the pedicle morphometry, but its values are smaller than the true (direct) ones, p. 458 (PDF 6); the authors also say the CT shortfall is slight and not significant, p. 458 (PDF 6), while their Results call it significant at L4-L5, p. 456 (PDF 4). Pitfall: CT underestimates width and convergence at L4-L5 and overestimates chord length.
- Pitfall: lateral radiographs underestimate the sagittal angle by 3 to 6 deg and overestimate the intersegmental pedicle distance by 3 to 6 mm against direct measurement (Table 2; my subtraction).
- Population pitfall: Western dimensions (Olsewski, Zindrick) overstate Indian pedicle widths, especially at L4-L5; diameter and length of screws need to be substantially smaller than in the Western literature, p. 458 (PDF 6). Some Indian (and Korean) pedicles are as narrow as 3 mm, p. 457 (PDF 5).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Measure pedicle width (TD) on the axial section through the isthmus as the narrowest outer-cortex chord; take it both perpendicular to the pedicle axis and along the vertebra's left-right axis (Figure 1 draws it left-right; the paper does not say which, so recording both is Inference), since the two differ by about 14 percent at L5. Expect Indian male direct means 7.05, 7.85, 9.01, 11.6, 16.19 mm L1 to L5. Table 1, p. 455 (PDF 3); Figure 1, p. 454 (PDF 2).
2. Use TD's steep rise at L3-L4 and L4-L5 (+2.6 and +4.6 mm in males) to place L4 and L5; do not rely on TD to split L1 from L2 (+0.8 mm direct, and only 0.09 mm on CT: 7.5 against 7.59). Table 1, p. 455 (PDF 3).
3. Compute the shape ratio TD/SD; expect about 0.45 at L1 rising to about 1.0 at L5 (Inference from Tables 1-2, p. 455, PDF 3). A pedicle as wide as tall is an L5 cue in this population.
4. Measure the transverse angle against the vertebra's own midsagittal plane, never the scanner's axis; expect 9 to 15 deg at L1-L4 and about 29 deg at L5 (direct), 24 deg on CT. Inference (from means only; no SDs are printed): a jump of more than 10 deg between consecutive instances marks L4 to L5 (male means jump 14.6 deg direct and 12.0 deg on CT). Table 1, p. 455 (PDF 3); p. 458 (PDF 6).
5. On a lateral radiograph or lateral fluoroscopy, take only sagittal measures: pedicle height (SD, about 15 mm, flat), sagittal angle against the superior end plate, and the spacing of pedicle shadows between levels; these are the three the paper took on lateral films. p. 453 (PDF 1); Table 2, p. 455 (PDF 3). Inference: do not try TD, TA or chord length from a lateral view; the paper took them only on sectioned bone and axial CT.
6. Read the sagittal angle as positive when cephalad. Expect 8 to 10 deg (direct) or 5 to 6 deg (radiograph) at L1-L4; at L5 expect anything from about -9 to +15 deg, and report cephalad and caudad L5 pedicles separately, not as one mean. pp. 454, 456, 458 (PDF 2, 4, 6).
7. Correct radiographic values toward direct ones before comparing with bone data: SA on film reads about 3 to 6 deg lower, intersegmental pedicle spacing about 3 to 6 mm higher (Table 2, p. 455, PDF 3; my subtraction). Do not trust this paper's "magnification factor 0"; apply your own. p. 453 (PDF 1).
8. When a source says "interpedicular distance", check whether it means the craniocaudal spacing of adjacent pedicles (this paper, about 32 to 35 mm direct) or the transverse canal width between left and right pedicles on an AP film; they are different quantities. p. 456, p. 458 (PDF 4, 6); Inference.
9. Measure chord length from the posterior surface of the arch through the pedicle to the anterior body cortex twice, along the convergent pedicle axis and parallel to the midsagittal plane, because Figure 1 labels it "along pedicle axis" but draws it nearly parallel to the midline (p. 454, PDF 2). Expect a flat 46 to 50 mm L1 to L4 in males, with L5 shortest on direct measurement (45.87); do not use it to name a level. Table 1, p. 455 (PDF 3); p. 458 (PDF 6).
10. Expect the pedicle entry point at the transverse-process midline and in the lateral third of the facet at L1 to L4, and lateral to the facet's lateral border at L5 (all 20 cadavers). p. 456 (PDF 4); p. 457 (PDF 5). Two cadavers had it at the bottom of the transverse process at some levels (specimen 5 at L3-L4, specimen 9 at L1-L3 and L5), about 6 of 100 level observations (Inference by count). Inference, not tested in the paper (which used dissected bone): on AP fluoroscopy a pedicle oval lying lateral to the superior facet's lateral border is an L5 cue.
11. Flag any lumbar pedicle under 5 mm wide as a real but rare variant in this population, not a segmentation failure by default; check the mask before rejecting it. p. 454 (PDF 2); p. 457 (PDF 5).
12. When the patient is Indian (Inference: perhaps South Asian more widely, which the paper does not test), shift pedicle width priors down from white series (L5 about 16 against 21 mm in males) and convergence up (L5 about 29 against 17 deg); Chinese series lie lower still in width at L5 (12.8). p. 456-457 (PDF 4-5).
13. Do not use this paper for T12, T12/L1, S1 or transitional anatomy; it has none. p. 453 (PDF 1).
14. Treat CT-derived pedicle widths and angles at L4-L5 as slightly low and chord lengths as slightly high relative to bone (CT minus direct, males: TD -1.8 and -1.6 mm, TA -2.4 and -5.0 deg at L4 and L5; CL +1 to +3.5 mm). Table 1, p. 455 (PDF 3); p. 456 (PDF 4).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13, rib anomalies or block vertebrae, and does not say how levels were counted. The only variant frequencies are morphological: caudad L5 pedicles (this paper's own count is not given, only that it was significantly smaller than Zindrick's, p. 456, PDF 4; Table 2, p. 455, PDF 3, quotes Olsewski's L5 radiographic sagittal angle as 7 in 30 and -10 in 12, which the olsewski1990 note in this set reads as 30 cephalad and 12 caudad of 42 pedicles of 21 spines, although Mitra's row is labelled n = 49; Inference) and a low entry point in about 6 of 100 level observations (Inference, p. 456, PDF 4). Neither is a numbering anomaly; a caudad L5 pedicle is a weak shape cue for L5, not a prior on the count.

(b) Measures the generator holds or could add, and definition differences:
- Pedicle width: TD here corresponds to Panjabi's PDW (Inference). Panjabi digitized the pedicle loop without saying it was the narrowest section (sibling note on Panjabi 1992; Inference); TD here is the narrowest. TD's axis of measurement is ambiguous (Figure 1 drawn mediolateral); a mask extractor must state whether it measures perpendicular to the pedicle axis (smaller at L5 by up to 14 percent) or along the vertebra's x axis.
- Pedicle height: SD here corresponds to PDH. This paper's SD stays flat at L5 (15.67 male direct; 14.8 on film), whereas Panjabi's PDH rises to about 19.6 at L5 (sibling note; Inference). A generator built on Panjabi will predict taller L5 pedicles than this Indian series shows.
- Transverse angle: TA here is the same quantity as Panjabi's PDIs (angle to the sagittal plane). This paper's values are larger at L5 (29.3 deg direct) than Panjabi's (about 24.5 deg) and smaller at L1 (9 against about 14.5) (sibling note; Inference).
- Sagittal angle: SA here is referenced to the superior end-plate line on a lateral view, signed (cephalad positive). Panjabi's PDIt is referenced to the vertebra's transverse plane and is unsigned in his text, and the panjabi1992 note says his Figure 1 draws the pedicle axis with its posterior end higher, whereas this paper's Figure 2 draws a cephalad axis with its anterior end higher (sibling note; Inference). These differ by any tilt of the end plate relative to Panjabi's frame and perhaps in sign; extract both from a mask, keep them distinct, and check the sign before mapping one onto the other.
- Chord length along the pedicle axis: not in Panjabi's lumbar set (Inference); a candidate addition, but it does not separate L1 to L4, and this paper's figure leaves open whether its CL is along the axis or parallel to the midline.
- Intersegmental pedicle spacing (this paper's IPD): not a per-vertebra measure; it can be derived from body heights plus disc heights in the generator, or computed between consecutive instances in the decoder as a transition feature (Inference).
- Medial and lateral cortical thickness: needs intensities, not a mask; not for the generator.
- Derived shape ratio TD/SD: size-free, monotonic L1 to L5 (0.45 to 1.03 here); a good classifier feature (Inference).

(c) Measurement error to use: the paper gives only instrument resolution (0.1 mm, 1 deg) and no between-subject SDs, so it cannot set likelihood widths. Modality offsets (male means, my subtraction): CT minus bone TD up to -1.8 mm, TA up to -5 deg, CL up to +3.5 mm; lateral film minus bone SD within +/-0.9 mm, SA -3.4 to -6.1 deg, IPD +3 to +5.7 mm. Use SDs from Olsewski, Hou or Zindrick in this set for spread.

(d) Covariates: population (Indian, Nagpur cadavers; smaller pedicle widths than white series, larger convergence, closer to Chinese and Korean series), sex (2 females, narrower TD at every level, not analysed), age (males 20 to 70, no age analysis), size (short stature suggested as a cause of narrow pedicles, no stature data). The paper gives no size factor to calibrate.
