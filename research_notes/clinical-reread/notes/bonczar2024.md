## What the paper is

- Design: systematic review with random-effects meta-analysis of published lumbar morphometry, following PRISMA, with the CATAM and AQUA quality tools named (p. 3, PDF 3). Software: STATISTICA 13.1, MetaXL 5.3, Comprehensive Meta-analysis 4.0 (p. 4, PDF 4). Heterogeneity was to be judged by chi-square and I-squared with the Cochrane bands (0 to 40, 30 to 60, 50 to 90, 75 to 100 percent; the paper calls both of the top two bands "substantial", p. 4, PDF 4), and two pooled values were called different only when their confidence intervals did not overlap, with p < 0.05 as significance (p. 4, PDF 4). No I-squared value is reported anywhere.
- Search: PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, Google Scholar and Cochrane Library, in three stages (broad terms; eight title/abstract strings pairing "lumbar spine" or "lumbar vertebrae" with anatomy, morphology, dimensions, size, width, length, variation, morphometry; a manual reference search), with no date, language, article-type or full-text limit (pp. 2-3, PDF 2-3). The printed strings spell "lumber spine" (p. 2, PDF 2).
- Flow (Fig. 1, p. 2, PDF 2): 3658 records; 2096 duplicates or irrelevant removed; 1562 screened; 1378 excluded; 184 full reports; 97 excluded for irrelevance or incomplete data and 69 as case reports, abstracts or letters; 18 studies included.
- Inclusion: original articles with extractable data on overall lumbar anatomy. Exclusion: conference reports, case reports, case series, reviews, letters, patients with "a noticeable pathology that could potentially distort" the anatomy, and studies with no relevant or incompatible data (p. 3, PDF 3). Two independent reviewers screened and extracted; disagreements resolved by contacting authors or by a third person (pp. 3-4, PDF 3-4).
- Population and denominators (Table 1, p. 3, PDF 3): 1481 "individuals" in 18 studies, the "research group" column summed. By continent: Asia 1076 (14 studies), Oceania 206 (2 New Zealand studies), Europe 199 (Spain 73, UK 126). Rows (first author, year as printed in Table 1, country, method, n): Naidoo 2022 UAE MRI 50; Irshad 2022a Pakistan CT 26 (women 45 +/- 7.3 y, men 48 +/- 9.7 y); Irshad 2022b Pakistan radiograph 81 (women 29 +/- 6.9, men 34 +/- 7.6); Cook 2021 New Zealand CT 196 (42 +/- 19.5 y); Singh 2021 India CT 302; Morita 2021 Japan CT 227 (women 66.5 +/- 15.2, men 67 +/- 13.1); Agichani 2018 India radiograph 120; Gomez-Olivencia 2017 Spain digital calipers 73; Wang 2015 China CT 5; Mahato 2011 India radiograph 50 (32 +/- 7.82); Been 2010 Israel Vernier caliper 97; Tan 2003 Singapore 3D digitiser 10 (65.7 y, 61.6 kg, 1.66 m); Kadioglu 2003 Turkey CT 29 (35 y); Tan 2001 Singapore 3D digitiser 12 (67 y, 62 kg, 1.66 m); Wolf 2001 Israel CT 55 (57.64 +/- 17.84); Robertson 2000 New Zealand radiograph 10; Zhou 2000 UK CT 126 (women 49, men 50); Panjabi 1992 "Asia, India", radiograph, 12 (46.3 y, 67.8 kg, 1.678 m).
- By method (sums of Table 1): CT 8 studies, 966; radiograph 5 studies, 273; MRI 1 study, 50; calipers 2 studies, 170; 3D digitiser 2 studies, 22. Inference: living patients (CT, MRI, radiograph), cadavers (the digitiser studies; Tan's are cadavers by the discussion, p. 9, PDF 9) and skeletal collections (the caliper studies, whose titles in the reference list are Neandertal comparisons, refs 6 and 10, p. 11, PDF 11, so their 97 and 73 are presumably modern comparison samples) are pooled as one "individuals" count; Wang 2015 is titled as a deer and sheep study compared with the human spine (ref 34, p. 12, PDF 12), and Table 1 gives it 5 subjects, presumably human.
- The 18 included studies are refs 1, 6, 7, 10, 15, 16, 18, 20, 22, 23, 25, 27, 30, 32-34, 36 and 38 (p. 4, PDF 4). Their titles in the reference list (pp. 11-12, PDF 11-12) add population and scope detail that Table 1 lacks: Cook compares Maori and New Zealand Europeans on CT (ref 7); Agichani is a Central Indian population (ref 1); Irshad's two papers are from Punjab, one on body and disc morphometry and one on canal dimensions in subjects with and without low back pain (refs 15, 16; which one is "2022 (a)" and which "(b)" is not stated, Inference only by list order); Kadioglu measured lumbar pedicles only, Eastern Anatolian (ref 18); Morita measured thoracic and lumbar pedicles, Japanese (ref 22); Robertson measured lumbar and lumbosacral pedicles (ref 27); Zhou measured the lower lumbar vertebrae from digitised CT (ref 38); Naidoo built 3D models from 2D MRI (ref 23); Tan 2002 and Tan 2004 are Singaporean Asians and Chinese Singaporeans, the second covering cervical, thoracic and lumbar levels (refs 32, 33); Wolf measured for operation-workspace specifications (ref 36). Inference: since several sources measured only pedicles or only the lower levels, each table cell pools a different subset of the 18 studies and of the 1481 subjects.
- Sex mix, age range and body size of the whole pool are not given; study mean ages run from 29 to 67 years (Table 1). Age, weight and height were extracted but not correlated with the measures, which the authors say was to avoid biasing the results (p. 10, PDF 10). Weight and height are given for three studies only (Tan 2003, Tan 2001, Panjabi 1992; Table 1).
- Levels: L1 to L5 only, each vertebra separately, 27 measures per level (Tables 2 to 6, pp. 5-9, PDF 5-9) plus a sex table of anterior body height and interpedicular distance (Table 7, p. 10, PDF 10). No thoracic, sacral or junctional segment.
- How levels were identified or counted in the source studies: not stated. The authors number L1 to L5 from the top (p. 1, PDF 1). Transitional segments were not considered (p. 10, PDF 10).
- Pooling across methods: the authors say an overall analysis was allowed because results by different methods "did not differ statistically significantly" (p > 0.05) (p. 4, PDF 4); no method-wise result is shown.
- Number of studies or subjects behind each pooled cell: not reported.
- Each table gives, per measure and level: mean, SE, variance, lower and upper 95 percent limits, Z and p (Tables 2-7, pp. 5-10, PDF 5-10); the results text on pp. 4-6 (PDF 4-6) restates the end-plate, height, canal width and canal area values level by level.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- The paper gives no prevalence of any anomaly. The authors state that transitional anomalies of the lumbar spine were not considered and that this lowers the clinical use of the result (p. 10, PDF 10).
- Inference: one included study, Mahato 2011 (radiographs, India, n = 50; ref 20, p. 11, PDF 11), is titled as a study of spines with L5-S1 transitions, so transitional L5 or S1 segments may have entered the pooled L5 values without being separated.
- Numbering: L1 to L5 from the top (p. 1, PDF 1); no rule for counting (from C2, from T12 ribs, or from the sacrum) is given.
- Developmental notes that bear on anomalies and on masks (p. 7, PDF 7): ossification starts about the 8th week of gestation from three primary centres, one in the centrum (which forms the body) and one in each neural process (which, the paper says, forms the pedicles); it begins at the thoracolumbar junction and spreads cranially and caudally; neural processes fuse to the centrum between 3 and 6 years; five secondary centres appear at puberty (spinous tip, both transverse processes, superior and inferior body surfaces), the body's centres adding to its cranio-caudal growth; ossification completes about age 25. The paper does not tie any of this to an anomaly.

## Measures, each as a recipe

General for every measure below:
- The Tables give the pooled mean, its standard error (SE), the variance of the pooled mean (it equals SE squared), a 95 percent confidence interval of the mean (mean +/- 1.96 SE) and a Z and p that test the mean against zero (pp. 5-10, PDF 5-10). Inference: the SE is the uncertainty of the pooled mean under a random-effects model, not the spread of individual vertebrae; no SD per subject is given, so no measure here can be turned into an individual-level sigma without the source studies.
- The only definitions are the schematic of Fig. 2 (p. 4, PDF 4): an axial view (SCW, SCD, SCA, PW, PH, PA, PIS, SPL, SPW, EPAU/EPAL), a lateral view (PIT, VBHA, VBHP, EPWU, EPWL) and a frontal view (EPDU, EPDL). No landmark text, instrument per measure, or plane of section is given.
- Pitfall in Fig. 2: the lateral view draws EPWU and EPWL as the anteroposterior span of the end plates, and the frontal view draws EPDU and EPDL as the mediolateral span (p. 4, PDF 4). Inference: the labels are swapped against the values, which follow the usual convention (width mediolateral, 41 to 51 mm; depth anteroposterior, 31 to 34 mm). Use width = mediolateral, depth = anteroposterior. The caption adds a second slip: it expands EPWL as "End-plate depth lower", the same words as EPDL (p. 4, PDF 4).
- The paper's own test for a difference is non-overlap of the 95 percent CIs (p. 4, PDF 4). Under the "Across levels" lines, "separated by the CI rule" means the reader checked the printed lower and upper limits of two levels (Tables 2-6) and found no overlap; this is Inference from the paper's rule, not a test the paper ran between levels.
- Inference for all "From a radiograph alone" lines: the paper says nothing about projections or magnification; the lines below are the reader's mapping of each measure to a projection.
- Values are given as L1, L2, L3, L4, L5 means with SE in parentheses.

### End-plate depth, upper and lower (EPDU, EPDL)

- Landmarks: anteroposterior extent of the cranial (upper) or caudal (lower) end plate of the body (Fig. 2, labels swapped, see above). Plane: midsagittal of the body. Instrument: mixed (CT, MRI, radiograph, calipers, digitiser). Levels L1 to L5.
- Values, upper: 31.04 (1.07), 32.12 (1.09), 33.17 (1.07), 33.83 (1.19), 34.09 (0.91) mm. Lower: 32.27 (1.20), 32.91 (1.17), 33.76 (1.11), 34.07 (0.95), 33.18 (1.18) mm. Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9 (PDF 5-9); text pp. 4-6 (PDF 4-6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Take the body subregion (or the part of the instance anterior to the pedicle roots), fit the end-plate surface (the cranial or caudal boundary voxels of the body), define the vertebra's anteroposterior axis in the end-plate plane (body centroid toward canal centroid), and take the extent of the end-plate surface along that axis through the midsagittal line. Decide whether the ring apophysis and osteophytes are in or out, and keep it fixed.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, the length of the end-plate line from anterior to posterior cortex; magnified by the source-to-image geometry.
- Across levels: upper depth rises monotonically, L1 to L5, by 3.1 mm in all; adjacent steps are 0.3 to 1.1 mm, each no larger than about one pooled SE (largest step 1.08 against SEs of 1.07 to 1.09). Lower depth rises to L4 (34.07) and falls at L5 (33.18), so L5 is the one level whose lower end plate is shallower than its upper (33.18 against 34.09). Inference: by the CI rule depth separates no pair of levels at all, not even L1 from L5 (upper L1 CI 28.94 to 33.14 against L5 32.32 to 35.86).
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported per sex. Asian-weighted pool (p. 10, PDF 10).
- Error: none reported.

### End-plate width, upper and lower (EPWU, EPWL)

- Landmarks: mediolateral extent of the cranial or caudal end plate (Fig. 2, labels swapped). Plane: coronal or axial at the end plate.
- Values, upper: 41.09 (1.60), 42.98 (1.57), 44.82 (1.62), 47.50 (1.87), 48.81 (1.91) mm. Lower: 43.97 (1.74), 46.10 (2.12), 49.12 (2.05), 50.63 (1.90), 49.82 (2.01) mm. Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9 (PDF 5-9).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Same frame as depth; the extent of the end-plate surface along the mediolateral axis, through the end-plate centroid or as the maximum chord perpendicular to the midsagittal plane; exclude the pedicle roots.
- From a radiograph alone: AP radiograph, the width of the body at the end-plate line; magnified.
- Across levels: upper width rises monotonically, 41.1 to 48.8 mm; lower width rises to L4 (50.6) and falls slightly at L5 (49.8). Within each vertebra the lower end plate is wider than the upper by 2.9 (L1), 3.1 (L2), 4.3 (L3), 3.1 (L4) and 1.0 (L5) mm. Inference: the lower end plate of each level is also wider than the upper end plate of the level below (43.97 against 42.98, 46.10 against 44.82, 49.12 against 47.50, 50.63 against 48.81). Width/depth ratio rises, upper 1.32 (L1) to 1.43 (L5), lower 1.36 to 1.50 (Inference, computed from the means). Inference by the CI rule: no adjacent pair separates on width; only L1 against L5 on upper width separates (37.96 to 44.22 against 45.08 to 52.55), while lower width does not even separate L1 from L5 (40.56 to 47.38 against 45.88 to 53.75).
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported per sex.
- Error: none reported.

### Vertebral body height, anterior and posterior (VBHA, VBHP)

- Landmarks: distance between the cranial and caudal end plates at the anterior cortex (VBHA) and at the posterior cortex (VBHP), lateral view (Fig. 2, p. 4, PDF 4).
- Values, anterior: 24.54 (0.96), 25.75 (1.22), 26.83 (1.16), 26.86 (1.08), 27.44 (1.16) mm. Posterior: 26.57 (1.06), 27.02 (1.06), 26.67 (1.02), 25.76 (1.03), 24.11 (0.70) mm. Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9 (PDF 5-9).
- By sex (Table 7, p. 10, PDF 10), anterior height, men: 23.66 (0.46), 24.63 (0.89), 25.90 (0.15), 25.87 (0.08), 26.92 (0.56); women: 23.17 (0.03), 24.66 (0.02), 25.53 (0.61), 25.77 (0.98), 26.67 (1.44) mm. The authors note women exceed men only at L2 (24.66 against 24.63) (p. 9, PDF 9).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the midsagittal plane of the body subregion, measure the cranio-caudal distance between the superior and inferior end-plate surfaces along the vertebra's own long axis at the anterior cortex and at the posterior cortex (just anterior to the canal). Exclude anterior osteophytes.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, anterior and posterior heights of the body outline. The ratio VBHA/VBHP is free of magnification.
- Across levels: anterior height rises L1 to L5 (24.5 to 27.4), flat between L3 and L4 (26.83 and 26.86). Posterior height peaks at L2 (27.02) and falls to its minimum at L5 (24.11). Inference: the wedge ratio VBHA/VBHP is 0.92 (L1), 0.95 (L2), 1.01 (L3), 1.04 (L4), 1.14 (L5): the body is taller behind at L1 and L2, about even at L3, and taller in front at L4 and most of all at L5. This flip is the strongest scale-free level cue the paper holds for a lateral view.
- Sex, age, size, population: men and women differ by -0.03 to 0.49 mm in anterior height (Table 7); every pair of male and female confidence intervals overlaps, so by the paper's own rule (p. 4, PDF 4) none differs (Inference from the rule).
- Error: none reported. Inference: the female SEs of 0.03 and 0.02 mm at L1 and L2 (Table 7) are implausibly small for a pooled height and suggest one study or a reporting error.

### End-plate area, upper and lower (EPAU, EPAL)

- Landmarks: area of the cranial or caudal end-plate surface in the axial view; Fig. 2 draws a dotted contour inside the body outline (p. 4, PDF 4), not explained.
- Values, upper: 932.26 (56.46), 1017.74 (61.40), 1132.32 (63.14), 1151.43 (72.55), 1182.51 (65.77) mm2. Lower: 1039.83 (55.77), 1120.01 (60.84), 935.91 (401.38), 1199.99 (69.64), 1131.35 (61.99) mm2. Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9 (PDF 5-9).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Count the voxels of the body's cranial or caudal end-plate layer projected onto the end-plate plane (or the area of the body's axial section 1 mm inside the end plate). State whether the ring apophysis is included.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (axial measure). Inference: an ellipse-like estimate from AP width and lateral depth is possible; the pooled area/(width x depth) is 0.73, 0.74, 0.76, 0.72, 0.71 for upper and 0.73, 0.74, 0.56, 0.70, 0.68 for lower (computed from the means), below the ellipse value 0.785, so a kidney-shaped section.
- Across levels: upper area rises monotonically; lower area rises L1 to L2, drops at L3 (935.91, SE 401.38, CI 149.22 to 1722.59) and peaks at L4. Inference: the L3 lower value is an outlier, smaller than its own width and depth imply (about 1180 mm2 at the 0.71 ratio of the other levels); disregard it. L5 lower area (1131) is smaller than L5 upper (1183).
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported.
- Error: none reported; the L3 lower SE marks heterogeneity.

### End-plate inclination, transverse plane, upper and lower

- Landmarks: not defined and not drawn in Fig. 2. Units degrees.
- Values, upper: 2.18 (0.23), 3.66 (0.24), 1.93 (0.13), 4.21 (0.29), 5.71 (2.02) deg. Lower: -0.55 (1.69), -0.09 (0.86), 2.11 (0.18), 3.24 (0.32), 8.20 (3.72) deg. The lower values at L1 and L2 do not differ from zero (p 0.75 and 0.91). Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9 (PDF 5-9).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Fit a plane to each end-plate surface and report its tilt against a reference plane of the vertebra (for example the plane normal to the posterior wall line or to the vertebra's long axis); the paper does not say which reference or which direction is positive, so a mask value cannot be matched to these numbers without the source definition (Panjabi's own definition, if this is his measure).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference, the sagittal tilt of an end plate against the posterior wall can be taken on a lateral radiograph; whether that is this measure is unknown.
- Across levels: the upper value zigzags (2.18, 3.66, 1.93, 4.21, 5.71: a dip at L3) and is largest at L5; the lower value rises steadily from about zero at L1 and L2 to 8.20 deg at L5. The L5 values carry large SEs (2.02 and 3.72; lower p = 0.03), so the L5 peak is uncertain. Inference: the lower rise matches the L5 anterior wedge.
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported. Error: none reported.

### Spinal canal width (SCW)

- Landmarks: mediolateral width of the vertebral canal in the axial view (Fig. 2, p. 4, PDF 4), drawn between the medial pedicle walls.
- Values: 22.04 (0.91), 22.15 (0.94), 22.62 (0.80), 23.19 (0.82), 26.46 (0.89) mm. Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9 (PDF 5-9); abstract p. 1 (PDF 1).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the axial slice through the pedicle isthmi, fill the ring bounded by the body's posterior wall and the arch (or use a canal label if the segmenter has one), and take the minimum distance between the medial pedicle cortices.
- From a radiograph alone: AP radiograph as the interpedicular distance between the inner pedicle cortices; magnified.
- Across levels: nearly flat L1 to L4 (22.0 to 23.2, steps of 0.1 to 0.6 mm), then a step of 3.3 mm at L5. Inference by the CI rule: L5 separates from L1, L2 and L3, but its CI (24.71 to 28.20) overlaps L4's (21.59 to 24.79) by 0.08 mm, so by the paper's own rule the L4 to L5 step is not a significant difference; L1 to L4 do not separate. The abstract and conclusions call the rise "progressive" from L1 to L5 (pp. 1, 10, PDF 1, 10); the table shows a flat run and one step.
- Sex, age, size, population: see interpedicular distance.
- Error: none reported.

### Interpedicular distance (IPD), by sex only

- Landmarks: not defined; named only in the results text (p. 6, PDF 6) and Table 7 (p. 10, PDF 10). Which studies supplied it is not said. Inference: the same span as canal width, perhaps from radiographs.
- Values, men: 21.81 (0.12), 22.96 (0.36), 24.51 (0.14), 26.71 (0.92), 30.65 (3.36) mm. Women: 20.95 (0.27), 21.85 (0.47), 23.39 (0.08), 25.80 (1.11), 29.27 (3.18) mm.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: as canal width.
- From a radiograph alone: AP radiograph; magnified. Inference: the L5 values (29 to 31 mm) exceed the pooled L5 canal width (26.46), consistent with a different set of studies or with radiographic magnification.
- Across levels: rises at every step, most from L4 to L5 (3.9 mm in men, 3.5 mm in women).
- Sex: men larger by 0.86, 1.11, 1.12, 0.91, 1.38 mm (3.5 to 5.1 percent) (Inference, from the means). By the paper's overlap rule only L1 and L3 differ (Inference from the rule).
- Error: none reported; the L5 SE (3.2 to 3.4 mm) marks heterogeneity.

### Spinal canal depth (SCD)

- Landmarks: anteroposterior depth of the vertebral canal in the axial view (Fig. 2), from the posterior body wall to the inner arch at the midline.
- Values: 16.02 (1.07), 15.08 (1.00), 14.69 (0.93), 15.06 (1.04), 15.69 (1.37) mm. Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9 (PDF 5-9).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the midsagittal plane of the canal at pedicle level, the distance from the posterior cortex of the body to the anterior cortex of the lamina (or the spinolaminar junction).
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, from the posterior body line to the spinolaminar line; magnified.
- Across levels: U-shaped, minimum at L3 (14.69), largest at L1 (16.02); total range 1.3 mm, within the SE. Inference: no level separation.
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported.
- Error: none reported.

### Spinal canal area (SCA)

- Landmarks: area of the vertebral canal in the axial view (Fig. 2).
- Values: 251.01 (16.47), 227.28 (18.57), 213.87 (24.17), 215.45 (35.98), 245.12 (32.12) mm2. Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9 (PDF 5-9).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Area of the filled canal hole in the axial slice through the pedicle isthmi.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible.
- Across levels: falls from L1 to a minimum at L3, then rises to L5; the discussion's claim of a progressive rise (p. 8, PDF 8) does not match the table. Inference: SCA/(SCW x SCD) is 0.71, 0.68, 0.64, 0.62, 0.59 from L1 to L5, so the canal turns from round-oval toward triangular-trefoil caudally, in line with the paper's statement that the lumbar foramen is triangular (p. 1, PDF 1).
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported.
- Error: none reported.

### Pedicle width (PW), overall, right and left

- Landmarks: mediolateral width of the pedicle in the axial view (Fig. 2).
- Values, overall: 7.51 (0.58), 8.23 (0.20), 9.71 (0.18), 11.52 (0.24), 14.58 (1.03) mm. Right: 7.30, 7.64, 9.56, 11.98, 15.68 mm. Left: 7.73, 8.02, 9.77, 12.59, 15.93 mm. Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9 (PDF 5-9); discussion p. 7 (PDF 7).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Along the pedicle axis (body to arch), take the minimal mediolateral outer width of the pedicle in axial sections (the isthmus); left and right separately.
- From a radiograph alone: AP radiograph, the width of the pedicle oval; magnified.
- Across levels: rises monotonically; steps of 0.7 (L1-L2), 1.5 (L2-L3), 1.8 (L3-L4) and 3.1 (L4-L5) mm. Inference by the CI rule: L2|L3, L3|L4 and L4|L5 separate (8.61 against 9.35, 10.07 against 11.05, 11.99 against 12.55); L1|L2 does not (L1 6.36 to 8.65 holds L2's 7.84 to 8.61). Pedicle width is the one measure here that separates three of the four adjacent pairs. The side-specific widths, with larger SEs (0.42 to 1.34), separate fewer pairs.
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported. No left-right difference by the authors' rule (p. 7, p. 11, PDF 7, 11).
- Error: none reported. Inference: overall and side values come from different study sets (L5 overall 14.58 against 15.68 and 15.93 by side).

### Pedicle height (PH), overall, right and left

- Landmarks: cranio-caudal height of the pedicle; labelled in the axial view of Fig. 2 without its own arrow.
- Values, overall: 15.40 (0.28), 14.90 (0.19), 14.52 (0.17), 14.20 (0.34), 14.73 (1.06) mm. Right: 15.71, 15.31, 15.31, 15.49, 18.05 mm. Left: 15.47, 15.55, 14.36, 15.13, 18.16 mm. Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9 (PDF 5-9).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In sagittal sections through the pedicle, the minimal cranio-caudal height between the superior and inferior pedicle cortices (between the notches).
- From a radiograph alone: AP radiograph, the height of the pedicle oval; lateral radiograph, the pedicle between the superior and inferior vertebral notches. Magnified.
- Across levels: overall falls L1 to L4 and rises a little at L5 (abstract p. 1, discussion p. 7, PDF 1, 7). The side values do not show the fall (about 14.4 to 15.7 mm to L4, the lowest the left L3 at 14.36) and jump to about 18 mm at L5. The discussion lists studies that disagree (p. 7, PDF 7): two of them are among the 18 included, Singh (CT, 302 Indian patients, ref 30) with a fall L1 to L5 and Wolf (CT, ref 36) with a fall to L3 and a rise at L4 and L5; three are cited but not included (refs 3, 4, 29 are absent from the included list, p. 4, PDF 4): Arora (cadavers, 25 men and 30 women; Delhi by its title) and Seema (100 radiographs; adult Punjabi men by its title) with a rise L1 to L5, and Alam (CT, 49 patients; Pakistani by its title) with a fall. Inference: pedicle height is not a reliable level cue; no adjacent pair separates by the CI rule.
- Inference: the width/height ratio of the pedicle from the overall means is 0.49, 0.55, 0.67, 0.81, 0.99 from L1 to L5: a tall narrow oval at L1 becoming round at L5. From the side-specific means it is 0.46, 0.50, 0.62, 0.77, 0.87 (right) and 0.50, 0.52, 0.68, 0.83, 0.88 (left), so the L5 value depends on which source subset is used (about 0.87 to 0.99). The ratio cancels magnification on one projection; Inference also: on an AP view the pedicle shadow is the projection of an obliquely converging pedicle, so its apparent width is not the isthmus width.
- Sex, age, size, population: not reported.

## Measurement error and reliability

- No intra- or inter-observer error, repeatability, instrument resolution or radiographic magnification is reported or discussed.
- Extraction was double (two reviewers), with a third person or the source authors resolving disagreement (pp. 3-4, PDF 3-4).
- Heterogeneity: tests are named (p. 4, PDF 4) but no I-squared or chi-square value is printed; the only signs of heterogeneity are the large SEs (L3 lower end-plate area 401.38 mm2; left PIS up to 8.26 deg; L5 interpedicular distance about 3.3 mm). At the other extreme several sex-specific SEs are implausibly small for a random-effects pool (anterior height, women L1 0.03 and L2 0.02, men L3 0.15 and L4 0.08; interpedicular distance, men L1 0.12 and L3 0.14, women L3 0.08 mm; Table 7, p. 10, PDF 10). Inference: those cells likely rest on one or two studies.
- The authors state that results by different methods did not differ (p > 0.05) (p. 4, PDF 4), but the per-method comparison is not shown, and the limitations name differing researchers and methods as a possible bias (p. 10, PDF 10).
- The authors list as limits: dependence on the accuracy of source publications, differing researchers and methods as a source of bias, analyses not done for lack of consistent data, no correlation with age, weight or height (left out, they say, to avoid bias), transitional anomalies not considered, and an Asian-weighted sample, n = 1076, which may reflect Asian anatomy rather than the global population (p. 10, PDF 10).
- The data availability statement says no datasets were generated or analysed (p. 11, PDF 11), so the per-study extracted values behind each pooled cell are not available.
- Quality appraisal (CATAM, AQUA) is named (p. 3, PDF 3) but no score or risk-of-bias table is given.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Pedicle screws: pedicle width rises from 7.51 mm at L1 to 14.58 mm at L5; a screw-pedicle mismatch risks cortical perforation, pedicle fracture and screw loosening (pp. 7-8, PDF 7-8, citing Seema).
- End plates: normal end-plate size guides vertebral body replacement or augmentation (vertebroplasty, kyphoplasty) and the design and choice of implants and prostheses (p. 8, PDF 8).
- Canal: canal size guides decompression (laminectomy, canal decompression); knowing it helps avoid over-decompression, which risks instability, and under-decompression, which may leave symptoms (p. 8, PDF 8). The canal widens caudally in step with the cord giving way to the cauda equina (p. 8, PDF 8). No stenosis threshold is given.
- Endoscopy: percutaneous endoscopic transforaminal discectomy, notably YESS, reaches the disc through Kambin's triangle and removes nucleus through the annulus with a rongeur; indications run from a single herniation to lumbar stenosis, metastatic tumours, discal cysts and revision for recurrent herniation (p. 9, PDF 9); morphometric knowledge is said to help navigation through the region and lower intraoperative complications (p. 10, PDF 10).
- Purpose stated by the authors: the pooled values describe individuals without pathological change and are offered as normal reference points when diagnosing spinal pathology (p. 6, PDF 6), and, with CT or radiographs, as an aid to choosing a prosthesis for each patient (p. 9, PDF 9).
- Disc replacement: disc degeneration drives chronic low back pain; total disc replacement avoids fusion; second-generation prostheses control motion in all directions; vertebral body morphometry helps (p. 10, PDF 10).
- Other procedures named: surgery for herniated nucleus pulposus, discectomy, hernia excision, reconstruction after fracture (p. 9, PDF 9).
- Sex: males larger on every pooled sex measure except L2 anterior height (p. 9, PDF 9); Singh's Indian CT series had every measure larger in men; Alam found greater sagittal pedicle inclination in women (p. 9, PDF 9).
- Population: Tan (60 lumbar vertebrae of 12 Singaporean cadavers) found Asians larger than Panjabi's Caucasian series on numerous measures despite smaller physique (p. 9, PDF 9).
- Pitfall, the paper's own wording: the introduction says the anterior body is taller than the posterior (p. 1, PDF 1), but the pooled table shows the reverse at L1 and L2 (Tables 2-3).
- Pitfall, figure: width and depth labels are swapped in Fig. 2 (p. 4, PDF 4), and the caption expands EPWL with the words of EPDL; PIS and PIT are named for the plane they are measured from, not the plane they lie in (Inference from the drawing).
- Pitfall, text against table: the discussion claims canal width and area and both end plates' depth and width rise steadily to L5 (p. 8, PDF 8); the tables show canal area lowest at L3, lower end plates largest at L4, and canal width flat to L4. The conclusions say the transverse processes widen "significantly" toward L5 (p. 10, PDF 10), yet L4 is narrower than L3 and no adjacent CI pair separates.
- Pitfall, sources: the pedicle-height debate in the discussion (p. 7, PDF 7) and the sex findings of Alam (p. 9, PDF 9) draw partly on studies outside the 18 pooled ones (Arora, Seema, Alam; refs 3, 4, 29 against the included list, p. 4, PDF 4).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Treat every value here as a pooled adult mean, weighted to Asian series (1076 of 1481), with an SE of the mean, not a per-vertebra spread; do not use the SE as the sigma of a level likelihood (pp. 3, 5-10, PDF 3, 5-10).
2. Before using any width or depth, read width as mediolateral and depth as anteroposterior, whatever Fig. 2's labels say (p. 4, PDF 4).
3. On an AP radiograph or fluoroscopy frame, measure each pedicle oval's width and height and use width/height as a scale-free level cue: about 0.5 at L1 rising to about 0.9 to 1.0 at L5 (Inference: ratios computed from the pooled means of Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9; the side-specific means give 0.87 to 0.88 at L5). Pedicle width itself rises at every step, and by the paper's CI rule separates L2|L3, L3|L4 and L4|L5 but not L1|L2 (Tables 2-6). Guard: the AP shadow of a converging pedicle is not its isthmus width (Inference).
4. On a lateral view, measure anterior and posterior body heights and take their ratio: in the pooled means it is below 1 at L1 and L2, about 1 at L3, above 1 at L4 and about 1.14 at L5 (Inference: ratios from the means of Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9). Treat a body clearly taller behind as more likely upper lumbar than L4 or L5, but as a likelihood, not a rule: no per-vertebra spread is given and no single height separates adjacent levels by the CI rule.
5. On an AP view, look for the widening of the interpedicular distance at L5 (about 3.3 mm above L4 in pooled canal width, 3.5 to 3.9 mm in interpedicular distance) as a cue to the last lumbar level; know that by the paper's own CI rule the L4 and L5 canal widths overlap by 0.08 mm (Table 5 against Table 6, pp. 8-9), so the step is a weak cue alone; do not use canal width to separate L1 to L4, where it changes by under 1.2 mm in all (Tables 2-7, pp. 5-10).
6. Do not use canal depth, spinous process length, pedicle height or pedicle transverse-plane tilt to separate adjacent levels; their level-to-level changes are within the pooled SE or the sources disagree (pp. 5-9, p. 7, PDF 5-9, 7).
7. On an AP view, use transverse-process span with care: L5 widest, L3 wider than L4, L1 narrowest; check that a long process at the top is not a rib (Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9; rib check is Inference).
8. To decide thoracic against lumbar at the top of the sequence, use form, not size: mammillary processes on the superior articular processes, vertical, sagittally set facets with posteromedially facing superior facets and curved articular surfaces, a short thick spinous process projecting straight back, and a canal larger than the thoracic one and triangular (p. 1 and p. 6, PDF 1 and 6). Inference: on a lateral radiograph the facet orientation and spinous-process shape are the visible ones; on an axial CT or a mask, the facet orientation and canal shape.
9. Do not take this paper's L5 values as free of transitional segments: they were not excluded and one source studied L5-S1 transitions; count from C2 or from the ribs and look at the sacrum before applying L5 priors (p. 10, PDF 10; ref 20, p. 11).
10. When computing pedicle angles from a mask, fix one sign convention for both sides; do not match left-side values from this paper, whose signs conflict with the right side (Tables 2-6, pp. 5-9).
11. Discard the L3 lower end-plate area (935.91 mm2, SE 401.38, CI 149.22 to 1722.59); when a mask-derived end-plate area is checked, expect area/(width x depth) near 0.68 to 0.76 (Table 4, p. 7; ratio is Inference from the means of Tables 2-6).
12. In a child or adolescent, expect the neural processes unfused to the body until age 3 to 6, and five secondary centres (spinous tip, both transverse processes, superior and inferior body surfaces) from puberty until ossification completes about age 25 (p. 7, PDF 7). Inference: a segmenter may split an instance at these unfused junctions or drop the secondary centres; check for split or missing pieces before measuring.
13. The paper gives sex-specific values only for anterior body height and interpedicular distance (Table 7, p. 10). Apply a sex offset to interpedicular distance (men about 0.9 to 1.4 mm wider, significant by the CI rule only at L1 and L3) and none to anterior body height, where no level differs by the rule (Inference from the rule); for other measures the paper has no sex data beyond the report that Singh found every measure larger in men (p. 9, PDF 9).
14. Do not treat the pooled values as a per-method reference: CT, MRI, radiographs, calipers on dry bone and cadaver digitisers are pooled together (Table 1, p. 3), and the claimed absence of method differences is not shown (p. 4, PDF 4).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper reports no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13, rib-count variants or any other anomaly and states that transitional anomalies were not considered (p. 10, PDF 10).

(b) Measures the generator holds or could add:
- Overlap with Panjabi-type measures (Inference about the generator's set): end-plate width and depth upper and lower, anterior and posterior body height, end-plate area upper and lower, canal width, depth and area, pedicle width and height, pedicle angles in two planes, spinous process length, transverse process width, end-plate inclination. Pooled L1 to L5 means here can serve as a second, Asian-weighted reference against Panjabi's 12 cadaver spines, which are themselves one of the 18 sources.
- Additions worth taking: pedicle area by side; the derived scale-free ratios (pedicle width/height, VBHA/VBHP, end-plate width/depth, canal area/(width x depth)), which are the best level cues here and suit radiographs (Inference).
- Which pooled measures discriminate adjacent lumbar levels (Inference, by the paper's CI-overlap rule on Tables 2-6): pedicle width (L2|L3, L3|L4, L4|L5), pedicle area (L4|L5 only), canal width (no adjacent pair; L5 against L1 to L3 only). No end-plate dimension, body height, canal depth or area, spinous length or transverse span separates an adjacent pair. These are pooled-mean CIs, narrower than the spread of individual vertebrae, so the real overlap between levels is larger still.
- Bias risks for a mask-extracted value: (1) Fig. 2's swapped width/depth labels; (2) PIS and PIT named for the reference plane, not the plane of measurement; (3) spinous process length near 60 mm implies an anterior reference other than the laminar base; (4) pooled values mix CT, MRI, radiograph (magnified, by an unstated factor), dry bone and cadaver digitiser data, so a CT mask value may sit systematically off a pooled mean; (5) end-plate area depends on whether the ring apophysis and osteophytes are counted; (6) mask surfaces at about 1 mm voxels cut widths and depths by up to a voxel on each side (Inference).

(c) Measurement error to use: none given. Do not use the pooled SE as observer error or individual sigma. Inference: take observer error from another paper in this set.

(d) Covariates: sex (Table 7) only for anterior body height (no sex effect) and interpedicular distance (men about 1 mm, 3.5 to 5 percent, larger). Age, weight and height were not analysed (p. 10, PDF 10). Population: Asian-weighted; the authors cite an Asian (Singapore) series larger than Panjabi's Caucasian one on numerous measures (p. 9, PDF 9). Inference: no population covariate can be fitted from this paper.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Not reported: I-squared, number of studies and of subjects per cell, individual SDs, method-wise subgroups, quality scores, any observer error, any measure definition beyond a schematic, how levels were counted, sex mix of the pool, and values for SPW.
- Text against table: canal area does not rise steadily (p. 8 against Tables 2-6); lower end-plate depth and width do not rise to L5 (p. 8 against Tables 5-6); the introduction's anterior-taller wedge is false at L1 and L2 (p. 1 against Tables 2-3); "no left-right difference" (p. 1) fails for PIS by the authors' own non-overlap rule (Tables 2-6; Inference from the rule).
- Overall against side-specific pedicle values disagree at L5 (height 14.73 overall against 18.05 and 18.16 by side) and in trend (the overall fall in height L1 to L4 is absent by side) (Tables 2-6). Inference: different source subsets.
- Table 1 against discussion: Panjabi 1992 is listed as Asia, India, radiograph (p. 3, PDF 3), but the discussion calls Panjabi's series Caucasian (p. 9, PDF 9). Inference from memory, to check against the Panjabi 1992 note in this set: Panjabi measured 12 cadaveric spines with a three-dimensional digitiser in the United States; if so the Asian count is 1064, not 1076, and the radiograph count is 261.
- Table 1 years against the reference list: Tan 2001 row against ref 32 (2002), Tan 2003 row against ref 33 (2004), Singh 2021 row against ref 30 (2022) (pp. 3, 11-12, PDF 3, 11-12).
- Inclusion against the exclusion of pathology: one Irshad 2022 paper studies subjects with and without low back pain (ref 16), Mahato 2011 spines with L5-S1 transitions (ref 20), Been 2010 and Gomez-Olivencia 2017 are Neandertal comparisons (refs 6, 10), Wang 2015 a deer and sheep comparison with the human spine (ref 34) (titles, pp. 11-12, PDF 11-12). Inference: the normative label ("individuals without pathological changes", p. 6, PDF 6) is weaker than stated.
- The L3 lower end-plate area (935.91, CI 149.22 to 1722.59) is not compatible with the L3 lower width and depth (Table 4, p. 7).
- Female anterior-height SEs of 0.03 and 0.02 mm at L1 and L2 (Table 7, p. 10) are implausibly small, as are several others in Table 7 (men's anterior height L4 0.08; interpedicular distance men L1 0.12, L3 0.14, women L3 0.08).
- Fig. 2's caption expands EPWL as "End-plate depth lower", the words it also gives EPDL (p. 4, PDF 4).
- Text against table, canal width and transverse processes: "progressive" canal widening (pp. 1, 8, 10) is a flat L1 to L4 run and one step at L5 whose CI touches L4's; "significantly widening" transverse processes (p. 10) dip at L4 and separate no adjacent pair (Tables 2-6; Inference from the CI rule).
- The second-stage strings spell "lumber spine" while "lumbar vertebrae" is spelled correctly in each (p. 2, PDF 2). Inference: if run as printed, they would miss titles that say "lumbar spine" but not "lumbar vertebrae", so that stage may have found less than intended; the slip may also be in the paper only.
- The introduction cites an empty pair of brackets for congenital and acquired spinal stenosis (p. 2, PDF 2).
- Per-study extracted data are not published (data availability statement, p. 11, PDF 11), and Table 7 does not say which studies gave sex-specific values.
- Against other papers, Inference from memory, to verify in their own notes: (1) direct-measurement series such as Panjabi 1992 put lumbar pedicle medial convergence rising from about 10 deg at L1 to about 30 deg at L5; this paper's right-side PIS rises the same way but reaches only 21 deg, and its left side does not rise; (2) classical radiographic and skeletal series (Eisenstein in this set) put the lower normal bound of the lumbar midsagittal canal near 15 mm, while this paper's pooled mean depth is 14.7 to 16.0 mm, so the pooled depth is probably not the same measure or mixes cohorts; (3) classical anatomy gives L3 the longest transverse processes, while here the L5 span is widest and L3 second.
