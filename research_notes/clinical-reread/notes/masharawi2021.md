## What the paper is

- Design: observational, retrospective, cross-sectional CT morphometry of the lumbar neural arch, comparing single-level degenerative spondylolisthesis (DS) at L4-L5 with age- and sex-matched controls (p. 2, PDF 2).
- Population: living patients scanned at a hospital in Israel, aged 50 to 80; Table 6 labels the sample "Israeli" (p. 12, PDF 12); no ethnicity is given. 100 lumbar CTs (100 people, one scan each) "randomly chosen" from a hospital database of 500 CTs (the abstract calls it 500 lumbar CTs of spondylolisthesis, the methods 500 CTs of DS) (p. 1 and p. 2, PDF 1-2).
  - DS group: 50 people (25 men, 25 women) with low back pain, lower-limb symptoms and single-level DS grade 1-2 at L4-5; the diagnosis was made separately by two senior spine surgeons on supine CT and standing radiographs (p. 2, PDF 2).
  - Controls: 50 people (25 men, 25 women, 50-80 years) without low back pain or DS, "matched according to gender and age", examined in the hospital with no radiological findings and no back pain complaint (p. 2-3, PDF 2-3).
  - Table 1 (p. 2, PDF 2), mean (SD): control men age 64.7 (7.8), weight 78.8 (12.4) kg, height 174 (8.2) cm, BMI 26.1 (3.8); control women 61.2 (7), 67.6 (10.3), 162.9 (5.4), 25.5 (4.1); DS men 65.6 (9.9), 82.2 (17), 174 (7.5), 27 (4.4); DS women 68.6 (9.3), 69.2 (10), 159.3 (3.8), 27.2 (3.7). DS women were significantly older and shorter than control women (asterisks, p < 0.05), so the women were not matched for age. Men were taller and heavier than women in both groups (p. 4, PDF 4).
- Exclusions: slips other than at L4-L5, previous surgery, vertebral fractures, spondyloarthropathies, scoliosis, osteoporosis, transitional vertebra, isthmic spondylolisthesis (p. 2, PDF 2); the abstract lists only the first five (p. 1, PDF 1). How a transitional vertebra or osteoporosis was judged, and on which images, is not stated.
- Registration: called retrospective (p. 2, PDF 2) yet "prospectively registered" with the NIH (#HT5106) (p. 2, PDF 2).
- Modality and instrument: CT (scanner, slice thickness, kernel and reformat not reported), measured "from 2D projections" (2D slices: axial and parasagittal, Fig. 1) in K-Pacs Workstation 1.0.1 by one examiner (SA, first author, 6 years' experience) (p. 3, PDF 3). Facet arthrosis graded 6 months later by two examiners (first and second authors) blinded to diagnosis, by agreement, on Pathria's 0-3 scale (p. 3, PDF 3).
- Levels: the methods say "all vertebrae from L1-S1" (p. 3, PDF 3); the tables give L1 to L5 for every morphometric measure, foramen heights for L1-L2 to L5-S1 (Fig. 5 axis, p. 8, PDF 8), and arthrosis for the joints T12-L1 to L5-S1 (Table 3, p. 8, PDF 8). No S1 morphometry is reported.
- Level identification and counting: not described. Transitional vertebrae were excluded without a count.
- Count: 9,500 measurements = 19 measurements per level x 5 levels x 100 CTs (p. 4, PDF 4). Inference: the 19 are the 17 tabulated measures (7 facet, 3 canal and foramen, 7 pedicle) plus left and right arthrosis grade.
- Statistics: Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality (all p > 0.05, 0.11 to 0.999); multivariate regression of age, weight, height and BMI on the measures; "analysis of variances" for group differences; ICC(3,1) for reliability; Pearson r against vertebral body and disc data from the same people in an earlier paper (ref 13, Abu-Leil 2016) (p. 3-4, PDF 3-4). Sample size: a 1 mm difference in means and 1 mm in SD, power 85%, alpha 0.05, gave 45 per group (p. 4, PDF 4). No correction for multiple comparisons is mentioned.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- Transitional vertebrae: an exclusion criterion only (p. 2, PDF 2); no prevalence, no definition, no projection. The paper says nothing else about numbering anomalies (no L6, T13, rib counts, sacralisation or lumbarisation).
- Degenerative spondylolisthesis (not a numbering anomaly, but a level-specific state the decoder meets): the background, citing other studies, says DS is most often at L4-L5, then L3-L4 and L5-S1 (ref 4); about 66% of cases have a double-level slip (ref 5); prevalence 8.4% in women against 2.7% in men (ref 6, Copenhagen osteoarthritis study), rising sharply with age and rare below 50 (refs 4, 7); in women it is associated with higher BMI and lower estrogen (refs 6, 8) (p. 1-2, PDF 1-2). These are cited figures, not this paper's own counts.
- Within this paper, DS was by selection single-level at L4-L5, grade 1-2, recognised on supine CT and standing radiographs (p. 2, PDF 2).
- What DS does to the measures (this paper's own data), in short: intervertebral foramen height is lower at every lumbar level by 1.85-3.94 mm, most at L4-L5 (about 3.9 mm); lower-lumbar pedicles are taller, longer and (in men, right L4) wider by 1-2.14 mm; in women the lower facets (right L4, L5 both sides) are wider and the facets at L2 and L4 more sagittal, and grade-3 facet arthrosis is commoner; the superior inter-facet width is larger at L3 and L4 in both sexes (Table 2 only) (abstract p. 1, PDF 1; results p. 4-7, PDF 4-7; Tables 2-5).
- Other neural-arch findings the background cites, not this paper's data: wider pedicles in several lumbar degenerative diseases (refs 3, 18-21), and an MRI study (Goyal, ref 22) in which the bony anatomy of DS differs from that of isthmic spondylolisthesis (p. 2, PDF 2), one reason isthmic slips were excluded (Inference).

## Measures, each as a recipe

General: every measure was taken on 2D CT slices in a supine scan (p. 3, PDF 3; p. 10, PDF 10). Fig. 1 (p. 3, PDF 3) is the only definition of landmarks; panels I-IV are axial slices, panel V a parasagittal slice. Values below are control means (SD), men then women, L1 to L5, unless marked DS. Table 2 and Table 5 head every column "Mean in mm(SD)" even for angles, which are degrees.

Table 5's group labels are inconsistent. In the left and right pedicle length rows and the male left pedicle width rows the printed labels read "Control, Control", "DS, Control" or "DS., Control". Inference: the first row of each sex is the control and the second the DS group, as in every other row, because (1) the text's DS-minus-control differences reproduce only that way (L4 left length men 4.44 - 3.37 = 1.07, women 4.75 - 3.28 = 1.47; L4 right length men 4.55 - 3.18 = 1.37; L5 left length women 3.86 - 2.60 = 1.26; p. 7, PDF 7), (2) Fig. 6's plotted lines follow that reading (p. 10, PDF 10), and (3) Table 6's control pedicle width (7.3, 7.9, 9.3, 10.4, 14.1) is the mean of the four control rows only when the male left-width row printed "DS" is the control. The values below use that reading.

### VCL: superior vertebral canal length (in fact the anteroposterior canal depth)

- Landmarks: Fig. 1 II, A to B: in the midline, from the posterior wall of the vertebral body (A) to the inner surface of the arch at the base of the spinous process (B) (p. 3, PDF 3; the caption says only "the distance between A and B", the landmarks are read from the panel). The example reads 1.57 cm. Inference: "superior" means it was measured in the upper part of the vertebra; the paper never says. The slice shown passes through the pedicles and transverse processes.
- Plane: axial CT slice through the pedicles; instrument the K-Pacs Workstation 1.0.1 software (p. 3, PDF 3); its measuring tool and resolution are not described.
- Levels: L1-L5 (Table 4, p. 9, PDF 9).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the vertebra's own transverse plane at the pedicles, take the closed vertebral foramen (the hole bounded by the body's posterior wall, pedicles and laminae) and its extent along the anteroposterior axis in the midsagittal plane, from the posterior body cortex to the inner laminar cortex at the spinous base. This is the bony canal of one vertebra, not the dural sac and not SPINEPS's spinal-canal label.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Lateral radiograph, from the midpoint of the posterior body line to the spinolaminar line at pedicle level, divided by the magnification. Not from an AP.
- Across levels: falls from L1 to L3 by about 2 mm, flat at L4, rises toward L5 (p. 5, PDF 5): men 16.42 (2.91), 14.81 (2.44), 13.49 (1.61), 13.62 (1.75), 14.39 (2.22); women 16.33 (1.80), 15.14 (1.64), 14.50 (1.50), 14.21 (2.12), 14.84 (3.09). Smallest at L3-L4, largest at L1; adjacent-level steps (0.1-1.6 mm) are below one SD.
- Sex, age, size, population: women deeper than men at L3 (14.50 vs 13.49, bold = sex difference p < 0.05). Age, height, weight and BMI had no effect (0.53 < p < 0.994) (p. 4, PDF 4). DS: deeper at L3 in both sexes (men 15.17 vs 13.49, women 15.84 vs 14.50) and at L4 in women (15.46 vs 14.21) (p. 5, PDF 5; Table 4).
- Error: intra-tester ICC(3,1) 0.85 to 1 for all measures, on 10 CTs one week apart (p. 3-4, PDF 3-4); no per-measure ICC, no SEM in mm. Between-subject SD 1.5-3.1 mm in controls, up to 6.8 mm in DS men at L5.

### VCW: superior vertebral canal width (interpedicular width)

- Landmarks: Fig. 1 II, C to D: the transverse width of the canal on the same axial slice, a horizontal line between the medial walls of the two pedicles, crossing A to B (p. 3, PDF 3; landmarks read from the panel, whose label is partly hidden).
- Plane: axial at the pedicles. Levels L1-L5 (Table 4, p. 9, PDF 9).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. On the same section as VCL, the largest extent of the vertebral foramen along the left-right axis, between the medial pedicle cortices.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. AP radiograph, interpedicular distance between the medial pedicle cortices, divided by magnification; the radiograph gives the narrowest distance over the pedicles' height, the CT line one slice's width, so they are not interchangeable without a correction.
- Across levels: rises L1 to L2, flat at L3, rises toward L5 (about 3 mm) (p. 5, PDF 5): men 22.68 (2.67), 22.59 (2.35), 22.47 (2.73), 22.59 (2.66), 25.23 (3.69); women 20.89 (1.61), 21.65 (2.28), 21.97 (2.49), 22.29 (3.52), 23.80 (3.45). Flat L1-L4 in men; the L4 to L5 step (+2.6 mm men, +1.5 mm women) is the only one of any size and still below one SD.
- Sex, age, size, population: men wider at L1 (22.68 vs 20.89, bold). DS women wider at L1 (22.15 vs 20.89) and L5 (26.31 vs 23.80) (p. 5, PDF 5; Table 4).
- Error: as VCL; SD 1.6-3.7 mm.

### VFH: (inter)vertebral foramen height

- Landmarks: Fig. 1 V, A to B: on a parasagittal slice through the foramina, the vertical distance from the inferior margin of the upper vertebra's pedicle to the superior margin of the lower vertebra's pedicle (the example reads 1.38 cm) (p. 3, PDF 3). The caption says only "the distance between A and B"; the pedicle-to-pedicle reading comes from the panel and from the discussion, which calls the pedicles the upper and lower bony borders of the foramen (p. 10, PDF 10). The text calls it intervertebral foramen height, Table 4 "Vertebral foramen height"; it is the neural foramen, not the canal. Whether one side or both were measured is not said; one value per level is given.
- Plane: parasagittal CT slice through the pedicles. Levels: Table 4's columns L1-L5 are plotted in Fig. 5 at the foramina L1-L2 to L5-S1, with the same values (for example men's control 16.66 at L1-L2) (p. 8-9, PDF 8-9), so "L4" is the L4-L5 foramen, the slip level, and the L5-S1 foramen needed the S1 pedicle, the only S1 landmark in the paper.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. A pairwise measure of two adjacent instances: in the parasagittal plane through each pedicle's centre, the gap along the craniocaudal axis between the lowest voxel of the upper instance's pedicle and the highest voxel of the lower instance's pedicle. Needs the pedicle located (SPINEPS gives no pedicle label; take the posterior-element voxels between body and articular processes).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Lateral radiograph: the foramina are seen in profile and the height from pedicle to pedicle can be read, both sides superimposed, divided by magnification.
- Across levels: rises L1 to L3, flat L3-L4, falls toward L5-S1 by about 3 mm (p. 5, PDF 5): men 16.66 (2.05), 17.10 (2.72), 17.07 (2.73), 15.78 (3.20), 13.64 (3.00); women 15.14 (1.80), 16.71 (2.23), 16.49 (2.38), 15.30 (2.06), 13.50 (2.23). Peak at L2-L3/L3-L4, lowest at L5-S1; the L4-L5 to L5-S1 drop (about 2 mm) is the largest step.
- Sex, age, size, population: men taller at the L1 and L3 columns (bold), that is the L1-L2 and L3-L4 foramina. DS lower at every level (abstract range 1.85-3.94 mm): DS men 14.15, 14.83, 15.08, 11.84, 10.99; DS women 13.29, 13.61, 13.24, 11.46, 10.97 (Table 4, p. 9, PDF 9).
- Error: as VCL; SD 1.8-3.4 mm.

### PH: pedicle height

- Landmarks: Fig. 1 V, C to D: on the parasagittal slice, the vertical (craniocaudal) extent of the pedicle between the foramen above and the foramen below (example 9.88 mm) (p. 3, PDF 3; landmarks read from the panel). Whether the narrowest section or a fixed point was used is not stated. Left and right measured separately.
- Plane: parasagittal CT. Levels L1-L5 (Table 5, p. 11, PDF 11).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Along the pedicle axis, the section of least area between body and articular processes; its extent along the vertebra's craniocaudal axis.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Lateral radiograph, the pedicle's vertical dimension (both sides superimposed); AP radiograph, the height of the pedicle oval, distorted by the pedicle's convergence. Divide by magnification.
- Across levels: falls toward L5. By the text, left falls L1 to L2 by 1 mm, flat at L3, falls again at L4-L5, about 3 mm in all; right falls about 2 mm (p. 5, PDF 5). The table gives more: left L1 to L5 falls 3.1-3.2 mm and right 3.3-3.7 mm, and the left L1 to L2 step is only 0.4-0.6 mm (computed from Table 5). The discussion says the other series also show pedicle height falling from L1 to L5 (p. 7, PDF 7), and every series in Table 6 does (p. 12, PDF 12). Left: men 12.68 (3.40), 12.07 (3.29), 11.85 (2.50), 10.91 (3.15), 9.50 (3.11); women 11.98 (2.93), 11.57 (2.72), 12.20 (3.47), 10.51 (2.70), 8.88 (2.41). Right: men 13.56 (2.83), 12.13 (2.62), 12.16 (2.41), 11.23 (3.20), 9.88 (3.22); women 12.72 (2.27), 11.86 (2.73), 11.93 (1.97), 11.02 (2.48), 9.41 (2.59).
- Sex, age, size, population: men taller at left L1 and right L3 (bold). DS taller at L4 (left: men +1.87, women +1.75) and L5 (left women +1.89; right women 11.16 vs 9.41) (p. 7, PDF 7; Table 5). Table 6 (p. 12, PDF 12): this study's control PH (12.4, 12.06, 12.03, 10.9, 9.4) is about 1.6-5.8 mm below every other series listed at the same level (Abbas, Israeli, n = 180: 14.9 to 11.7; Marasini, Nepalese, 246: 15 to 12.6; Kadioglu, Eastern Anatolian, 29: 14.7 to 13.4; Mitra, Indian, 20: 16.4 to 15.2; Olsewski, American, 42: 18.2 to 13.8).
- Error: control SD 2.3-3.5 mm, large for a 9-14 mm dimension. Inference: Table 6's L1 and L2 PH (12.4, 12.06) do not equal the mean of the four control rows (12.7, 11.9), while L3-L5 do.

### PL: pedicle length (a short anteroposterior pedicle neck, not a screw path)

- Landmarks: Fig. 1 IV, A to B: on an axial slice, a short anteroposterior segment within the pedicle, from where it leaves the posterolateral corner of the body (A) to where the transverse process and lamina begin (B) (p. 3, PDF 3). The caption says only "the distance between A and B"; the landmarks are read from the panel (Inference). Values are 2.6 to 7.6 mm, far shorter than any screw-path or chord length.
- Plane: axial CT. Levels L1-L5, left and right (Table 5, p. 11, PDF 11).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. On the axial section at mid-pedicle height, the anteroposterior extent of the pedicle neck between the posterior body cortex and the anterior margin of the transverse process root; very sensitive to where those two boundaries are drawn.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Not reliably; a lateral radiograph superimposes the transverse processes and both pedicles.
- Across levels: falls about 4 mm from L1 to L5 (p. 5, PDF 5). Left: men 6.74 (1.22), 5.59 (1.23), 3.88 (1.25), 3.37 (0.8), 2.72 (0.98); women 6.87 (1.38), 6.24 (1.48), 4.85 (1.14), 3.28 (1.13), 2.60 (1.00). Right: men 6.99 (1.41), 5.51 (1.39), 3.81 (1.30), 3.18 (0.79), 2.63 (0.91); women 7.08 (1.33), 6.37 (1.41), 4.91 (1.39), 3.90 (2.68), 2.88 (1.20). In men the L2 to L3 step (1.7 mm both sides) is the largest; in women the L2 to L3 step (1.4-1.5 mm) and, on the left, the L3 to L4 step (1.57 mm) are about equal (computed from Table 5).
- Sex, age, size, population: women longer at L3 (bold). DS longer at L3-L5: men left L4 +1.07, right L3 +1 and L4 +1.37; women left L4 +1.47 and L5 +1.26 (p. 7, PDF 7).
- Error: SD 0.8-1.5 mm in controls (2.68 once), up to 1.8 in DS.

### PW: pedicle width

- Landmarks: Fig. 1 IV, C to D: on an axial slice, a transverse line across the pedicle from its medial (canal) cortex to its lateral cortex (p. 3, PDF 3; landmarks read from the panel). Whether at the isthmus is not stated.
- Plane: axial CT. Levels L1-L5, left and right (Table 5, p. 11, PDF 11).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. The narrowest section along the pedicle axis, its extent along the vertebra's left-right axis within that section, outer cortex to outer cortex.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. AP radiograph, the width of the pedicle oval, divided by magnification, foreshortened by the pedicle's convergence (worst at L5).
- Across levels: increases along the lumbar spine, sharply from L3 to L5 (about 6 mm by the text; 4.1-5.9 mm by Table 5) (p. 5, PDF 5). Left: men 7.63 (2.91), 8.83 (2.48), 10.50 (2.66), 10.78 (3.96), 15.51 (4.56); women 7.36 (1.34), 7.21 (1.29), 8.65 (2.04), 10.25 (2.59), 12.74 (3.84). Right: men 7.35 (2.20), 8.43 (2.20), 9.46 (2.97), 10.65 (3.48), 15.38 (4.46); women 6.95 (1.30), 7.23 (1.58), 8.66 (1.97), 10.02 (3.09), 12.90 (3.87). The L4 to L5 step is the largest (men +4.7 mm, women +2.5-2.9 mm).
- Sex, age, size, population: men wider at L1, L2, L3 and L5 on both sides (bold), by up to 2.8 mm at L5. DS women narrower at L1 (6.06 vs 7.36, about 1.3 mm) and L2 (6.42 vs 7.21, 0.8 mm); DS men wider on the right at L4 (12.79 vs 10.65, +2.14) (p. 7, PDF 7). Table 6 (p. 12, PDF 12) places this study's PW (7.3, 7.9, 9.3, 10.4, 14.1) within the range of Indian, Nepalese, Israeli, Eastern Anatolian, Chinese and American series (L5 from 11.3, Marasini, to 16.6, Olsewski; L1 from 5.3, Cheung, to 9, Singh).
- Error: SD 1.3-4.6 mm.

### Transverse pedicle (undefined)

- Table 5 has a row "Transverse Pedicle" (p. 11, PDF 11), never defined in the methods or Fig. 1 and headed in mm. Inference: perhaps a transverse pedicle angle in degrees, though it stays near 10-14 to L5, while Panjabi's pedicle inclination to the sagittal plane (PDIs) rises from about 14.5 deg at L1 to 24.5 deg at L5 (from the panjabi1992 note). Control values: men 10.25 (3.91), 10.38 (4.79), 10.67 (4.41), 10.58 (3.93), 13.83 (5.28); women 11.46 (3.55), 11.12 (4.11), 12.35 (3.78), 13.80 (4.58), 12.81 (6.94). L4 is bold (sex difference: men 10.58, women 13.80). Women's L4 control 13.80 vs DS 12.70 is marked p < 0.01, which the SDs (4.58, 4.87; n = 25) cannot give (Inference: t about 0.8). Not usable.

### FW: facet width (left and right)

- Landmarks: Fig. 1 III, A to C: on an axial slice through the facet joint, the length of the joint line from its anteromedial end (A) to its posterolateral end (C) (example 2.08 cm; the other side, B to D, 2.04 cm) (p. 3, PDF 3; landmarks read from the panel). The methods speak of "superior and inferior facet widths" (p. 3, PDF 3), but the tables give only left and right.
- Plane: axial CT through the joint. Levels L1-L5 (Table 2, p. 6, PDF 6). Which joint a vertebra's value belongs to is not stated; the angles are said to be of the "superior" facets (p. 3, PDF 3), which would make the value at Ln the Ln-1/Ln joint (Inference).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Find the interface between the upper instance's inferior articular process and the lower instance's superior articular process on one side; in an axial section through the joint, the length of that interface curve's chord from anteromedial to posterolateral end.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (an axial dimension). Inference: oblique films show the joint but not this length.
- Across levels: increases distally, by "4 mm" L1 to L5 in the text (p. 4, PDF 4) but by only 2.2-2.7 mm in the control means of Table 2 (computed). Left: men 16.72 (2.21), 16.93 (1.62), 18.45 (1.89), 18.16 (2.32), 19.45 (2.69); women 15.03 (1.86), 15.50 (2.40), 16.02 (1.55), 16.73 (1.81), 17.71 (3.23). Right: men 16.88 (2.62), 16.58 (2.24), 18.16 (1.96), 17.85 (2.08), 19.09 (2.30); women 14.98 (2.26), 15.41 (2.27), 16.17 (2.11), 16.03 (1.59), 17.52 (3.14). Adjacent steps are 0.1-1.6 mm, below one SD.
- Sex, age, size, population: men wider at L1-L3 left and L1, L3 right (bold), by about 1.4-2.4 mm. DS women wider at L4 right (+1.73) and L5 (left +2.2, right +2.85); DS men narrower at L3 left (16.62 vs 18.45; text says 1.9 mm, table gives 1.83) (p. 4-5, PDF 4-5; Table 2).
- Error: SD 1.2-4.9 mm.

### SIFW: "superior" inter-facet width

- Landmarks: Fig. 1 III, A to B: on the axial slice, the distance between the anteromedial ends of the left and right facet joint lines (example 1.97 cm) (p. 3, PDF 3; landmarks read from the panel). Inference: "superior" and "inferior" here are the top and bottom of the axial image (anterior and posterior), not cranial and caudal, and not Panjabi's superior and inferior articular processes; the quadrilateral A-B-D-C spans the two joints.
- Levels L1-L5 (Table 2, p. 6, PDF 6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. The distance between the anteromedial endpoints of the two facet interface curves on the same axial section; it approximates the width of the canal between the joints at the lateral recesses.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (Inference; an AP film superimposes the joints on the canal).
- Across levels: by the text rises L1 to L2 (about 1 mm; Table 2 gives 0.3-0.5 mm), flat L2-L3 (men's L3 is 1 mm below L2), rises to L5, about 7 mm in all (p. 4, PDF 4): men 16.12 (1.78), 16.58 (2.12), 15.56 (2.31), 17.13 (2.45), 22.74 (4.76); women 13.83 (2.40), 14.09 (2.98), 14.76 (1.61), 16.01 (2.85), 20.80 (5.54). The L4 to L5 step (+5.6 men, +4.8 women) is about 1.1-1.5 pooled SD (Inference).
- Sex, age, size, population: men wider at L1, L2 and L5 (bold), by about 2-2.5 mm. DS wider at L3 (men 17.26 vs 15.56, women 16.14 vs 14.76, p < 0.05) and L4 (men 20.47 vs 17.13, women 20.22 vs 16.01, p < 0.01) (Table 2); the text and abstract do not mention this.
- Error: SD 1.6-6 mm.

### IIFW: "inferior" inter-facet width

- Landmarks: Fig. 1 III, C to D: the distance between the posterolateral ends of the two facet joint lines on the axial slice (example 4.42 cm) (p. 3, PDF 3).
- Levels L1-L5 (Table 2, p. 6, PDF 6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. The distance between the posterolateral endpoints of the two facet interface curves on the same axial section.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Not as defined; an AP radiograph shows the lateral margins of the articular pillars, a related but different width.
- Across levels: flat L1 to L2, then rises sharply to L5, about 16 mm in all (p. 4, PDF 4): men 31.92 (3.79), 31.08 (4.68), 33.51 (4.74), 37.19 (6.21), 48.56 (7.71); women 29.75 (3.65), 29.10 (3.28), 31.32 (3.50), 36.01 (4.93), 46.05 (8.73). ### TFA: transverse facet angle (left and right)

- Landmarks: Fig. 1 I: three lines on an axial slice from a common apex near the posterior third of the body in the midline: a midline line to the spinous tip, and one line along each facet joint; the caption defines TFA as the angle between "line A and line C", but no line C is labelled in panel I; the panel annotates 20.5 deg and 14.9 deg between each facet line and the midline (p. 3, PDF 3). Inference: TFA is the angle between one facet joint line and the midsagittal line, so a smaller angle is a more sagittal facet, a larger one more coronal. The results support the direction: the DS women's smaller values at L4 are called "more sagittally-oriented" (p. 5, PDF 5). The methods call these "superior transverse facet angles" (p. 3, PDF 3).
- Plane: axial CT through the joint. Levels L1-L5 (Table 2, p. 6, PDF 6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In an axial section of the joint, fit a line to the facet interface between the two instances on one side (principal axis of its voxels); measure its angle to the vertebra's midsagittal plane (from the body's left-right symmetry or the spinous process).
- From a radiograph alone: not possible on AP or lateral (Inference; an axial angle).
- Across levels: falls L1 to L2 (about 2 deg), then rises L2 to L5 by 12-17 deg (p. 4, PDF 4). Left: men 33.10 (7.95), 29.13 (7.94), 31.58 (10.45), 37.70 (9.39), 46.58 (9.10); women 34.05 (6.91), 33.56 (8.94), 34.64 (9.32), 41.25 (9.10), 45.68 (11.15). Right: men 31.38 (8.36), 29.71 (7.24), 33.08 (8.30), 36.22 (11.48), 44.17 (13.17); women 33.24 (7.97), 30.52 (8.56), 35.50 (6.89), 39.84 (9.22), 43.25 (12.50). Most sagittal at L2, most coronal at L5.
- Sex, age, size, population: no sex difference marked. DS women more sagittal at left L4 (33.25 vs 41.25, 8 deg, p < 0.01) and, by Table 2 though not the text, right L1 (28.57 vs 33.24) and right L4 (34.00 vs 39.84, p < 0.05). "No significant differences in facet orientation" in men (p. 5, PDF 5).
- Error: SD 6-16 deg; no angular reliability reported separately.

### TIFA: transverse inter-facet angle

- Landmarks: Fig. 1 I: the angle between the two facet lines A and B on the axial slice (p. 3, PDF 3).
- Levels L1-L5 (Table 2, p. 6, PDF 6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. The angle between the two fitted facet interface lines in the same axial section.
- Across levels: lowest at L2, highest at L4-L5: men 62.70 (15.80), 57.63 (13.45), 61.42 (15.48), 70.48 (15.47), 71.00 (11.23); women 68.90 (13.22), 63.44 (16.10), 68.64 (13.35), 75.58 (10.86), 74.72 (10.94) (Table 2, p. 6, PDF 6; Fig. 3, p. 5, PDF 5).
- Sex, age, size, population: DS women smaller (more sagittal) at L2 (54.64 vs 63.44, p < 0.01) and L4 (65.57 vs 75.58, p < 0.05); the "10 deg" of the text holds at L4 (p. 5, PDF 5).
- Error: SD 9-19 deg. ### Facet arthrosis grade (Pathria)

- Definition: Pathria's 0-3 scale, 0 normal, 1 narrowing, 2 narrowing plus sclerosis (or hypertrophy, Table 3 note), 3 severe osteoarthritis with narrowing, sclerosis and osteophytes; designed for radiographs, used on CT and MRI with good reliability in other studies (refs 24-26) (p. 3, PDF 3; Table 3, p. 8, PDF 8).
- Graded by two blinded examiners by agreement, per side, at T12-L1 to L5-S1 (Table 3, p. 8, PDF 8; Fig. 4, p. 7, PDF 7).
- Grade 3 prevalence, controls: men L1-L2 33.3/33.3 (right/left), L2-L3 16.7/29.2, L3-L4 33.3/20.8, L4-L5 58.3/50, L5-S1 66.7/62.5; women 50/42.3, 50/30.8, 42.3/23.1, 50/38.5, 57.7/57.7. DS: men 55/55, 50/60, 60/60, 45/65, 85/80; women 33.3/50, 46.7/40, 66.7/46.7, 90/86.7, 83.3/90 (Table 3). Grade 3 is not monotonic by level, but it is highest at L5-S1 in every group (DS women equally at right L4-L5, 90%); L5-S1 is the most degenerate joint even in controls (computed from Table 3).
- Inference from the percentages: every percentage is a multiple of 1/24 for control men, 1/26 for control women, 1/20 for DS men and 1/30 for DS women, so the graded denominators were 24, 26, 20 and 30, not 25 each; each group still sums to 50, so the sex split behind Table 3 differs from Table 1's 25 and 25 rather than scans being missing. At T12-L1 the rows sum to only 42-65%, so T12-L1 was in the field of about half the scans.
- From a radiograph alone: Pathria's scale was built for radiographs (p. 3, PDF 3); the cited paper's title names oblique radiographs (ref 23, p. 13, PDF 13).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- T12-L1: only arthrosis was graded; it was visible in about half the scans (Inference from Table 3 sums); grade 3 in 20.8-33.3% of controls and 43.3-55% of DS (Table 3, p. 8, PDF 8).
- L1: the deepest canal (VCL about 16.4 mm), the longest pedicle neck (about 7 mm), the tallest pedicles in most rows (12.0-13.6 mm; women's left is taller at L3, 12.20), the narrowest pedicles (6.95-7.63 mm, though women's left is as narrow at L2, 7.21); canal width differs most between the sexes here (men 22.68, women 20.89) (Tables 4, 5).
- L2: the most sagittal facets (TFA lowest, about 29-34 deg; TIFA lowest, 57.6-63.4 deg) (Table 2; p. 4, PDF 4). DS women's TIFA is 8.8 deg smaller here.
- L3: the shallowest canal together with L4 (VCL 13.5-14.5 mm); the foramen L3-L4 still near its peak; the pedicle neck shortens most between L2 and L3 (Tables 4, 5). DS: canal deeper in both sexes, SIFW wider.
- L4 and the L4-L5 segment (the slip level by selection): VFH at L4-L5 shows the largest DS reduction (about 3.9 mm; 15.78 to 11.84 men, 15.30 to 11.46 women) though Table 4 marks it not significant; DS pedicles taller and longer, DS women's facets more sagittal and wider, grade-3 arthrosis 86.7-90% in DS women against 38.5-50% in control women (Tables 2-5).
- L5 and L5-S1: the most coronal facets (TFA about 43-47 deg), the widest facets (17.5-19.5 mm), the largest inter-facet widths (IIFW about 46-49 mm, SIFW about 20.8-22.7 mm), the widest canal (VCW 23.8-25.2 mm), the widest pedicles (12.7-15.5 mm), the shortest pedicles in height (8.9-9.9 mm) and neck (2.6-2.9 mm), the lowest foramen (L5-S1, about 13.5-13.6 mm), and the commonest grade-3 arthrosis (57.7-66.7% of controls, 80-90% of DS) (Tables 2-5). Inference: L5 is set apart from L4 by several measures at once, while L1, L2 and L3 differ from each other by less than one SD in almost every measure.
- L5-S1: the foramen's lower border is the S1 pedicle, the only S1 landmark the paper uses; no other S1 measure is reported although the methods promise L1-S1 (p. 3, PDF 3; Table 4, Fig. 5).
- C2/C3 and C7/T1: not covered; T12/L1 only as a facet arthrosis grade.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Intra-tester: first author repeated all measurements on 10 CTs after one week; ICC(3,1) between 0.85 and 1 "for all measurements" (p. 3-4, PDF 3-4).
- Inter-tester: ICC(3,1) between 0.85 and 0.92 (p. 4, PDF 4), though all morphometry was taken by one examiner (p. 3, PDF 3); which measures the second tester repeated, and on how many scans, is not said.
- Thresholds: ICC > 0.75 good, < 0.75 poor (p. 4, PDF 4).
- No per-measure ICC, no SEM, no limits of agreement in mm or deg; no CT resolution or slice thickness. Arthrosis grading was by agreement, so no kappa is given.
- Between-subject SDs in controls (an upper bound on measurement error plus biology): canal 1.5-3.7 mm; foramen 1.8-3.2 mm; pedicle height 2.3-3.5 mm; pedicle width 1.3-4.6 mm; pedicle neck 0.8-1.5 mm (one 2.68); facet width 1.6-3.2 mm; inter-facet widths 1.6-8.7 mm; facet angles 6.9-13.2 deg; TIFA 10.9-16.1 deg (Tables 2, 4, 5).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- DS definition: an anterior slip of one vertebra over the one below in a degenerated segment (p. 1, PDF 1). Radiculopathy with intermittent neurogenic claudication accompanies DS in the aged but also spinal stenosis, degenerative scoliosis and segmental instability (p. 2, PDF 2).
- Facet orientation and DS: earlier studies link sagittal facets at L4-L5 with DS; one (ref 38) gives a 25-fold risk with more sagittal L4-L5 facets; Hosoe and Ohmori found no clear link; Love et al. (ref 41) held that the sagittal orientation at L4-L5 in DS comes from arthritic remodelling rather than directly from the slip (p. 9, PDF 9). Here only women with DS had more sagittal facets (L2 and L4), and wider lower facets, which the authors read as adaptation to load and as one reason DS is commoner in women; men's facet orientation did not differ (p. 9, PDF 9).
- In DS men degenerate facets run from L1 to S1; in DS women they concentrate at L4-S1 (p. 1 and p. 5, PDF 1 and 5; p. 9, PDF 9).
- Pedicles bound the foramen above and below, so taller pedicles shorten the foramen and narrow it, the authors' explanation for foraminal stenosis in DS (p. 10, PDF 10).
- Only in DS women did greater L4 body wedging (lordotic) and posterior disc height at L4-5 go with greater pedicle dimensions at L4 and L5 (0.41 < r < 0.5) (p. 7, PDF 7); the same subjects (ref 13) had osteophytes along L1-S1, thinner discs, kyphotic upper (L1-L3) discs, less lordotic L4-L5 discs in women and more lordotic L5 bodies in men (p. 9, PDF 9). The authors combine these into a mechanism: kyphotic degenerate upper discs with degenerate, sagittal facets in the lordotic L4-L5 segment would let L4 slip forward, added to the cyclical model of ref 13 (p. 9, PDF 9). Ref 13 itself had found generalised disc degeneration at every lumbar level in DS, with lower disc heights and a kyphotic upper lumbar posture (p. 2, PDF 2).
- Pitfall: all CTs were supine, so slip, foramen height and the "adaptive" interpretation were measured unloaded, and no spinopelvic or posture parameter was available (p. 10, PDF 10).
- Limitation the authors admit: symptoms and disability were outside the paper's scope, so the shorter foramina are only presumed to contribute to radiculopathy and neurogenic claudication (p. 10 and p. 12, PDF 10 and 12).
- The authors claim their values match other published series from several populations, healthy and diseased, which share the pattern of pedicle width rising and pedicle height falling from L1 to L5 (p. 7, PDF 7; Table 6, p. 12, PDF 12).
- Pitfall: Pathria's scale was made for radiographs and is used here on CT (p. 3, PDF 3).
- Pitfall (names): "canal length" is the anteroposterior depth; "vertebral foramen height" is the intervertebral (neural) foramen; "pedicle length" is the short pedicle neck on an axial slice, not a screw length; "superior/inferior inter-facet width" are the anterior/posterior ends of the joint pair on one axial slice (Inference for the last).
- Pitfall: this study's pedicle height is about 1.6-5.8 mm below the other series in Table 6 (p. 12, PDF 12), which the authors do not discuss; its landmark choice probably differs (Inference). No screw-sizing advice is given.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Before measuring the neural arch, check that the case is not a transitional or slipped spine: this paper's normal values come from scans without transitional vertebrae, scoliosis, fractures or surgery (p. 2, PDF 2); apply them only after the lumbosacral count is settled.
2. Measure canal depth (VCL) on the axial slice through the pedicles, in the midline from the posterior body wall to the inner arch at the spinous base (Fig. 1 II, p. 3, PDF 3); expect about 16.4 mm at L1 falling to 13.5-14.5 mm at L3-L4 and rising slightly at L5 (Table 4, p. 9, PDF 9). Inference: use the minimum at L3-L4 as a pattern over several vertebrae, not as a single-level cue, since every adjacent step is below one SD.
3. Measure canal width (VCW) on the same slice between the medial pedicle walls (Fig. 1 II); expect a flat 21-23 mm from L1 to L4 and a step up to 23.8-25.2 mm at L5 (Table 4). In women expect about 1.8 mm less than men at L1.
4. Measure each intervertebral foramen's height on a parasagittal CT slice (Inference: a lateral radiograph should serve too, both sides superimposed) from the lower edge of the upper pedicle to the upper edge of the lower pedicle (Fig. 1 V, p. 3, PDF 3); expect a peak of about 16.5-17 mm at L2-L3 and L3-L4 and the lowest, about 13.5 mm, at L5-S1 (Table 4). Inference: a foramen 3-4 mm lower than expected at L4-L5, with lower foramina above it too, suggests degenerative spondylolisthesis (Table 4 shows the loss at every level, largest at L4-L5); do not mistake that loss for a level shift.
5. Measure pedicle width on the axial slice across the pedicle (Fig. 1 IV); expect it to grow from about 7-7.6 mm at L1 to 12.7-15.5 mm at L5, with the largest single jump from L4 to L5 (Table 5, p. 11, PDF 11); expect men about 2.5-2.8 mm wider than women at L5.
6. Expect pedicle height on the parasagittal slice to fall from about 12-13.6 mm at L1 to 8.9-9.9 mm at L5 (Table 5); every series in Table 6 falls the same way (p. 12, PDF 12), while Panjabi's cadaveric pedicle height rises at L5 (see Gaps), so the direction depends on the definition: fix the definition before using PH to name L5 (Inference).
7. Measure the facet joint line on the axial slice through each joint and its angle to the midsagittal line (Fig. 1 I and III, p. 3, PDF 3); expect the most sagittal joints at L2 (about 29-34 deg) and the most coronal at L5 (about 43-47 deg) (Table 2, p. 6, PDF 6). Treat the angle as weak evidence: its SD is 7-13 deg against a 12-17 deg rise from L2 to L5.
8. Measure the distance between the posterolateral ends of the two facet joints (IIFW) on the same axial slice (Fig. 1 III); expect about 29-34 mm at L1-L3, 36-37 mm at L4 and 46-49 mm at L5 (Table 2). Inference: the 10-11 mm jump from L4 to L5 (about 1.4-1.6 pooled SD) is the strongest L4/L5 step among this paper's measures.
9. In a woman over 50, more sagittal L4 facets (left TFA about 33 deg rather than 41) and wider lower facets go with DS at L4-L5 (Table 2; p. 5, PDF 5); in men this sample showed no facet-orientation difference with DS (p. 5, PDF 5).
10. Expect severe facet arthrosis (Pathria grade 3) to be common in anyone 50-80: 38.5-58.3% of controls at L4-L5, 57.7-66.7% at L5-S1, and 80-90% of DS patients at L5-S1 (Table 3, p. 8, PDF 8); Inference: osteophytes will distort facet and foramen landmarks most at the lowest two joints, so widen the tolerance there.
11. Do not use "pedicle length" from this paper as a screw-path or chord length; it is a 2.6-7.6 mm neck measured on an axial slice (Fig. 1 IV; Table 5).
12. Read Table 5 with the group labels corrected (first row control, second DS, in each sex) for pedicle length and male left pedicle width; the printed labels contradict the text, Fig. 6 and Table 6 (p. 7, 10, 11, 12; PDF 7, 10, 11, 12).
13. Read Table 4's foramen height columns L1 to L5 as the foramina L1-L2 to L5-S1, as Fig. 5 plots them (p. 8-9, PDF 8-9); assign a foramen value to the pair of instances around it, not to one vertebra.
14. In a slipped spine, do not use SIFW to find L5: DS raises SIFW at L4 to 20.2-20.5 mm, against 16.0-17.1 mm in controls and near the control L5 value of 20.8-22.7 mm (Table 2, p. 6, PDF 6). Inference: with a slip at L4-L5, an L4 can look like an L5 on this measure; weigh IIFW, which DS changed significantly at no level (Table 2), over SIFW.
15. Measure facet and inter-facet values on one axial slice through the joint, from the joint lines' two ends (Fig. 1 III, p. 3, PDF 3); the paper does not say which joint (above or below the vertebra) a vertebra's value belongs to, so record the joint by its two instances.

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none from this paper. Transitional vertebrae were excluded with no count (p. 2, PDF 2). The cited DS figures (8.4% of women, 2.7% of men, mostly at L4-L5, then L3-L4 and L5-S1; about 66% double-level) are priors for a slip state, not for a numbering transition, and come from other studies (p. 1, PDF 1).

(b) Measures and definition differences:
- Canal width (VCW) is the interpedicular width at pedicle level, close to Panjabi's spinal canal width (Inference); this paper's values (men 22.5-25.2, women 20.9-23.8) are about 1-3.3 mm below Panjabi's cadaveric 23.7-27.1 level for level (from the panjabi1992 note, Inference that the planes match).
- Canal depth (VCL) has the same landmarks as Panjabi's SCD (posterior body wall to the inner arch at the spinous base, by the panjabi1992 note) and the same shape (lowest at L3), but is about 2.6-5.3 mm smaller level for level (13.5-16.4 here against 17.5-19.7 in Panjabi). Inference: older degenerate living subjects (osteophytes, facet hypertrophy), the CT slice chosen, or the soft-tissue window; a mask-derived depth must state its slice and landmark, and a prior built on Panjabi will run deeper than this sample.
- Pedicle height: this paper's PH falls from L1 to L5 (12.7 to 9.4 in controls), as every series in Table 6 does (p. 12, PDF 12), and is about 1.6-5.8 mm below them; Panjabi's PDH rises at L5 (to 19.6, panjabi1992 note), the odd one out among the series known here (Inference). A generator built on Panjabi will draw much taller L5 pedicles than this definition measures. Inference: use the narrowest section along the pedicle axis on the mask and document it; do not pool with this paper's PH.
- Pedicle width matches the range of other series (Table 6) and Panjabi's rise toward L5, but is smaller at L5 (14.1 against Panjabi's 18.6, panjabi1992 note).
- Facet width (FW, joint-line length in the axial plane) and the transverse facet angle (to the midsagittal line) are candidates for the generator's facet dimensions and angles; check whether the generator's facet angle is to the sagittal or the coronal plane before comparing, since here a larger angle means a more coronal facet. Panjabi's acute angle of the lumbar superior facets to the sagittal plane (L1 43, L2 42, L3 48, L4 58, L5 61 deg, from the panjabi1993f note) has the same shape as TFA (lowest at L1-L2, rising to L5) but runs about 8-22 deg above it (TFA control means 29-47 deg). Inference: a best-fit plane of the whole facet on dry bone against a joint line on one axial slice of living older subjects; do not pool the two.
- SIFW and IIFW are constructed on one axial slice from the joint lines' ends; they are not Panjabi's superior and inferior inter-facet widths, which join the centroids of the two superior or two inferior facets (panjabi1993f note), and should be added, if at all, as new variables. Inference: a centroid width should fall between the two ends of the joint pair, and at L5 the mean of SIFW and IIFW (men 35.7, women 33.4 mm) lies near Panjabi's L5 IFWs (35.0 mm). IIFW gives the strongest L4/L5 step in this paper (Inference).
- VFH is a pairwise (inter-instance) measure; it fits the decoder's transition model rather than a per-instance emission, and is depressed at a degenerative slip.
- Pedicle "length" here is not a standard variable; do not map it onto any Panjabi measure.

(c) Measurement error: only ICC(3,1) 0.85-1 intra and 0.85-0.92 inter (p. 3-4, PDF 3-4); no mm or deg error. Use the control between-subject SDs listed under Measurement error as the combined variance; for a pure measurement error the paper gives nothing.

(d) Covariates: sex matters for widths (men wider facets at L1-L3, wider inter-facet widths at L1-L2 and L5, wider canal at L1, wider pedicles at L1-L3 and L5, taller foramina at L1-L2 and L3-L4, taller pedicles at left L1 and right L3; women deeper canal and longer pedicle neck at L3, and a larger undefined "Transverse Pedicle" value at L4) (bold cells, Tables 2, 4, 5). Age (50-80 only), height, weight and BMI showed no effect (0.53 < p < 0.994) (p. 4, PDF 4), with 25 per cell and a narrow age range. Population: Israeli, living, aged 50-80, supine CT; body size by Table 1 (p. 2, PDF 2): men about 174 cm and 79-82 kg, women 159-163 cm and 68-69 kg. A DS state shifts lower-lumbar pedicle and foramen values and, in women, facet width and orientation. Inference: these are older, degenerate spines (grade-3 facet arthrosis in 17-67% of control joints), so a generator for younger adults should not take them as its centre.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Table 5's group labels are wrong in five row pairs (pedicle lengths in both sexes and the male left pedicle width): the text's differences, Fig. 6 and Table 6 all fit only the reading "first row control, second row DS" (p. 7, 10, 11, 12; PDF 7, 10, 11, 12). Inference, but supported three ways.
- Fig. 4's lower-right panel repeats the lower-left one, both titled "Left Facet Arthrosis Prevalence of Males"; the male right side is shown nowhere but Table 3 (p. 7, PDF 7).
- The text's table and figure numbers are off: it cites Fig. 3 for arthrosis (Fig. 4), Table 3 for the canals and foramina (Table 4), Table 4 for the pedicles (Table 5), and Table 5 for the correlations, which no table holds (p. 4-7, PDF 4-7). The correlation is given as "p < 0.5" (p. 7, PDF 7); probably 0.05.
- Arthrosis denominators: the percentages imply 24, 26, 20 and 30 graded scans, not 25 per sex, each group still summing to 50 (Inference from Table 3).
- The abstract's grade-3 range for DS, 40-90%, misses DS women's 33.3% at right L1-L2 (Table 3); the claim that grade 3 reached at least 50% at every level in DS men misses 45% at right L4-L5.
- Table 4 marks no significance at L4 for VFH though the difference there (about 3.9 mm, SD 2-3) is the largest (Inference: t about 4.9); Table 5 marks women's L4 "Transverse Pedicle" p < 0.01 for a 1.1 difference with SD about 4.7 (Inference: not significant). Bold (sex difference) is applied to one sex's row only for canal length at L1 (Table 4).
- TIFA does not equal the sum of the two TFAs at L5 (14-20 deg short), while it nearly does at L1-L4 (Inference from Table 2).
- "Transverse Pedicle" is never defined; the methods' "superior and inferior facet widths" are reported as left and right; TFA's "line C" is not labelled in Fig. 1 I; the methods promise L1-S1 but no S1 values are given.
- Table 6's L1 and L2 pedicle heights (12.4, 12.06) do not follow from Table 5's control rows (12.7, 11.9), while L3-L5 do (Inference).
- The control group is described as drawn from a database of 500 CTs of DS (p. 2, PDF 2); the ethics statement names one hospital on p. 2 and a different one on p. 12 (PDF 2 and 12). DS women were older and shorter than control women although matching is claimed (Table 1).
- Inter-tester reliability is reported though one person measured everything.
- Not given: CT parameters, slice selection rules, how levels were counted, how transitional vertebrae were recognised, any per-measure error in mm or deg, S1 values, thoracic or cervical levels.
- Text against table: facet width rises "4 mm" from L1 to L5 by the text, 2.2-2.7 mm by Table 2; SIFW rises "1 mm" from L1 to L2, 0.3-0.5 mm by Table 2; right pedicle height falls "~2 mm", 3.3-3.7 mm by Table 5; the left L1 to L2 fall of "1mm" is 0.4-0.6 mm; the left facet angle at L4 differs by 8 deg in DS women, not the "10" deg the text gives (p. 4-5, PDF 4-5; computed).
- Design: called retrospective, yet said to be prospectively registered (p. 2, PDF 2).
- Conflicts with other papers in this set (Inference, from the panjabi1992 and panjabi1993f notes): Panjabi's pedicle height rises at L5 where this paper's, and every series in Table 6, falls; Panjabi's canal depths are about 2.6-5.3 mm larger with the same landmarks and the same L3 minimum; Panjabi's L5 pedicle width (18.6) exceeds this paper's 14.1; Panjabi's lumbar facet angles to the sagittal plane run 8-22 deg above TFA with the same shape. Table 6 itself shows this study's pedicle height as the lowest of the six series that give one.
