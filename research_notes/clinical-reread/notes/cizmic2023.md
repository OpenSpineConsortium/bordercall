# Cizmic 2023: Lumbar spinal canal midsagittal and interpedicular diameters on CT in Bosnia and Herzegovina

Source: thread "Reread every paper for clinical detail", project symphony_4, 2026-10-28.

Citation as printed on the first page: Midhat Cizmic, Emir Hadziosmanovic, Rusmir Baljic, Adi Ahmetspahic, Zulejha Merhemic. Morphometric Study of Lumbar Spinal Canal Dimensions in Bosnia and Herzegovina Population. Acta Inform Med. 2023; 31(3): 200-205. doi: 10.5455/aim.2023.31.200-205. Original paper.

Copy read: PMC open-access Cloud Service (pmc-oa-opendata), PMC10540747.1, fetched 2026-10-28 by this thread; sha256 begins 571257aba4fa267b. Six pages, all read as page images by the reader and again by the skeptic; every table (1-11), Charts 1-3 and Figure 1 re-read at 200 dpi. Printed page = PDF page + 199.

## What the paper is

- Design: a single-centre retrospective descriptive CT morphometry study of the bony lumbar canal, L1 to L5, by sex, with a comparison against published means of other populations, p. 200 (PDF 1), p. 201 (PDF 2). The methods also call it "retrospective, prospective, comparative and clinical" (p. 201, PDF 2), which contradicts the abstract.
- Population: 200 adult patients of Bosnia and Herzegovina, 100 men and 100 women, who had lumbosacral multislice CT for low back pain at the Radiology Department of the General Hospital "Prim. dr. Abdulah Nakas", Sarajevo, between 1 January 2013 and 31 December 2014; referred by various specialists, p. 201 (PDF 2). Living symptomatic patients, not volunteers and not cadavers. Ethnicity is not stated beyond "Bosnia and Herzegovina population".
- Age: men mean 43.84, SD 10.99, range 20-60 years; women mean 43.78, SD 6.00, range 27-50 years (Table 1, p. 201, PDF 2; text p. 201, PDF 2). No body height or weight recorded.
- Diagnoses made on the CT reading (Table 2, p. 201, PDF 2): every one of the 200 carries one of four disc diagnoses; the text ranks extrusion as the most severe degenerative form (p. 202, PDF 3). Men: bulged disc 33, disc herniation 46, protrusion 8, extrusion 13. Women: bulged disc 22, disc herniation 52, protrusion 16, extrusion 10. Totals: herniation 98/200 (49%), bulge 55/200 (27.5%), protrusion 24/200 (12%), extrusion 23/200 (11.5%) (p. 201-202, PDF 2-3). The authors say the disc diagnoses did not hinder reading the bony contours because the bone window was used (p. 202, PDF 3).
- Exclusions: congenital and acquired spinal deformities; benign and malignant spinal lesions; traumatic and inflammatory changes; "pronounced degenerative changes" (p. 201, PDF 2). How many were screened or excluded is not given.
- Modality and instrument: 40-slice CT, Siemens Somatom Definition AS; 120 kV, 266 mAs, acquisition 40 x 0.6 mm, 2 mm layers; axial scans with coronal and sagittal multiplanar reconstructions from T12 to S1; a bone window of 2300-2500 HU and a soft-tissue window of 300-500 HU (Inference: these are window widths; levels not given). Measured on Siemens Syngo software, in the axial plane, with MPR linking axial, coronal and sagittal views (p. 201, PDF 2).
- Levels covered: L1, L2, L3, L4, L5 only; 100 values per sex per level per measure (Table 5, p. 202, PDF 3). T12 and S1 were in the scan range but nothing is reported for them.
- How levels were identified and counted: not stated. No mention of counting from the sacrum or from the ribs, of transitional vertebrae, or of how L1 was fixed.
- Statistics: SPSS 19.0 and Excel 11 (p. 201, PDF 2); the acknowledgments thank a named person for the statistical analysis (p. 205, PDF 6). No test is named, neither for the sex comparison nor for the comparisons with other populations, and no p value or test statistic is printed for any sex comparison; Tables 6-11 carry only "<0,05" at every level (pp. 203-204, PDF 4-5), and how a sample of 100 was tested against another study's published mean is not said.
- Number format: Tables 3 and 4 print means with two decimals that are Table 5's four-decimal means truncated, not rounded (for example male L1 MSG 18.8180 printed 18.81, male L5 MSG 17.7890 printed 17.78, male L2 IPD 25.3150 printed 25.31) (Tables 3-5, p. 202, PDF 3). Ranges are printed with two decimals that always end in 0, so readings were taken to 0.1 mm.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts or any other numbering anomaly, and does not say how levels were numbered. The abstract and background take the lumbar spine as five vertebrae (p. 200, PDF 1). The only related statement is the exclusion of "congenital ... spinal deformities" (p. 201, PDF 2); whether that removed transitional anatomy is not said.

Inference: with a T12-to-S1 scan range, a transitional L5 or an L6 would silently fall into the L5 row (the row with the largest SDs and the extreme outliers, Table 5), and a T13 or a missing twelfth rib would shift every row. Treat the row labels as unverified for anomalies.

## Measures, each as a recipe

Both measures are bony canal measures read in the bone window, defined in the methods (p. 201, PDF 2) and drawn in Figure 1 (p. 201, PDF 2): an axial CT slice of a lumbar vertebra through the pedicles and transverse processes, a vertical midline line labelled MSAG and a horizontal line labelled IPD crossing inside the canal; the caption expands the abbreviations as MSGD (midsagittal spinal canal diameter) and IPD (interpedicular spinal canal diameter). In Figure 1 the IPD line lies in the anterior part of the canal, near where the pedicles join the body, not at mid-depth. The abstract and objective call the second measure "interpeduncular" (IP) and Chart 2's axis says "Mediosagittal" (p. 200, PDF 1; p. 201, PDF 2; p. 202, PDF 3); the chart legends are in Bosnian (Muskarci = men, Zene = women). Ranges are read to 0.1 mm; Table 5 gives means to four decimals.

### MSG (also MSGD, MSAG): midsagittal diameter of the spinal canal

- Landmarks: in the midsagittal line, from the posterior edge of the vertebral body to the anterior (inner) edge of the lamina of the arch at the point where the spinous process leaves the arch (p. 201, PDF 2). In Figure 1 the line runs from the posterior body cortex to the inner cortex of the laminar junction at the base of the spinous process.
- Plane and slice: the methods say both diameters were measured "in the axial plane" and, in the same sentence, that the MSG was measured "in the midsagittal plane" (p. 201, PDF 2). Figure 1 draws it on an axial slice, so the likeliest reading is the midline of the axial image (Inference); a reading on the sagittal MPR cannot be ruled out from the text. Which axial slice is not specified in words; Figure 1 shows a slice through the pedicles and transverse processes (Inference: mid-pedicle level, the same slice as IPD). Whether the axial plane was reoriented to each vertebra or left at the gantry angle is not stated.
- Instrument: Syngo distance tool on screen, bone window (p. 201, PDF 2).
- Levels: L1-L5.
- Values, mean +/- SD (min-max), mm: means and SDs from Table 5 rounded to 0.01 (Table 3 prints the truncated means, for example men 18.81, 16.11, 17.78 at L1, L3, L5), ranges from Tables 3 and 5 (p. 202, PDF 3):
  - Men: L1 18.82 +/- 1.67 (15.5-24.3); L2 17.56 +/- 1.97 (13.0-23.7); L3 16.12 +/- 1.84 (12.3-20.2); L4 16.45 +/- 2.22 (11.7-22.4); L5 17.79 +/- 2.84 (11.9-30.8).
  - Women: L1 19.31 +/- 1.58 (15.5-23.9); L2 18.55 +/- 1.86 (14.3-22.9); L3 17.21 +/- 2.02 (13.5-21.8 by Table 5; Table 3 prints 12.2-21.8); L4 17.14 +/- 2.16 (12.3-23.9) by Table 5, while Table 3 prints 17.38; L5 17.83 +/- 2.55 (12.9-26.1).
  - Both sexes (Table 3): L1 19.06 (15.5-24.3); L2 18.05 (13.0-23.7); L3 16.66 (12.3-21.8); L4 16.79 (11.7-23.9); L5 17.81 (11.9-30.8). Overall range 11.70-30.80 mm (p. 202, PDF 3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Build the vertebra's own frame: craniocaudal axis normal to the mean end-plate plane of the body subregion, AP axis from the body centroid toward the spinous process, LR axis orthogonal. Take the section through mid-pedicle height. Fill the closed bony ring (body plus posterior elements) and subtract the bone: the remainder is the vertebral foramen. MSG is the foramen's extent along the AP axis on the midline (the plane through the spinous process), from the posterior surface of the body to the anterior surface of the arch at the laminar junction. Close small gaps in the ring first (partial-volume breaks at thin laminae, a split arch). SPINEPS's spinal-canal label is the canal contents along the column, not one vertebra's bony foramen; do not use it for MSG.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference (the paper uses CT only). Lateral radiograph: from the posterior body line at mid-pedicle height to the spinolaminar line, divided by the magnification; not available on an AP view. The spinolaminar line is faint at L5.
- Across levels: an "hourglass" (p. 203, PDF 4; abstract p. 200): largest at L1, narrowing through L2 to a waist at L3 (and L4), widening again to L5 (p. 202-203, PDF 3-4). Both sexes: L1 19.06, L2 18.05, L3 16.66, L4 16.79, L5 17.81 (Table 3). Steps (Inference, computed): L1->L2 -1.01, L2->L3 -1.39, L3->L4 +0.13, L4->L5 +1.02 mm. Over the pooled between-subject SD (about 1.6 at L1 to 2.7 mm at L5, Inference computed from Table 5) the separation indices are about 0.56, 0.70, 0.06 and 0.42: MSG separates L2 from L3 best, cannot tell L3 from L4, and L5 from L4 only weakly. In men the minimum is clearly L3 (16.12 vs 16.45); in women it is L3 by Table 3 (17.20 vs 17.38) but L4 by Table 5 (17.21 vs 17.14), a tie in practice.
- Sex, age, size, population: women larger than men at L1 (+0.49), L2 (+0.99), L3 (+1.09) and L4 (+0.69 by Table 5, +0.93 by Table 3) mm; equal at L5 (+0.04) (Table 5, p. 202, PDF 3). The authors call the female excess significant at every level (abstract p. 200; p. 202, PDF 3; conclusion p. 204, PDF 5). Inference: a Welch t from Table 5's means and SDs gives p about 0.03, < 0.001, < 0.001, 0.03 and 0.91 for L1-L5, so L5 is not different. Age and body size were not analysed. Population: Bosnian means are above Tobin (US, Chicago, 1970-79 birth cohort, 151 men and 221 women; by 1.3-3.0 mm), Griffith (Chinese, 540 men and 540 women; by 1.1-3.7 mm at L1-L4) and Yadav (Indian, 174 men and 128 women; by 2.6-4.0 mm) (Tables 6-8, p. 203, PDF 4; differences computed from Tables 6-8 with Cizmic's printed values); Griffith's L5 is larger than the Bosnian L5 (19.67 vs 17.78 men, 18.12 vs 17.83 women, Table 7). Yadav's 302 patients had abdominopelvic CT and no lumbar pain (p. 203, PDF 4), so that comparison sets a symptomatic cohort against an asymptomatic one.
- Error: not reported (no intra- or inter-observer study, no repeat readings, reader count not given). Between-subject CV, Inference computed from Table 5: about 8-9% at L1 rising to 14-16% at L5.

### IPD (IP): interpedicular diameter of the spinal canal

- Landmarks: the transverse distance between the inner (medial) edges of the two pedicles, taken at the canal's widest point (p. 201, PDF 2; Figure 1).
- Plane: axial; slice not specified in words; Figure 1 shows the pedicle level. Not stated whether "widest point" means across the slices of the pedicle or within one chosen slice.
- Instrument: Syngo distance tool, bone window (p. 201, PDF 2).
- Levels: L1-L5.
- Values, mean +/- SD (min-max), mm: means and SDs from Table 5 rounded to 0.01 (Table 4 prints the truncated means, for example men 25.00 and 25.31 at L1 and L2, women 24.96 at L2), ranges from Tables 4 and 5 (p. 202, PDF 3):
  - Men: L1 25.01 +/- 2.25 (21.3-33.5); L2 25.32 +/- 2.21 (21.7-33.2); L3 26.22 +/- 2.36 (22.2-34.6); L4 27.81 +/- 3.06 (21.6-37.2); L5 32.69 +/- 4.18 (23.5-41.8).
  - Women: L1 24.56 +/- 2.33 (20.1-31.0); L2 24.97 +/- 2.28 (20.5-31.5); L3 25.51 +/- 2.34 (21.1-32.0); L4 26.84 +/- 3.00 (21.6-34.4); L5 31.20 +/- 3.69 (22.1-42.8).
  - Both sexes, mean +/- SD (range); the SDs are printed only in the results text, not in Table 4 (p. 202, PDF 3): L1 24.78 +/- 2.29 (20.1-33.5); L2 25.14 +/- 2.24 (20.5-33.2); L3 25.86 +/- 2.37 (21.1-34.6); L4 27.32 +/- 3.06 (21.6-37.2); L5 31.94 +/- 4.00 (22.1-42.8). Overall range 20.10-42.80 mm.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the vertebra's frame, in the sections that cut both pedicles, the LR width of the vertebral foramen between the medial surfaces of the left and right pedicle; take the maximum over those sections to match "widest point". Use the posterior-elements subregion for the pedicle walls and the bony foramen, not a soft-tissue canal label.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. AP radiograph: the classic interpedicular distance between the medial cortices of the two pedicle ovals, divided by the magnification. Not measurable on a lateral view.
- Across levels: a steady rise from L1 to L5 in both sexes (p. 202, PDF 3; p. 204, PDF 5; Chart 3). Steps (Inference, computed from Table 4): L1->L2 +0.36, L2->L3 +0.72, L3->L4 +1.46, L4->L5 +4.62 mm (men +4.88, women +4.36). Over the pooled SD the separation indices are about 0.16, 0.31, 0.54 and 1.31: IPD is useless for L1 against L2, weak for L2/L3, fair for L3/L4 and the best single canal cue for the L4 to L5 step.
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger at every level, by 0.44, 0.35, 0.71, 0.97 and 1.49 mm L1 to L5, the gap growing from L2 to L5 (Table 5, p. 202, PDF 3). The discussion says the difference is significant at L3, L4 and L5 only (p. 204, PDF 5); the abstract and the conclusion say at all levels (p. 200, PDF 1; p. 204, PDF 5). Inference: a Welch t from Table 5 gives p about 0.17, 0.28, 0.03, 0.02 and 0.008, which supports the discussion. Age and size not analysed. Population: the Bosnian IPD is larger than Griffith's Chinese (by 3.1-4.8 mm at every level, n = 540 men and 540 women, Table 9), M.M. Alam's Pakistani (by 0.8-5.2 mm, n = 33 men and 16 women, Table 10) and Urrutia's Mexican (by 0.9-2.3 mm, both sexes, 60 subjects, Table 11) (differences computed from the tables, p. 204, PDF 5); the text adds Egyptian and Korean series with no table (p. 204, PDF 5).
- Error: not reported. Between-subject CV about 9% at L1-L3, 11% at L4, 12-13% at L5 (Inference from Table 5).

### IPD/MSG ratio (derived; not in the paper)

- Inference, computed from the both-sexes means of Tables 3-4: 1.30 at L1, 1.39 at L2, 1.55 at L3, 1.63 at L4, 1.79 at L5. Monotone and largely free of overall size; the canal becomes relatively wider and shallower caudally. Useful as a size-free level cue alongside the absolute values.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1 (thoracolumbar side): the widest MSG in both sexes (19.06 mm both, 18.81 men, 19.31 women) and the narrowest IPD (24.78 both; 25.0 men, 24.56 women) (p. 202, PDF 3; conclusion p. 204-205, PDF 5-6). The authors explain the wide sagittal canal at L1 by its position at the thoracolumbar transition: the level of the "upper end of the lumbar extension of the spinal cord", where the thoracic convexity turns into the lumbar concavity, between the relatively "immobile" thoracic and "mobile" lumbar spine (citing Davis, ref 22), so that the canal there reflects a protective adaptation and not only the size of its contents (citing Amonoo-Kuofi, ref 19) (p. 203, PDF 4). They add that the vast majority of studies put the widest MSG at L1, the one exception being the Chinese series (Griffith), widest at L5 (p. 203, PDF 4). Inference: the cord phrase is unclear; the lumbar enlargement of the cord usually lies at about the T10-T12 vertebral levels and the conus (the cord's lower end) at about L1-L2, so the anatomical claim cannot be used as a landmark rule.
- L2: intermediate on both measures; MSG 18.05, IPD 25.14 (Tables 3-4).
- L3: the narrowest MSG for both sexes combined (16.66, range 12.3-21.8) and in men (16.11); in women 17.20 by Table 3 (p. 202, PDF 3; pp. 204-205, PDF 5-6), though 17.21 against 17.14 at L4 by Table 5. This is the waist of the hourglass; the discussion says the canal narrows "at levels L3 and L4" before widening toward L5 (p. 202, PDF 3).
- L4: MSG nearly equal to L3 (16.79 both sexes); IPD begins its steeper rise (27.32). The smallest single MSG in the series, 11.70 mm, is a man's L4 (Table 5).
- L5: the widest IPD (31.94 both; 32.69 men, 31.20 women) and the largest spread of both measures (SD 4.18/3.69 IPD, 2.84/2.55 MSG); the MSG rebounds to 17.81 and the sex difference in MSG vanishes (Table 5). The largest single MSG, 30.80 mm in a man, is 4.6 SD above the male L5 mean (Inference: an outlier, possibly a transitional or misnumbered segment or a measurement at a different landmark).
- T12/L1 and L5/S1 as junctions: T12 and S1 were in the scan range but not measured; nothing is said about the junctions beyond the L1 explanation above.

## Measurement error and reliability

- No reliability study of any kind: no intra- or inter-observer agreement, no repeat measurements, no statement of who measured or how many readers (p. 201, PDF 2).
- Technical resolution: 0.6 mm acquisition, 2 mm reconstructed layers (p. 201, PDF 2); ranges printed to 0.1 mm.
- Between-subject SDs (Table 5, p. 202, PDF 3): MSG 1.58-2.84 mm; IPD 2.21-4.18 mm. These mix true variation and measurement error and cannot be separated here.
- Distributions look right-skewed (Inference from Table 5): the maxima lie 2.2-4.6 SD above the means, the minima 1.6-2.5 SD below.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication: reference values for judging lumbar canal stenosis in patients with low back pain; the authors say clinical and imaging findings must be combined to diagnose stenosis (p. 204, PDF 5). The background defines stenosis as a congenital or acquired reduction of the osteoligamentous canal that compresses the dural sac or the nerve roots (p. 200, PDF 1), and gives the frequency of its symptoms as the reason it is studied.
- Stenosis criteria quoted (not derived here): MSG above 13 mm normal; 10-13 mm "relative stenosis"; below 10 mm "absolute stenosis"; IPD 16-18 mm normal; below 15 mm stenotic (p. 200-201, PDF 1-2, citing ref 7; p. 204-205, PDF 5-6, citing an IMAIOS web page, ref 25). Inference: ref 7 as listed is a 1974 paper on the facet joints' role in segmental kinematics (p. 205, PDF 6), not an obvious source for stenosis thresholds; the IPD criteria leave 15-16 mm and above 18 mm undefined.
- The authors conclude that the mean MSG at every level and both sexes is above 13 mm (smallest mean 16.11 men L3, 17.2 women L3) and the mean IPD far above 16-18 mm, so the population shows no tendency to stenosis (p. 204-205, PDF 5-6). Inference: the individual minima (MSG 11.7 at male L4, 11.9 at male L5, 12.3 at female L4 and male L3) fall in the 10-13 mm relative-stenosis band, so some patients met that criterion; the paper does not count them. No IPD value in the series (minimum 20.1 mm) comes near 15 mm, so the 16-18 mm "normal" band is below everything measured; the IPD threshold is not usable as a normal range for CT IPD measured this way.
- Disc pathology (herniation, bulge, protrusion, extrusion) is said not to affect the bony measures read in the bone window (p. 202, PDF 3). Pitfall: these are bony canal sizes; they say nothing about soft-tissue (disc, ligamentum flavum) narrowing, which is what compresses the dural sac.
- Pitfall: the sample is a symptomatic disc-disease cohort (every patient has a disc diagnosis, Table 2), presented as normative data (abstract, p. 200, PDF 1).
- Pitfall: the MSG is a midline measure and does not capture the lateral recesses (Inference; the paper does not discuss canal shape or the trefoil L5 canal).
- Population differences: the authors find Bosnian MSG and IPD larger than US, Chinese, Indian, Pakistani, Mexican, Egyptian and Korean series and stress population-specific norms (pp. 203-205, PDF 4-6). Pitfall (Inference): the compared studies measured with different slices and planes (Griffith reoriented to a mid-pedicle plane perpendicular to the body, by the set's Griffith note), so part of the gap may be method, not population.
- Other populations as reported (pp. 202-204, PDF 3-5): Eisenstein (South Africans) and Malas (Turkish, MRI) found women's MSG wider; Alam (Pakistani) found men's MSG larger, not significant; Lee (Korean), Eisenstein and Amonoo-Kuofi (Nigerian) found the L1-maximum, L3-minimum hourglass; Yadav (Indian) and M.M. Alam (Pakistani) found narrowing to L4 then widening at L5; Rema and Rajagopalan and Wang and Shih found a progressive MSG fall L1 to L5; Griffith (Chinese) found the widest MSG at L5; Tobin (US, 918 Chicago residents in two birth cohorts, 1940-49 n = 546 and 1970-79 n = 372; Cizmic compared only the younger cohort, as the older one is older than its own sample) found the L1 maximum and L3 waist with a significantly smaller sex difference at every level; Yadav (302 Indian patients with abdominopelvic CT and no lumbar pain) found the narrowest MSG at L4, still an hourglass; Alam M et al. (ref 18; 49 Pakistani subjects on CT, 33 men and 16 women by Table 10) found IPD larger in men at L3 and L5; Tacar (200 healthy Turkish adults) found IPD larger in men at every level by 0.5-1.5 mm (p < 0.001); Lee, Zhou and Griffith found IPD rising L1 to L5; M.M. Alam's Pakistani IPD dips at L3 then widens at L4-L5; Urrutia (60 Mexicans, CT) found IPD rising from 22.46 +/- 1.35 at L1 to 29.78 +/- 3.50 at L5, a canal they describe as diamond-shaped.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Measure the bony canal, bone window, on the axial section through the pedicles as Figure 1 draws it: MSG in the midline from the posterior body wall to the inner surface of the arch where the spinous process leaves it; IPD between the medial pedicle walls at the canal's widest point (p. 201, PDF 2, Figure 1). The text does not name the slice and also says the MSG was taken "in the midsagittal plane", so check the axial reading against the sagittal reformat at the same level (Inference). From a mask, use the filled-ring foramen of each vertebra, never a soft-tissue canal label (Inference).
2. Read IPD as a monotone craniocaudal gradient: expect about 24.8, 25.1, 25.9, 27.3, 31.9 mm L1 to L5 (Table 4 and text, p. 202, PDF 3). The mean L4 to L5 step is 4.6 mm, the largest of the series; Inference: treat a rise of about 4-5 mm between two consecutive instances as evidence for that step, weighed against the L5 SD of 3.7-4.2 mm. Do not try to separate L1 from L2 with IPD (0.36 mm apart against an SD of about 2.3 mm).
3. Read MSG as an hourglass: highest at L1 (about 19 mm), waist at L3-L4 (about 16.7 mm), rebound at L5 (about 17.8 mm) (Table 3, p. 202, PDF 3; the "hourglass", p. 203, PDF 4). Inference from the separation indices above: use the L2 to L3 fall as its best step; never use MSG to tell L3 from L4.
4. Combine the two as the ratio IPD/MSG (Inference): about 1.30, 1.39, 1.55, 1.63, 1.79 L1 to L5; it removes much of the patient-size factor.
5. Compare each instance with its neighbours, not only with the population table: the between-subject SDs (MSG 1.6-2.8, IPD 2.2-4.2 mm, Table 5, p. 202, PDF 3) exceed every adjacent-level step except the IPD L4 to L5 step (Inference, computed from Tables 3-5).
6. Condition on sex: women's MSG is 0.5-1.1 mm larger at L1-L4 and equal at L5; men's IPD is 0.4-1.5 mm larger, the gap growing toward L5 (Table 5, p. 202, PDF 3).
7. Condition on population and size: these Bosnian MSG means sit about 1-4 mm above the US (Tobin), Chinese (Griffith, L1-L4 only) and Indian (Yadav) CT means, and the IPD means about 1-5 mm above the Chinese, Pakistani and Mexican ones (Tables 6-11, pp. 203-204, PDF 4-5; differences computed); let a population or size factor shift the whole column rather than reading absolute values as level evidence (Inference). Note the reverse at L5: Griffith's Chinese L5 MSG is larger than the Bosnian (Table 7).
8. Use the female L4 MSG mean 17.14 mm (Table 5), not 17.38 (Table 3), and the female L3 MSG minimum 13.5 mm (Table 5), not 12.2 (Table 3): Table 5 agrees with the both-sexes means and range (p. 202, PDF 3).
9. Widen the L5 likelihood: L5 has the largest SDs and the most extreme values (MSG max 30.8, IPD max 42.8, Table 5, p. 202, PDF 3); use heavy-tailed or log-scale likelihoods (Inference).
10. Do not trust the paper's L1-L5 labels as anomaly-checked: the counting method is not given though T12 and S1 were scanned (p. 201, PDF 2). Before naming, count ribs and find the sacrum independently (Inference).
11. Flag an instance whose MSG falls below 13 mm (relative stenosis) or 10 mm (absolute), or whose IPD falls below 15 mm, as the cited criteria define (p. 200-201, PDF 1-2; p. 204-205, PDF 5-6); do not apply the 16-18 mm IPD "normal" band as a normal range.
12. On a lateral radiograph or fluoroscopy frame, take only the MSG analogue (posterior body line to spinolaminar line); on an AP, only the IPD (medial pedicle cortices); divide both by the magnification (Inference; the paper is CT only).
13. Do not model sex as one overall size scale for the canal: women have the deeper canal (MSG) at L1-L4 and men the wider one (IPD) at every level, so the two measures move in opposite directions with sex (Table 5, p. 202, PDF 3). Inference: give the generator a sex term per measure, or a sex-specific IPD/MSG ratio, rather than one sex multiplier.
14. Expect the bony canal to look normal on CT in patients with disc disease: every patient here had a bulge, herniation, protrusion or extrusion (Table 2, p. 201, PDF 2) and the mean bony diameters were all above the stenosis cut-offs (p. 204-205, PDF 5-6). Do not use the bony MSG or IPD to infer soft-tissue stenosis (Inference).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of any transitional or numbering anomaly and no denominator for one; its 200 patients were reported only as L1-L5 (pp. 200-202, PDF 1-3).

(b) Measures the generator holds or could add:
- MSG corresponds to Panjabi's spinal canal depth (SCD) and IPD to his spinal canal width (SCW) (Inference: by the set's Panjabi note, Panjabi never says whether SCW is the interpedicular distance or the canal's widest chord, nor at what height it was taken). Sex-specific per-level means and SDs for L1-L5 are in Table 5 (p. 202, PDF 3) and could serve as a second likelihood for living adults on CT.
- Inference (from the set's Panjabi 1992 note): Panjabi's SCD 19.0, 18.2, 17.5, 18.6, 19.7 and SCW 23.7 rising to 27.1 L1 to L5. ## Gaps and conflicts

- Not stated: how levels were counted; any anomaly; which axial slice was used for MSG, and whether it was read on the axial image or on the sagittal reformat (the methods say both, p. 201, PDF 2); whether the plane was reoriented; how "widest point" was found; who measured and how often; which statistical tests were used; how many patients were screened and excluded.
- Table 3 against Table 5 (p. 202, PDF 3): female L4 MSG mean 17.38 vs 17.142, and female L3 MSG minimum 12.20 vs 13.50. Table 5 is the consistent one: the both-sexes L4 mean 16.79 matches the average of 16.454 and 17.142 (16.798, truncated as Table 3 truncates), while 17.38 would give 16.91, and the both-sexes L3 minimum 12.30 excludes a female 12.20. Chart 2 (p. 202, PDF 3) and Tables 6-8 (p. 203, PDF 4) carry the 17.38, and the "narrowest at L3" statement for women depends on it.
- Text against Table 2: extrusion in men is given as 8% in the text (p. 202, PDF 3) but 13 of 100 in Table 2 (p. 201, PDF 2); the 11.5% total matches Table 2. Chart 1 (p. 201, PDF 2) has two rings matching Table 2 (outer male, inner female, from the percentages and the leader lines), with "Femanle" misspelt and the 8% (male protrusion) and 10% (female extrusion) labels set beside the ring labels; the Male leader line ends on the outer ring and the Femanle one on the inner ring, which agrees.
- Abstract and conclusion against discussion: IPD sex difference significant at all levels (abstract p. 200, PDF 1; conclusion p. 204, PDF 5) vs at L3-L5 only (discussion, p. 204, PDF 5). MSG female excess called significant at all levels, but at L5 the means differ by 0.04 mm (Table 5).
- Conclusion against Table 7: the conclusion says Bosnian MSG and IPD are significantly larger than other populations at all levels (p. 204, PDF 5), but Griffith's L5 MSG exceeds the Bosnian one in both sexes (Table 7, p. 203, PDF 4). The text says the Griffith difference is significant at L1-L4 but not at L5 and then prints "(p < 0.05)"; Table 7 gives p < 0.05 at L5 too.
- Design described as retrospective (abstract, p. 200, PDF 1) and as "retrospective, prospective" (methods, p. 201, PDF 2).
- Keywords (psychometric properties, reliability, validity, responsiveness, outcome measure, p. 200, PDF 1) do not fit the paper.
- Citation errors: the Pakistani Alam study is cited as ref 9, which is Yadav's Indian study (p. 203, PDF 4); refs 11 and 18 are the same Alam paper (p. 205, PDF 6); the Urrutia diamond statement carries ref 21 (Wang and Shih) (p. 204, PDF 5); Alam M et al.'s IPD (ref 18, 49 subjects) is called significantly larger in men at L3 and L5 with "(p>0.05)" (p. 204, PDF 5).
- Inference (from the set's Yadav 2020 note): Table 8's "L1" values for Yadav (16.19 men, 16.33 women) are Yadav's D12 (T12) values; Yadav's L1 is 15.68 in men. The other Table 8 rows match Yadav's L2-L5.
- Inference (from the set's Eisenstein 1977 note): Cizmic credits Eisenstein with wider female MSG and the L1-maximum hourglass; Eisenstein's dry-bone table shows the sexes within 0-1 mm and L1 equal to L5 (a "venturi tube" waisted at L2-L4).

- Population mislabel: the sentence on the Griffith comparison calls the populations "Bosnian-Herzegovinian and Egyptian" (p. 203, PDF 4); Griffith's series is Chinese (Table 7 and ref 10).
- Griffith is cited as ref 9 (Yadav) in the IPD discussion as well (p. 204, PDF 5), besides ref 10 elsewhere.
