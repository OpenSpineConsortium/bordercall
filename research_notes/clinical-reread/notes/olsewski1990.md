## What the paper is

- Design: a three-part morphometric study of the lumbar pedicles, written to guide transpedicular
  screw fixation and to test sex differences and the accuracy of radiographic measurement
  (abstract, p. 541, PDF 1).
- Part 1, direct measurement: lumbosacral spines from 49 embalmed cadavers, 24 men and 25 women,
  from the Department of Anatomical Sciences, SUNY at Buffalo School of Medicine. Men 60-90 years
  (mean 74), women 60-98 years (mean 77) (p. 542, PDF 2). Both pedicles at each level L1-L5 were
  measured (p. 542, PDF 2). Table I, p. 544 (PDF 4), by sex.
- Part 2, radiograph against direct: AP, lateral and "transverse" (axial) radiographs of 21
  disarticulated lumbar spines, 12 men and 9 women, chosen at random from the 49 (p. 543, PDF 3).
  Sexes pooled (p. 545, PDF 5). Table II, p. 544 (PDF 4).
- Part 3, living patients: plain radiographs and CT of the lumbosacral spine of 51 patients, 23 men
  (20-49 years, mean 35) and 28 women (21-50 years, mean 38), seen for low-back problems in the
  Departments of Orthopaedics or Neurosurgery, Buffalo General Hospital (p. 543, PDF 3). Table III,
  p. 545 (PDF 5), by sex.
- Population: Buffalo, New York, United States. No ethnicity or race is reported for any part;
  the authors say heights and weights were not available "for all specimens and patients", and
  none are reported (p. 547, PDF 7). There are no race groups in this paper; the only grouping is
  sex (and, between parts, age).
- Denominators: the n in parentheses in each table counts pedicles, not people (Inference: the
  maxima fit two pedicles per vertebra: Table I 47 of a possible 48 male L5 pedicles; Table II 42
  = 21 x 2; Table III 46 = 23 x 2 and 56 = 28 x 2). n varies by cell from 24 to 47 (Table I) with
  no stated reason for the missing pedicles; the women's cells never exceed 39 of a possible 50
  pedicles (25 women), so up to a fifth or more of the female pedicles are missing from every
  Table I cell (Inference by arithmetic). The two pedicles of one vertebra, and the ten of one
  spine, are analysed as if independent (Inference: the paper says nothing of clustering).
- Instruments: vernier caliper for every diameter and distance, precision +/- 0.1 mm (Inoxyd
  Helios, Hannover); protractor for every angle, +/- 1 deg; one investigator; each data point the
  average of three trials (pp. 541-542, PDF 1-2). Orientation by a
  clear Plexiglas stage with grid lines above and below the specimen under a Wild Heerbrugg
  dissecting microscope (model M5-22991; eyepiece x8, objective x6, reducing lens x0.5) (p. 542,
  PDF 2, Fig. 3).
- Radiography (Part 2): Buffalo General Hospital radiology; technique held constant and calibrated
  for "no measurable magnification", target-to-film 47 inches (119.4 cm), focal spot 0.6 mm
  (p. 543, PDF 3).
- Radiography and CT (Part 3): routine AP and decubitus lateral radiographs; CT on a Siemens Somatom
  DRH (version G/G1/H), contiguous 4 mm cuts, cut parallel to the superior end-plates (to compensate
  for lordosis) and through the middle of the pedicles where possible; gantry not always parallel
  at L5-S1 (p. 543, PDF 3). Transverse and sagittal diameters from the AP radiographs, sagittal
  angles from the lateral radiographs, transverse angle and posterior-to-anterior distance from CT
  (p. 543, PDF 3). Radiographic and CT technique was the same for every patient (p. 543, PDF 3).
- Magnification (Part 3): a collimator test tool (Radiation Measurements, Middleton, Wisconsin)
  imaged at the patients' settings: 100 mm read 108 mm on the AP film, so diameters were multiplied
  by 0.93; on the CT scans a known 100 mm read 55 mm, so the CT distance was multiplied by 1.82
  (Inference: the scans were read at reduced scale on printed film); angles were not corrected
  (p. 544, PDF 4).
- Statistics: Student t test for sex differences at each level (Parts 1 and 3) and for differences
  at each level with sexes pooled; Student t test for radiograph against direct in Table II
  (Table II footnote); analysis of variance across all three parts at each level; significance
  p <= 0.05 (p. 544, PDF 4). Whether the Table II test was paired is not stated, and no result of
  the pooled between-level tests or of the analysis of variance is reported anywhere.
- Levels: L1 to L5 only. No thoracic, sacral or cervical level is measured.
- How levels were identified and counted: not stated. The cadaver material is called
  "lumbosacral spines" (p. 542, PDF 2), so the sacrum was presumably attached and levels counted up
  from it (Inference). No word on transitional segments.
- Exclusion: in Part 3 a patient whose radiograph showed "a gross abnormality of the lumbosacral
  spine" was excluded (p. 543, PDF 3); the count excluded and the kind of abnormality are not given.
  No exclusion criteria are stated for the cadavers. Pedicles whose sagittal angle was caudad were
  removed from the L1-L4 means and reported separately (pp. 545-546, PDF 5-6).
- CT in Part 3 was retrospective, from routine low-back scans, which usually did not include L1:
  no CT transverse angle or distance at L1, and only 2 (men) and 6 (women) pedicles at L2 (p. 546,
  PDF 6; Table III), that is about one man and three women (Inference).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation,
  L6, T13, rib counts, cervical ribs or block vertebrae (pp. 541-549, PDF 1-9). Level numbering
  method is not given.
- The only exclusion that could touch anomalies: Part 3 dropped patients with a gross lumbosacral
  abnormality on the radiograph, uncounted and undefined (p. 543, PDF 3). Inference: an LSTV may or
  may not have been counted as "gross"; the Part 3 sample cannot be assumed to contain the usual
  share of transitional anatomy.
- A pedicle orientation variant with counts (useful as a prior for a sagittal-angle feature, not
  for numbering): a caudad-directed pedicle axis in the sagittal plane.
  - Part 1 cadavers, L1-L4: 6 caudad pedicles: one L1 in a man (-4 deg), one L2 in a woman (-3),
    four L3 (two in the same woman, -4 and -5; one in another woman, -12; one in a man, -8), so six
    pedicles in five vertebrae. In four of these vertebrae the other pedicle pointed cephalad
    (p. 545, PDF 5). Inference: 6 of
    about 283 L1-L4 pedicles with a sagittal angle (277 cephalad counted in Table I, plus these 6).
  - Part 1 cadavers, L5: caudad in 8 of 46 male pedicles and 4 of 37 female pedicles (Table I,
    p. 544, PDF 4: cephalad n 38 and 33, caudad n 8 and 4), 12 of 83 pooled (14 percent,
    Inference). Both pedicles of each L5 pointed the same way (p. 545, PDF 5); Inference: 4 male and
    2 female L5 vertebrae, 6 of about 42 (23 male L5s from 46 pedicles; about 19 female L5s from 37
    pedicles, an odd count, so one female L5 had only one pedicle measured).
  - Part 2 (21 spines), L1-L3: 5 caudad pedicles (L1 -4 direct / -5 radiographic; L2 -3 / -6; L3
    two at -4 and -5 direct, -6 radiographic, and one at -8 by both); the text says that in three
    of "the five vertebrae" the other pedicle was cephalad (p. 545, PDF 5). L5: 12 of 42 pedicles
    caudad (Table II, p. 544, PDF 4). Inference: the direct values of the five L1-L3 pedicles are
    exactly those of five of the six Part 1 caudad pedicles (L1 man -4, L2 woman -3, L3 woman -4 and
    -5, L3 man -8), and Table II's 12 caudad L5 pedicles equal Table I's total of 12; so the 21
    "random" spines hold all six caudad L5 vertebrae and 5 of the 6 caudad L1-L4 pedicles of the
    49 spines (see Gaps).
  - Part 3 living young adults, lateral radiographs: six caudad pedicles at L1 (two at -3, two at
    -4, two at -5, each pair from one patient, so three patients) and two at L5 (both -15, one
    patient) (p. 546, PDF 6). Inference: L5 caudad in 2 of 94 pedicles (1 of 47 patients), far
    fewer than in the elderly cadavers (12 of 83 pedicles).
  - Zindrick et al. found more caudad sagittal angles at all lumbar levels (p. 546, PDF 6).

## Measures, each as a recipe

Naming used by the paper: "transverse diameter" is the pedicle's width (mediolateral), "sagittal
diameter" its height (craniocaudal); "transverse angle" is measured in the axial plane, "sagittal
angle" in the sagittal plane.

### Transverse diameter of the pedicle (pedicle width)

- Definition: the narrowest dimension of the pedicle in the transverse plane (p. 542, PDF 2; Fig. 1
  draws the arrow across the pedicle between the canal and the lateral surface on a top view;
  Inference from the drawing: the arrow lies close to the vertebra's left-right axis, not
  explicitly perpendicular to the pedicle axis).
- Instrument: caliper on the bone (Part 1); AP radiograph (Parts 2 and 3), the pedicle oval's width,
  x0.93 in Part 3 (p. 543-544, PDF 3-4).
- Levels: L1-L5, both sides.
- Values, mean +/- SD mm (n pedicles):
  - Table I direct, elderly cadavers (p. 544, PDF 4): men 9.5 +/- 2.9 (39), 9.6 +/- 2.2 (40),
    11.7 +/- 2.5 (40), 14.7 +/- 2.7 (44), 21.1 +/- 3.4 (47); women 7.7 +/- 1.9 (34), 7.9 +/- 1.9
    (33), 9.6 +/- 2.4 (34), 12.5 +/- 2.3 (36), 18.4 +/- 3.6 (39), L1 to L5. Women smaller at every
    level, p <= 0.05.
  - Table II, 21 spines, sexes pooled (p. 544, PDF 4): direct 8.2 +/- 2.3, 8.4 +/- 2.1,
    10.2 +/- 2.5, 13.2 +/- 2.5, 20.1 +/- 3.7; radiographic 8.2 +/- 1.8, 8.3 +/- 1.8, 10.0 +/- 1.9,
    12.6 +/- 2.3, 16.6 +/- 2.5 (significantly smaller only at L5).
  - Table III, living, AP radiograph corrected (p. 545, PDF 5): men 9.0 +/- 1.9 (44), 9.9 +/- 2.0
    (46), 11.8 +/- 2.0 (46), 13.7 +/- 2.0 (46), 16.7 +/- 2.2 (36); women 6.3 +/- 1.2 (54),
    7.1 +/- 1.3 (54), 8.2 +/- 1.4 (54), 10.0 +/- 1.7 (54), 13.5 +/- 2.0 (46). Women smaller at every
    level, p <= 0.05.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Build a vertebra frame (z normal to the superior end-plate
  of the body subregion, x left-right normal to the median plane found from the canal centre and
  spinous process, y anteroposterior). Take the pedicle as the bony bridge of the posterior-element
  subregion between the posterior body wall and the origin of the transverse and articular
  processes, lateral to the canal. Find the narrowest mediolateral chord of that bridge in axial
  sections through it, outer cortex to outer cortex (the caliper measured the outside of the bone).
  The paper does not say whether the chord is perpendicular to the pedicle axis or to the median
  plane; at L5, where the axis turns 17-23 deg (this paper) or about 30 deg (others), the two differ
  by a factor of cos(angle), about 0.87-0.96, roughly 1-3 mm. Report which convention is used.
- From a radiograph alone: AP radiograph, the width of the pedicle oval, after magnification
  correction (p. 543, PDF 3). Pitfall at L5: radiograph 16.6 against direct 20.1 mm (Table II), even
  on a disarticulated spine with much less overlap than in vivo; the authors suggest this may relate
  to van Schaik's finding that the lateral borders of the L5 pedicles are not clearly visible on AP
  films (p. 548, PDF 8). Lateral radiograph: not possible.
- Across levels: rises stepwise L1 to L5; L1 and L2 nearly equal (9.5 and 9.6 in men, 7.7 and 7.9
  in women), then larger steps to L3, L4 and the largest step L4 to L5 (Table I). Inference, effect
  sizes (difference over mean SD), men: L1/L2 0.04, L2/L3 0.9, L3/L4 1.2, L4/L5 2.1; women 0.1, 0.8,
  1.2, 2.0. It separates L5 from L4 well, L4 from L3 moderately, and L1 from L2 not at all.
- Shape: "with the exception of" L5, the pedicles are oval and narrowest in the transverse plane
  (p. 547, PDF 7). Inference, width/height ratio from Table I: men 0.56, 0.60, 0.73, 0.90, 1.21;
  women 0.50, 0.52, 0.64, 0.84, 1.14, L1 to L5; the ratio crosses 1 between L4 and L5. On
  radiographs (Table II) 0.45, 0.48, 0.59, 0.81, 1.20; living (Table III) men 0.54, 0.59, 0.69,
  0.84, 1.13 and women 0.41, 0.47, 0.55, 0.70, 1.02.
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger at every level (Table I and III). Inference: men exceed
  women by 23, 22, 22, 18 and 15 percent of the women's value (Table I, L1-L5), or women fall short
  of men by 19, 18, 18, 15 and 13 percent of the men's value; the abstract's "5 to 20 per cent"
  fits the second reading. In the living (Table III) the gap is larger: 43, 39, 44, 37 and 24
  percent of the women's value (30, 28, 31, 27 and 19 percent of the men's). A woman's L4
  width (12.5) lies between a man's L3 and L4 (11.7, 14.7); a woman's L3 (9.6) equals a man's L1-L2.
  Age: the elderly cadaver women (7.7-18.4) are wider than the young living women by radiograph
  (6.3-13.5); the methods differ, so this is not a clean age effect (Inference).
- Error: caliper +/- 0.1 mm, mean of three trials (p. 542, PDF 2); between-subject SD 1.2-3.6 mm;
  radiograph against direct bias 0.0 to -0.6 mm at L1-L4 and -3.5 mm at L5 (Table II, Inference by
  subtraction).
- Against earlier series: the stepwise increase L1 to L5 agrees with earlier work, but this study's
  men had consistently larger widths than the earlier series, which pooled the sexes (p. 546,
  PDF 6; Fig. 7). Read off Fig. 7 (approximate): at L5 Saillant about 16.7, Krag (1986) about 15,
  Zindrick about 17.6 and Berry about 11 mm, against 21.1 (men) and about 18 (women) here.

### Sagittal diameter of the pedicle (pedicle height)

- Definition: the narrowest dimension of the pedicle in the sagittal plane (p. 542, PDF 2; Fig. 2,
  side view, vertical arrow across the pedicle between the vertebral notches).
- Instrument: caliper (Part 1); AP radiograph, the height of the pedicle oval (Parts 2 and 3),
  x0.93 in Part 3 (p. 543, PDF 3).
- Values, mean +/- SD mm (n):
  - Table I (p. 544, PDF 4): men 17.0 +/- 1.7 (38), 16.0 +/- 2.2 (39), 16.1 +/- 2.1 (42),
    16.4 +/- 2.5 (42), 17.4 +/- 3.4 (47); women 15.3 +/- 2.0 (31), 15.3 +/- 2.0 (32), 15.0 +/- 1.5
    (33), 14.9 +/- 1.5 (36), 16.2 +/- 1.9 (39). Women significantly smaller at every level except
    L2, where 15.3 against 16.0 is not significant (p. 544, PDF 4). The text says the greatest
    transverse and sagittal diameters were at L5 in both sexes (p. 544, PDF 4).
  - Table II pooled: direct 16.4 +/- 1.7, 15.4 +/- 1.5, 15.4 +/- 1.6, 15.4 +/- 2.0, 16.2 +/- 2.1;
    radiographic 18.2 +/- 1.7, 17.2 +/- 1.7, 16.9 +/- 1.3, 15.6 +/- 1.5, 13.8 +/- 1.9.
    Radiograph larger at L1-L3 and smaller at L5, significant; L4 not (p. 545, PDF 5).
  - Table III living (p. 545, PDF 5): men 16.7 +/- 1.9 (44), 16.8 +/- 2.4 (46), 17.2 +/- 2.6 (46),
    16.3 +/- 2.0 (45), 14.8 +/- 1.7 (38); women 15.4 +/- 1.4 (54), 15.1 +/- 1.5 (56), 15.0 +/- 1.5
    (55), 14.2 +/- 1.7 (56), 13.2 +/- 2.2 (50). Women smaller at every level.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the same pedicle bridge, the narrowest craniocaudal
  chord in parasagittal sections through the pedicle, between the superior and inferior notch
  margins, outer cortex.
- From a radiograph alone: AP, the height of the pedicle oval after magnification correction.
  Inference: a lateral radiograph also shows pedicle height (both sides superimposed), but the paper
  did not measure it there.
- Across levels: nearly flat by direct measurement, slightly U-shaped in men (tallest L1 and L5,
  lowest L2-L3) and lowest L4 then tallest L5 in women (Table I). On radiographs and in the living
  series it falls toward L5 (Table II radiographic 18.2 to 13.8; Table III men 17.2 at L3 to 14.8
  at L5), the opposite of the direct trend at L5. Inference: by direct measurement it separates no
  adjacent pair (effect sizes 0.5 or less, except women L4/L5 0.8).
- Against earlier series: men's heights here were consistently larger than in the earlier series
  at every level (p. 546, PDF 6; Fig. 8). Read off Fig. 8 (approximate): Saillant, Zindrick and
  Berry run about 15-16 mm at L1 and fall to about 13-14.6 mm at L5, the opposite of this paper's
  direct rise to L5 (17.4 men, 16.2 women) and like this paper's own radiographic fall (13.8,
  Table II). Inference: the L5 trend of pedicle height depends on method and definition.
- Sex, age, size, population: men 4.6-11 percent larger (Table I; Inference), 8-15 percent (Table
  III; Inference).
- Error: caliper +/- 0.1 mm; SD 1.3-3.4 mm; radiograph bias +1.8, +1.8, +1.5, +0.2, -2.4 mm L1-L5
  (Table II, Inference by subtraction).

### Transverse angle of the pedicle

- Definition: the angle formed by the pedicle's central axis with a line drawn parallel to
  the vertebra's median plane, in the transverse plane (pp. 542-543, PDF 2-3; Fig. 4). The axis
  converges toward the midline anteriorly (Fig. 4 drawing; the paper gives no sign convention, all
  values are positive).
- Instrument: protractor under the microscope with the vertebra aligned so its median plane lay
  parallel to the stage's vertical grid lines and the superior end-plate was viewed with the
  inferior end-plate/posterior body junction just hidden (p. 542-543, PDF 2-3); transverse
  radiograph of the disarticulated vertebra (Part 2); axial CT parallel to the superior end-plate
  (Part 3).
- Values, mean +/- SD deg (n):
  - Table I (p. 544, PDF 4): men 7 +/- 2 (37), 7 +/- 2 (35), 8 +/- 2 (42), 11 +/- 4 (44),
    17 +/- 7 (45); women 5 +/- 2 (29), 6 +/- 2 (29), 7 +/- 2 (33), 10 +/- 4 (36), 18 +/- 8 (37).
    Women smaller at L1 and L2 only (p. 545, PDF 5).
  - Table II pooled: direct 6 +/- 2, 6 +/- 2, 7 +/- 3, 11 +/- 5, 22 +/- 5; radiographic 6 +/- 3,
    8 +/- 4, 13 +/- 4, 16 +/- 5, 29 +/- 6; radiograph larger at L2-L5 (p. 545, PDF 5).
  - Table III CT, living (p. 545, PDF 5): L1 no data; men 9 +/- 1 (2), 11 +/- 2 (24), 13 +/- 3 (34),
    23 +/- 8 (34); women 10 +/- 1 (6), 12 +/- 3 (43), 13 +/- 4 (46), 23 +/- 5 (46), L2 to L5. No sex
    difference.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Fit the pedicle axis (line through the centroids of
  successive cross-sections of the pedicle bridge, continued into the body), project it on the
  axial plane of the vertebra frame, and take its angle to the median plane. The median plane must
  come from the vertebra (canal, spinous process, body symmetry), not the scanner, or axial
  rotation adds to one side and subtracts from the other.
- From a radiograph alone: not from an AP or lateral in a living patient; the paper used CT in vivo
  and an axial projection of a disarticulated vertebra ex vivo. Inference: the AP position of the
  pedicle oval relative to the body edge is at most an indirect cue.
- Across levels: flat L1-L3 (5-8 deg), a rise at L4 (10-11) and the largest at L5 (17-18 direct,
  23 on CT) (Table I; p. 545, PDF 5). Inference, effect sizes, men: L1/L2 0, L2/L3 0.5, L3/L4 1.0,
  L4/L5 1.1; women 0.5, 0.5, 1.0, 1.3. Separates L5 and L4 from the rest; does not separate L1-L3.
- Sex, age, size, population: no meaningful sex difference; the 1-2 deg gap at L1-L2 is called
  probably of no clinical importance (p. 547, PDF 7).
- Error: protractor +/- 1 deg; SD 1-8 deg, largest at L5. Radiograph minus direct: 0, +2, +6, +5,
  +7 deg L1-L5 (Table II, Inference by subtraction). CT gantry not always parallel at L5-S1 (p. 543,
  PDF 3).
- Between studies: this paper's transverse angles are "substantially smaller" at all levels than
  every earlier series except Saillant's, and smaller than Saillant's at L5 (p. 546, PDF 6; Fig. 9,
  p. 547, PDF 7). Read off Fig. 9 (approximate): Krag, Zindrick and Berry about 8-11 deg at L1 and
  27-31 deg at L5, against 5-7 and 16-18 here; Saillant about 0, 2, 5, 10 and 30 deg L1 to L5, so
  below this paper at L1-L3 and far above it at L5.

### Sagittal angle of the pedicle

- Definition: the angle formed by the pedicle's central axis with a line drawn parallel to
  the vertebral body's superior end-plate, in the sagittal plane; positive if cephalad,
  negative if caudad (p. 543, PDF 3; Fig. 5). Fig. 5 draws the pedicle axis running from the
  posterior-inferior arch up to the anterior-superior corner of the body, so "cephalad" means the
  axis rises as it runs anteriorly (Inference from the drawing).
- Instrument: protractor under the microscope with the vertebra on its side, superior end-plate
  parallel to a horizontal grid line and the far pedicle superimposed behind the near one, to copy
  an ideal lateral radiograph; then reversed for the other pedicle (p. 543, PDF 3); lateral
  radiograph (Parts 2 and 3).
- Values, mean +/- SD deg (n):
  - Table I (p. 544, PDF 4): men 5 +/- 1 (36), 6 +/- 2 (35), 6 +/- 2 (42), 6 +/- 2 (44), L5
    cephalad 5 +/- 2 (38) and caudad -8 +/- 5 (8); women 6 +/- 2 (26), 5 +/- 2 (27), 6 +/- 2 (32),
    7 +/- 2 (35), L5 cephalad 8 +/- 3 (33) and caudad 9 +/- 5 (4). The women's L5 caudad cell prints
    "9", without a minus sign; the text gives -9 (p. 545, PDF 5). Women's L5 cephalad larger than
    men's, p <= 0.05; no sex difference L1-L4.
  - Table II pooled: direct 6 +/- 2, 6 +/- 2, 6 +/- 2, 7 +/- 2, L5 7 +/- 4 (30) and -9 +/- 5 (12);
    radiographic 7 +/- 3, 6 +/- 2, 7 +/- 3, 8 +/- 3, L5 7 +/- 4 (30) and -10 +/- 5 (12). The table
    marks L1 and L4 radiographic as significantly different; the text calls the two methods only
    slightly different at all levels (p. 545, PDF 5).
  - Table III living, lateral radiograph: men 7 +/- 2 (42), 6 +/- 2 (44), 7 +/- 2 (44), 7 +/- 3
    (44), 7 +/- 3 (42); women 6 +/- 2 (46), 6 +/- 2 (50), 6 +/- 3 (50), 6 +/- 3 (50), 5 +/- 5 (50);
    caudad pedicles excluded (p. 546, PDF 6). No sex difference.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Project the pedicle axis on the vertebra's sagittal plane
  and measure its angle to the superior end-plate plane fitted to the body subregion's upper
  surface; positive when the anterior end is cranial.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, pedicles superimposed, angle of the pedicle axis to
  the superior end-plate line (p. 543, PDF 3).
- Across levels: flat, 5-7 deg cephalad at L1-L4 in both sexes and both series (pp. 545-546,
  PDF 5-6); at L5 bimodal, cephalad or caudad, with both pedicles of one L5 going the same way
  (p. 545, PDF 5). It does not separate L1-L4 from one another.
- Pitfall the authors stress: earlier series averaged cephalad and caudad L5 values algebraically;
  done with this paper's data, that gave a value similar to the published ones, which the authors
  call a false picture of the real angles; they recommend reporting the two directions separately
  (pp. 546-547, PDF 6-7; Fig. 10). Inference by arithmetic from Table I: the algebraic means would
  be about +2.7 deg (men) and +6.2 deg (women), a value that describes neither population.
- Against earlier series (Fig. 10, approximate reading): Zindrick about +2 deg at L1-L2, about 0 at
  L3-L4 and about -3 at L5; Saillant about +12 at L1 falling to about -3 at L4-L5; this paper flat at
  +5 to +7 with L5 split into about +5/+8 and -8/-9.
- Sex, age, size, population: no sex difference L1-L4; at L5 women's cephalad angle larger in
  cadavers (8 against 5) but not in the living (5 against 7).
- Error: protractor +/- 1 deg; SD 1-5 deg.

### Posterior-to-anterior distance along the pedicle axis

- Definition: the shortest span from the back (posterior aspect) of the laminar cortex forward to
  the front (anterior aspect) of the vertebral body's cortex, along a line through the middle of the pedicle (the
  same line used for the transverse angle) (pp. 542-543, PDF 2-3; Fig. 6). Fig. 6 starts the line
  at the posterior cortex where the transverse process meets the superior articular process and
  ends it on the anterior body cortex, converging medially (Inference from the drawing: the screw
  path from the usual entry point).
- Instrument: vernier caliper under the microscope (Part 1; pp. 541-543, PDF 1-3); transverse
  radiograph of the disarticulated vertebra (Part 2); axial CT, x1.82 (Part 3) (pp. 543-544,
  PDF 3-4).
- Values, mean +/- SD mm (n):
  - Table I (p. 544, PDF 4): men 45.0 +/- 2.7 (31), 46.8 +/- 3.2 (33), 49.0 +/- 3.8 (40),
    50.4 +/- 4.6 (41), 46.2 +/- 4.0 (45); women 42.5 +/- 2.6 (24), 43.5 +/- 3.0 (26), 44.8 +/- 2.6
    (31), 45.9 +/- 3.0 (34), 42.3 +/- 4.0 (37). Women shorter at every level.

  through the pedicle's mid-section, keep the shortest. Note that the chord crosses the pedicle and

  published L5 values span about 38 to 51 mm. Inference, effect sizes, men: L1/L2 0.6, L2/L3 0.6,
  L3/L4 0.3, L4/L5 -1.0; women 0.4, 0.5, 0.4, -1.0. The L4-to-L5 drop is the one usable step.
- Sex, age, size, population: women 2.5-4.5 mm shorter by Table I (the text says 2.5 to 5.0 mm,
  p. 547-548, PDF 7-8); 3.6-6.3 mm on CT (Table III, Inference by subtraction).
- Error: no stated instrument error beyond +/- 0.1 mm; SD 2.6-5.8 mm; radiograph overestimates by
  2.5, 4.2, 5.0, 8.0, 5.2 mm L1-L5 (Table II; p. 546, PDF 6).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1: shortest posterior-to-anterior distance (45.0 men, 42.5 women); pedicle width equal to L2;
  width/height about 0.5-0.56, the tallest-narrowest lumbar pedicle; transverse angle 5-7 deg, women
  smaller (p. 545, PDF 5; Table I). Routine low-back CT usually missed L1, so no CT data (p. 546,
  PDF 6). Three young patients had both L1 pedicles caudad on lateral films (p. 546, PDF 6).
  T12/L1 junction: not covered.
- L2: width barely above L1; in the cadavers the only level where the sagittal diameter did not
  differ significantly by sex (p. 544, PDF 4; in the living it did, Table III); women's transverse
  angle smaller in the cadavers (p. 545, PDF 5).
- L3: the most frequent caudad pedicles in cadavers (four of six at L1-L4, p. 545, PDF 5); first
  clear widening of the pedicle.
- L4: the longest screw path (50.4 men, 45.9 women), in contrast with earlier studies that put the
  maximum at L2 (p. 547, PDF 7); transverse angle rises to 10-11 deg.
- L5: widest pedicle (21.1 men, 18.4 women), the only level wider than tall; largest transverse
  angle (17 men, 18 women; 23 on CT); bimodal sagittal angle, caudad in about one in seven cadaver
  pedicles; screw path shorter than L4 (pp. 544-545, PDF 4-5). On AP radiographs the L5 width and
  height are underestimated (16.6 against 20.1; 13.8 against 16.2), which the authors suggest may
  relate to the lateral pedicle border being hard to see at L5 (p. 548, PDF 8; Table II).
- L5/S1: the CT gantry could not always be set parallel to the end-plates there (p. 543, PDF 3), so
  L5 axial angles and distances carry extra error.
- C2/C3, C7/T1, T12/L1: not covered.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Instrument: caliper +/- 0.1 mm, protractor +/- 1 deg; one investigator, three trials averaged per
  point (pp. 541-542, PDF 1-2). The text calls this the "error of measurement"; it is resolution, not
  a repeatability study (Inference). No intra- or inter-observer statistics, no ICC.
- Method agreement (Table II, p. 544, PDF 4; text pp. 545-546, PDF 5-6), radiograph minus direct on
  the same 21 spines, pooled: width 0, -0.1, -0.2, -0.6, -3.5 mm (L5 significant); height +1.8,
  +1.8, +1.5, +0.2, -2.4 mm (L1-L3 and L5 significant); transverse angle 0, +2, +6, +5, +7 deg
  (L2-L5 significant); sagittal angle within about 1 deg; distance +2.5 to +8.0 mm (all
  significant). The radiographs were made with no measurable magnification, so these are not
  magnification errors (p. 548, PDF 8).
- Magnification in clinical films: AP x1.08 at their settings (correction 0.93); CT scans read at
  0.55 of true size (correction 1.82) (p. 544, PDF 4); angles uncorrected. Inference: a scale error
  of this size on CT means any CT distance read without a calibrated scale is unusable.
- CT: 4 mm contiguous cuts; cuts through the middle of the pedicle only "whenever possible" (p. 543,
  PDF 3).
- Between-subject SD (the noise a level classifier sees): width 1.2-3.7 mm, height 1.3-3.4 mm,
  transverse angle 1-8 deg, sagittal angle 1-5 deg, distance 2.6-5.8 mm (Tables I-III; the 0.6 mm
  SD of the CT distance at L2 in men comes from 2 pedicles and is not a usable spread).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indications for transpedicular fixation in the lumbar spine (p. 541, PDF 1): screws through the
  pedicle, the strongest part even of an osteoporotic vertebra, hold until fusion; techniques built
  for young scoliotic patients suit the lordotic lumbar spine poorly; most fusion patients are over
  fifty and many are severely osteoporotic, so other instrumentation holds poorly; wide laminectomy
  for stenosis can destabilise unless fused; a lateral fusion after wide decompression, especially
  with facets sacrificed, often fails without internal fixation. The authors argue that accurate
  shape and orientation data by sex and age, and a check of radiographic against direct values,
  are needed to plan screws and avoid fixation failure and injury to vital structures (p. 541,
  PDF 1).
- Sex is the paper's "major new observation": dimensions smaller in women, angles nearly equal
  (p. 547, PDF 7). Earlier norms pooled the sexes, and men's widths and heights here exceed every
  earlier series (p. 546, PDF 6; Figs. 7 and 8), so pooled norms understate a man's pedicle and
  overstate a woman's (Inference).
- Screw diameter: the pedicles of men may safely take "substantially larger" screws than earlier
  data suggested (p. 547, PDF 7; Figs. 7 and 8); whether larger screws help is unproved, but the data
  should set upper size limits for each sex, above which the pedicle may break or the screw cut out
  (pp. 547-548, PDF 7-8). The narrowest dimension to fit is the transverse width at L1-L4 (oval
  pedicles); at L5 the height is narrower (p. 547, PDF 7; Table I).
- Screw length: knowing the posterior-laminar-to-anterior-body distance prevents anterior cortical
  perforation and injury to vital structures; it is 2.5-5.0 mm shorter in women (p. 547-548,
  PDF 7-8). Inference: a length chosen from male norms is too long in an average woman by half to one
  5 mm screw-length step.
- Pitfall: images overestimate the screw path. Radiographic distances exceed direct ones by 2.5-8.0
  mm (Table II), and CT distances in the young living series (Table III) exceed the cadaver direct
  ones; planning length from images without allowance risks anterior breach (Inference from Tables
  II and III).
- Pitfall: radiographs and CT can differ significantly from the real bone even with no detectable
  magnification; at L5 the AP film understates width (p. 548, PDF 8).
- Pitfall: radiographic transverse angles exceed direct ones at L2-L5 by 2-7 deg (Table II).
- Pitfall: L5 sagittal angles must be averaged separately by direction (p. 547, PDF 7).
- Pitfall: routine CT for low-back symptoms usually omitted L1 (p. 546, PDF 6).
- Sex: dimensions are smaller in women while the angles barely differ, which the authors relate to
  men being taller and heavier on average, though they had no heights or weights to test it
  (p. 547, PDF 7).
- No stenosis thresholds, canal measures, syndromes or safe zones are given.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. On an AP radiograph, measure each lumbar pedicle oval's width and height and correct for
   magnification before comparing with norms (0.93 at 108 percent magnification in this study's
   setup) (p. 544, PDF 4).
2. Use the width/height ratio to find L5: it is the only lumbar pedicle wider than tall (Table I,
   p. 544, PDF 4; p. 547, PDF 7). Expect about 0.4-0.6 at L1-L2, 0.55-0.75 at L3, 0.7-0.9 at L4 and
   1.0-1.25 at L5 (Inference from Tables I-III); in women on radiographs the L5 ratio may be only
   about 1.0 (Table III), so do not require a large margin.
3. Do not try to tell L1 from L2 by pedicle width, transverse angle or sagittal angle: they are equal
   within 0.3 mm and 1 deg (Table I). Use other features or the sequence.
4. Expect pedicle width to step up L2 to L3 to L4 and most of all L4 to L5 (Table I); use the L4/L5
   step (about 6 mm, two SDs) as the strongest single lumbar boundary cue from pedicles.
5. Before mapping a width to a level, adjust for sex or body size: an average woman's L4 pedicle is
   as wide as a man's L3-L4, her L3 as a man's L1-L2 (Table I). Inference: without the adjustment
   a female lumbar vertebra matched to male or pooled norms will tend to be named one level too
   cranial; in the living the gap is wider still (women 24-44 percent narrower, Table III).
6. On an AP radiograph distrust the L5 pedicle width and height (3.5 mm and 2.4 mm low against bone,
   Table II; the authors link it, tentatively, to an unclear lateral pedicle border, p. 548, PDF 8).
   Inference: treat a small-looking L5 pedicle on AP as still compatible with L5.
7. On the lateral radiograph, measure the sagittal angle against the superior end-plate with both
   pedicles superimposed (p. 543, PDF 3); expect 5-7 deg cephalad at L1-L4 and do not use it to
   separate them (pp. 545-546, PDF 5-6). At L5 expect two populations, cephalad about +5 to +8 and
   caudad about -8 to -10, never averaged (p. 547, PDF 7).
8. On axial CT, cut parallel to the superior end-plate; measure the transverse angle to the
   vertebra's own median plane; expect 5-8 deg at L1-L3, 10-13 at L4 and 17-23 at L5 (Tables I and
   III). Check the slice orientation at L5-S1 before trusting an L5 angle (p. 543, PDF 3).
9. On axial CT, measure the screw-path distance from the posterior laminar cortex at the entry point
   to the anterior body cortex through the pedicle middle; expect it to rise from L1 to a maximum at
   L4 and drop at L5 (Table I; p. 545, PDF 5). Correct for film minification (1.82 here) and expect
   image values 2.5-8 mm above the bone (Table II).
10. When a mask-derived angle is compared with this paper, check the sign and reference: this
    paper's transverse angles run about 9-14 deg lower than Krag, Zindrick and Berry at L5 (Fig. 9,
    p. 547, PDF 7); a value near 30 deg at L5 is normal in those series and high here.
11. Treat a single caudad pedicle in a vertebra as a variant seen at L1-L3 (in four of the five
    cadaver vertebrae with a caudad pedicle at L1-L4 the other pedicle was cephalad, p. 545, PDF 5);
    at L5 both pedicles go the same way (p. 545, PDF 5). Inference: a caudad pedicle is not by
    itself evidence for a different level; one caudad and one cephalad pedicle in the same vertebra
    argues against L5 in this series.
12. On a CT ordered for low-back symptoms, do not assume the most cranial vertebra in the field is
    L1: such scans usually did not include L1 here (p. 546, PDF 6). Inference: count from the sacrum
    and confirm the top of the stack with a scout or a wider field.
13. Use norms from the same modality and the same sex: CT screw-path distances in young living
    patients (Table III, 46-55 mm) exceed caliper distances in elderly cadavers (Table I, 42-50 mm),
    radiographic transverse angles exceed direct ones by up to 7 deg (Table II), and the sex gap in
    width is larger in the living series than in the cadavers (Tables I and III).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13,
rib count anomalies or block vertebrae, and does not say how levels were numbered. The only
prevalence is the pedicle-orientation variant: L5 caudad sagittal angle in 12 of 83 elderly cadaver
pedicles (8/46 men, 4/37 women; both pedicles of an L5 go the same way) and 2 of 94 young living
pedicles (one patient); at L1-L4, 6 cadaver pedicles of about 283 and 6 living pedicles at L1 (three
patients) (Table I, p. 544, PDF 4; pp. 545-546, PDF 5-6). Inference: if the generator draws a
sagittal pedicle angle, draw it at L5 from a two-component mixture (caudad weight about 0.15 in the
elderly, about 0.02 in the young), and at L1-L4 from a single cephalad component with a small caudad
tail; do not let a caudad pedicle shift the level likelihood.

(b) Measures and their match to Panjabi (Inference, from the panjabi1992 note in this set):
- Olsewski "transverse diameter" = Panjabi PDW (pedicle width); "sagittal diameter" = PDH (pedicle
  height). Both appear to take the bone's outer surface (caliper here; a digitized loop around the
  pedicle in Panjabi, per the panjabi1992 note). Olsewski states "narrowest"; the Panjabi note
  says Panjabi does not state where along the pedicle the digitized loop lay. A mask extraction
  should take the narrowest section, as here.
- Values differ at L5: Panjabi PDH at L5 19.6 mm (rising) against 17.4 men / 16.2 women here; PDW at
  L5 18.6 against 21.1 men / 18.4 women here. Panjabi's L1-L2 PDW (8.6, 8.2) sits between this
  paper's men and women. Both papers have pedicle height rising at L5, while Zindrick and Berry
  (Fig. 8 here) have it falling; a generator fit on Panjabi will therefore agree with this paper's
  direct L5 height trend but not with a radiograph-derived one (Table II: 13.8 at L5).
- Olsewski "transverse angle" = Panjabi PDIs (angle to the sagittal plane, seen in the top view);
  Olsewski "sagittal angle" = Panjabi PDIt (angle to the transverse plane, side view). The names are
  swapped between the papers: map by plane of view, not by name. Panjabi's PDIs runs about 14.5 deg
  at L1 to 24.5 at L5, about 7 deg above this paper's 7 and 17-18; a generator fit on Panjabi will
  sit high against angles extracted with Olsewski's method. Panjabi's PDIt (2.5 to 5.5, drawn with the
  posterior end higher) and Olsewski's sagittal angle (5-7, cephalad = anterior end higher, Fig. 5)
  may have opposite sign conventions; check before pooling.
- The posterior-to-anterior screw-path length is not a Panjabi measure. It could be added: from a
  mask it is a single chord along the fitted pedicle axis through arch and body. It separates L4 from
  L5 (drop of about 4 mm, about one SD).
- Mask bias sources: (1) Width measured along the vertebra's x axis instead of perpendicular to the
  pedicle axis reads wider by 1/cos(angle), up to about 3 mm at L5. (2) CT partial volume and
  segmentation boundary error (order of one voxel, 0.5-1 mm) is 6-12 percent of an 8 mm L1 pedicle.
  (3) Where SPINEPS gives no pedicle subregion, the pedicle must be cut from the posterior-element
  subregion; the body/arch boundary choice moves the narrowest section. (4) Axes from a mask are 3D;
  this paper's angles are 2D projections on planes fixed by the superior end-plate and median plane.

(c) Measurement error to use: instrument resolution 0.1 mm and 1 deg (no repeatability study).
For radiograph-derived features use the Table II biases (radiograph minus bone): width 0 to -0.6 mm
L1-L4 and -3.5 mm at L5; height +1.5 to +1.8 mm at L1-L3, +0.2 at L4, -2.4 at L5; transverse angle 0
to +7 deg; distance +2.5 to +8.0 mm. Between-subject SDs are in the tables (width SD grows with
level, 1.9-2.9 at L1 to 3.4-3.7 at L5).

(d) Covariates: sex is the only measured covariate; men's linear dimensions exceed women's by about
5-23 percent in elderly cadavers and up to 44 percent (width) in young living patients, while angles
do not differ (pp. 544-547, PDF 4-7). No height, weight or body size (p. 547, PDF 7). Age is
confounded with method (elderly cadavers by caliper against young adults by radiograph and CT). No
race or ethnicity. Inference: a size factor alone may not capture the width gap, which is
proportionally larger than the height and distance gaps; let sex scale pedicle width separately.

## Gaps and conflicts

- No level-identification method, no transitional anatomy, no exclusions for anomalies in cadavers.
- No ethnicity, height or weight; the "race groups" a reader may expect are absent.
- n per cell varies without explanation; Table II's radiographic L5 distance (49.2 +/- 5.8) has no
  n printed (p. 544, PDF 4).
- Table I women's L5 caudad sagittal angle prints "9 +/- 5" with no minus sign; the text says -9
  (p. 545, PDF 5).
- Text against table, sex gap in distance: the text says 2.5 to 5.0 mm by direct measurement
  (pp. 547-548, PDF 7-8); Table I gives 2.5, 3.3, 4.2, 4.5 and 3.9 mm (Inference by subtraction).
- Abstract against tables: "5 to 20 per cent" greater in men. Inference by computation: as a share
  of the men's value, Table I's gaps run 4.4 (L2 height) to 19 percent (L1 width), which fits; as a
  share of the women's value the widths are 15-23 percent greater, which does not; and the living
  series (Table III) reaches 30 percent of the men's width. The abstract also says
  radiograph and CT angles and distances were greater "from the second to the fifth" level, but CT
  was never compared with bone on the same specimens (Part 3 is a different, younger cohort), and the
  radiographic distance was also significantly larger at L1 (Table II).
- Part 2 text: five caudad pedicles at L1-L3 lie in four vertebrae (two L3 pedicles from one woman),
  yet the text speaks of three of "the five vertebrae" with a cephalad partner (p. 545, PDF 5).
  Inference: it should read three of four vertebrae.
- The 21-spine subset is said to be chosen at random (p. 543, PDF 3), yet (Inference, from the
  matching values on p. 545 and Tables I and II) it holds all 12 caudad L5 pedicles of the 49
  spines and 5 of the 6 caudad L1-L4 pedicles. Inference by hypergeometric arithmetic: if 21 of 49
  spines were drawn at random, the chance that all six caudad L5 vertebrae fall among them is about
  0.4 percent. Either the draw was not random with respect to these pedicles, or Table II's direct
  L5 cells are not a plain subset of Table I (which the L5 transverse angle, 22 against 17-18, also
  suggests).
- The abstract implies all 49 cadaver spines were measured on radiographs; only 21 were (p. 541
  against p. 543, PDF 1 and 3).
- The methods promise pooled between-level t tests and an analysis of variance across the three
  parts (p. 544, PDF 4); no result of either is reported, so no level-to-level significance can be
  quoted from this paper.
- Algebraic sum at L5: the authors say their algebraic mean resembles the published values
  (pp. 546-547, PDF 6-7), which Fig. 10 puts near -3 deg; computed from Table I it would be about
  +2.7 (men) and +6.2 (women) deg (Inference), not obviously similar.
- The women's L5 sagittal angle counts (33 cephalad + 4 caudad = 37) give an odd total although
  both pedicles of each L5 pointed the same way (Table I; p. 545, PDF 5): one female L5 contributed
  one pedicle (Inference).
- Table II direct L5 transverse angle 22 +/- 5 in 42 pedicles against 17 +/- 7 (men) and 18 +/- 8
  (women) in Table I for the full cohort that contains them. Inference: possible only if the other
  40 pedicles averaged about 13 deg; the subset values may come from a separate measuring session.
- Table II marks the radiographic sagittal angle at L1 and L4 as significantly different, while the
  text calls the differences only slight at all levels (p. 545, PDF 5).
- The figures of the comparison (Figs. 7-11) label this study "Olsewski 1988".
- Discussion says agreement with Saillant is good except at L5, where this paper's transverse angle
  is substantially smaller (p. 546, PDF 6), but Fig. 9 shows Saillant at about 0, 2 and 5 deg at
  L1-L3 against 5-8 here (approximate reading), below this paper at every upper level.
- Against other papers in this set (Inference): Zindrick 1987 gives L5 transverse width 18.0 and
  transverse angle 29.8 with a flat lumbar screw-path depth around 50-52 mm peaking at L2-L3, while
  this paper has 21.1/18.4 mm, 17-18 deg and a peak at L4; Panjabi 1992's PDIs is about 7 deg above
  this paper's transverse angles at every level; Krag's L5 width is about 13.8-13.9 mm (Panjabi
  1992's Table 6 per the panjabi1992 note; on the pedicle axis per the krag1988 note), far below,
  while this paper's own Fig. 7 plots Krag 1986 at about 15 mm. Fig. 7 here shows the published L5
  widths ranging from about 11 (Berry) to 21 mm (this paper's men), so L5 width is strongly
  definition dependent.
- Fig. 10 plots Zindrick's L5 sagittal angle at about -3 deg (approximate reading), while the
  zindrick1987 note gives Zindrick's printed mean as -1.8 (Inference: a plotting or reading
  difference of about 1 deg).
- Panjabi's PDIs at L1 (about 14.5 deg) is roughly twice this paper's 5-7 deg, and Berry's L1 value
  in Fig. 9 here (about 8) lies between them (Inference, from the panjabi1992 note and Fig. 9).
- Elderly embalmed specimens only for the bone measurements; osteophytes and age changes at the
  pedicle base are not discussed.
