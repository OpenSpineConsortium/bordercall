Citation as printed on the first page: James F. Griffith, Junbin Huang, Sheung-Wai Law, Fan Xiao, Jason Chi Shun Leung, Defeng Wang, Lin Shi. Population reference range for developmental lumbar spinal canal size. Quantitative Imaging in Medicine and Surgery (Quant Imaging Med Surg) 2016;6(6):671-679 (Vol 6, No 6, December 2016). doi: 10.21037/qims.2016.12.17. Submitted 21 Nov 2016, accepted 16 Dec 2016. The PDF has 14 pages: journal pp. 671-679 are PDF pages 1-9, and PDF pages 10-14 are the article's own unnumbered supplement (Tables S1-S6, Figures S1-S3).

Copy read: publisher's free copy (Quant Imaging Med Surg), project files literature/synthetic_spine/griffith2016.pdf; sha256 begins 5abf05f7d813036a. Every page was read as an image; Table 1 (rotated) and Figures S1-S3 were read at 200-250 dpi.

## What the paper is

- Design: a prospective cross-sectional CT reference-range study of the developmental (mid-pedicle) size of the lumbar osseous spinal canal, L1 to L5, by sex, p. 671 (PDF 1), p. 672 (PDF 2).
- Population: 1,080 ambulatory adult patients having abdominopelvic CT at "our institution" between Feb 2014 and Jan 2015, p. 672 (PDF 2); the authors' affiliations are departments of Prince of Wales Hospital, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, p. 671 (PDF 1), and the study was approved by that university's joint ethics committee (CRE-2013.058) with written informed consent, p. 678 (PDF 8). The paper says over 99% of "the patient population" was ethnically Chinese (Inference: this may describe the hospital's patients rather than the 1,080) and that patients with non-Chinese names were not included, p. 672 (PDF 2). 540 males (mean age 50.53 +/- 16.98 years) and 540 females (50.65 +/- 17.02), ages 21 to 80; quota sampling of 90 patients per sex per 10-year age band (20-29, 30-39, ... ), recruitment of a band stopping at 90, p. 672 (PDF 2). These were patients, not healthy volunteers; the authors argue canal growth ends by 17 years so adult disease does not alter the mid-pedicle canal, p. 672 (PDF 2).
- Denominators: 1,080 patients x 5 levels = 5,400 levels measured, p. 672 (PDF 2). Every per-level table has N = 540 per sex. Supplement Table S5, for vertebral body CSA only, splits each sex into three 20-year groups of 180 (21-40, 41-60, 61-80), suppl. (PDF 14); no canal measure is tabulated by age group.
- Exclusions: skeletal disorders such as dwarfism or scoliosis (printed as "skeletal orders"); prior lumbar surgery; a childhood chronic inflammatory condition; a major lumbar morphological abnormality such as vertebral fracture or dysraphism, p. 672 (PDF 2).
- Body size: height (cm) and weight (kg) recorded before CT, p. 672 (PDF 2). Males 168.1 +/- 6.8 cm vs females 157.0 +/- 5.9 cm; 66.9 +/- 12.8 vs 55.5 +/- 10.6 kg; BMI 23.7 +/- 4.1 vs 22.5 +/- 4.0; all P < 0.0001, p. 673 (PDF 3). Older patients of both sexes were slightly shorter (age-height r -0.355 in men, -0.370 in women), older men slightly lighter (r -0.095, P 0.027) and older women of higher BMI (r 0.243), p. 673 (PDF 3), Table 1, p. 674 (PDF 4). Inference: height falls with age while canal width rises with age, so the width-age correlation is not a height effect.
- Modality and instrument: 64-slice MDCT (GE LightSpeed VCT), reconstructive resolution 0.6 mm, bone window, p. 672 (PDF 2). For each level the volume was reoriented to give a plane at right angles to the vertebral body, and the mid-pedicle axial image was found automatically, orthogonal to the vertebra's mid-sagittal plane, p. 672 (PDF 2). One operator did all measurements, p. 672 (PDF 2). Software: for the canal, a seed-growing segmentation program after rigid co-registration (of what to what is not said), then manual correction of the boundary, p. 672 (PDF 2); for the body, an attenuation threshold with manual correction, citing Yushkevich 2006 on user-guided 3D active contour segmentation (ref 8), p. 673 (PDF 3), p. 679 (PDF 9). Inference: that reference describes the ITK-SNAP tool; the paper does not name its software. The paper uses "mid-vertebral" (abstract and discussion) and "mid-pedicle" or "mid-pedicular" (methods) for the same plane, p. 671-672 (PDF 1-2), p. 678 (PDF 8). Statistics in SPSS 22: ICC for reliability, independent two-sample t tests for sex and quartile differences, Pearson correlation with age, height, weight and BMI, P < 0.05, p. 673 (PDF 3).
- Levels covered: L1, L2, L3, L4, L5 only, at the mid-pedicle level of each vertebra; nothing at disc levels, nothing thoracic or sacral, p. 672 (PDF 2).
- How levels were identified and counted: not stated anywhere in the paper. No mention of how L1 or L5 was named, of rib counting, of the sacrum, or of transitional segments.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts or cervical ribs, and does not say how lumbar levels were numbered. The only related statement is the exclusion of a "major lumbar morphological abnormality" such as vertebral fracture or dysraphism, plus dwarfism and scoliosis, p. 672 (PDF 2).

Inference: an abdominopelvic CT usually shows the lower thoracic ribs and the sacrum, so the authors probably counted from one end, but the paper does not say which. A transitional L5 or an L6 would put a transitional segment's values into the "L5" row, which has the largest and most variable canal (L5 width SD 4.25 mm in men, Table 4, p. 675 (PDF 5)). Treat the L5 row as possibly mixed.

## Measures, each as a recipe

All measures are taken on one reformatted axial CT image per vertebra: the mid-pedicle slice, perpendicular to the body and orthogonal to the mid-sagittal plane, p. 672 (PDF 2), Figure 1. All are osseous measures, with no soft tissue, ligamentum flavum or thecal sac. Means below are Tables 2-6, p. 675-676 (PDF 5-6), N = 540 per sex; SD in parentheses.

### 1. Spinal canal cross-sectional area (SC CSA), mm2

- Definition: the area of the osseous canal on the mid-pedicle axial image. It is outlined by seed-growing segmentation after rigid co-registration, then the canal boundary is corrected by hand, p. 672 (PDF 2), Figure 1A.
- Instrument: CT 0.6 mm, semi-automated software, one operator.
- Values (M / F): L1 273.7 (49.0) / 264.8 (42.5); L2 256.3 (52.1) / 252.7 (42.6); L3 247.8 (53.3) / 241.8 (43.6); L4 276.3 (64.4) / 258.1 (55.1); L5 338.3 (82.6) / 296.9 (72.4); L1-L5 278.5 (50.3) / 262.8 (42.5). Sex P: 0.002, 0.212, 0.043, < 0.0001, < 0.0001, < 0.0001. Table 2, p. 675 (PDF 5).
- Adjusted for height and weight (estimated mean, SE), M / F: L1 264.1 / 274.4 (P 0.005); L2 247.8 / 261.2 (0.0004); L3 240.9 / 248.6 (0.0498); L4 268.5 / 265.9 (0.589); L5 324.0 / 311.2 (0.040); L1-L5 269.1 / 272.3 (0.386); SE 2.3-3.9. Table S1, suppl. (PDF 10). So after adjustment women have the larger canal at L1-L3 and men only at L5, p. 673 (PDF 3).
- Smallest-quartile cut-off (25th percentile), Figure S1, suppl. (PDF 11). Left column = males and right = females by Inference, because the left/right labels match the text's male/female values (CSA 212/213 mm2 and L2 depth 13/14 mm), p. 678 (PDF 8). Males: L1 245.25, L2 221, L3 212, L4 235, L5 283.25 mm2. Females: L1 239.0, L2 225, L3 213, L4 222.25, L5 247 mm2. The discussion says a mid-vertebral CSA below 212 mm2 (men) or below 213 mm2 (women) puts a patient in the smallest 25%, without naming a level, p. 678 (PDF 8). Inference: these are the L3 labels of Figure S1, the narrowest level.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Find the mid-pedicle plane (below). In that plane take the cross-section of the whole vertebra instance mask and fill its holes; canal area = filled area minus bone area, keeping only the enclosed component posterior to the body. If the segmenter gives a canal or thecal-sac class, do not use it: Griffith's area is the osseous ring, which is larger than the dural sac. An unclosed ring (a defect in the lamina, or a pedicle cut by the slice) has no enclosed hole; close it with a chord between the ends.
- From a radiograph alone: not measurable. Inference: estimate area as k x depth x width, where depth comes from the lateral view and width (interpedicular distance) from the AP view, with a level-specific k. The ratio of the mean CSA to (mean depth x mean width), computed from Tables 2-4, is M 0.85, 0.83, 0.80, 0.73, 0.58 and F 0.86, 0.84, 0.81, 0.75, 0.60 for L1 to L5 (an ellipse would be 0.785). Inference: the canal outline grows less elliptical and more triangular caudally, most of all at L5.
- Across levels: smallest at L3 in both sexes, larger above and below. The L3 mean was about 9% below L1 and about 23% below L5, p. 673 (PDF 3), Table 2. Largest at L5. Inference (adjacent-level separation, mean difference / pooled between-subject SD, Table 2): men L1/L2 0.34, L2/L3 0.16, L3/L4 0.48, L4/L5 0.84; women 0.28, 0.25, 0.33, 0.60. On its own, CSA barely separates L1-L2-L3; it separates L4 from L5 best.
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger at every level but L2 before adjustment, p. 673 (PDF 3). No detectable change with age (M r = 0.071, P 0.101; F r = -0.040, P 0.349), Table 1, p. 674 (PDF 4). Correlation with height M r 0.186, F r 0.298 (both P < 0.001), Table 1. Correlation with weight only in women (r 0.092, P 0.033); none with BMI, Table 1. Inference from r x SD_y / SD_x: about +1.4 mm2 per cm of height in men and +2.1 mm2 per cm in women (L1-L5 mean). Population: a Lausanne study found canal CSA 8-16% larger in Swiss subjects than here, p. 678 (PDF 8).
- Error: see "Measurement error and reliability".

### 2. Spinal canal depth (anteroposterior diameter), mm

- Definition: on the mid-pedicle axial image, from the vertebral body's posterior margin back to the neural arch cortex where the spinous process begins (its base), p. 673 (PDF 3), Figure 1B (the sagittal arm of the cross). Inference from Figure 1B: measured in the midline.
- Values (M / F): L1 15.46 (2.13) / 15.58 (2.02); L2 14.58 (2.36) / 14.87 (1.97); L3 14.04 (2.45) / 14.18 (2.08); L4 15.34 (2.99) / 15.03 (2.75); L5 19.67 (3.76) / 18.12 (3.28); L1-L5 15.82 (1.96) / 15.55 (1.72). Sex P: 0.363, 0.033, 0.300, 0.077, < 0.0001, 0.019. Table 3, p. 675 (PDF 5).
- Adjusted (M / F, SE 0.09-0.18): L1 15.02 / 16.02 (P < 0.0001); L2 14.24 / 15.21 (< 0.0001); L3 13.81 / 14.40 (0.001); L4 15.06 / 15.31 (0.294); L5 19.38 / 18.41 (0.001); L1-L5 15.50 / 15.87 (0.013). Table S2, suppl. (PDF 10). Adjusted for height and weight, women's canals are deeper at L1-L3 and men's at L5, p. 673 (PDF 3); the adjusted L1-L5 mean is also deeper in women (P 0.013), the reverse of the unadjusted mean, Table S2.
- Smallest-quartile cut-offs, Figure S2, suppl. (PDF 12). Males: L1 14, L2 13, L3 12.4, L4 13.2, L5 17.2 mm. Females: L1 14, L2 14, L3 13, L4 13.2, L5 15.9 mm. The text: depth below 13 mm (men) or below 14 mm (women) at L2 is the smallest quartile, p. 678 (PDF 8).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the mid-pedicle plane, along the vertebra's own AP axis through the midline (the axis in the mid-sagittal plane perpendicular to the craniocaudal axis): the distance from the posterior surface of the body (corpus subregion) to the anterior (inner) surface of the arch where the laminae join at the spinous process base. Do not take it at the disc or at the endplate.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference (the paper uses CT only). The lateral radiograph gives it at mid-pedicle height: from the posterior body line to the spinolaminar line. Correct for magnification (source-to-image distance and object depth); the paper gives no magnification factor. On AP it cannot be measured.
- Across levels: smallest at L3, larger above and below, jumping at L5, p. 673 (PDF 3), p. 675 (PDF 5). Inference (adjacent separation, Table 3): men L1/L2 0.39, L2/L3 0.22, L3/L4 0.48, L4/L5 1.27; women 0.36, 0.34, 0.35, 1.02. Depth separates L5 from L4 well and the upper levels poorly.
- Sex, age, size, population: unadjusted, the sexes differ little: women are deeper at L2 (14.87 vs 14.58, P 0.033), men at L5 (P < 0.0001) and for the L1-L5 mean (P 0.019); L1, L3 and L4 do not differ, Table 3, p. 675 (PDF 5). Age: none in men (r 0.008, P 0.860); a slight fall in women (r -0.102, P 0.018), Table 1, p. 674 (PDF 4), p. 675 (PDF 5). Height r: M 0.163, F 0.255 (P < 0.001). Inference: about +0.05 mm per cm of height in men and +0.07 mm per cm in women. Depth correlates with CSA at r 0.871 (M) and 0.865 (F), more strongly than width does (0.769, 0.761), Table 1. The authors state, citing Papp 1994 and Verbiest 1955 (refs 3, 20), that depth rather than width is the main contributor to developmental CSA, p. 676 (PDF 6). Development: AP canal growth is complete by about 5 years, so depth is fixed early, p. 671 (PDF 1).
- Error: see below. One CT pixel (0.6 mm) is about 4% of a 14 mm depth (Inference).

### 3. Spinal canal width (transverse diameter, interpedicular distance), mm

- Definition: between the inner margins of the two pedicles, on the mid-pedicle axial image, p. 673 (PDF 3), Figure 1B (the transverse arm of the cross).
- Values (M / F): L1 20.87 (2.50) / 19.83 (2.16); L2 21.19 (2.62) / 20.13 (2.28); L3 22.13 (2.70) / 20.96 (2.35); L4 24.55 (3.24) / 22.95 (2.90); L5 29.55 (4.25) / 27.41 (3.69); L1-L5 23.66 (2.59) / 22.26 (2.25). All P < 0.0001. Table 4, p. 675 (PDF 5).
- Adjusted (M / F, SE 0.12-0.20): L1 20.63 / 20.07; L2 20.99 / 20.34; L3 21.89 / 21.20; L4 24.28 / 23.21; L5 29.09 / 27.87; L1-L5 23.38 / 22.54. All P <= 0.003. Table S3, suppl. (PDF 10). Men are wider at every level even after adjustment, p. 675 (PDF 5).
- Smallest-quartile cut-offs, Figure S3, suppl. (PDF 13). Males: L1 19.2, L2 20, L3 20.6, L4 22.5, L5 27 mm. Females: L1 18, L2 19, L3 19.3, L4 21, L5 25 mm.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the mid-pedicle plane, the shortest left-right distance between the medial surfaces of the two pedicles, along the vertebra's own transverse axis. Inference, from memory of SPINEPS's label set: it has no separate pedicle subregion; the pedicle lies in the arch label, between the body and the articular processes. Development: transverse canal width keeps growing until 15-17 years, p. 671 (PDF 1).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. The AP radiograph gives the interpedicular distance between the medial cortices of the pedicle ovals, corrected for magnification. On the lateral it cannot be measured.
- Across levels: rises steadily from L1 to L5, unlike CSA and depth, p. 675 (PDF 5), p. 676 (PDF 6). Inference (adjacent separation, Table 4): men L1/L2 0.12, L2/L3 0.35, L3/L4 0.81, L4/L5 1.32; women 0.14, 0.36, 0.75, 1.34. Width is the best single canal measure for telling L4 from L5 and L3 from L4, and is useless for L1 against L2. Inference: the width/depth ratio rises from L1 (M 1.35, F 1.27) to about 1.5-1.6 at L3-L5.
- Sex, age, size, population: men wider at all levels. Height r: M 0.126 (P 0.003), F 0.143 (P < 0.001). Weight: F r 0.102 (P 0.018) only. Age: width correlates positively with age in both sexes (M r 0.281, F r 0.267, both P < 0.001), Table 1, p. 674 (PDF 4). The text never mentions this; see Gaps. Inference: r x SD_y / SD_x gives about +0.04 mm per year in men and +0.035 mm per year in women, roughly 2 to 2.6 mm wider at 80 than at 21.
- Error: see below.

### 4. Vertebral body cross-sectional area (VB CSA), mm2

- Definition: the area of the vertebral body on the same mid-pedicle axial image. It is outlined semi-automatically by an attenuation threshold between body and surrounding soft tissue (ref 8), then corrected by hand, with the pedicle bases demarcated so the pedicles are excluded, p. 673 (PDF 3), Figure 1C.
- Values (M / F): L1 1,201.0 (172.3) / 961.7 (138.1); L2 1,273.4 (179.3) / 1,030.0 (145.0); L3 1,399.3 (194.5) / 1,144.6 (159.5); L4 1,511.5 (206.7) / 1,251.9 (173.9); L5 1,711.4 (240.9) / 1,426.5 (206.4); L1-L5 1,419.3 (185.5) / 1,162.9 (153.7). All P < 0.0001. Table 5, p. 675 (PDF 5).
- Adjusted (M / F, SE 7.6-10.7): L1 1,165.0 / 997.7; L2 1,231.6 / 1,071.7; L3 1,350.3 / 1,193.5; L4 1,456.7 / 1,306.7; L5 1,646.4 / 1,491.4; L1-L5 1,370.0 / 1,212.2. All P < 0.0001. Table S4, suppl. (PDF 14).
- By age (Table S5, suppl. (PDF 14), N = 180 per sex per group), L1-L5 mean (SD):
  - Men: 1,378.8 (190.5) at 21-40, 1,431.1 (174.1) at 41-60, 1,448.0 (185.6) at 61-80.
  - Women: 1,129.8 (146.8), 1,146.0 (146.2), 1,213.0 (155.9).
  - Per level, 21-40 / 41-60 / 61-80:
    - Men L1 1,159.5 / 1,203.1 / 1,240.4; L2 1,234.1 / 1,279.5 / 1,306.7; L3 1,358.0 / 1,406.0 / 1,433.8; L4 1,476.5 / 1,523.6 / 1,534.5; L5 1,666.0 / 1,743.4 / 1,724.7.
    - Women L1 926.4 / 957.0 / 1,001.8; L2 993.8 / 1,020.5 / 1,075.5; L3 1,113.7 / 1,127.1 / 1,192.9; L4 1,217.3 / 1,228.6 / 1,309.7; L5 1,397.8 / 1,396.6 / 1,485.0.
  - Table S5's P values (all < 0.0001) compare men with women within each age group; no test across age groups is printed. Inference from the means: in women the rise comes mostly after 60 (L5 flat from 21-40 to 41-60, then +88 mm2); in men it is steadier and male L5 is highest at 41-60 (1,743.4) and slightly lower at 61-80 (1,724.7).
- Quartiles (Table 7, p. 677 (PDF 7)). Men, largest vs smallest 25%, L1 to L5: 1,413.1 vs 1,007.6; 1,493.9 vs 1,062.6; 1,635.8 vs 1,172.3; 1,764.9 vs 1,271.7; 2,009.6 vs 1,428.6. Women: 1,137.5 vs 797.4; 1,214.4 vs 852.4; 1,347.3 vs 952.2; 1,471.1 vs 1,043.6; 1,690.2 vs 1,178.3. The gap is about 28-30% at every level.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. The corpus subregion's cross-sectional area in the mid-pedicle plane, cut at the pedicle bases. This is a mid-body (waist) area, not an endplate area; it will be smaller than an endplate area from Panjabi's tables.
- From a radiograph alone: not measurable. Inference: approximate it as an ellipse from AP body width and lateral mid-body depth.

It rises with age, almost twice as fast in women (age r M 0.165, F 0.294, both P < 0.001), Table 1, p. 676 (PDF 6). The authors relate this to the known enlargement of vertebral CSA with low bone density, a compensatory mechanism (refs 24, 25), p. 678 (PDF 8). Correlations: height M 0.331, F 0.264; weight M 0.255, F 0.269; BMI M 0.118 (P 0.006), F 0.177 (all others P < 0.001), Table 1. Inference: about +9.0 mm2 per cm of height in men and +6.9 in women; about +1.8 mm2 per year in men and +2.7 in women.

### 5. Ratio SC CSA / VB CSA

- Definition: canal CSA divided by body CSA, both at the mid-pedicle level, p. 673 (PDF 3).
- Values (M / F): L1 0.23 / 0.28; L2 0.20 / 0.25; L3 0.18 / 0.21; L4 0.18 / 0.21; L5 0.20 / 0.21; L1-L5 0.20 / 0.23. All P <= 0.0002. Table 6, p. 676 (PDF 6). Adjusted: L1 0.23 / 0.28; L2 0.21 / 0.25; L3 0.18 / 0.21; L4 0.19 / 0.21; L5 0.20 / 0.21; L1-L5 0.20 / 0.23. Table S6, suppl. (PDF 14).
- The canal is about one-fifth of the body area, p. 676 (PDF 6). Relative to the body, women's canals are larger, the reverse of the absolute comparison, also after adjustment, p. 676 (PDF 6).
- Across levels: highest at L1 in both sexes; in men lowest at L3-L4 (0.18) with a small rise at L5 (0.20); in women flat at 0.21 from L3 to L5, Table 6, p. 676 (PDF 6).
- Covariates: the ratio falls with age in women only (r -0.249, P < 0.001; men r -0.031, P 0.474), because women's VB CSA grows with age, p. 676 (PDF 6), Table 1. It falls slightly with weight (M -0.108, F -0.138) and BMI (M -0.098, F -0.174), Table 1.
- From a mask: Inference. The ratio of measures 1 and 4; it is scale-free, so it needs no magnification correction and no patient size factor.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- Mid-pedicle levels L1-L5 only; nothing is said about T12/L1 or L5/S1 as junctions, nor about any disc level.
- L1: largest CSA of the upper three levels, and the highest canal/body ratio (M 0.23, F 0.28), Tables 2 and 6. Cut-offs (M/F): CSA 245.25 / 239.0 mm2, depth 14 / 14 mm, width 19.2 / 18 mm, suppl. Figs S1-S3 (PDF 11-13).
- L2: the only level where unadjusted CSA does not differ by sex (P 0.212), Table 2, and the only level where unadjusted depth is larger in women (P 0.033), Table 3, p. 675 (PDF 5). The text's quoted depth cut-off is at L2 (13 mm M, 14 mm F), p. 678 (PDF 8). Width barely changes from L1 to L2 (+0.3 mm in both sexes, Table 4). Literature cut-offs for a stenotic mid-vertebral AP diameter run from below 10 to below 14 mm; below 11 mm at L2 and L3 is the most widely accepted (Mamisch 2012 Delphi, ref 23), p. 676-678 (PDF 6-8).
- L3: the narrowest level for CSA and depth in both sexes, p. 673 (PDF 3), p. 675 (PDF 5). CSA cut-off 212 mm2 (M), 213 mm2 (F) by Figure S1, suppl. (PDF 11); the discussion quotes these two values without a level, p. 678 (PDF 8). The Lausanne study also found the smallest CSA at L3, p. 678 (PDF 8).
- L4: CSA and depth rise again from their L3 minimum, and width continues its rise, with the steps from L3 to L4 larger than those above (Tables 2-4). Adjusted CSA and depth do not differ by sex (P 0.589, 0.294), Tables S1, S2, suppl. (PDF 10); unadjusted depth does not either (P 0.077), Table 3.
- L5: the largest values of every absolute measure (CSA, depth, width, VB CSA; not the ratio, which is highest at L1), Tables 2-6. Here men exceed women even after adjustment for CSA, depth and width (Tables S1-S3). The SDs are largest at L5 (width 4.25 / 3.69 mm), Table 4. The quartile gap for CSA is widest at L5 (-46.9% M, -45.4% F), Table 7. Inference: the low CSA/(depth x width) ratio at L5 (0.58-0.60) reflects the triangular (trefoil) canal outline usual at L5; the paper does not describe canal shape.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Protocol: on 20 randomly selected subjects (100 axial levels), one reader chose the image and measured CSA, depth and width at L1-L5 twice, 1 week apart, blinded to the first result. A second reader independently chose the image and measured the same by hand. The second computerised reading was compared with the manual readings, p. 673 (PDF 3).
- Intra-operator ICC of the computerised measures by level: L1 0.85, L2 0.87, L3 0.91, L4 0.85, L5 0.96, p. 676 (PDF 6).
- Computerised vs manual agreement (ICC) by level: L1 0.80, L2 0.89, L3 0.88, L4 0.83, L5 0.95, p. 676 (PDF 6).
- Not given: the ICC per measure (CSA vs depth vs width; it is reported only by level, apparently pooled), the ICC form (single or average measures, consistency or absolute agreement), absolute error in mm or mm2, inter-observer agreement of the computerised method, the sex mix of the 20 subjects, and the reliability of VB CSA and of the ratio.
- Design limit: the computerised-versus-manual comparison used a second reader who also chose the image, so reader and method differences are mixed in its ICC (0.80-0.95), p. 673 (PDF 3). Inference: it is closer to an inter-observer figure than to a pure method comparison.
- Instrument: CT reconstruction 0.6 mm, p. 672 (PDF 2). Inference: a reformatted CT plane carries no projection magnification.
- Inference: SEM = SD x sqrt(1 - ICC). With depth SD about 2-3 mm and ICC 0.85-0.96, SEM is about 0.4-1.2 mm. With CSA SD about 45-80 mm2, SEM is about 10-30 mm2.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Why it matters: lumbar canal stenosis is the most common reason for lumbar surgery in patients older than 65 (Katz 2008, ref 1), p. 671 (PDF 1). Symptomatic stenosis = developmental canal size plus acquired bony and soft-tissue encroachment, mostly degenerative, p. 676 (PDF 6). Developmental size bears on nerve root compression, stenosis and the need for decompression (refs 9, 10); the larger the developmental canal, the lower the risk of neurological compromise (ref 2), p. 676 (PDF 6). The reference range is offered so that the degree of developmental stenosis can be judged objectively in each patient, p. 671 (PDF 1), p. 678 (PDF 8).
- Where to measure: take developmental size at the mid-pedicle level, away from the acquired narrowing at the disc-vertebral level, p. 671 (PDF 1).
- Development: AP canal growth is complete by about 5 years; transverse width keeps growing to 15-17 years; all canal growth is complete by 17 (refs 3, 4), p. 671 (PDF 1). Prenatal factors (gestational age, placenta size, nutrition, birth weight) and maternal factors (age, parity, socioeconomic class, smoking) may shape it (refs 5-7, among them Clark, Panjabi and Wetzel 1985 on infant malnutrition), p. 671 (PDF 1).
- Conditions that change an adult canal: apart from uncommon diseases such as dural ectasia, intraspinal tumours and intradural cysts, no disease or metabolic condition is known to alter the developmental canal (citing ref 2), p. 672 (PDF 2). Inference: these act by widening the canal, so an outlier above the range is the pitfall; the paper does not say which way they act. Excluded as distorting: dwarfism, scoliosis, prior surgery, childhood chronic inflammatory disease, vertebral fracture, dysraphism, p. 672 (PDF 2).
- Thresholds:
  - There is no accepted definition of a developmentally narrow canal (ref 21), p. 676 (PDF 6). Here the smallest quartile is the arbitrary cut-off, p. 673 (PDF 3), p. 678 (PDF 8).
  - A US-based study by Peter F. Ullrich Jr et al. proposed a CSA below 145 mm2 as "developmental stenosis"; the authors call this too low for their population, whose smallest-quartile line is 212 mm2 (men) and 213 mm2 (women), p. 678 (PDF 8) (Inference: the L3 values of Figure S1). The text cites Ullrich by name, with no reference number, and he is not in the reference list.
  - AP diameter: cut-offs from below 10 to below 14 mm have been used for mid-vertebral stenosis (refs 21-23), below 11 mm at L2 and L3 the most widely accepted (ref 23), p. 676-678 (PDF 6-8). The authors' smallest-quartile AP line at L2 is 13 mm (men) and 14 mm (women), with other values at other levels, p. 678 (PDF 8).
- Sex: absolute canals are larger in men, but relative to the body women's are larger. Normalising by body size reverses the sex comparison, p. 676 (PDF 6), p. 678 (PDF 8).
- Body size: taller people have slightly larger CSA and depth, p. 678 (PDF 8). The correlations with height are weak (r 0.126-0.298 for the canal measures, 0.264-0.331 for VB CSA), Table 1, p. 674 (PDF 4).
- Age: the discussion says canal CSA and depth do not change with age; VB CSA grows in old age, more in women (low bone density goes with a larger vertebral CSA, probably as compensation for weaker bone, refs 24, 25), which lowers the canal/body ratio in older women, p. 678 (PDF 8). The results, though, report a slight fall in depth with age in women (r -0.102, P 0.018), p. 675 (PDF 5), Table 1 (see Gaps).
- Population: each region, race and sex needs its own reference range, p. 672 (PDF 2), p. 676 (PDF 6). Swiss canals are 8-16% larger (Schizas 2014, ref 2); unlike the Lausanne study, this study found no increase in canal CSA with age, p. 678 (PDF 8). Earlier studies agree that nearly every canal dimension is smaller in women (refs 16-19, Eisenstein 1977 among them), p. 676 (PDF 6).
- Practical use: the template suits other populations' abdominopelvic CTs, of which over 100 million are done yearly (ref 15), and automated canal measurement on routine MRI is coming (refs 11-14), p. 676 (PDF 6).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. For each lumbar instance, define the mid-pedicle plane: reorient the volume so the axial plane is at right angles to the vertebral body and orthogonal to its mid-sagittal plane, then take the axial slice through the mid-pedicles, p. 672 (PDF 2). Inference: "mid-pedicle" is not defined further; take the slice halfway through the pedicles' craniocaudal extent. Never measure developmental canal size at the disc level, where acquired narrowing sits, p. 671 (PDF 1).
2. In that plane, measure the osseous canal area as the enclosed hole of the vertebral ring, not a soft-tissue or dural-sac label, p. 672 (PDF 2), Figure 1A.
3. Measure depth in the midline, from the posterior body wall to the inner cortex of the arch at the spinous process base. Measure width between the medial pedicle cortices, p. 673 (PDF 3), Figure 1B.
4. Measure VB CSA in the same plane and cut the pedicles off at their bases, p. 673 (PDF 3), Figure 1C.
5. For a level label, use width and VB CSA as monotone cranio-caudal gradients (both rise from L1 to L5), Tables 4-5, p. 675 (PDF 5). Use the CSA and depth minimum at L3 and the jump at L5 as landmarks, Tables 2-3. Inference: compare each instance with its neighbours, not with the population table alone, since between-subject SDs swamp adjacent-level differences in L1-L3.
6. Treat the largest step of the lumbar series, a rise in mean width of about 4.5-5 mm (F +4.46, M +5.00), in depth of about 3-4.5 mm (F +3.09, M +4.33), in CSA of about 40-60 mm2 (F +38.8, M +62.0) and in VB CSA of about 175-200 mm2 (F +174.6, M +199.9), between two consecutive instances as the L4-to-L5 step, Tables 2-5, p. 675 (PDF 5). Inference: these are differences of population means, not within-patient steps; an unusually large step can also mean a transitional or extra segment; verify with the sacrum and the ribs, which this paper does not address.
7. To judge a canal developmentally small, compare it with the sex- and level-specific smallest-quartile cut-off (suppl. Figs S1-S3, PDF 11-13; left column male by Inference). Example: L2 depth below 13 mm (M) or 14 mm (F), p. 678 (PDF 8); L3 CSA below 212 mm2 (M) or 213 mm2 (F), Figure S1 (PDF 11) and p. 678 (PDF 8). Do not use Ullrich's 145 mm2 for this population, p. 678 (PDF 8).
8. Inference (the paper names the conditions but not their effect): flag a canal well above the reference range as a possible dural ectasia, intraspinal tumour or intradural cyst, the uncommon diseases the paper says can alter the developmental canal, p. 672 (PDF 2).
9. On a lateral radiograph, use only depth, at mid-pedicle height, and correct it for magnification. On an AP radiograph, use only width (interpedicular distance). Inference; the paper uses CT only.
10. When body size must be removed, use the canal/body CSA ratio, but expect it to fall with age in women (r -0.249), as VB CSA grows with age and lower bone density, Table 1, p. 674 (PDF 4), p. 676 (PDF 6), p. 678 (PDF 8). Expect the sex comparison to reverse: relative to the body, women's canals are larger, p. 676 (PDF 6).
11. Apply these numbers only to adult Chinese patients aged 21-80. For other populations expect differences (Swiss CSA 8-16% larger), p. 678 (PDF 8). Inference: do not apply them below 17 years, when transverse canal growth may be incomplete, p. 671 (PDF 1).
12. Within one patient, expect canal width to be nearly equal at L1 and L2 (mean step +0.3 mm) and CSA and depth to differ little among L1-L3 (Tables 2-4, p. 675 (PDF 5)); do not name L1, L2 or L3 from canal size alone. Inference: VB CSA, which rises by about 70-125 mm2 per level in the mean, separates those levels better (Table 5).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13 or rib anomalies and does not say how levels were counted.

(b) Measures, compared with the generator and a CT mask:
- Canal depth (mid-pedicle, midline, posterior body to arch) and canal width (interpedicular, mid-pedicle) correspond to the generator's canal depth (SCD) and canal width (SCW). Inference, from this set's panjabi1992 note (12 dry lumbar spines, 3D digitizer): Panjabi's SCD by level is 19.0, 18.2, 17.5, 18.6, 19.7 mm, SCW 23.7 to 27.1 mm and canal area (SCA) 320, 281, 280, 290, 330 mm2, L1 to L5. Against Griffith's men, Panjabi's depth runs about 3.3-3.6 mm larger at L1-L4 and equal at L5, his width about 2.8 mm larger at L1 and 2.5 mm smaller at L5, and his area 25-46 mm2 larger at L1-L3, 14 mm2 larger at L4 and 8 mm2 smaller at L5. Both find the depth minimum at L3; Panjabi's area is flat from L2 to L4. Population (Hong Kong Chinese patients against 12 cadaveric spines, 8 men and 4 women), technique (CT voxel boundary against digitized points) and definition (where the width chord lies) all differ; pick one definition for both generator and extractor and do not mix the two tables at one level.
- Canal CSA is in Panjabi's lumbar set (SCA) but VB CSA at mid-pedicle is not (Inference, from the panjabi1992 note, whose body areas are end-plate areas). Mid-pedicle VB CSA could be added as a derived feature, with Griffith's L1-L5 means and SDs by sex as priors.
- Mid-body VB CSA is a waist area, smaller than an endplate area. Do not compare it with endplate width x depth without a correction (Inference).
- Inference: the shape factor CSA/(depth x width) falls from about 0.85 at L1 to about 0.59 at L5 (ratios of the Table 2-4 means). It is a scale-free level feature that needs no size factor.
- The canal/body CSA ratio is also scale-free (Table 6), but it is level-flat from L2 to L5 (0.18-0.21), so it is weak for labelling.

(c) Measurement error: intra-operator ICC 0.85-0.96, computerised vs manual 0.80-0.95, by level (p. 676 (PDF 6)); voxel 0.6 mm. Inference: SEM about 0.4-1.2 mm for depth and width and about 10-30 mm2 for CSA. A mask extractor at 1 mm voxels will add at least half a voxel of boundary error on each side.

(d) Covariates:
- Sex: separate tables (above). Men are larger in absolute width and VB CSA everywhere. Adjusted for height and weight, women are deeper and larger at L1-L3.
- Height (Inference, from r x SD ratio): canal CSA +1.4 (M) and +2.1 (F) mm2 per cm; depth +0.05 / +0.07 mm per cm; width +0.05 / +0.055 mm per cm; VB CSA +9.0 / +6.9 mm2 per cm. Canal size is only weakly tied to stature (r <= 0.30), so a single patient size factor should load more on VB CSA than on canal size.
- Age: canal CSA flat; depth flat in men and falling slightly in women (r -0.102); width rises (r about 0.27-0.28, Inference about +0.04 mm per year); VB CSA rises (about +1.8 M, +2.7 F mm2 per year, Inference).
- Population: Hong Kong Chinese, over 99%. Swiss CSA 8-16% larger, p. 678 (PDF 8).
- Correlations useful for a joint (multivariate) prior, Table 1:
  - Depth-width: r 0.610 (M), 0.553 (F).
  - Canal CSA-VB CSA: 0.293 (M), 0.250 (F).
  - Width-VB CSA: 0.439 (M), 0.372 (F).
  - Depth-VB CSA: 0.287 (M), 0.196 (F).
  - These are correlations of each subject's L1-L5 means, not within-level correlations.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Level identification and transitional anatomy: not described at all (see above). The L5 row may hold transitional segments.
- Canal width correlates with age (M r 0.281, F r 0.267, P < 0.001, Table 1, p. 674 (PDF 4)), but the text never mentions it. This sits awkwardly with the premise that the developmental canal is fixed after 17 years. Inference: it could be a birth-cohort (secular) effect, as the Lausanne group described narrowing canals in later-born people (ref 2, "a narrowing epidemic?"), or age-related remodelling of the medial pedicle cortex. Depth also falls slightly with age in women (r -0.102), which the text does mention, p. 675 (PDF 5).
- Figure S1 (suppl., PDF 11): the male L5 histogram looks bar for bar identical to the male L4 histogram, centred near 270 mm2, and its marker line sits near 235 mm2. Yet it is labelled 283.25 mm2, and Table 2 gives a male L5 mean of 338.3 (SD 82.6). The label agrees with the table (Inference: mean - 0.674 SD = 282.6), so the drawn male L5 histogram is probably a copy of the L4 panel. Trust the label and the table.
- Figures S1-S3 do not say which column is male. Left = male is inferred from the text's L3 CSA and L2 depth quotations, p. 678 (PDF 8).
- Some cut-off labels look rounded from the normal approximation (Inference). Female L2 depth is labelled 14 mm where mean - 0.674 SD = 13.5. Male L2 width is labelled 20 mm against 19.4. Male L1 CSA is labelled 245.25 against 240.6. They may be empirical percentiles.
- The methods say histograms were drawn to check that each measure was normally distributed and that the smallest-25% cut-off was chosen arbitrarily, p. 673 (PDF 3); they do not say whether the labelled cut-off is the empirical 25th percentile or a value from the fitted normal curve. Inference: labels such as 245.25, 222.25 and 283.25 look like interpolated empirical percentiles.
- The abstract says canal CSA did not change with weight, but the results report a very slight rise with weight in women (r 0.092, P 0.033), p. 671 (PDF 1), p. 673 (PDF 3), Table 1. The abstract also attaches "(P<0.05)" to this null finding, p. 671 (PDF 1).
- The discussion says depth did not change with age, p. 678 (PDF 8), while the results and Table 1 report a slight fall in women (r -0.102, P 0.018), p. 675 (PDF 5), p. 674 (PDF 4).
- The adjusted tables (S1-S4, S6, "estimated mean (SE)") are said to be adjusted for height and weight, but the statistical methods name only ICC, t tests and Pearson correlation, p. 673 (PDF 3); the adjustment model is not described. Inference: an analysis of covariance.
- Table S5 tests only men against women within each age group; it gives no test of the age trend in VB CSA, which rests on Table 1's correlations, suppl. (PDF 14).
- The claim that over 100 million abdominopelvic CTs are done yearly around the world cites a projection for the United States in 2007 (ref 15), p. 676 (PDF 6), p. 679 (PDF 9).
- Reliability is reported per level, not per measure. There is no absolute error and no reliability for VB CSA, p. 676 (PDF 6).
- Ullrich is cited by name with no reference number and is absent from the reference list, p. 678 (PDF 8).
- The abstract gives P < 0.0001 for the height effect, while Table 1 prints P < 0.001, p. 671 (PDF 1).
- The quartile comparison is said to be height-matched (p. 673) and adjusted for height and weight (p. 676); the paper does not say how.
- The exclusion says "skeletal orders", a typo for disorders, p. 672 (PDF 2).
- Inference, from this set's eisenstein1977 note: Eisenstein 1977 (ref 19) measured South African dry skeletons with a vernier caliper (with a small radiographic calibration substudy), not on radiographs. His canal AP diameter is the least midline AP distance, near the canal's cranial end, and his interpedicular distance is the greatest gap between the medial pedicle surfaces; Griffith's depth and width are taken in one mid-pedicle CT slice, so Eisenstein's AP will tend to read smaller and his width larger than Griffith's definitions at the same canal. Eisenstein's AP minimum lies at L2-L4 with L1 and L5 flared, and his interpedicular distance is nearly flat L1-L4 and wider at L5, matching Griffith's patterns. His dry-bone lower limits (AP 13 mm, interpedicular 18 mm) are close to Griffith's smallest-quartile lines at L1-L3 (depth 12.4-14 mm, width 18-20.6 mm, Figures S2-S3). Do not pool the two without converting definitions.
