## What the paper is

- Design: an osteological (dry bone) morphometric study of the lumbar pedicles with demographic covariates, aimed at screw caliber selection. p. 1639 (PDF 1), abstract.
- Population: the Hamann-Todd Osteological Collection in Cleveland, Ohio (more than 3300 treated and dried skeletons of people who died in Cleveland between 1893 and 1938, with height and weight documented at the time of death, sex and ethnic origin; the authors call it the largest modern collection of documented human skeletons). 503 skeletons were chosen at random, "in no particular order"; 404 male and 99 female; 281 Caucasian and 222 African American; age 17 to 93 years, mean 47; mean height 170 cm; mean weight 56 kg. p. 1640 (PDF 2), Subjects, Table 1. The abstract calls them 503 American human cadavers (p. 1639, PDF 1).
- Table 1 (p. 1640, PDF 2), age band: n (female/male; Caucasian/African American): 15-24: 35 (12/23; 3/32); 25-34: 73 (21/52; 24/49); 35-44: 114 (25/89; 57/57); 45-54: 129 (18/111; 78/51); 55-64: 77 (7/70; 59/18); 65-74: 45 (8/37; 35/10); 75-84: 24 (4/20; 22/2); 85-94: 6 (4/2; 3/3). Sex is not crossed with race anywhere.
- Inference: race is confounded with age (32 of the 35 people aged 15 to 24 are African American, 2 of the 24 aged 75 to 84), and the sample is 80% male (404/503).
- Inference: mean weight 56 kg at a mean height of 170 cm is a BMI near 19.4; weight at death in this collection likely reflects terminal illness, so weight is a weak proxy for living body size.
- Denominators: L1 to L5 of each of 503 skeletons. Inference: 2,515 vertebrae and 5,030 pedicles. Left and right pedicles were each measured and then averaged, so each level's analysis has n = 503. p. 1640 (PDF 2), Results; Figure 1 caption p. 1641 (PDF 3).
- Inclusion and exclusion: specimens with congenital diseases such as scoliosis were excluded (how many is not given); past medical and surgical history was largely unavailable. p. 1640 (PDF 2).
- Instrument: digital caliper reading to 0.01 mm, one examiner (C.C.Y.), all vertebrae of one skeleton measured before the next. p. 1640 (PDF 2).
- Levels covered: L1 to L5 only. The same group described upper thoracic (T1 to T6) and lower thoracic (T7 to T12) pedicle morphometry in two earlier papers (Spine 2014;39:E1201-9, whose title names 503 cadaveric specimens, and Spine 2015;40:E323-31). p. 1640 (PDF 2); refs 23 and 24, p. 1646 (PDF 8). Inference: probably the same 503 Hamann-Todd skeletons, which would give T1 to L5 on one cohort, but this paper does not say so.
- How levels were identified and counted: not stated.
- Statistics: means and SD for the whole sample and for subgroups by age, sex, race, height and weight; a multiple regression of each dimension on all five demographic variables; one-way ANOVA and Student t tests; chi-square tests for the proportions of narrow pedicles; P < 0.05; Statgraphics Centurion XVI version 16.2. p. 1640 (PDF 2); Figure 3 caption p. 1643 (PDF 5).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts, cervical ribs or block vertebrae, and does not say how L1 and L5 were identified or whether any skeleton had four or six lumbar-type vertebrae. p. 1640 (PDF 2).
- The only anomaly-related rule is the exclusion of congenital diseases such as scoliosis, with no count. p. 1640 (PDF 2).
- Narrow pedicles, reported as a screw-relevant variant (denominator 503 specimens, left and right averaged): PW < 4.5 mm at L1 0.80% and L2 0.40%, virtually none at L3 to L5 (the text's word; Figure 3 shows no bar there); PW < 5.5 mm at L1 4.77%, L2 2.78%, L3 0.20%. p. 1640 (PDF 2) and p. 1641 (PDF 3); Figure 3, p. 1643 (PDF 5). Inference: these are 4, 2, 24, 14 and 1 specimens.
- Inference: any transitional L5 or lumbarised S1 counted as L5 sits unrecognised inside the L5 row; the L5 PW maximum of 29.06 mm and the L5 PH range 9.72 to 19.94 mm could hold such a vertebra, but nothing in the paper lets one tell.
- Inference, outliers the paper does not discuss: the L1 maxima, PW 18.74 mm (about 5 SD above the L1 mean of 8.52, and above the L4 mean of 13.19) and PH 22.33 mm (about 4.7 SD above 15.75), p. 1640 (PDF 2), are what a mislabelled lower lumbar vertebra, a recording error or an anomalous L1 would give; they are not explained.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### Pedicle height (PH)

- Definition: the cranial-caudal dimension of the pedicle at its narrowest point along the pedicle's longitudinal axis, caliper applied from the lateral side in the sagittal plane. p. 1640 (PDF 2), Methods; Figure 1A, p. 1641 (PDF 3).
- Instrument: digital caliper, 0.01 mm, on dry bone; outer cortical surfaces (Inference: the jaws touch the outside of the cortex, so this is the external isthmus height, not the cancellous channel).
- Levels: L1 to L5; left and right averaged.
- Values, all 503 (Table 5 CS column, p. 1645, PDF 7; text p. 1640, PDF 2): L1 15.75, L2 15.06, L3 14.59, L4 13.81, L5 13.67 mm. Ranges: L1 10.84 to 22.33 mm, L5 9.72 to 19.94 mm. No overall SD is printed. Inference (pooled from Table 4): SD 1.41, 1.33, 1.31, 1.41, 1.66 mm.
- By sex (Table 4, p. 1643, PDF 5), male n = 404 against female n = 99: L1 16.04 +/- 1.35 against 14.57 +/- 0.95; L2 15.33 +/- 1.27 against 13.98 +/- 0.99; L3 14.80 +/- 1.28 against 13.73 +/- 1.07; L4 14.05 +/- 1.34 against 12.85 +/- 1.27; L5 13.90 +/- 1.65 against 12.77 +/- 1.36; P from 1.25E-22 (L1) to 4.59E-10 (L5).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference throughout; the paper uses no imaging): set a vertebral frame (cranial-caudal axis normal to the end-plates of the body part of the mask, anterior-posterior axis from body centroid to the arch midline, medial-lateral as their cross product); isolate each pedicle as the bridge of the posterior-element mask between the posterior body wall and the base of the superior articular process and pars (with SPINEPS subregions, the part of the arch subregion that joins the body); fit the pedicle's long axis through the centroids of successive sections; slice perpendicular to that axis at sub-millimetre steps; in each slice take the outer extent along the cranial-caudal direction; take the minimum over slices; average left and right. Use a sub-voxel surface (for example a half-maximum CT boundary or a smoothed mesh), since at 1 mm voxels a +/- 1 voxel error is about 7% of a 14 mm PH.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference; the paper gives no radiographic method and cites Olsewski et al's finding that direct and radiographic measurements differ significantly, p. 1639, PDF 1): a true lateral view shows the pedicle in profile between the superior and inferior vertebral notches, and its narrowest cranial-caudal height can be read there; the two pedicles superimpose, rotation splits them, and magnification (roughly 1.1 to 1.3 by geometry) must be removed by a calibration object or by expressing PH as a ratio to a body dimension on the same film. On an AP view the pedicle oval's vertical extent approximates PH as well.
- Across levels: decreases caudally, largest at L1 and smallest at L5 (Figure 2, p. 1641, PDF 3; abstract p. 1639, PDF 1). Steps: L1 to L2 -0.69, L2 to L3 -0.47, L3 to L4 -0.78, L4 to L5 -0.14 mm. Inference: the step divided by the between-subject SD is about 0.5, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.1, so PH alone separates no adjacent pair well and cannot tell L4 from L5.
- Sex, age, size, population: male larger at every level by 1.07 to 1.47 mm (Inference: female/male ratio 0.91 to 0.93). Adjusted for the other four covariates, PH rises 0.011 to 0.018 mm per year of age, 4.97 to 5.68 mm per metre of height, and -0.011 to 0.005 mm per kg of weight (Table 2, p. 1641, PDF 3). Unadjusted, tallest (>= 1800 mm, n = 62) minus shortest (<= 1600 mm, n = 69) is 1.78 to 2.07 mm (Table 3, p. 1642, PDF 4); heaviest (>= 64 kg, n = 91) minus lightest (<= 45 kg, n = 83) is 0.94 (L2), 0.77 (L1), 0.57 (L3), 0.47 (L4) and 0.37 mm (L5), the weight ANOVA significant at L1 to L4 and not at L5 (P 0.47) (Table 3; text p. 1643, PDF 5; the Discussion's "as much as 0.94 mm", p. 1644, PDF 6). By weight band PH rises steadily only at L1 and L2, rises then falls in the heaviest band at L3 and L4, and dips in the 55 to 63 kg band at L5 (p. 1643, PDF 5). By age band the age ANOVA is significant at L1 (P 2.37E-03), L2 (2.96E-03), L4 (0.01) and L5 (0.03), not at L3 (0.06) (Table 3). Caucasian against African American differ by 0.01 to 0.17 mm, none significant (P 0.25 to 0.92; Table 4, p. 1643, PDF 5).
- Error: averaged intra-rater CV 4.0% (1.7% to 6.2%) and inter-rater CV 5.2% (1.5% to 16.8%) on 21 random samples, not split by PH and PW (p. 1640, PDF 2). Left minus right significant only at L1 (15.57 against 15.94 mm) and L2 (14.96 against 15.17 mm), p. 1640 (PDF 2). Inference: 4 to 5% of 14 to 16 mm is about 0.6 to 0.8 mm.

### Pedicle width (PW)

- Definition: the medial-lateral dimension of the pedicle at its narrowest point along the pedicle's longitudinal axis, caliper applied from below (inferior approach) in the transverse plane. p. 1640 (PDF 2); Figure 1B, p. 1641 (PDF 3).
- Instrument: digital caliper, 0.01 mm, dry bone, outer cortex (Inference as above), left and right averaged.
- Values, all 503 (Table 5, p. 1645, PDF 7): L1 8.52, L2 8.66, L3 10.46, L4 13.19, L5 18.33 mm (the text gives 8.53 at L1 and 18.34 at L5, p. 1640, PDF 2). Ranges: L1 3.92 to 18.74 mm, L5 9.09 to 29.06 mm. Inference (pooled from Table 4): SD 2.03, 1.79, 1.94, 2.00, 2.44 mm.
- By sex (Table 4, p. 1643, PDF 5), male against female: L1 8.88 +/- 1.96 against 7.06 +/- 1.61; L2 9.01 +/- 1.69 against 7.24 +/- 1.47; L3 10.84 +/- 1.85 against 8.91 +/- 1.45; L4 13.58 +/- 1.89 against 11.60 +/- 1.59; L5 18.78 +/- 2.33 against 16.47 +/- 1.95; P 1.41E-20 to 8.54E-17.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): as for PH, but in each slice perpendicular to the pedicle axis take the outer extent in the medial-lateral direction within the slice, then the minimum along the axis. Measuring in the vertebra's transverse plane instead of perpendicular to the pedicle axis inflates PW by about 1 / cos of the pedicle's medial convergence angle, which is largest at L5; the paper did not measure that angle (p. 1644, PDF 6). At 1 mm voxels a +/- 1 voxel error is about 12% of an 8.5 mm L1 PW, so PW at L1 and L2 is the dimension a coarse mask resolves worst.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference; no method in the paper): on an AP view the pedicle appears end-on as an oval and its transverse width approximates PW; because the pedicle converges medially, the shadow is the projection of an oblique bar and is wider than the true minimum width, more so at L5; magnification must be removed. A lateral view cannot give PW.
- Across levels: increases caudally, smallest at L1 and largest at L5 (Figure 2, p. 1641, PDF 3). Steps: L1 to L2 +0.14, L2 to L3 +1.80, L3 to L4 +2.73, L4 to L5 +5.14 mm. Inference: step over between-subject SD about 0.1, 1.0, 1.4 and 2.3; PW cannot tell L1 from L2, separates L2 from L3 moderately and L4 from L5 strongly. PW is below PH at L1 to L4 and above it at L5; the curves cross between L4 and L5 (Figure 2).
- Sex, age, size, population: male wider at every level by 1.77 to 2.31 mm (Inference: female/male ratio 0.80 at L1 rising to 0.88 at L5, so the sex gap is relatively larger in the upper lumbar spine). Adjusted slopes: 0.008 to 0.026 mm per year, 2.07 (L1) to 7.59 (L5) mm per metre of height, 0.015 to 0.022 mm per kg (Table 2, p. 1641, PDF 3). Unadjusted, tallest minus shortest 1.74 to 2.67 mm; heaviest (>= 64 kg, n = 91) minus lightest (<= 45 kg, n = 83) 1.07 to 1.48 mm, rising steadily with weight band at every level (Table 3, p. 1642, PDF 4; text p. 1643, PDF 5; p. 1644, PDF 6). Age ANOVA significant only at L4 (P 0.01) and L5 (P 1.17E-05), not at L1 to L3 (P 0.73, 0.49, 0.10) (Table 3; text p. 1641, PDF 3). Race: African American wider at L1 (8.88 against 8.24, P 3.99E-04) and L2 (8.86 against 8.50, P 0.027), Caucasian wider at L5 (18.69 against 17.86, P 1.24E-04), no difference at L3 and L4 (Table 4, p. 1643, PDF 5).
- Error: as for PH (CV 4.0% intra, 5.2% inter, p. 1640, PDF 2). Inference: about 0.35 to 0.45 mm at L1 and 0.7 to 0.95 mm at L5. Within-sex biological CV is large at L1 (about 22%) and falls to about 12% at L5 (Inference from Table 4).

### Proportion of narrow pedicles (screw-acceptance thresholds)

- Definition: the share of specimens whose averaged PW is under 4.5 mm (the smallest screw) or under 5.5 mm. p. 1640 to 1641 (PDF 2 to 3); Figure 3, p. 1643 (PDF 5).
- Whole sample: < 4.5 mm L1 0.80%, L2 0.40%, L3 to L5 virtually none; < 5.5 mm L1 4.77%, L2 2.78%, L3 0.20%.
- By age band (Figure 3A; text p. 1641, PDF 3): L1 >= 70 y 1.92%, 50-69 y 1.30%, 31-49 y 0.47%, <= 30 y none (chi-square P = 0.514); L2 50-69 y 0.65%, 31-49 y 0.47%, others none (P = 0.848).
- By height (Figure 3B; text p. 1643, PDF 5): L1 <= 1600 mm 4.35%, 1601-1699 mm 0.60%, taller none (P = 0.004); L2 <= 1600 mm 2.90%, others none (P = 0.006).
- By weight (Figure 3C; text p. 1643, PDF 5): L1 <= 45 kg 3.61%, the next band 0.81%, heavier none (P = 0.013); L2 <= 45 kg 2.41%, others none (P = 0.017).
- Inference: the counts are 1, 2 and 1 (age), 3 and 1 (height), 3 and 1 (weight) of the 4 specimens at L1, so most narrow L1 pedicles belong to short, light people. The 5.5 mm proportions by subgroup are said to follow a similar trend but are not shown (p. 1644, PDF 6).
- From a mask or radiograph: apply the same thresholds to a PW measured as above (Inference).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1: tallest pedicle (PH 15.75 mm) and narrowest (PW 8.52 mm); the only level with left-right PH asymmetry beside L2 (15.57 left against 15.94 right); most narrow pedicles (PW < 4.5 mm 0.80%, < 5.5 mm 4.77%); minimum PW 3.92 mm; largest sex gap in relative PW (female about 80% of male). Age has no significant effect on PW (P 0.73). p. 1640 (PDF 2); Tables 3, 4, 5.
- L2: PW 8.66 mm, nearly the same as L1; PH 15.06 mm; L-R PH asymmetry 14.96 against 15.17 mm; PW < 4.5 mm 0.40%. African American PW larger than Caucasian (P 0.027). p. 1640 (PDF 2); Table 4.
- L3: PW 10.46, PH 14.59 mm; age did not reach significance for either PH (P 0.06) or PW (P 0.10); race no effect on PW (P 0.70); PW < 5.5 mm 0.20%, none under 4.5 mm. Tables 3 and 4.
- L4: PW 13.19 and PH 13.81 mm, nearly equal (the curves meet here in Figure 2, p. 1641, PDF 3); the one level where African American PH exceeded Caucasian, not significantly. p. 1644 (PDF 6); Table 4.
- L5: widest PW (18.33 mm, range 9.09 to 29.06) and shortest PH (13.67 mm, range 9.72 to 19.94); PW exceeds PH; the only level where both dimensions kept rising into the oldest age band (text p. 1641, PDF 3; PW 17.32 mm at <= 30 y to 19.15 mm at >= 70 y, P 1.17E-05; PH 13.49 to 14.00 mm, P 0.03); the largest height slope for PW (7.59 mm per m); weight had no significant effect on PH (P 0.47); Caucasian PW larger than African American (P 1.24E-04). Tables 2, 3, 4, 5.
- T12/L1 junction: not covered; T12 is in the companion lower-thoracic paper (ref 24, p. 1646, PDF 8).
- L5/S1 junction: S1 was not measured.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Reliability on 21 random samples: averaged inter-rater CV 5.2% (range 1.5% to 16.8%), intra-rater CV 4.0% (1.7% to 6.2%); the paper does not say which dimension, how many repeats, or who the second rater was, although it says one examiner measured all specimens. p. 1640 (PDF 2).
- Caliper resolution 0.01 mm; the examiner "subjectively measured the gross specimens", placing the caliper at the narrowest points along the longitudinal axis, so the site of the minimum is a judgement, not a fixed landmark. p. 1640 (PDF 2).
- Left and right differed significantly only for PH at L1 and L2 (by 0.37 and 0.21 mm). p. 1640 (PDF 2).
- Between-study disagreement: the authors attribute the wide spread of published values to differing samples, methods and populations, and note that most earlier studies had 10 to 180 specimens (p. 1640, PDF 2, citing ref 22; p. 1644, PDF 6). Table 5 (p. 1645, PDF 7) sets their means beside nine other series; for example L5 PW ranges from 10.7 mm (Berry, American, n = 30) to 20.1 mm (Olsewski, American, n = 41 to 42) and, among the other series, L5 PH from 13.7 (Berry) to 19.8 mm (Hou, Chinese, n = 40).
- Inference: the error CV (4 to 5%) is about a third to four fifths of the within-sex biological CV of PH (about 6.5% in women at L1 to 12% in men at L5, Table 4), so a single PH reading carries a sizeable share of noise; PW at L1 to L3 (biological CV 16 to 23%) is relatively better measured.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication: transpedicular screws give segmental fixation across a multilevel fusion with a biomechanical advantage over hook and lateral mass fixation; the pedicle is the strongest part of the vertebra even in osteoporotic bone; size variation makes lumbar screw placement contested, and Ofiram et al argued screws are safer in the lower thoracic than the upper lumbar spine. p. 1639 (PDF 1). The older multilevel hook-and-rod systems (Harrington, Luque) reduced spinal mobility, which the authors call particularly relevant in the lumbar region (p. 1639, PDF 1); lumbar pedicle screws are now called ubiquitous (p. 1644, PDF 6).
- PW, not PH, is "the limiting factor" for screw caliber because it is the smaller dimension. p. 1644 (PDF 6); Key Points p. 1646 (PDF 8). Inference pitfall: by the paper's own means PW exceeds PH at L5 (18.33 against 13.67 mm), so at L5 the height is the smaller dimension.
- Surgeons prefer the largest fully threaded screw a pedicle accepts; a screw too large breaches the medial cortex (spinal canal penetration) or the lateral cortex (nerve root injury, as the paper pairs them). p. 1644 (PDF 6).
- Virtually every lumbar level accepts a 4.5 mm screw; acceptance rises with height and weight, and a similar trend holds at 5.5 mm. p. 1644 (PDF 6); Figure 3, p. 1643 (PDF 5). The narrow pedicles cluster at L1 and L2 in short (<= 160 cm) and light (<= 45 kg) people.
- The authors say their data allow cut-offs of demographic characteristics that would predict a more successful transpedicular procedure (p. 1644, PDF 6), but print no such cut-off, sensitivity or prediction rule.
- Inference pitfall: "accepting" a screw is defined only as outer PW >= the screw diameter; the caliper reads cortex to cortex, so a 4.5 mm screw in a pedicle 4.5 to 5 mm wide outside has almost no room inside the cortical shell; the acceptance percentages are optimistic.
- Age cut-offs cannot be relied on to choose screw caliber; race did not consistently matter. p. 1644 (PDF 6).
- Sex, body height and body weight are the three demographic factors that significantly change lumbar pedicle size. Key Points, p. 1646 (PDF 8); Conclusion p. 1644 (PDF 6).
- The authors speculate that heavier people load the skeleton more and so may have stronger bone and better screw purchase. p. 1644 (PDF 6).
- Population differences in the literature: smaller pedicles in Koreans than in Westerners (Kim et al), differences between Indian and Caucasian samples (Chadha et al, Acharya et al). p. 1644 (PDF 6).
- Imaging pitfall, in the authors' view: lumbar lordosis, the pedicle's complex inner structure and its spatial orientation prevent accurate preoperative planning by imaging, and Olsewski et al found direct and radiographic measurements differ significantly; they hold direct caliper measurement of cadavers to be the most accurate. p. 1639 (PDF 1) and p. 1644 (PDF 6).
- Not measured, by the authors' statement: the transverse and sagittal pedicle angles (TPA, SPA), which they call important for planning but leave to earlier goniometer and imaging studies. p. 1644 (PDF 6). Inference: the paper also gives no chord length, pedicle length or inner (cancellous) width, without comment.
- Secular trend: the authors compare their 1893 to 1938 cadavers with three American series (Zindrick 1987, Berry 1987, Olsewski 1990) and find similar means, concluding that the pedicles of these people, who died about 100 years earlier, have not changed significantly from those of the contemporary population. p. 1644 (PDF 6); Table 5. Inference: those comparison series are themselves 25 to 28 years older than this paper, no test is reported, and Berry's L5 PW of 10.7 mm (against 18.33 here) is hardly similar.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. For each lumbar candidate in a CT instance mask, measure PW and PH at the pedicle isthmus: the narrowest outer medial-lateral and cranial-caudal extents along the pedicle's long axis, on both sides, then average them, as Yu did with a caliper (p. 1640, PDF 2; Figure 1, p. 1641, PDF 3).
2. Expect PW to rise from about 8.5 mm at L1 to 18.3 mm at L5 and PH to fall from about 15.8 to 13.7 mm (Table 5, p. 1645, PDF 7; Figure 2, p. 1641, PDF 3). Lean on the PW jumps L3 to L4 (+2.7 mm) and L4 to L5 (+5.1 mm); never use PW to separate L1 from L2 (8.52 against 8.66) or PH to separate L4 from L5 (13.81 against 13.67).
3. Compute the ratio PW / PH from the means (Inference, from Table 5, p. 1645, PDF 7: about 0.54, 0.58, 0.72, 0.96, 1.34 from L1 to L5; women 0.06 to 0.08 lower at every level, from Table 4, p. 1643, PDF 5). It is less size-dependent than either dimension but not size-free (Inference: the sex gap remains, and at L1 PW grows more slowly with height than PH, Table 2). Among L1 to L5 in this sample only L5 has a mean ratio above 1. Do not take a ratio above 1 alone as proof of L5: the paper never examined L6, a transitional L5 or S1, and gives no per-person spread of the ratio.
4. Before comparing a value with these norms, account for sex and stature: men have PW 1.8 to 2.3 mm and PH 1.1 to 1.5 mm larger (Table 4, p. 1643, PDF 5); per 10 cm of height, after the other covariates, PH gains about 0.50 to 0.57 mm and PW 0.21 mm (L1) to 0.76 mm (L5) (Table 2, p. 1641, PDF 3).
5. Once height, sex, age and race are allowed for, add only about 0.15 to 0.22 mm of PW per 10 kg of weight, and nothing meaningful to PH (-0.11 to +0.05 mm per 10 kg) (Table 2, p. 1641, PDF 3; text p. 1643, PDF 5).
6. Treat age as a small effect: by Table 2's coefficients 50 years add 0.55 to 0.90 mm to PH and 0.40 to 1.30 mm to PW (p. 1641, PDF 3); the text on that page gives the two ranges the other way round, so take the table.
7. Do not apply a fixed race correction: the Caucasian against African American difference in PW reverses between L1-L2 and L5 and stays under 1 mm (Table 4, p. 1643, PDF 5); the Discussion states the direction backwards (p. 1644, PDF 6), so trust the table.
8. Flag a PW under 4.5 mm (expected in 0.8% at L1, 0.4% at L2, virtually never at L3 to L5) or under 5.5 mm (4.8%, 2.8%, 0.2% at L1 to L3, none reported at L4 and L5) (p. 1640 to 1641, PDF 2 to 3); at L1 to L2 expect it mainly in short (<= 160 cm), light (<= 45 kg) patients (Figure 3, p. 1643, PDF 5). Inference: a value that small at L3 to L5 points first to a segmentation error or a wrong level, then to a dysplastic pedicle.
9. Do not read meaning into a difference smaller than the measurement error: 4 to 5% CV even for a caliper on dry bone (p. 1640, PDF 2), about 0.6 to 0.8 mm for PH, and more for a voxel mask; left-right PH differences of 0.2 to 0.4 mm at L1 and L2 are normal (p. 1640, PDF 2).
10. On radiographs (Inference; the paper gives no method and warns that direct and radiographic values differ, p. 1639, PDF 1): take PH from a true lateral view at the narrowest part of the pedicle shadow and PW from the AP pedicle oval; remove magnification with a calibration object or report ratios; expect AP PW to read too wide where pedicles converge most (L5).
11. Do not use this paper to name the junction levels: it has no T12 and no S1, and does not say how L1 or L5 were identified; for T12 against L1 use the companion lower-thoracic paper (ref 24, p. 1646, PDF 8).
12. When comparing with other series, check the definition first: Table 5 (p. 1645, PDF 7) mixes caliper and CT series, and L5 PW varies from 10.7 to 20.1 mm across American series alone.
13. Treat a single extreme value with suspicion before renaming a level: this sample's L1 range runs to PW 18.74 and PH 22.33 mm (p. 1640, PDF 2), several SD from the L1 mean (Inference: likely errors or mislabelled bones, unexplained in the paper); decide a level from the whole sequence of instances, not one pedicle.
14. Measure PH at the narrowest point of the pedicle, as Yu did, and expect it to keep falling to L5 (13.67 mm); series that report L5 PH of 15 to 20 mm (Olsewski 16.2, Mitra 15.80, Hou 19.8 in Table 5, p. 1645, PDF 7) used other definitions or sites, so never mix a narrowest-height mask value with a prior built on those series (Inference for the cause).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of L6, T13, transitional vertebrae or rib anomalies and does not say how levels were counted (p. 1640, PDF 2).

(b) Measures the generator holds or could add:
- Pedicle width and pedicle height per level L1 to L5 are in the generator (Panjabi's PDW and PDH). Yu's definition is the outer (cortical) minimum dimension at the isthmus, PW in the transverse plane and PH in the sagittal plane, left and right averaged (p. 1640, PDF 2). Inference, from the panjabi1992 note in this set (not from this paper): Panjabi digitised a loop around each pedicle of 60 lumbar vertebrae from 12 spines and did not say the loop lay at the narrowest section. His PDW agrees with Yu's PW (L1 8.6 against 8.52, L5 18.6 against 18.33 mm), but his PDH has its minimum at L3 (14.4) and rises to 19.6 mm at L5, where Yu's narrowest PH falls to 13.67 mm. So the generator's L5 pedicle height (and any PDH/PDW ratio at L4 to L5) does not match a narrowest-height value taken from a mask: either the generator's L5 PDH is re-based on Yu's definition, or the mask code measures where Panjabi did. Table 5 (p. 1645, PDF 7) shows the same split among series: L5 PH 13.67 to 14.67 mm in Yu, Berry and Zindrick, 15.2 to 19.8 mm in Marchesi, Mitra, Olsewski and Hou.
- Yu's n = 503 sex-specific means and SDs (Table 4, p. 1643, PDF 5) make a stronger L1 to L5 prior than a dozen spines, for an American sample of 1893 to 1938, 80% male.
- Inference, mask bias: a mask-derived PW measured in the vertebral transverse plane rather than perpendicular to the pedicle axis reads too wide (worst at L5); a dilated or smoothed mask reads both dimensions too large, and at about 1 mm voxels one voxel is about 12% of L1 PW; a mask that does not separate the pedicle from the superior articular process or transverse process root can miss the isthmus and read too large.
- Candidate addition (Inference): the ratio PW / PH per level, less dependent on the size factor than either dimension and the most level-specific single feature here (0.54 to 1.34 across L1 to L5; as PH / PW, 1.85, 1.74, 1.39, 1.05, 0.75, against Panjabi's PDH/PDW of about 1.84 at L1 to L2 falling to about 1.0 at L5 per the panjabi1992 note, the L5 gap again coming from the height definition). The paper gives no correlation between PW and PH or between levels within a person, which a generator drawing a joint vector needs.

(c) Measurement error to use: caliper CV 4.0% intra-rater and 5.2% inter-rater (p. 1640, PDF 2). Inference: as an absolute floor, about 0.6 to 0.8 mm for PH and 0.35 to 0.95 mm for PW (L1 to L5); a CT mask will add voxel and boundary error on top.

(d) Size, sex and population covariates:
- Height (adjusted for age, sex, race, weight; Table 2, p. 1641, PDF 3): PH +4.97 to +5.68 mm per m; PW +2.07 (L1), +2.71, +3.34, +4.20, +7.59 (L5) mm per m. Inference: per 10 cm that is 3.3 to 4.2% of PH and 2.4 to 4.1% of PW; as an elasticity at the mean height of 1.70 m, about 0.56 to 0.71 for PH (no steady trend across levels) and 0.41 at L1 to 0.70 at L5 for PW. A fully isometric size factor would give 1.0, so pedicles scale less than proportionally with stature; a single multiplicative size factor applied equally to every dimension would overstate the stature effect on pedicles, most at L1 PW.
- Sex (unadjusted means, Table 4): female/male ratio 0.91 to 0.93 for PH and 0.80 (L1) to 0.88 (L5) for PW. The regression's sex coefficient is not printed, so how much of this gap remains after height is unknown.
- Weight (adjusted, Table 2): PH -0.011 to +0.005 mm per kg; PW +0.015 to +0.022 mm per kg.
- Age (adjusted, Table 2): PH +0.011 to +0.018, PW +0.008 to +0.026 mm per year; unadjusted means rise to the 50 to 69 band and dip at >= 70 except at L5 (Table 3, p. 1642, PDF 4).
- Population: Caucasian against African American differences under 1 mm, PW reversing direction along the spine (Table 4). Other populations in Table 5 (p. 1645, PDF 7): Indian series (Mitra, Chadha) have L5 PW 13.47 to 15.85 mm and Chinese (Hou) 12.9 mm against 18.33 mm here; Swiss (Marchesi) 18.8 mm.
- Regression intercepts are printed (PH 4.95, 5.26, 4.43, 4.26, 3.33 mm; PW 2.84, 1.59, 2.35, 3.44, 2.17 mm for L1 to L5; Table 2), but without the sex and race coefficients and their coding the equations cannot be used to predict a person's values.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Text against table, age: p. 1641 (PDF 3) says a 25 to 75 year comparison lengthens PH by 0.40 to 1.3 mm and widens PW by 0.55 to 0.90 mm; Table 2's coefficients times 50 years give the reverse (PH 0.55 to 0.90, PW 0.40 to 1.30 mm). The height and weight comparisons in the text agree with Table 2.
- Discussion against results, race: p. 1644 (PDF 6) says Caucasian PW is bigger at L1 and L2 and smaller at L5; Table 4 (p. 1643, PDF 5) and the Results text say the opposite (African American wider at L1 to L3, Caucasian wider at L4 and L5).
- Inference: Figure 2's error bars (p. 1641, PDF 3), read against its grid, appear to be exchanged between the two curves: the bars on PH are about +/- 1.8 to 2.45 mm and those on PW about +/- 1.3 to 1.65 mm, while the SDs pooled from Table 4 are about 1.3 to 1.7 mm for PH and 1.8 to 2.4 mm for PW; the PH bars exceed every PH SD in Tables 3 and 4 (largest 1.83) and the PW bars are below every PW SD of the sex and race groups in Table 4 (smallest 1.45).
- Weight bins disagree: Table 3 (p. 1642, PDF 4) and the band definition in the Body Weight text (p. 1643, PDF 5) use 46-54 and 55-63 kg, while Figure 3C's legend and the narrow-pedicle sentence on the same page (p. 1643, PDF 5) use 46-55 and 56-63 kg.
- Text gives L1 PW 8.53 and L5 PW 18.34 mm (p. 1640, PDF 2); Table 5 and the abstract give 8.52 and 18.33 mm (p. 1645, PDF 7; p. 1639, PDF 1).
- The sex Results say the t test was significant "for all lower lumbar levels" (p. 1644, PDF 6), while Table 4 (p. 1643, PDF 5) shows every level L1 to L5 significant for both dimensions.
- The Discussion calls this the first study of how pedicle size varies with body weight and with body height (p. 1644, PDF 6), though the same group's lower thoracic paper of the same year carries the same subtitle (ref 24, p. 1646, PDF 8).
- The authors claim demographic cut-offs can be extrapolated (p. 1644, PDF 6) but give none.
- The "quartiles" of age, height and weight are fixed bands of unequal size (82/215/154/52; 69/168/204/62; 83/123/206/91), not quartiles.
- One examiner is said to have measured everything, yet an inter-rater CV is reported with no second rater named; neither CV is given per dimension or per level (p. 1640, PDF 2).
- The narrow-pedicle percentages are called percentages of pedicles but match counts of specimens out of 503 after left-right averaging (Inference from the arithmetic).
- No overall SD, no regression P values or R-squared, no sex or race regression coefficients, no correlation between PH and PW or between levels, and no sex-by-race crossing.
- Level identification and numerical variants (L6, sacralisation, T13) are not addressed at all; congenital exclusions are not counted.
- Height and weight are values documented at the time of death, not in life (p. 1640, PDF 2). Inference: the cohort's mean weight (56 kg at 170 cm, BMI about 19.4) is low for its height, so weight is a poor proxy for living body size.
- Table 5's footnote runs "Mitra et al" straight into "CT:" with no separator; read literally, the caliper series are this study, Berry, Olsewski, Hou, Marchesi and Mitra, and the CT series Zindrick, Chadha and Krag (Inference from the sibling notes: Zindrick used specimen roentgenograms as well as CT, and Mitra measured three ways, so the labels simplify); Zindrick et al appear twice (an "American" column and a "Mean Values" column) without explanation; Hou et al's L5 PH of 19.8 mm and Berry et al's L5 PW of 10.7 mm lie far from every other series (Inference: definitional differences or transcription errors in the source studies or here).
- Inference, from the other notes in this set: the PW pattern (narrow at L1 to L2, widening to L5) agrees with Panjabi 1992, Zindrick and Olsewski; Hou 1993 finds L1 narrower than T12 in every column, which Yu cannot test. The PH pattern does not agree everywhere: Panjabi 1992 and Hou 1993 have PH rising to about 19.6 to 19.8 mm at L5, against Yu's 13.67 mm, a definitional difference (site along the pedicle) that a mask-based extractor must settle by following one stated definition.
