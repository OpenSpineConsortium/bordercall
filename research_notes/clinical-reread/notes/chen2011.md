## What the paper is

- Design: a retrospective CT morphometry of thoracolumbar endplates, with a small cadaver arm to check the CT values against cut specimens. Aim: the geometry, "especially the concavity", of the endplates from T10 inferior to L3 superior, to inform the design of vertebral body replacement (VBR) constructs, chiefly titanium mesh cages (TMC), and a second aim, to check that dimensions taken from CT match the real bone, p. 1815 (PDF 2). The authors' motive: earlier morphometry (Berry, Panjabi 1992, Gilad, Hall, Zhou; refs 5-9) gave sagittal and transverse diameters of the vertebral body, but little on endplate geometry in the thoracolumbar region, and few studies checked CT-derived sizes against specimens, pp. 1814-1815 (PDF 1-2).
- CT population: 83 patients (mean age 43 years, range 21 to 65) who had a thoracic or abdominal CT for abdominal or thoracic symptoms and had no spinal complaint, chosen retrospectively from the hospital PACS, p. 1815 (PDF 2). Country and ethnicity are not stated. Inference: Chinese patients of the authors' Chongqing hospital.
- Groups: four groups by age (over 40 years or not) and sex, named A1, A2, B1, B2, with 22, 19, 21 and 21 subjects, p. 1815 (PDF 2). Which label is which is not given, and no per-group value is printed.
- Sex split: Tables 2 and 3 give "Male (n = 430)" and "Female (n = 400)" (Table 5 prints no n), and Table 1 gives n = 830, p. 1817-1818 (PDF 4-5). Inference: n counts endplates, 10 per patient, so 43 men and 40 women (22 + 21 and 19 + 21 fit the four groups), and each endplate row of Table 1 holds 83 values.
- Cadaver arm: 10 human thoracolumbar spines from T10 to L3, CT scanned, then cut along the midsagittal plane into two "identical" halves and photographed (Fig. 1, a T10-T12 specimen with a ruler, p. 1815 (PDF 2)). The same marking was done on their CT images but only SCA and SD were measured; the photographs were measured in Adobe Photoshop CS v8.01, p. 1816 (PDF 3). Age, sex, ethnicity and preservation of the cadavers are not given. Table 4 has n = 100 (Inference: 10 specimens x 10 endplates), p. 1818 (PDF 5).
- Modality: CT on a GE LightSpeed VCT; all images multiplanar reformatted with 0.625 mm slice thickness, p. 1815 (PDF 2). The figure overlays show 120 kV, 298 mA, 0.6 s rotation, gantry tilt 0.0, a 50 cm scan field of view, pitch 0.984:1 and a bone window (WW 1800, WL 600), Figs. 2-3, p. 1816 (PDF 3). The overlay also prints "0.729mm/39.38"; its meaning is not stated. Inference: 39.38 is the table feed in mm per rotation (a 40 mm detector at pitch 0.984) and 0.729 mm may be the displayed pixel or reformat spacing; the in-plane pixel size is not given in the text.
- The figures show one endplate of each kind: Fig. 2 is a superior endplate on the MSP (anterior on the viewer's left; Cm lies below chord AP, inside the body below the disc), and Fig. 3 is an inferior endplate on the SCP (Cs lies above chord RL, inside the body above the disc), p. 1816 (PDF 3). Inference (read off Fig. 3): Cs sits slightly toward L from the middle of RL, about 55% of RL from R. Which level each figure shows is not said.
- Planes: the midsagittal plane (MSP) is the image in which the whole contour of that vertebra's spinous process is seen; the specified coronal plane (SCP) is the coronal plane through the most concave point of the endplate on the MSP, p. 1815 (PDF 2). Whether the reformats were turned to each vertebra (double oblique) is not said. Inference: rotated vertebrae and laterally slanted columns were excluded, which suggests scanner-aligned planes.
- Measurement: one trained user (initials CH) marked the landmarks by hand with the PACS's own measuring tools; the same software gave the angles and lengths, p. 1815 (PDF 2).
- Levels: ten endplates, T10 inf, T11 sup and inf, T12 sup and inf, L1 sup and inf, L2 sup and inf, L3 sup, that is both facing endplates of the five discs T10/T11 to L2/L3, Table 1, p. 1817 (PDF 4). The T10 superior and L3 inferior endplates were not measured.
- How levels were identified: not stated; no word on rib counting, counting from C2 or from the sacrum, or on transitional segments.
- Exclusion: marked osteophytes, significant degeneration, vertebral fracture, a neoplasm in a vertebral body; vertebrae visibly rotated axially with respect to the scanner axis; columns with a large lateral slant, p. 1815 (PDF 2). Inference: since n = 830 = 83 x 10, no endplate of an included patient was dropped, so the vertebra-level exclusions in practice excluded patients.
- Statistics: SPSS 16.0; means and SD; one-way ANOVA; two-tailed paired t tests; an "inter-modality reliability test" (Cronbach alpha); significance P < 0.05, p. 1816 (PDF 3).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about transitional vertebrae, rib counts, T13, L6, lumbosacral transitional vertebrae or how levels were numbered; Fig. 1 labels T10 and T12 on a cadaver half without saying how they were identified, p. 1815 (PDF 2). Inference: on a thoracic or abdominal CT a short or absent twelfth rib could lead to a T12/L1 mislabel, and no check is described. Inference: the one level-specific shape finding with a junction flavour is T12, whose superior and inferior endplate widths do not differ while T11, L1 and L2 widen caudally (Table 3, p. 1818 (PDF 5); P = 0.674 men, 0.848 women, p. 1817 (PDF 4)); that is normal anatomy, not an anomaly, but it is a candidate shape signature of T12 as the normal transition from thoracic to lumbar form. Inference: in a spine with 11 or 13 rib-bearing vertebrae this signature may move with the transition, but the paper tests nothing of the kind.

## Measures, each as a recipe

Landmarks, p. 1815 (PDF 2), Figs. 2-3, p. 1816 (PDF 3): on the MSP, A (anterior rim of the endplate), P (posterior rim) and Cm (the most concave point); on the SCP, R (right rim), L (left rim) and Cs (the most concave point). C' is the foot of the perpendicular from Cm onto line AP, "the projective point of Cm in AP". Fig. 3 is in radiological convention (R on the viewer's left). All values below are mean +/- SD.

Abbreviation clash: this paper's SCA (sagittal concave angle) is not Panjabi's SCA (spinal canal area), and its SD (sagittal diameter) is not a standard deviation. Inference: rename them in code (for example ep_sag_angle, ep_sag_diam).

### Sagittal concave angle (SCA)

- Definition: the angle A-Cm-P with its vertex at Cm, "the obtuse angle formed by the rims" and the most concave point, p. 1815 (PDF 2). 180 deg is a flat endplate; a smaller angle is a deeper concavity.
- Plane and instrument: MSP of a 0.625 mm reformat, PACS angle tool, one observer.
- Values (Table 1, p. 1817 (PDF 4), both sexes pooled, 83 per endplate): T10 inf 163.1 +/- 3.9; T11 sup 163.1 +/- 3.9; T11 inf 163.3 +/- 4.6; T12 sup 163.9 +/- 3.7; T12 inf 163.0 +/- 4.6; L1 sup 163.7 +/- 4.4; L1 inf 162.5 +/- 4.8; L2 sup 163.3 +/- 4.9; L2 inf 162.9 +/- 4.3; L3 sup 163.5 +/- 4.2 deg.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: take the vertebral-body part of the mask (or the endplate subregion where the segmenter gives one); build the vertebra's midsagittal plane through the body centroid and the spinous process (the paper's MSP shows the whole spinous process); in that section extract the cranial (or caudal) boundary curve of the body facing the disc; A and P are the anterior and posterior ends of that curve at the rim; Cm is the curve point farthest from chord AP on the body side; SCA is the angle at Cm. Use a sub-voxel surface (smoothed isosurface or a fitted curve), since the dip is about 2 mm.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: a true lateral shows a projected endplate profile, but it superimposes every sagittal section and the lateral rims, and any endplate tilt to the beam turns the rim into an ellipse that hides the dip; the angle is magnification-free only for a beam parallel to the endplate. The paper gives no radiographic method.
- Across levels: flat. No difference among the ten endplates (one-way ANOVA P = 0.843); range 162.5 (L1 inf) to 163.9 deg (T12 sup), p. 1816 (PDF 3), Table 1. It separates no level and no superior from inferior endplate.
- Sex, age, size, population: no difference among the four age-sex groups (least P = 0.140), p. 1816 (PDF 3). No body size data.
- Error: CT 162.4 +/- 5.7 against cadaver 162.1 +/- 6.1 deg, mean difference 0.3 deg, Cronbach alpha 0.8191 (Table 4, p. 1818 (PDF 5); text p. 1817 (PDF 4)). No intra- or inter-observer figure. Inference: with an endplate 28 mm long and the deepest point at 43% from P, SCA changes by about 8 deg per mm of depth, so a one-voxel error in Cm moves SCA by more than the between-subject SD.

### Location of the concave region in the MSP (LCR)

- Definition: the length C'P divided by AP, in percent, measured from the posterior rim, p. 1815 (PDF 2). Under 50% means the deepest point lies behind the midpoint.
- Plane and instrument: MSP of a 0.625 mm reformat; C' is the foot of the perpendicular from Cm to AP, drawn with the PACS tools; one observer, p. 1815 (PDF 2). Levels: the ten endplates T10 inf to L3 sup.
- Values (Table 1, p. 1817 (PDF 4)): T10 inf 44.2 +/- 6.8; T11 sup 43.5 +/- 6.5; T11 inf 43.9 +/- 5.1; T12 sup 43.3 +/- 5.3; T12 inf 44.2 +/- 5.3; L1 sup 43.6 +/- 6.1; L1 inf 43.0 +/- 5.0; L2 sup 42.8 +/- 5.0; L2 inf 42.7 +/- 6.5; L3 sup 42.5 +/- 5.6%.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: project Cm onto chord AP to get C'; LCR = |C'P| / |AP|. Make sure P is the posterior rim of the body (posterior wall corner), not a point on the pedicle base.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: a true lateral could give it as a ratio (magnification cancels) if the dip is visible; not tested.
- Across levels: flat, 42.5 to 44.2% (ANOVA P = 0.783); a slight drift toward the posterior rim caudally (44.2 at T10 inf to 42.5 at L3 sup) that is not significant, p. 1816 (PDF 3).
- Sex, age, size, population: no effect of age or sex (least P = 0.668), p. 1816 (PDF 3). No body size data.
- Error: not given; LCR was not part of the cadaver check (only SCA and SD were), p. 1816 (PDF 3). The between-subject SD is 5 to 7 percentage points, Table 1.
- Inference: in a 28 mm endplate the deepest point sits about 12 mm in front of the posterior rim, about 2 mm behind the midpoint.

### Sagittal diameter of the endplate (SD) and the vertebral average (aSD)

- Definition: the length of line AP on the MSP, rim to rim of the endplate, p. 1815 (PDF 2). aSD is the mean of the superior and inferior SD of one vertebra, used as the sagittal length of that vertebra, p. 1816 (PDF 3). Inference: for T10 and L3 only one endplate was measured, so their aSD is that one endplate (Table 2 T10 matches Table 5 T10 inf).
- Plane and instrument: MSP of a 0.625 mm reformat, PACS length tool, one observer, p. 1815 (PDF 2); also measured on the 10 cadaver halves (photographs in Photoshop) and on their CT, p. 1816 (PDF 3). Levels: T10 inf to L3 sup.
- Values per endplate, male / female (Table 5, p. 1819 (PDF 6)): T10 inf 27.6 +/- 2.8 / 24.2 +/- 2.0; T11 sup 27.5 +/- 2.4 / 24.1 +/- 2.5; T11 inf 27.2 +/- 1.8 / 24.1 +/- 3.1; T12 sup 27.5 +/- 2.0 / 23.8 +/- 3.0; T12 inf 28.1 +/- 2.3 / 24.7 +/- 2.7; L1 sup 29.4 +/- 2.1 / 25.7 +/- 2.7; L1 inf 29.4 +/- 2.4 / 25.4 +/- 2.3; L2 sup 29.2 +/- 2.6 / 26.0 +/- 2.4; L2 inf 29.3 +/- 2.6 / 26.0 +/- 2.8; L3 sup 29.1 +/- 2.7 / 25.6 +/- 3.1 mm.
- aSD per vertebra, male / female (Table 2, p. 1817 (PDF 4)): T10 27.6 +/- 2.8 / 24.2 +/- 2.0; T11 27.4 +/- 2.1 / 24.1 +/- 2.8; T12 27.8 +/- 2.2 / 24.2 +/- 2.9; L1 29.4 +/- 2.2 / 25.6 +/- 2.5; L2 29.3 +/- 2.6 / 26.0 +/- 2.6; L3 29.1 +/- 2.8 / 25.7 +/- 3.1 mm. Segment means: thoracic (T10-T12) 27.6 +/- 2.3 male, 24.2 +/- 2.7 female; lumbar (L1-L3) 29.3 +/- 2.5 male, 25.8 +/- 2.7 female.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: on the same midsagittal section as SCA, the chord A to P of the endplate face; this is a rim-to-rim chord, smaller than the full anteroposterior extent of the body mask, and osteophytes (excluded in the paper) must be trimmed.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: a true lateral gives the anterior-to-posterior corner distance of the endplate, scaled by the magnification (unknown in the paper; typically 1.1 to 1.3 for clinical lateral films). A tilted endplate projects a double contour; take the midpoint of the two rims.
- Across levels: superior and inferior SD of each vertebra do not differ (all P > 0.05), p. 1816 (PDF 3). Within the thoracic segment (male P = 0.575, female P = 0.974) and within the lumbar segment (male P = 0.863, female P = 0.667) aSD does not differ; lumbar exceeds thoracic by about 1.6 mm (paired t between segments: male P < 0.000000002, female P < 0.0000007), p. 1816 (PDF 3), Table 2. So SD separates T10-T12 from L1-L3 as groups, with the step at the T12/L1 disc (T12 inf to L1 sup 28.1 to 29.4 male, 24.7 to 25.7 female), and separates no neighbours within a segment.
- Sex, age, size, population: women about 88% of men at every level (all P < 0.0001), p. 1816 (PDF 3); Inference (computed from Table 5): 0.86 to 0.89. Age has no effect (least P = 0.643). No height or weight.
- Error: CT 28.8 +/- 3.7 against cadaver 29.1 +/- 3.8 mm, mean difference 0.3 mm, Cronbach alpha 0.8806 (Table 4, p. 1818 (PDF 5); text p. 1817 (PDF 4)). Slice thickness 0.625 mm. No observer study.

### Coronal concave angle (CCA)

- Definition: the angle R-Cs-L with its vertex at Cs on the SCP, the coronal plane through Cm, p. 1815 (PDF 2). Inference (from LCR): the SCP therefore lies about 43% of SD in front of the posterior rim, not at the middle of the body.
- Plane and instrument: SCP of a 0.625 mm reformat, PACS angle tool, one observer, p. 1815 (PDF 2). Levels: T10 inf to L3 sup.
- Values (Table 1, p. 1817 (PDF 4), both sexes pooled): T10 inf 170.9 +/- 3.2; T11 sup 171.5 +/- 3.2; T11 inf 170.9 +/- 3.8; T12 sup 173.6 +/- 3.1; T12 inf 170.9 +/- 3.8; L1 sup 175.7 +/- 3.6; L1 inf 173.4 +/- 4.1; L2 sup 175.5 +/- 4.7; L2 inf 171.3 +/- 3.8; L3 sup 173.8 +/- 4.8 deg. Example on Fig. 3: 171.4 deg, p. 1816 (PDF 3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: take the coronal section through Cm (perpendicular to the midsagittal plane, containing the vertebra's cranio-caudal axis); R and L are the lateral rims of the endplate face; Cs is the face point farthest from chord RL on the body side; CCA is the angle at Cs. The position of Cs along RL is not reported (it looks near the middle on Fig. 3).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: an AP radiograph shows the coronal endplate profile only where the beam is parallel to the endplate; tilt makes an ellipse whose near and far rims hide the dip. Not tested.
- Across levels: coronal concavity is shallower than sagittal everywhere (170.9 to 175.7 deg against 162.5 to 163.9). Superior endplates are flatter than inferior ones of the same vertebra from T11 to L2: differences T11 0.6 deg (P = 0.346), T12 2.7 (P < 0.0001), L1 2.3 (P < 0.002), L2 4.2 (P < 0.000001), p. 1816 (PDF 3), Table 1. Inferior endplates sit at 170.9 at T10, T11 and T12, then 173.4 at L1 and 171.3 at L2; superior endplates rise from 171.5 at T11 to 173.6 at T12 and 175.7 at L1, then 175.5 at L2 and 173.8 at L3. No test across levels is reported for CCA.
- Sex, age, size, population: no difference among the four age-sex groups (least P = 0.351), p. 1816 (PDF 3). No body size data.
- Error: not checked against the cadavers; no observer study. Inference: across a 38 mm width with Cs near the middle, CCA changes by about 6 deg per mm of depth.

### Transverse diameter of the endplate (TD)

- Definition: the length of line RL on the SCP, rim to rim of the endplate, p. 1815 (PDF 2). It is the width at the coronal section through the deepest sagittal point (p. 1815), and the authors set their rim-to-rim lengths apart from the maximum border-to-border widths of earlier studies, p. 1819 (PDF 6).
- Plane and instrument: SCP of a 0.625 mm reformat, PACS length tool, one observer, p. 1815 (PDF 2). Levels: T10 inf to L3 sup.
- Values, male / female (Table 3, p. 1818 (PDF 5); repeated in Table 5, p. 1819 (PDF 6)): T10 inf 35.6 +/- 2.4 / 30.7 +/- 2.5; T11 sup 34.5 +/- 2.8 / 29.6 +/- 2.4; T11 inf 37.7 +/- 3.3 / 33.4 +/- 2.5; T12 sup 38.0 +/- 3.4 / 32.9 +/- 2.5; T12 inf 37.6 +/- 3.8 / 33.1 +/- 3.6; L1 sup 38.4 +/- 4.0 / 34.4 +/- 2.6; L1 inf 42.7 +/- 3.5 / 37.7 +/- 3.1; L2 sup 41.4 +/- 4.0 / 36.0 +/- 2.8; L2 inf 44.5 +/- 4.0 / 38.3 +/- 3.2; L3 sup 42.4 +/- 3.8 / 37.2 +/- 3.4 mm.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: on the coronal section through Cm, the chord between the lateral rims of the endplate face, osteophytes trimmed. Do not take the maximum left-right extent of the body mask, which is a different and larger quantity.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: an AP radiograph gives the endplate width between the lateral corners at the endplate level, scaled by magnification; it projects the widest silhouette, not the section through Cm, so it will run somewhat larger. Not possible from a lateral.
- Across levels: within each vertebra the superior TD is about 91% of the inferior (all P < 0.008) except T12 (male P = 0.674, female P = 0.848), p. 1817 (PDF 4), Table 3 (paired P: T11 < 0.00006 male, < 0.0000003 female; L1 < 0.0001 both; L2 < 0.006 male, < 0.008 female). So bodies widen caudally within T11, L1 and L2 but not within T12. Inference (computed from Table 3): inferior TD steps from 37.6 at T12 to 42.7 at L1 in men (+5.1 mm) and 33.1 to 37.7 in women (+4.6), while superior TD moves only 38.0 to 38.4 (men) and 32.9 to 34.4 (women); the inferior endplate width is therefore the better separator of T12 from L1. Inference (computed from Table 3): the mirror holds one level up; superior TD steps from 34.5 at T11 to 38.0 at T12 in men (+3.5) and 29.6 to 32.9 in women (+3.3), while inferior TD does not move (37.7 to 37.6 men, 33.4 to 33.1 women). So T12 has an L1-like superior width and a T11-like inferior width, which is why its two widths are equal. Inference (computed from Table 3): across each disc, the upper vertebra's inferior endplate is wider than the lower vertebra's superior endplate at T10/T11, L1/L2 and L2/L3 (by 1.1 to 2.1 mm in both sexes); at T11/T12 the two faces are nearly equal (men 37.7 against 38.0, the lower 0.3 mm wider; women 33.4 against 32.9, the upper 0.5 mm wider); at T12/L1 the lower one (L1 sup) is wider in both sexes (men 37.6 against 38.4, women 33.1 against 34.4).
- Sex, age, size, population: women about 88% of men (all P < 0.0008); age no effect (least P = 0.602), p. 1817 (PDF 4). Inference (computed from Table 3): 0.86 to 0.90. No body size data.
- Error: not checked against the cadavers; no observer study.

### Concavity depth (not reported by the paper)

- The paper reports concavity only as angles and the location ratio; no depth in mm is tabled. Fig. 2 shows an example with labels "1.8 mm" and "25.8 mm" beside the construction lines, p. 1816 (PDF 3). Inference: these are the depth Cm-C' and the length AP.
- Inference (computed from Tables 1 and 5 with the triangle A-Cm-P, Cm at LCR from P): the sagittal dip is about 1.9 to 2.2 mm in men and 1.65 to 1.9 mm in women at every endplate (for example L1 inf 2.2 / 1.9 mm, T12 sup 1.9 / 1.65 mm); the Fig. 2 example (25.8 mm, about 163 deg, 43%) gives 1.9 mm, matching its 1.8 mm label. With Cs assumed central and TD from Table 3, the coronal dip is about 0.65 to 0.8 mm at L1 sup and L2 sup, 0.9 to 1.3 mm at T11 sup, T12 sup and L3 sup, and 1.1 to 1.7 mm at the inferior endplates (L2 inf largest, 1.7 mm men). These are derived from means of angles and are approximate.
- Inference: because SCA does not differ by sex while SD is 12% smaller in women, the implied sagittal depth is about 12% smaller in women.

### Endplate shape ratio (derived, not in the paper)

- Inference (computed from Table 5): TD/SD rises overall from T10 to L2 and is larger at the inferior than the superior endplate of T11, L1 and L2, but not of T12: men T10 inf 1.29, T11 sup 1.25, T11 inf 1.39, T12 sup 1.38, T12 inf 1.34, L1 sup 1.31, L1 inf 1.45, L2 sup 1.42, L2 inf 1.52, L3 sup 1.46; women similar (1.23 at T11 sup to 1.48 at L1 inf). It is not monotone (T12 inf and L1 sup sit below T11 inf). Size-free, so useful when magnification or body size is unknown, but it needs both an AP and a lateral view.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- Coverage: T10 inferior to L3 superior only, which spans the thoracolumbar junction (T12/L1) but not C2/C3, C7/T1 or L5/S1, Table 1, p. 1817 (PDF 4).
- T10 inf: SD 27.6 / 24.2 mm (men / women), TD 35.6 / 30.7; CCA 170.9; SCA 163.1; LCR 44.2%, Tables 1, 3, 5.
- T10/T11 disc: the T10 inferior endplate is wider than the T11 superior (35.6 against 34.5 men; 30.7 against 29.6 women), Table 3, p. 1818 (PDF 5).
- T11: the narrowest endplate of the series (T11 sup 34.5 / 29.6); widens caudally to 37.7 / 33.4 (P < 0.00006 / < 0.0000003); superior and inferior CCA do not differ significantly (0.6 deg, P = 0.346), Tables 1 and 3.
- T11/T12 disc: the facing widths are nearly equal (T11 inf 37.7 against T12 sup 38.0 in men, 33.4 against 32.9 in women), Table 3 (Inference, computed). The superior width steps up by about 3.4 mm from T11 sup to T12 sup in both sexes, while the inferior width is unchanged from T11 inf to T12 inf (Inference, computed from Table 3).
- T12: the only vertebra whose superior and inferior widths do not differ (38.0 against 37.6 men, 32.9 against 33.1 women; P = 0.674 / 0.848), p. 1817 (PDF 4); superior CCA flatter by 2.7 deg (P < 0.0001), Table 1. SD of T12 inf 28.1 / 24.7 is a little above T12 sup 27.5 / 23.8 (not significant, all P > 0.05, p. 1816).
- T12/L1 junction: the sagittal step between thoracic and lumbar aSD (about 1.6 mm) falls here, p. 1816 (PDF 3), Table 2; L1 sup is wider than T12 inf (by 0.8 mm men, 1.3 mm women), the only disc of the five where the lower face is clearly the wider (Inference, computed from Table 3); L1 sup is the flattest coronal endplate of the series (175.7 deg), Table 1.
- L1: widens caudally by 4.3 mm in men (38.4 to 42.7, the largest within-vertebra widening of the series) and 3.3 mm in women (34.4 to 37.7; T11's 3.8 mm is larger in women); superior CCA flatter by 2.3 deg (P < 0.002); SCA of L1 inf is the smallest of the series (162.5 deg), not significantly, Tables 1 and 3.
- L2: widest endplate measured (L2 inf 44.5 / 38.3 mm); largest superior-inferior CCA difference (4.2 deg, P < 0.000001), Tables 1 and 3.
- L3 sup: TD 42.4 / 37.2, SD 29.1 / 25.6, CCA 173.8, LCR 42.5% (the most posterior), Tables 1, 3, 5.

## Measurement error and reliability

- One observer marked every landmark by hand in the PACS; no intra- or inter-observer repeat is reported, p. 1815 (PDF 2).
- CT against cadaver halves (10 spines, n = 100, SCA and SD only): SCA 162.4 +/- 5.7 against 162.1 +/- 6.1 deg (mean difference 0.3 deg, alpha 0.8191); SD 28.8 +/- 3.7 against 29.1 +/- 3.8 mm (mean difference 0.3 mm, alpha 0.8806), Table 4, p. 1818 (PDF 5), text p. 1817 (PDF 4). The authors take alpha of 0.7 to 0.8 as satisfactory, citing Bland and Altman, and call the CT values accurate and reliable, p. 1819 (PDF 6).
- Inference: the "mean difference" of 0.3 equals the difference of the two printed means (162.4 - 162.1; 29.1 - 28.8), so it says nothing of how far single paired values differ. Cronbach alpha measures consistency, not agreement; no SD of the paired differences or limits of agreement are given, and how the photographs were scaled (the ruler of Fig. 1, presumably) is not said. CCA, TD and LCR were never validated.
- The authors note that, to their knowledge, most earlier morphometric studies by radiograph, CT, MRI or cadaver ran no inter-modality reliability test, and that van der Houwen reached the same conclusion on CT accuracy, p. 1819 (PDF 6).
- Resolution: 0.625 mm reformats, p. 1815 (PDF 2); the in-plane pixel is not stated. Inference: a sagittal dip of about 2 mm spans about three 0.625 mm voxels, a coronal dip of about 1 mm under two; angle errors from one voxel exceed the between-subject SD.
- Between-subject SD (useful as generator spread): SCA 3.7 to 4.9 deg, LCR 5.0 to 6.8%, CCA 3.1 to 4.8 deg (pooled sexes); SD 1.8 to 3.1 mm and TD 2.4 to 4.0 mm within sex, Tables 1, 3, 5.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indications for VBR: acute thoracolumbar fractures, post-traumatic deformity, neoplastic disorders and vertebral body infection often require resection and reconstruction with a VBR construct, p. 1814 (PDF 1). The thoracolumbar region is a frequent site of fractures, tumours and vertebroplasty, p. 1815 (PDF 2).
- TMC advantages over auto- and allograft: modifiable shape and size, no donor site morbidity, no disease transmission, and room for the resected body's own cancellous bone, p. 1814 (PDF 1).
- Subsidence of the TMC into the vertebral body is the most common problem, caused by the limited contact area and point loading at the construct-endplate interface, p. 1817 (PDF 4). A little subsidence may give immediate stability and help fusion; more leads to segmental kyphosis, a lost anterior column support, pseudarthrosis, a deformity that progresses, and failed anterior or posterior instrumentation, p. 1817-1818 (PDF 4-5). Several remedies have been proposed (refs 4, 10) but none meets every demand; close engagement of implant and endplate matters for stability and fusion (ref 11), hence the need for the exact endplate shape, p. 1818 (PDF 5).
- The endplate is thin dense subchondral bone, thinnest centrally and thickest peripherally, and is arched with a depressed centre, p. 1818 (PDF 5), citing Edwards.
- Conformity can be had by removing the endplate to flatten it, which weakens the body, or by shaping the construct to the endplate, p. 1818 (PDF 5). The endplate contributes 45 to 75% of peak vertebral strength (Rockoff); removing it entirely cuts compressive strength by nearly 39%, and the central region is 38% weaker than the posterolateral in the thoracic and lumbar spine (Lowe), so preserve the periphery during endplate preparation, p. 1818 (PDF 5).
- Pearl: the most concave point lies in the posterior part of the endplate, not the centre, in the midsagittal plane, confirmed by van der Houwen; implant design should follow it, p. 1819 (PDF 6).
- Pearl: superior and inferior endplates of a vertebra differ in width (superior about 91% of inferior except T12) and in coronal concavity (inferior deeper at T12, L1 and L2), p. 1816-1817 (PDF 3-4). The authors conclude only that the endplate's shape, above all its concavity, should be weighed in VBR design, p. 1814 (PDF 1) and p. 1819 (PDF 6). Inference: a cage's cranial and caudal ends would then need different footprints and domes.
- Sex differences in sagittal and transverse diameters should not be ignored, p. 1819 (PDF 6).
- Osteoporosis: bone loss usually starts in the fourth decade; bone density may fall by as much as 30% in elderly men and 50% in elderly women (Ferguson). More concavity accompanies BMD loss (Twomey), so the concave angle would fall in elderly women; yet osteoporotic fractures shorten mainly the anterior body and spare the posterior wall, which would raise it; van der Houwen found endplate depth increases about 0.01 mm a year, p. 1818 (PDF 5). This study found no age or sex effect on SCA or CCA, but BMD was unknown, p. 1818-1819 (PDF 5-6).
- Lumbar degeneration has been linked to lumbar endplate concavity (Miao); the thoracolumbar spine is less prone to degeneration and this was not studied, p. 1819 (PDF 6).
- Pitfall of definition: earlier studies measured the maximum sagittal and coronal widths of the body (border to border); this paper measures rim-to-rim endplate lengths, which the authors hold closer to VBR design and placement, p. 1819 (PDF 6).
- Segmental lordosis or kyphosis also matters for VBR fit: an implant that does not restore the local curvature leaves sagittal alignment uncorrected and loads the endplate asymmetrically. Modular systems with angled or self-adjusting endplates (refs 19, 20) address curvature, concavity matching addresses the interface; the authors call it too early to rank them and suggest combining both, p. 1819 (PDF 6).
- The authors hold that CT data can be used to compute the best-suited geometry of a new implant, p. 1814 (PDF 1) and p. 1819 (PDF 6).
- Pitfall of the sample: patients with marked osteophytes, degeneration, fracture or tumour, rotated vertebrae or a laterally slanted column were excluded, so the values describe straight, healthy spines aged 21 to 65, p. 1815 (PDF 2).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. For each vertebral instance, build a midsagittal plane in which the whole contour of the spinous process is seen, as the paper's MSP is defined (p. 1815, PDF 2); Inference: in a mask, also make it pass through the body centre. Flag a vertebra with visible axial rotation or a column with a large lateral slant, which the paper excluded (p. 1815, PDF 2), and do not hold its angles to this paper's values.
2. On that plane, place A and P on the anterior and posterior rims of each endplate face, after trimming osteophytes (excluded by the paper, p. 1815, PDF 2); SD is |AP|.
3. Take Cm as the endplate point farthest from chord AP; compute SCA as the angle A-Cm-P and LCR as |C'P| / |AP| with C' the foot of the perpendicular (p. 1815, PDF 2; Fig. 2, p. 1816, PDF 3). Fit the surface below voxel size first, since the dip is about 2 mm and 1 mm of error moves SCA about 8 deg (Inference).
4. Build the coronal section through Cm, perpendicular to the midsagittal plane; place R and L on the lateral rims and Cs at the deepest point; TD is |RL| and CCA the angle R-Cs-L (p. 1815, PDF 2; Fig. 3, p. 1816, PDF 3). Do not substitute the maximum body width (p. 1819, PDF 6).
5. Expect SCA near 163 deg (SD 4 to 5) and LCR near 43% from the posterior rim (SD 5 to 7) at every endplate from T10 inf to L3 sup; never use them to tell levels apart (Table 1, p. 1817, PDF 4). Treat an LCR above about 57% (deepest point anterior) as unusual and check the rim placement (Inference: mean + 2 SD).
6. Use SD only to split thoracic (T10-T12) from lumbar (L1-L3) within one patient: expect a step of about 1.6 mm between the T10-T12 and L1-L3 means, falling between T12 (27.8 / 24.2) and L1 (29.4 / 25.6), no significant change within each segment, and no significant difference between superior and inferior SD within a vertebra (Table 2, p. 1817, PDF 4; p. 1816, PDF 3). Inference: with a between-subject SD of 2 to 3 mm, compare within one patient, never against the population mean.
7. Use the within-vertebra width change: expect the inferior endplate about 10% wider than the superior at T11, L1 and L2, and no significant widening at T12 (Table 3, p. 1818, PDF 5; p. 1817, PDF 4). Inference: a vertebra with equal widths between a caudally widening T11-like vertebra above and L1-like vertebrae below is the T12 candidate; with SDs of 3 to 4 mm this is a weak cue, to be combined with ribs and other measures.
8. Use the inferior endplate width as the stronger T12/L1 separator (about +5 mm from T12 inf to L1 inf in men, +4.6 in women) rather than the superior width (+0.4 / +1.5 mm), and the superior width as the T11/T12 separator (+3.5 mm men, +3.3 women from T11 sup to T12 sup) rather than the inferior width (no change) (Inference, computed from Table 3, p. 1818, PDF 5).
9. Expect the superior endplate to be coronally flatter than the inferior at T12, L1 and L2 (by 2.3 to 4.2 deg) and L1 sup and L2 sup to be the flattest (about 175.5 deg); expect inferior endplates near 171 deg except L1 inf (173.4 deg) (Table 1, p. 1817, PDF 4). Inference: with SDs of 3 to 5 deg and a voxel error of several degrees, use these only as weak, within-patient cues.
10. Scale SD and TD by about 0.88 for a woman; do not scale SCA, LCR or CCA by sex; do not adjust any of them for age between 21 and 65 (pp. 1816-1817, PDF 3-4).
11. On a lateral radiograph or fluoroscopy frame, take SD as the corner-to-corner endplate length corrected for magnification and read the thoracic-lumbar step only as a within-film trend; on an AP view, take TD between the lateral corners. Treat angle measures as unreliable unless the beam is parallel to the endplate (Inference; the paper has no radiographic method).
12. Keep this paper's names apart from Panjabi's: SCA here is an angle, not the spinal canal area; SD here is a length, not a standard deviation (Inference).
13. Before trusting a level label at T12 or L1 on a thoracic or abdominal CT, count the vertebrae from a fixed reference; this paper's labels come with no stated counting method (p. 1815, PDF 2).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper reports no transitional segment, rib anomaly, T13 or L6, and no numbering method (p. 1815, PDF 2).

(b) Measures:
- The generator's Panjabi end-plate depth (EPDu, EPDl) and width (EPWu, EPWl) correspond to this paper's SD and TD, but the definitions differ: here both are rim-to-rim chords on a single section (SD on the midsagittal plane, TD on the coronal plane through the sagittal deepest point, about 57% of SD behind the anterior rim), from CT of living Chinese adults (Inference for the population). Inference (from the sibling notes panjabi1991t.md and panjabi1992.md, Panjabi's dry-bone values): Panjabi's end-plate depths at the matching endplates (EPDl T10 31.6, EPDu T11 31.9, EPDl T11 31.8, EPDu T12 32.8, EPDl T12 33.4, and about 34 to 35 at L1) run 4 to 6 mm above this paper's male SD (27.2 to 29.4 mm); Panjabi's widths agree at T10 inf and T11 sup (EPWl T10 35.4, EPWu T11 34.9 against 35.6 and 34.5 here), are 1.0 to 1.4 mm higher at T11 inf and T12 sup (39.1 against 37.7; 39.0 against 38.0), and run 2.8 to 4.5 mm higher at L1 sup (EPWu 41.2 against 38.4) and T12 inf (EPWl 42.1 against 37.6). Panjabi's thoracic spines were 8 men and 4 women, pooled, so the fair comparison is a mostly male mix, and this paper's pooled values would sit lower still. A mask's full extent will sit nearer Panjabi's outer values; do not pool without an offset or a common definition.
- A mask-derived TD taken as the maximum body width will be biased upward against this paper's TD (p. 1819, PDF 6); take the chord at the coronal section through the sagittal deepest point to match.
- Candidate additions: SCA, CCA and LCR (level-invariant in the sagittal plane, so useful as shape checks rather than level cues); the implied sagittal concavity depth (about 2 mm, derived); the inferior-to-superior TD ratio per vertebra (about 1.10 at T11, L1, L2; 0.99 to 1.01 at T12); the superior-minus-inferior CCA per vertebra; the TD/SD ratio (1.23 to 1.52, rising overall from T10 to L2 and larger at the inferior endplate of T11, L1 and L2 but not of T12). All derived ratios are Inference from Tables 1, 3 and 5.
- The generator covers C3 to L5; this paper gives only T10 inf to L3 sup and nothing on T10 sup or L3 inf.

(c) Measurement error: per-measure observer error is not given. Use the CT-cadaver mean differences (0.3 deg SCA, 0.3 mm SD, alpha 0.82 and 0.88; Table 4, p. 1818, PDF 5) as a lower bound on bias, the 0.625 mm reformat as the instrument floor, and for mask-derived angles an error of about 6 to 8 deg per mm of depth error (Inference). Between-subject spread: SD 1.8 to 3.1 mm, TD 2.4 to 4.0 mm (within sex), SCA 3.7 to 4.9 deg, CCA 3.1 to 4.8 deg, LCR 5 to 7% (Tables 1, 3, 5).

(d) Covariates: sex scales SD and TD by about 0.88 (women against men, pp. 1816-1817, PDF 3-4); no sex effect on SCA, LCR or CCA; no age effect on anything over 21 to 65 years, tested only as over 40 against 40 or under (p. 1815, PDF 2); no height, weight or BMD. Population: patients of a Chongqing hospital (Inference: Chinese); cadaver population unknown.

## Gaps and conflicts

- No concavity depth in mm, though depth is the quantity an implant or a mask most directly gives; only angles and the location ratio are reported (Table 1, p. 1817, PDF 4). The position of Cs along RL is not reported.
- No test of CCA across levels; the abstract says CCA was "constantly larger in superior endplate" (p. 1814, PDF 1), but at T11 the 0.6 deg difference is not significant (P = 0.346, p. 1816, PDF 3).
- Table 2 against Table 5: L3 female aSD 25.7 +/- 3.1 against L3 sup 25.6 +/- 3.1, and L3 male SD 2.8 against 2.7, although L3 has only its superior endplate measured (pp. 1817 and 1819, PDF 4 and 6).
- Groups A1 to B2 are not mapped to age and sex; no group values are printed; age is tested only as a dichotomy at 40 years with an upper limit of 65 (p. 1815, PDF 2).
- No observer reliability, no limits of agreement, no validation of CCA, TD or LCR, and no cadaver demographics (pp. 1815-1818, PDF 2-5).
- How levels were counted, and whether reformats followed each vertebra's axes, are not stated (p. 1815, PDF 2).
- The test behind the sex comparisons of SD and TD (all P < 0.0001 and < 0.0008) is not named; the methods list only one-way ANOVA, paired t tests and the reliability test (p. 1816, PDF 3). The P values of the four-group comparisons are given only as the least P, with no group means.
- Table 2 prints n = 430 and 400 for per-vertebra aSD values; Inference: these are the endplate counts carried over, since 43 men x 6 vertebrae would be 258 (p. 1817, PDF 4).
- The abstract states the sex difference as P < 0.05 and the CT-cadaver agreement as alpha > 0.8; the results give the sharper values (P < 0.0001 for SD, P < 0.0008 for TD; alpha 0.8191 and 0.8806), pp. 1814, 1816-1817 (PDF 1, 3-4). Not a conflict, but cite the results.
- Inference (from the reference list, p. 1820, PDF 7): the 45 to 75% strength share credited to the endplate cites Rockoff 1969, whose title concerns trabecular against cortical bone in lumbar vertebrae; whether that study isolated the endplate cannot be told from this paper.
- Van der Houwen's sagittal diameters in Table 5 agree with this paper's within about 1 mm at every shared endplate in both sexes (for example L2 inf 29.0 against 29.3 men, 26.0 against 26.0 women), so the disagreement is in the transverse diameters only, p. 1819 (PDF 6) (Inference, computed).
- Inference (from the sibling note vanderhouwen2009.md and this note's derived depths): the sagittal depth implied by this paper's angles (about 1.8 to 2.1 mm at L1 sup) is roughly twice van der Houwen's middle-point depth at the superior endplates (0.83 at L1 sup, 1.00 at L2 sup) and near his inferior values (1.46 at L1 inf, 1.86 at L2 inf). Likely reasons: this paper takes the single deepest point against the AP chord on a 0.625 mm section, van der Houwen fixed points against a rim best-fit plane on 3.13 mm average slabs, and deriving depth from mean angles is approximate.
- Conflict with van der Houwen as printed in Table 5 (p. 1819, PDF 6): the authors call the results consistent, but from L1 inf to L3 sup this paper's widths run 2.2 to 3.4 mm above van der Houwen's in men (for example 42.7 against 39.7 at L1 inf, 44.5 against 41.1 at L2 inf) and 1.7 to 5.6 mm above in women (38.3 against 32.7 at L2 inf); they agree within 0.6 mm only at T12 inf (both sexes) and L1 sup (men). Van der Houwen's women show L2 inf narrower than L2 sup (32.7 against 33.0) where this paper finds it wider (38.3 against 36.0). (Differences computed.)
- Inference (from the sibling note vanderhouwen2009.md): van der Houwen found the caudal (inferior) endplate 1.7 to 1.9 times as deep as the cranial from L1 to L4 and superior depth rising 0.01 mm a year; this paper finds no superior-inferior difference in the sagittal angle and no age effect, though its coronal angle is deeper at inferior endplates. Its age range (21 to 65) and dichotomy at 40 may hide a small trend.
- Inference (from the sibling note panjabi1991t.md): Panjabi's T12 lower end-plate width exceeds the upper by about 3 mm (42.1 against 39.0), where this paper finds T12's two widths equal (38.0 against 37.6 in men).
