# Ahn 2001: The lumbar interpediculate distance is widened in adults with the Marfan syndrome

Source: thread "Reread every paper for clinical detail", project symphony_4, 2026-10-28.

Citation as printed on the first page: Nicholas U Ahn, Uri M Ahn, Leelakrishna Nallamshetty, Peter S Rose,
Jacob M Buchowski, Elizabeth S Garrett, Khaled M Kebaish and Paul D Sponseller. The lumbar interpediculate
distance is widened in adults with the Marfan syndrome. Data from 32 cases. Acta Orthop Scand 2001; 72 (1): 67-71
(submitted 99-07-05, accepted 00-08-02, as printed on p. 67).

Copy read: Acta Orthopaedica's open archive, actaorthop.org/actao/article/download/20022/23827, fetched
2026-10-28 by this thread; sha256 begins 921a130b5e2b1f44. Five pages (journal pp. 67-71 = PDF 1-5), all read as
page images; a text layer exists and agrees with the images on the numbers checked.

## What the paper is

- Design: a case series of adults with Marfan syndrome measured on plain radiographs, compared with historical
  normal controls taken from the literature (Hinck et al. 1966); no contemporary control group (p. 67-68, PDF 1-2).
- Question: whether the lumbar interpediculate distance (IPD) is wider in Marfan adults than normal, and whether a
  per-level threshold can screen for Marfan, as protrusio acetabuli serves as a skeletal criterion in the DePaepe
  1996 diagnostic criteria (p. 67, PDF 1).
- Population: 32 living adult volunteers with Marfan syndrome diagnosed by the DePaepe (1996) revised criteria,
  diagnosis confirmed by the geneticist or by the authors (p. 68, PDF 2). 18 female and 14 male (14 by subtraction;
  the paper prints only "18 were female", p. 68, PDF 2). United States: recruited from the human genetics clinic at
  Johns Hopkins Hospital, from National Marfan Foundation chapters and from a Marfan internet newsgroup (p. 67-68,
  PDF 1-2). Ethnicity, height, weight and age of the final 32 are not reported.
- Recruitment flow (p. 68, PDF 2):
  - Clinic arm: 222 patients with a Marfan diagnosis in the clinic records; 83 aged 30 to 50 years; 51 of these
    without previous back surgery, spinal tumour, spinal stenosis or lumbar scoliosis over 20 deg; 16 available.
  - Volunteer arm: notices to the Foundation chapters and the newsgroup asked for volunteers who met "the above
    exclusion criteria"; 47 replied, 27 met the criteria, 16 could take part. Whether "the above" includes the
    30-50 year window of the clinic arm is not said (Inference: it may, since that window is part of the same
    paragraph's screen).
  - Inference: both arms are self-selected volunteers ("could participate", "were available"), so the 32 are not a
    consecutive or random Marfan series.
- Exclusion criteria as stated in the methods: previous back surgery, spinal tumour, lumbar scoliosis over 20 deg
  (p. 67, PDF 1); the clinic screen also names spinal stenosis (p. 68, PDF 2). Scoliosis was excluded because
  rotation of the vertebrae alters the projection and makes the IPD inaccurate (p. 67, PDF 1).
- Modality: standard AP and lateral radiographs of the lumbosacral spine (printed "from L1-S5") at a "standard
  distance of 101 mm" (p. 68, PDF 2). Inference: the unit is a misprint for 101 cm, that is the 40-inch
  target-film distance that Hinck et al. used for their norms (hinck1966 note), so the two series share film
  geometry. The abstract mentions only AP radiographs (p. 67, PDF 1).
- Measurement: IPD at each lumbar level L1-L5 as the shortest distance between the medial pedicle surfaces
  (p. 68, PDF 2). Instrument (caliper, ruler, digitiser), number of readers and blinding are not stated;
  no magnification correction is stated.
- Controls: normal adult IPDs of Hinck et al. 1966 (methods name "Hinck and Clark (1966)", p. 68; the table footnote
  prints "Hinck et al. 1996"), with n at each level L1 59, L2 57, L3 53, L4 52, L5 50 (Table, p. 68, PDF 2).
  Inference from the hinck1966 note: those are 40-inch AP films of white hospital patients over 18 from Portland,
  Oregon, sexes combined, uncorrected for magnification. The discussion explains the choice: the earlier normal
  series of Elsberg and Dyke (1934) and Schwarz (1956) gave no sample size or SD, so only Hinck's could be used for
  a statistical comparison, and Hinck's is called the most useful and most widely cited (p. 69, PDF 3).
- Background the authors give for trusting the film IPD: the plain-film norms (Schwarz 1956, Hinck 1966) have since
  been checked against CT and anatomic measurement in later studies (Eisenstein 1977, Ullrich 1980, Malmivaara
  1986) (p. 67, PDF 1). Malmivaara's title in the reference list is a radiographic against direct measurement of
  the canal at T10-L1 (p. 70, PDF 4). No correlation coefficient or offset is quoted.
- Statistics: unpaired t-test of the Marfan means against Hinck's means, possible because Hinck published n, mean
  and SD (p. 68, PDF 2). A per-level "cut-off value" = normal mean + 2 SD, called a specificity of 95%; the
  sensitivity at that cut-off computed by assuming the Marfan IPDs at each level are normally distributed
  (p. 68, PDF 2; figure caption p. 69, PDF 3).
- Levels covered: L1 to L5 only. How levels were identified and counted is not stated. Inference: the 5 patients
  with lumbosacral transitional vertebrae were not excluded, since every level has n = 32 (Table, p. 68, PDF 2) and
  no exclusion for an anomaly is listed (p. 67-68, PDF 1-2); they were then measured at L1-L5 under a numbering the
  paper does not describe.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- Lumbosacral transitional vertebrae: 5 of the 32 Marfan patients (15.6%) (p. 68, PDF 2; repeated p. 70, PDF 4).
  Recognised on the AP and lateral lumbosacral films (Inference: the only images taken). Type (sacralisation,
  lumbarisation, Castellvi class), side and the level numbering used in these 5 are not given.
  Inference (my arithmetic): Wilson 95% interval 6.9% to 31.8%.
- The authors cite Tallroth et al. (1995) for a high prevalence of developmental anomalies in the Marfan lumbar
  spine: biconcave vertebrae, transitional vertebrae and a lengthened transverse process distance; no number is
  quoted (p. 70, PDF 4). They offer the 5 transitional vertebrae as support for abnormal growth and formation of
  the vertebral elements in Marfan (p. 70, PDF 4).
- Spondylolisthesis on the lateral radiograph: 4 patients, 2 isthmic and 2 degenerative, mean slip 26% (p. 68,
  PDF 2). The discussion instead calls it 5/32 (p. 70, PDF 4) but then speaks of "2 of the 4 cases" as degenerative
  (p. 70, PDF 4). The level of the slips is not stated. Inference (my arithmetic): 4/32 = 12.5%, Wilson 95%
  interval 5.0% to 28.1%. Comparison cited: Sponseller et al. (1995), 5 spondylolistheses among 82 skeletally
  mature Marfan patients (6.1%; Inference, my arithmetic: Wilson 95% 2.6% to 13.5%), youngest 16 years (p. 70,
  PDF 4). The authors say their population had a "higher mean age" than Sponseller's but never print that age
  (p. 70, PDF 4).
- Dural ectasia: not imaged in this study (only AP and lateral radiographs were taken, p. 68, PDF 2). Cited:
  Pyeritz et al. (1988) found dural ectasia, the dural sac ballooning in its most caudal part, in the lumbosacral
  spine of two thirds of Marfan patients (p. 70, PDF 4; the reference's title calls dural ectasia a common feature
  of the syndrome, p. 70, PDF 4); Arroyo and Garcia (1989), one Marfan patient with dural ectasia, an anterior
  sacral meningocele and widened IPDs (p. 67 and p. 70, PDF 1 and 4); Nelson (1958), 4 Marfan patients, 2 with
  widened and 2 with normal IPDs (p. 67, PDF 1). The authors propose dural ectasia as one cause of the widened
  IPDs here, and also as a cause of the spondylolistheses through bony erosion and destabilisation of the
  lumbosacral spine (p. 70, PDF 4); neither link is tested.
- Scoliosis and kyphosis: cited Sponseller 1995 and 1997, irregular patterns of scoliosis and kyphosis in children
  with Marfan (p. 70, PDF 4); lumbar scoliosis over 20 deg was an exclusion here (p. 67, PDF 1).
- Nothing on T13, L6 as such, 11 or 13 rib pairs, cervical ribs or block vertebrae.
- What the anomaly does to the measures: not analysed. Inference: in a patient with an LSTV, the vertebra called
  L5 may be transitional and its IPD shifted toward the sacral value; this may contribute to the large L5 SD
  in the Marfan group (4.7 mm, the largest of the table, p. 68, PDF 2).

## Measures, each as a recipe

### Interpediculate distance (IPD)

- Definition: "the shortest distance between the medial surfaces of the pedicles" (p. 68, PDF 2), at L1, L2, L3,
  L4 and L5. The methods do not name the film it was read on; the abstract says the IPDs came from standard AP
  radiographs of the lumbar spine (p. 67, PDF 1), and a transverse distance can only come from the AP
  (Inference). The same quantity is called interpedicular distance in the discussion (p. 69, PDF 3).
- Instrument: not stated. Magnification: not corrected; film geometry printed as 101 "mm" (Inference: 101 cm,
  40 in) (p. 68, PDF 2).
- Values, mean / SD in mm, n (Table, p. 68, PDF 2):
  - Normal adults (Hinck): L1 25.0 / 2.2 (59); L2 25.5 / 2.3 (57); L3 26.0 / 2.7 (53); L4 26.9 / 3.0 (52);
    L5 29.7 / 3.7 (50).
  - Marfan adults: L1 29.5 / 3.0 (32); L2 30.3 / 2.5 (32); L3 32.1 / 2.4 (32); L4 34.8 / 2.8 (32);
    L5 39.6 / 4.7 (32). The same means are given in the results text (p. 68, PDF 2).
  - Unpaired t-test, Marfan against normal: p < 0.001 at every level (Table, P-value column, p. 68, PDF 2).
    Inference (my arithmetic, Welch t from the Table): t about 7.5 at L1 rising to 12.2 at L4 and 10.1 at L5.
  - The Table caption attaches a different p, "< 0.0001", to the cut-off values, saying they differ from the means
    of both the normal and the Marfan spines (p. 68, PDF 2). Inference: that cannot hold at L1 or L2, where the
    cut-off (29.4, 30.1) lies only 0.1 and 0.2 mm below the Marfan mean (29.5, 30.3); the caption's claim is
    unclear and should not be used.
  - Figure (p. 69, PDF 3): five panels, L1 to L5, each with two normal density curves drawn from the Table's means
    and SDs (solid for Hinck's normals, dotted for Marfan) and a solid vertical line at the cut-off. They are fitted
    model curves; no individual patient value is plotted anywhere in the paper.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference throughout; the paper is radiographic):
  1. Build the vertebra's local frame: craniocaudal axis normal to the end plates, AP axis from the body centroid
     toward the spinous process, left-right axis orthogonal to both.
  2. Isolate the two pedicles: the bone that bridges the posterior body wall and the articular-laminar mass on
     each side (from the posterior-elements subregion where SPINEPS gives one, restricted in AP to the segment
     between the posterior body wall and the front of the articular processes).
  3. Film-equivalent IPD: project the pedicle voxels along the AP axis onto the coronal plane; for each
     craniocaudal row crossing both pedicles, take (medial-most x of the right pedicle) minus (medial-most x of the
     left pedicle); the IPD is the smallest such gap. This is what a reading of the projected pedicle rings gives.
  4. The true axial alternative, the minimum left-right gap between the medial pedicle walls in any axial slice
     through the pedicles, agrees with step 3 when the walls are parallel to the AP axis and is never smaller.
  5. To compare with Ahn's or Hinck's film values, multiply the true CT value by the film magnification
     (about 1.07 to 1.17 for a supine AP lumbar film at 40 in, Inference carried from the hinck1966 note), or
     compare ratios between levels, which cancel magnification.
  6. In a patient with dural ectasia the pedicles' medial cortices may be thinned or eroded, so the mask shows a
     wider bony gap at the pedicles; that is the very signal the paper measures, not a segmentation error. (The
     paper itself raises bony erosion from dural ectasia only as a cause of spondylolisthesis, p. 70, PDF 4, and
     says nothing of pedicle erosion.)
- From a radiograph alone: AP (or PA) radiograph only; the lateral cannot give a transverse dimension. Values are
  film values, magnified and uncorrected (p. 68, PDF 2). Axial rotation of the vertebra, as in a lumbar scoliosis
  over 20 deg, alters the projection and makes the IPD inaccurate (p. 67, PDF 1). AP fluoroscopy can give it with
  the C-arm's own magnification (Inference).
- Across levels (Table, p. 68, PDF 2; arithmetic mine, Inference):
  - Both groups rise monotonically from L1 to L5, largest at L5.
  - Steps between adjacent means, normal: L1-L2 +0.5, L2-L3 +0.5, L3-L4 +0.9, L4-L5 +2.8 mm. Marfan: +0.8, +1.8,
    +2.7, +4.8 mm. In both groups L4/L5 is the largest step; L1-L3 are nearly flat in normals (within one SD) and
    cannot be told apart by IPD.
  - Excess of Marfan over normal: L1 +4.5, L2 +4.8, L3 +6.1, L4 +7.9, L5 +9.9 mm; ratio Marfan / normal 1.18,
    1.19, 1.23, 1.29, 1.33. The widening grows caudally, so it is not a uniform scale factor.
  - Standardised difference (pooled SD): L1 1.8, L2 2.0, L3 2.3, L4 2.7, L5 2.4. L4 separates the groups best,
    which matches the paper's choice of L4 (p. 68 and p. 70, PDF 2 and 4); L5 has the largest raw excess but the
    largest SDs.
  - Overlap with other levels: the Marfan L1 mean (29.5) nearly equals the normal L5 mean (29.7); the Marfan L2-L5
    means all exceed the normal L5 mean; the Marfan L5 mean (39.6) exceeds the upper limit of Hinck's adult 90%
    range at L5 (37 mm, hinck1966 note).
- Sex, age, size, population: no split by sex, age or body size; 18 female and 14 male; age of the final sample not
  reported (the clinic arm drew from patients aged 30-50, p. 68, PDF 2); height not reported. Controls are
  Hinck's combined-sex adults (Inference from the hinck1966 note: males about 1 mm larger than females). Inference:
  Marfan patients are tall, so part of the widening could be body size, but a size effect would scale every level
  by one ratio, whereas the ratio rises from 1.18 at L1 to 1.33 at L5, which fits a caudal process such as dural
  ectasia better.
- Error: not measured in this study. The authors cite earlier work showing the IPD can be read on plain films
  with small variation between observers (Schwarz 1956, Simril and Thurston 1955, Hinck and Clark 1966, Roberson
  1973, McCall 1993) and take the agreement of the normal series of Elsberg and Dyke (1934), Schwarz (1956) and
  Hinck (1966) as evidence that IPD is reliable and reproducible (p. 69, PDF 3). Inference (my arithmetic): the
  standard error of each Marfan mean is SD / sqrt(32), 0.42 to 0.53 mm at L1-L4 and 0.83 mm at L5.

### "Cut-off value" (screening threshold per level)

- Definition: normal mean + 2 x normal SD at that level, from Hinck's data; an IPD above it is read as positive for
  Marfan (p. 68, PDF 2; Table footnote b; figure caption p. 69, PDF 3).
- Values (Table, p. 68, PDF 2): L1 29.4, L2 30.1, L3 31.4, L4 32.9, L5 37.1 mm; specificity stated 95% at every
  level; sensitivity L1 51%, L2 53%, L3 61%, L4 75%, L5 70%. The L4 cut-off is rounded to 33 mm in the discussion
  (p. 70, PDF 4).
- How sensitivity was computed: from a normal distribution fitted to the Marfan mean and SD at each level, not by
  counting the 32 patients (p. 68, PDF 2). Inference (my arithmetic): every printed sensitivity is reproduced
  exactly as P(Z > (cut-off - Marfan mean) / Marfan SD).
- Inference: the authors seem to have taken the two-sided 95% normal range (mean +/- about 2 SD) and used its upper
  end as a one-sided screen. Under normality mean + 2 SD gives a one-sided specificity of 97.7%, not 95%; a true
  one-sided 95% cut (mean + 1.645 SD) would be L1 28.6, L2 29.3, L3 30.4, L4 31.8, L5 35.8 mm, with model
  sensitivities about 62%, 66%, 76%, 86% and 79%.
- Inference: the rounded L4 cut-off, 33 mm, equals the upper limit of Hinck's adult 90% tolerance range at L4
  (21-33 mm, hinck1966 note).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: only after putting the CT value on the film scale (see IPD, step 5) (Inference).
- From a radiograph alone: AP radiograph at about 40 in source-film distance, as for IPD.
- Error: no sampling uncertainty is given for the cut-offs or sensitivities; the Marfan sample is 32 (Inference:
  the sensitivity at L4 rests on a normal model of 32 values; had 24 of 32 been counted above the cut-off, 75%,
  the Wilson 95% interval would be 57.9% to 86.7%, my arithmetic). The cut-offs inherit the sampling error of
  Hinck's means and SDs (n 50 to 59 per level).

### Spondylolisthesis slip (lateral radiograph)

- Reported only as present on the lateral radiograph, 2 isthmic and 2 degenerative, mean slip 26% (p. 68, PDF 2).
  The slip measurement method, the levels and the individual values are not given.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the AP offset of the posterior-inferior corner of the upper body from the
  posterior-superior corner of the lower body, as a percentage of the lower end plate's AP depth, in the
  midsagittal plane.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral (p. 68, PDF 2).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1: normal 25.0 / 2.2, Marfan 29.5 / 3.0, cut-off 29.4, sensitivity 51% (Table, p. 68, PDF 2). The least
  discriminating level; the Marfan L1 matches a normal L5 in absolute value (Inference).
- L2: normal 25.5 / 2.3, Marfan 30.3 / 2.5, cut-off 30.1, sensitivity 53% (Table, p. 68, PDF 2).
- L3: normal 26.0 / 2.7, Marfan 32.1 / 2.4, cut-off 31.4, sensitivity 61% (Table, p. 68, PDF 2).
- L4: normal 26.9 / 3.0, Marfan 34.8 / 2.8, cut-off 32.9 (33 in the text), sensitivity 75%, the best level for
  screening (Table, p. 68; p. 70, PDF 2 and 4).
- L5: normal 29.7 / 3.7, Marfan 39.6 / 4.7, cut-off 37.1, sensitivity 70%; largest absolute widening and largest
  spread (Table, p. 68, PDF 2).
- L5/S1 and the lumbosacral region: 5 LSTVs in 32 (p. 68 and p. 70, PDF 2 and 4). 4 (or 5) spondylolistheses in
  32, at levels the paper never states; the discussion links them to destabilisation of the lumbosacral spine
  (p. 68 and p. 70, PDF 2 and 4). Dural ectasia is described in the cited literature as ballooning of the sac's
  most caudal part, in the lumbosacral spine, with an anterior sacral meningocele in one cited case (p. 67 and
  p. 70, PDF 1 and 4). S1 and the sacrum were on the films ("L1-S5") but no sacral IPD is reported (p. 68, PDF 2).
- T12/L1: not studied; L1 is the most cranial level measured. The paper cites Malmivaara (1986), a radiographic
  against direct study of the canal at T10-L1, only as support that film IPDs have been correlated with CT and
  anatomy (p. 67 and reference list p. 70, PDF 1 and 4). C2/C3 and C7/T1: not covered.

## Measurement error and reliability

- No intra- or inter-observer data, no repeat readings, no reader count, no instrument resolution (p. 68, PDF 2).
- Validity is argued from the literature too: the plain-film norms have been correlated with CT and anatomic
  measurement (Eisenstein 1977, Ullrich 1980, Malmivaara 1986; p. 67, PDF 1); no offset or agreement figure is
  given.
- Reliability is argued from the literature: little interobserver variability on plain films (Simril and Thurston
  1955, Schwarz 1956, Hinck and Clark 1966, Roberson 1973, McCall 1993) and agreement of normal values among
  Elsberg and Dyke 1934, Schwarz 1956 and Hinck 1966 (p. 69, PDF 3). The earlier series of Elsberg and Dyke and of
  Schwarz gave no sample sizes or SDs, so only Hinck's could serve for statistical comparison (p. 69, PDF 3).
- Projection error: scoliosis over 20 deg excluded because rotation alters the projected IPD (p. 67, PDF 1).
- Magnification: not corrected; both series at about the same source-film distance (Inference, see above).
- Biological spread: normal SD 2.2 (L1) to 3.7 (L5) mm; Marfan SD 2.4 (L3) to 4.7 (L5) mm (Table, p. 68, PDF 2).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication: the IPD as a cheap, noninvasive skeletal criterion or screening test for Marfan syndrome, in the way
  protrusio acetabuli is used in the DePaepe criteria (p. 67 and p. 69, PDF 1 and 3).
- Pearl: lumbar IPDs above the per-level cut-offs are "highly indicative of Marfan syndrome" (Table caption,
  p. 68, PDF 2); an L4 IPD over 33 mm is the best single screen, specificity 95% and sensitivity 75% as the paper
  states them (p. 67 abstract and p. 70, PDF 1 and 4).
- Mechanisms proposed for the widening (p. 70, PDF 4): (1) abnormal growth and formation of the vertebral elements
  from the connective tissue disorder (with the developmental anomalies Tallroth described and the 5 LSTVs here);
  (2) dural ectasia, present in the lumbosacral spine in two thirds of Marfan patients (Pyeritz 1988). Neither was
  tested here.
- Spondylolisthesis in Marfan: may follow dural ectasia eroding bone and destabilising the lumbosacral spine, or
  weak connective tissue support of the spinal columns allowing translation (p. 70, PDF 4). The higher prevalence
  than Sponseller's 5 of 82 may come from stricter DePaepe diagnosis or from the older age of this sample; 2 of the
  4 slips being degenerative points to age (p. 70, PDF 4).
- Differential of a widened IPD (p. 69, PDF 3): trauma (Quesada 1975), tumours (Elsberg and Dyke 1934, Nelson 1958,
  Natarajan 1975), diastematomyelia (Nelson 1958), neurofibromatosis (Mitchell 1967), and now Marfan.
- Differential of a narrowed IPD (p. 69, PDF 3): achondroplasia (Schorr 1977), diastrophic dwarfism (Bethem 1980),
  acrodysostosis (Butler 1988, Hamanishi 1993), cloacal exstrophy (Weaver 1997).
- Pitfall, projection: a rotated vertebra (lumbar scoliosis over 20 deg) gives an inaccurate projected IPD
  (p. 67, PDF 1).
- Pitfall, level: Inference: because the Marfan canal widens more at each more caudal level, a Marfan lumbar
  vertebra carries the IPD of a more caudal normal vertebra (Marfan L1 about a normal L5; Marfan L2 to L5 wider than
  any normal lumbar mean; Table, p. 68, PDF 2). Combined with a transitional segment (5 of 32), this makes a level
  look like the one below it, or like a sacral segment, if IPD is read as an absolute level cue. Dural ectasia, as
  the cited mechanism, is therefore a pitfall for numbering, not only a diagnosis.
- Pitfall, comparison: the norms are 1966 film values from another population; no contemporary, age- and
  sex-matched controls (p. 68, PDF 2; Inference on the consequences).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. On an AP lumbar radiograph or AP fluoroscopy frame, measure each lumbar IPD as the shortest distance between
   the medial cortices of the left and right pedicle shadows (p. 68, PDF 2; AP films, abstract p. 67, PDF 1);
   do not try it on a lateral, which shows no transverse width (Inference: geometry).
2. Before using IPD, check rotation: where a lumbar curve exceeds about 20 deg, mark the IPD at those levels
   unreliable, since the rotated vertebra's projection makes it inaccurate (p. 67, PDF 1). Inference: also flag a
   level whose spinous process sits off the midline between the pedicles, a sign of axial rotation the paper does
   not name.
3. Put every IPD on the film scale before comparing with the tables: Ahn's and Hinck's values are uncorrected film
   values at about 101 cm (40 in) source-film distance (p. 68, PDF 2; unit corrected by Inference); for a CT mask,
   multiply the true width by an assumed magnification, or compare between-level ratios, which cancel it.
4. Screen the lumbar profile against the normal mean + 2 SD cut-offs: L1 29.4, L2 30.1, L3 31.4, L4 32.9 (33),
   L5 37.1 mm (Table, p. 68, PDF 2). If L4 exceeds 33 mm, the paper's best single screen (p. 70, PDF 4), enter a
   "widened canal" state whose differential is Marfan, neurofibromatosis, tumour, trauma or diastematomyelia
   (p. 69, PDF 3). Inference: several levels above their cut-offs strengthen the call, though the paper tests
   each level alone and never a combination.
5. In the widened-canal state, stop using absolute IPD as evidence of level: a Marfan L1 (mean 29.5) reads like a
   normal L5 (29.7) (Table, p. 68, PDF 2). Use only the within-patient order (IPD still rises from L1 to L5 in
   Marfan) and other anchors: the last rib-bearing vertebra, the iliac crests, the sacral ala, a count from C2.
   (Inference for the anchors.)
6. Expect larger steps between adjacent levels in the widened state: L3-L4 +2.7 mm and L4-L5 +4.8 mm in Marfan
   against +0.9 and +2.8 in normals (Table, p. 68, PDF 2). Do not take a Marfan L3/L4 step for the normal L4/L5
   step and call the Marfan L4 an L5 (Inference).
7. In the widened-canal state raise the prior of a lumbosacral transitional vertebra: 5 of 32 Marfan adults had one
   (p. 68, PDF 2), and Tallroth is cited for a high prevalence of transitional vertebrae in Marfan (p. 70, PDF 4).
   Inference: count from a cranial anchor rather than from the sacrum.
8. On the lateral, look for spondylolisthesis (4 of 32, 2 isthmic and 2 degenerative, mean slip 26%; p. 68,
   PDF 2); the paper gives no levels but ties the slips to the lumbosacral spine (p. 70, PDF 4). Inference: a slip
   shifts the body outlines and should not break the chain of instances.
9. On the lateral, also look for posterior scalloping of the lumbar and sacral bodies and on CT or MRI for a
   widened dural sac or an anterior sacral meningocele, the signs of dural ectasia (Inference from memory; this
   paper cites the meningocele case, p. 67, PDF 1, and lists Mitchell 1967 on scalloped vertebrae among its
   references, p. 70, PDF 4, but measures neither).
10. If the IPD instead fails to widen caudally or falls below normal, think of the narrowed-IPD conditions
    (achondroplasia, diastrophic dwarfism, acrodysostosis, cloacal exstrophy; p. 69, PDF 3) and expect the
    opposite level bias, a vertebra reading like a more cranial one (Inference).
11. Report the IPD with its uncertainty: the paper gives no reading error of its own (p. 68, PDF 2); carry the
    biological SD of 2.2-3.7 mm (normal) and 2.4-4.7 mm (Marfan) at each level (Table, p. 68, PDF 2).
12. Use the L4 to L5 jump as the strongest within-patient IPD cue to the last lumbar vertebra: it is the largest
    adjacent step in both groups, +2.8 mm in normals and +4.8 mm in Marfan, against at most +0.9 and +2.7 mm
    between the levels above (Table, p. 68, PDF 2; steps my arithmetic). Inference: with a transitional segment
    the jump may sit one level off, so confirm with the sacral ala and the iliac crests.

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder:
- Lumbosacral transitional vertebra in adult Marfan syndrome (US volunteers, DePaepe criteria, 18 F / 14 M):
  5/32 = 15.6%, Wilson 95% 6.9-31.8% (count p. 68, PDF 2; interval my arithmetic). Type and numbering unknown, so
  it cannot be split into sacralised L5 against lumbarised S1.
- Spondylolisthesis in the same group: 4/32 = 12.5% (p. 68, PDF 2) or 5/32 (p. 70, PDF 4); cited Sponseller 1995:
  5/82 skeletally mature Marfan (p. 70, PDF 4).
- Dural ectasia (cited, Pyeritz 1988): lumbosacral, in two thirds of Marfan patients (p. 70, PDF 4).
- No general-population prevalence is given. Inference: model Marfan or dural ectasia as a patient-level latent
  state that raises the LSTV transition probability and changes the IPD emissions; its prior is small in a general
  surgical cohort.

(b) Measures the generator holds or could add, and definitional bias:
- IPD here is the shortest medial-pedicle gap as projected on an AP film, magnified. Panjabi's SCW (lumbar) is a
  true-size cadaveric canal width drawn across the canal near mid-depth, at the pedicles by inference, with no
  statement of whether it is the widest chord or the pedicle minimum (Inference from the panjabi1992 note, which
  gives L1 23.7 to L5 27.1 mm). A mask value computed as the widest canal width will run above a projected minimum;
  a film value runs above a true-size one by the magnification (Hinck L1 25.0 against Panjabi 23.7, L5 29.7 against
  27.1: ratios 1.05 to 1.10, Inference). Eisenstein 1977 used "the greatest interpedicular distance" on dry bone
  (Inference from the eisenstein1977 note), the opposite extreme to Ahn's and Hinck's shortest distance.
- Candidate addition: a Marfan or dural-ectasia mixture component for canal width with per-level multiplicative
  factors relative to normal of 1.18, 1.19, 1.23, 1.29, 1.33 (L1 to L5) and SDs of 3.0, 2.5, 2.4, 2.8, 4.7 mm on the
  film scale (Inference from the Table, p. 68, PDF 2). This keeps the size factor from absorbing a syndrome.
- Candidate addition: adjacent-level IPD ratios as a magnification- and size-free observable for AP films and
  fluoroscopy; in normals L5/L4 1.10 and L4/L3 1.03, in Marfan 1.14 and 1.08 (Inference, arithmetic on the Table).
- The paper gives nothing on end plates, body heights, canal depth, pedicle dimensions or facets.

(c) Measurement error: none measured. Use another source's reading error (Hinck's, about 0.3 mm, Inference from
the hinck1966 note) plus a projection term for rotation; the per-level SDs in the Table (p. 68, PDF 2) are
biological spread plus error. Sampling error of the Marfan means (Inference, my arithmetic): SD / sqrt(32), 0.42 to
0.53 mm at L1-L4 and 0.83 mm at L5; of the L4 sensitivity, roughly 58% to 87% (Wilson, as if 24 of 32).

(d) Covariates: sex mix 18 F / 14 M with no split; age not reported (clinic arm 30-50 years); height and weight not
reported though Marfan patients are tall; population US, ethnicity unstated (p. 68, PDF 2). Inference: the
caudally rising ratio argues that the widening is not a pure size effect, so the generator's patient size factor
should not be stretched to explain it.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Unit misprint: "standard distance of 101 mm" (p. 68, PDF 2); a film distance of 101 mm is impossible; 101 cm
  (40 in) is meant (Inference).
- "L1-S5" for the extent of the lumbosacral films (p. 68, PDF 2) is loose wording; no sacral IPD is reported.
- Abstract says AP radiographs only; methods say AP and lateral (p. 67 and p. 68, PDF 1 and 2).
- Exclusion criteria: spinal stenosis appears in the clinic screen (p. 68, PDF 2) but not in the methods' list
  (p. 67, PDF 1).
- Spondylolisthesis: 4 in the methods (p. 68) against 5/32 in the discussion (p. 70), which then speaks of the
  4 cases (PDF 2 and 4).
- p-values: the table column gives "< 0.001" for Marfan against normal; the caption gives "p < 0.0001" for the
  cut-off values differing from both groups' means, which cannot be true at L1 and L2, where the cut-off is
  0.1-0.2 mm below the Marfan mean (p. 68, PDF 2; Inference on the impossibility).
- Age: the discussion explains the higher spondylolisthesis prevalence by the "higher mean age" of the sample but
  gives no age, mean or range for the 32 (p. 68 and p. 70, PDF 2 and 4).
- Recruitment: whether the volunteer arm was held to the clinic arm's 30-50 year window is not said (p. 68, PDF 2).
- No per-patient data: the Figure shows only fitted normal curves (p. 69, PDF 3), so the normality the
  sensitivities rest on cannot be checked.
- Control citation: "Hinck and Clark (1966)" in the methods, "Hinck et al. 1966" in the text and references (three
  authors: Hinck, Clark, Hopkins), "Hinck et al. 1996" in the table footnote (p. 68 and p. 70, PDF 2 and 4).
- Specificity: mean + 2 SD is called 95%; under the stated normal assumption it is 97.7% one-sided (Inference).
- Sensitivities come from a normal model, not from counts of the 32 patients; no confidence intervals (p. 68,
  PDF 2).
- Not stated: instrument, readers, blinding, reliability, magnification correction, level identification and
  numbering, LSTV type and handling, level of each slip, age, height, ethnicity, and any per-patient IPDs.
- Dural ectasia is proposed but not imaged; the widening is not related to dural sac size in any patient
  (p. 70, PDF 4).
- Historical controls (1966, another population, sexes combined) against a 1990s sample; no matching (p. 68,
  PDF 2).
- Inference, against other papers in this set: Hinck screened out subjects with "significant anomalies"
  (hinck1966 note) while Ahn kept 5 LSTV patients, so the two groups differ in anomaly content as well as in
  diagnosis. Hinck's adult n per level (59, 57, 53, 52, 50) in Ahn's table matches the hinck1966 note.
