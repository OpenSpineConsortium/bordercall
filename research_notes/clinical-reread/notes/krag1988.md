## What the paper is

- Design: retrospective review of all thoracic and lumbar CT scans done at the Medical Center Hospital of Vermont from September 1982 to November 1983, plus a small cadaver validation; p. 27 (PDF 1).
- Population: 91 vertebrae, T9 to L5, from 41 living patients, 14 women and 27 men, aged 18 to 75 years (mean 36, median 28); p. 27 (PDF 1). Country USA (Vermont); ethnicity, height and weight are not reported. Final diagnoses of the 41: ten fractured vertebrae, seven herniated discs, five bulging discs, three unspecified cord lesions, two bony degenerative changes, fourteen normal studies (of the fourteen normal ones, six done for suspected herniated disc, five in multitrauma, two for suspected fracture, one for suspected degeneration); p. 27 (PDF 1).
- Pedicle denominators (Table 1, p. 29, PDF 3; Table 2, p. 30, PDF 4): T9 14, T10 18, T11 22, T12 24, L1 22, L2 14, L3 24, L4 24, L5 20; total 182 pedicles = 2 x 91. Inference: both pedicles of every vertebra were measured, so the vertebrae per level are T9 7, T10 9, T11 11, T12 12, L1 11, L2 7, L3 12, L4 12, L5 10. Several vertebrae come from one patient, and two pedicles from one vertebra, so the observations are not independent; the paper does not address this.
- Exclusions: vertebrae that were fractured, or next to internal fixation (metal artefact made measurement less reliable); p. 27 (PDF 1). No other inclusion rule is given; age and bone density were not analysed, bone density was not available retrospectively, and the subjects were not balanced for age; pp. 28-29 (PDF 2-3).
- Modality: Siemens Somatom 2 third-generation CT, 4-mm cuts, 5-s scan time; p. 27 (PDF 1). Data were recalled from magnetic diskettes; for each vertebra the one transverse section on which both pedicles looked largest (the "midpedicle cut") was printed on x-ray film at 80% of life size; p. 27 (PDF 1). Gantry tilt is not stated.
- Instrument: the film was backlit on a horizontal viewbox; lines drawn on it with a 0.5-mm soft-lead mechanical pencil and a 1 x 1 mm grid overlay (graph velum); lengths with dividers and a 0.5-mm ruled scale, read to the nearest 0.33 mm and multiplied by 1.25 for life size; angles with a hand-held protractor to the nearest 0.5 deg; pp. 27-28 (PDF 1-2). Who measured, and how many observers, is not stated.
- Cadaver validation: 8 cadaver vertebrae (1 T12, 3 L2, 3 L3, 1 L4), inferior end plates potted in methylmethacrylate and mounted in jigs, CT-scanned as the patients were and measured the same way, then cut with a band saw through the midpedicle in the transverse plane parallel to the end plates; chord length at 0 deg, pedicle diameter perpendicular to the pedicle axis and body length compared, caliper against CT; p. 28 (PDF 2), Fig. 7 p. 31 (PDF 5). Donor age, sex and source are not given.
- Levels: T9 to L5 only.
- Level identification: not described anywhere in the paper. Transitional segments are not mentioned.
- Comparison series: Saillant 1976 (ref. 9), plotted as means only in Figs. 2, 5 and 6; the authors note Saillant gave no SD or range and gave pedicle dimensions only in the sagittal and coronal planes, though the pedicle often runs obliquely and screws may be wanted along it; p. 27 (PDF 1). They also note that Zindrick et al. (ref. 10) published pedicle data after this study was done; p. 27 (PDF 1).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about transitional vertebrae, rib counts, L6, T13, sacralisation, lumbarisation or how the levels were numbered; nothing on pp. 27-32 (PDF 1-6). The only exclusions that touch anatomy are fractured vertebrae and those next to hardware (p. 27, PDF 1).

## Measures, each as a recipe

All measures are taken on one axial CT image per vertebra, the midpedicle cut, in a reference frame the authors build by hand (Fig. 1, p. 28, PDF 2):

- AP midline axis: the grid is rotated until one grid line runs across both transverse processes while the perpendicular grid line bisects both the vertebral body and the spinal canal; that perpendicular is the AP midline axis; pp. 27-28 (PDF 1-2), Fig. 1A.
- Line A: perpendicular to the AP midline axis, tangent to the most anterior point of the spinal canal; p. 28 (PDF 2), Fig. 1A.
- Line B: parallel to line A, along the flattest part of the posterior cortex of the lamina; p. 28 (PDF 2), Fig. 1A.
- Partition: anterior to A is "body"; between A and B is "pedicle"; posterior to B, in the region of the pedicle, is "facets"; p. 28 (PDF 2).
- Pedicle longitudinal axis: drawn through the middle of the pedicle by visual best fit with the grid; at the narrowest width of the pedicle a perpendicular to the axis is drawn, and the four screw paths pass through that intersection point (here called the isthmus centre); p. 28 (PDF 2), Fig. 1B.
- Sign: angulation anteromedially (anterior end toward the midline) is positive, anterolaterally negative; p. 28 (PDF 2).

Inference, for every mask recipe below: build the same frame from the instance mask. Take the slice plane perpendicular to the vertebra's own cranio-caudal axis (from the body's end-plate normals) at the height where the left and right pedicle cross-sections are largest. In that plane, take the AP axis as the mirror-symmetry axis of the whole instance (body plus posterior elements), checked against a perpendicular to the line joining the two transverse-process tips. The canal is the background hole enclosed by the instance in that plane; line A is the perpendicular to the AP axis at the canal's most anterior point. The paper itself picks the slice by eye on a scanner-axial stack.

### Chord length (screw-path length) at 0, 5, 10 and 15 deg (CL0, CL5, CL10, CL15)

- Landmarks: from line B, along a straight path through the isthmus centre at 0, 5, 10 or 15 deg to the AP midline (aimed anteromedially, entering posterolaterally), to where the path meets the anterior cortex of the body; p. 28 (PDF 2), Fig. 1C. Line B, not the posterior cortex, is the start so that variable facet anatomy on CT does not enter; p. 28 (PDF 2). The Fig. 1C caption calls it anterior to posterior cortex, and conclusion 4 calls it posterior lamina cortex to anterior body cortex; p. 28 (PDF 2), p. 32 (PDF 6).
- Plane: axial midpedicle CT slice. Levels: T9 to L5. Only 0 and 15 deg are reported per level (Fig. 2, Table 1); 5 and 10 deg appear only in the body-length factors (p. 29, PDF 3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: in the midpedicle plane, find the pedicle's narrowest width and its midpoint (the isthmus centre); cast a ray through it at angle theta from the AP axis, pointing anteromedially; start the length at the ray's crossing of line B (the posterior lamina surface, fitted as a line perpendicular to the AP axis, excluding the spinous process and facets) and end it where the ray leaves the anterior surface of the body mask. Needs body and posterior elements in one instance or both subregions joined.
- From a radiograph alone: not measurable directly; the authors propose predicting it from the mid-body AP depth on a lateral radiograph (see the factors under body length); p. 28 (PDF 2), p. 29 (PDF 3), p. 32 (PDF 6). Magnification of the lateral film is not discussed.
- Across levels (approx., Fig. 2, p. 28, PDF 2): CL0 T9 45.0, T10 44.2, T11 41.6, T12 40.9, L1 42.8, L2 44.4, L3 40.7, L4 39.8, L5 36.0 mm; CL15 T9 53.9, T10 51.8, T11 50.4, T12 50.9, L1 50.9, L2 51.8, L3 49.5, L4 47.4, L5 44.9 mm. Means over all levels 42 mm at 0 deg and 50 mm at 15 deg; p. 29 (PDF 3). Fairly constant from T9 to L5, shortest at L5. No adjacent step reaches the SD of both levels it joins; the largest steps are L4 to L5 at 0 deg (about 3.8 mm, SDs 4.6 and 6.6 mm) and L2 to L3 at 0 deg (about 3.7 mm, SDs 2.6 and 4.5 mm).
- SD (approx., half the error bar, Fig. 2): CL0 T9 4.1, T10 5.7, T11 5.2, T12 9.2, L1 5.0, L2 2.6, L3 4.5, L4 4.6, L5 6.6 mm; CL15 T9 4.6, T10 6.0, T11 5.3, T12 6.5, L1 2.7, L2 2.6, L3 4.3, L4 4.8, L5 6.1 mm. The text calls all SDs small; p. 29 (PDF 3).
- Distribution (Table 1, p. 29, PDF 3; per cent of pedicles per 5-mm bin): at 0 deg 78.6% lie between 35.1 and 50 mm; at 15 deg 68.4% lie between 45.1 and 55 mm; both axes together 84.1% between 35.1 and 55 mm; p. 29 (PDF 3). Extremes: T12 at 0 deg has 12% in 20-25 mm; L5 at 0 deg has 50% in 30-35 mm and 15% in 25-30 mm; T12 at 15 deg has 8% in 65-70 mm; T9 at 15 deg has 14% in 60-65 mm. Unweighted mean rows: 0 deg 1.3 / 3.2 / 10.3 / 20.1 / 33.8 / 24.7 / 5.6 for 20-25 to 50-55 mm; 15 deg 2.8 / 13.1 / 32.6 / 35.8 / 12.6 / 3.2 / 0.9 for 35-40 to 65-70 mm.
- 15 deg against 0 deg: 8 to 10 mm longer (p. 31, PDF 5), "approximately 1 cm" (conclusion 6, p. 32, PDF 6); per level from Fig. 2 the difference runs about 7.4 (L2) to 10.0 mm (T12).
- Saillant's chord means (approx., Fig. 2): T9 39, T10 40, T11 39, T12 38.5, L1 45, L2 46, L3 39, L4 43.5, L5 43 mm. The authors call the agreement close (p. 29, PDF 3), yet Saillant sits 4 to 6 mm below Krag's 0 deg mean at T9 to T11 and about 7 mm above it at L5. Which of Saillant's directions these are is not said.
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed.
- Error: see Measurement error; CT against caliper on 8 cadaver vertebrae lies close to y = x (Fig. 7).

### Pedicle length (pedicle root length, facet corrected)

- Landmarks: per Fig. 3 caption, along the 0 deg axis between lines A and B, and the Fig. 3 inset draws it as a straight line beside the canal from dashed line A to dashed line B; Fig. 1A caption: from line A to the posterior cortex, or to line B (facet corrected); p. 28 (PDF 2), p. 29 (PDF 3). The methods text says it was measured along all four axes from the posterior cortex to line A and that only the 5 deg data are shown because the others were so similar; p. 28 (PDF 2). See Gaps for this conflict.
- Plane: axial midpedicle CT slice. Levels: T9 to L5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: because A and B are parallel and both perpendicular to the AP axis, the facet-corrected length is simply the AP distance between line A (canal's anterior-most point) and line B (posterior lamina surface); along a path at theta it is that distance / cos(theta) (x1.004 at 5 deg, x1.035 at 15 deg), which fits the authors' remark that the angles gave similar values. Lines A and B bracket the canal, so this "pedicle length" is in effect the midline canal depth plus the lamina thickness at that slice, not the length of the bony pedicle itself; the authors themselves tie its caudal decrease to the shrinking cauda equina (p. 29, PDF 3).
- From a radiograph alone: not tested. Inference: on a lateral radiograph, the posterior body wall stands in for line A and the posterior laminar surface for line B, but the laminar surface overlaps the facets on a lateral film; correct for magnification.
- Across levels: text means T9 20.18 mm, T12 18.06 mm, L5 15.00 mm (p. 29, PDF 3); approx. from Fig. 3 (p. 29, PDF 3): T9 20.2, T10 17.8, T11 18.3, T12 18.3, L1 19.3, L2 18.7, L3 18.2, L4 17.3, L5 15.3 mm (the plotted T12 and L5 points sit 0.2 to 0.3 mm above the text means). Text: nearly constant T9 to T12, then decreasing to L5; overall range about 15 to 25 mm. SD (approx., Fig. 3): T9 3.1, T10 2.7, T11 2.35, T12 2.1, L1 1.45, L2 1.35, L3 1.55, L4 1.85, L5 2.4 mm. The two clear steps are T9 to T10 (down about 2.4 mm) and L4 to L5 (down about 2 mm); L1 is a small local peak.
- As a share of CL0: from 46% (T12) to 40% (T10); p. 29 (PDF 3); "40-46%" on p. 31 (PDF 5).
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed.
- Error: as for all lengths.

### Pedicle diameter (transverse pedicle width), three ways

- Perpendicular to the pedicle axis: the width along one perpendicular to the pedicle axis at its narrowest point; p. 28 (PDF 2), Fig. 1B.
- At 0 deg and at 15 deg: the distance between two lines parallel to the 0 deg or 15 deg path, each tangent to the medial and the lateral pedicle cortex; the two tangency points need not lie on one perpendicular; it is meant to give the largest screw that can pass along that direction through the pedicle into the body without breaching cortex; p. 28 (PDF 2), Fig. 1D.
- Inference: so defined, an off-axis width is the width of a straight corridor and should not exceed the narrowest width perpendicular to the axis; yet Fig. 5 shows the 15 deg width above the on-axis width at T10, T11, T12 and L5 and the 0 deg width above it at T11 and T12. The paper does not explain this (see Gaps).
- Inner or outer cortex: not stated. Fig. 1 draws the bone as a single outline, so the drawings do not settle it.
- Plane: axial midpedicle CT slice; only the transverse (medio-lateral) width; pedicle height in the sagittal plane was not measured. Levels: T9 to L5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: in the midpedicle plane, take each pedicle as the posterior-element mask between lines A and B on one side of the canal; fit its centreline (principal axis of that region); width perpendicular to the axis = minimum chord perpendicular to the axis along the pedicle; corridor width at theta = the extent of lateral offsets for which a full line at theta from A to B stays inside the mask. Partial-volume effects at 1-mm voxels should be smaller than at the paper's 4-mm cuts.
- From a radiograph alone: the authors did not measure pedicle width on frontal (AP) radiographs, and warn that their CT 0 deg widths may bear little relation to widths read off a frontal radiograph; p. 32 (PDF 6).
- Across levels: about 7 mm and fairly flat from T9 to L1, then rising to about 13 mm at L5; p. 29 (PDF 3). The three methods agree except at L4 and L5; even the smallest at L5 (0 deg) is 12.6 mm; p. 30 (PDF 4). Approx. from Fig. 5 (p. 30, PDF 4), perpendicular to the axis / 0 deg / 15 deg, in mm: T9 7.0 / 6.65 / 6.9; T10 7.15 / 7.2 / 7.5; T11 7.4 / 8.0 / 7.85; T12 6.8 / 7.3 / 7.7; L1 7.2 / 6.8 / 7.1; L2 8.6 / 8.2 / 8.7; L3 9.3 / 8.15 / 9.3; L4 11.0 / 9.5 / 11.05; L5 13.85 / 12.6 / 15.2. ### Pedicle axis angle (transverse pedicle angle)

- Landmarks: angle between the visually fitted pedicle longitudinal axis and the sagittal plane (AP midline axis) on the axial slice; anteromedial positive; p. 28 (PDF 2), Fig. 1B.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: angle in the midpedicle plane between the fitted pedicle centreline (between lines A and B) and the AP symmetry axis, positive when the anterior end points toward the midline; average left and right or keep both.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible on a lateral film; the paper gives no radiographic method. Inference: an AP film shows the pedicle as an oval end-on and does not give this angle directly.
- Across levels (approx., Fig. 6, p. 31, PDF 5): T9 6.6, T10 5.85, T11 0.3, T12 -0.3, L1 11.7, L2 13.35, L3 18.0, L4 19.5, L5 27.8 deg. SD (approx.): T9 3.35, T10 5.3, T11 4.0, T12 4.6, L1 3.6, L2 3.5, L3 2.8, L4 5.1, L5 9.0 deg. Text: fairly constant T9 to L1, then increasing L1 to L5; roughly 0-10 deg at T9-L1, 10-20 deg at L1-L4, 20-30 deg at L4-L5; SDs fairly small; p. 30 (PDF 4). Conclusion 5: only slightly anteromedial from T9 to L1, then increasing to L5; p. 32 (PDF 6). Inference: the figure puts the step between T12 (about 0) and L1 (about 12), about 3 SD, the sharpest level-to-level change of any measure in the paper; a second step lies at L4 to L5 (about 8 deg), and T10 to T11 falls about 5.5 deg.
- Comparison: the authors report significant differences from Saillant's means (p. 30, PDF 4), in the lumbar region (p. 31, PDF 5), which they put down to this being the least explicitly defined of their measures; the trend with level is the same; no test is named. Saillant approx., Fig. 6: T9 4, T10 4, T11 -0.5, T12 -4, L1 -0.7, L2 2.4, L3 4.4, L4 10.5, L5 29.6 deg.
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed.
- Error: protractor to 0.5 deg; no repeatability reported; the pedicle axis itself is fitted by eye (p. 28, PDF 2).

### Vertebral body length (mid-body AP depth)

- Landmarks: from line A to the anterior cortex along the AP midline axis, on the midpedicle slice; p. 28 (PDF 2), Fig. 1A, Fig. 4 caption. Called "vertebral body depth" (p. 28, PDF 2), "anteroposterior diameter" (conclusion 8) and "AP length of vertebral body" (p. 32, PDF 6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: in the midpedicle plane, the AP extent of the body mask along the symmetry axis, from the canal's anterior-most point to the anterior body surface. This is a depth at pedicle height, not an end-plate depth, and excludes end-plate rim and osteophytes.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph; the authors say it is "readily measurable on lateral view roentgenograms" (p. 28, PDF 2) and propose it for screw length on sagittal-plane films (p. 32, PDF 6). They did not validate it on radiographs and do not address magnification. Inference: on a lateral film the measurement runs from the posterior body wall to the anterior body surface at pedicle height; the posterior wall line is the posterior cortex of the body, close to line A at the midline.
- Across levels (approx., Fig. 4, p. 29, PDF 3): T9 32.85, T10 32.3, T11 30.2, T12 32.2, L1 31.5, L2 32.7, L3 31.2, L4 32.2, L5 33.35 mm; overall mean 32 mm; "almost constant"; p. 29 (PDF 3). SD (approx.): T9 2.55, T10 3.3, T11 3.9, T12 3.2, L1 2.9, L2 2.3, L3 3.3, L4 2.5, L5 3.2 mm. It separates no levels.
- Use: chord length = body length x factor; factors (mean +/- SD): 0 deg 1.32 +/- 0.12; 5 deg 1.44 +/- 0.09; 10 deg 1.53 +/- 0.09; 15 deg 1.60 +/- 0.09; mean over all angles 1.50; p. 29 (PDF 3). Inference: at a 32-mm body the factor SD alone means about +/-3.8 mm (0 deg) and +/-2.9 mm (15 deg) of prediction scatter, before any radiographic magnification error.
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed.
- Error: one clear outlier in Fig. 7 (approx. CT 29.5 mm against section 35 mm); the rest of the body-length points lie within about 1 to 2 mm of the line.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- T9: longest chords (CL0 about 45, CL15 about 54 mm); longest pedicle length (20.18 mm, SD about 3 mm); 3 of 14 pedicles (21%) under 5 mm wide, 2 under 4 mm (Table 2, p. 30, PDF 4); pedicle angle about 6.6 deg.
- T10: lowest pedicle-length share of CL0 (40%, p. 29, PDF 3); pedicle length drops about 2.4 mm from T9; 2 of 18 pedicles under 4 mm; widest pedicle-angle SD among the thoracic levels (about 5.3 deg).
- T11: pedicle angle falls to about 0 deg; body length lowest (about 30.2 mm) with the widest SD (about 3.9 mm).
- T12: pedicle angle about 0 or slightly negative (anterolateral) with SD about 4.6 deg; widest CL0 spread of any level (12% of pedicles at 20-25 mm, Table 1; SD about 9 mm, Fig. 2); highest pedicle-length share of CL0 (46%, p. 29, PDF 3); narrowest on-axis pedicle width (about 6.8 mm), 5 of 24 at 5.00-5.99 mm. Table 1's T12 rows do not sum to 100 (see Gaps).
- T12/L1 junction: Inference from Fig. 6: the pedicle transverse angle jumps from about 0 to about 12 deg; the authors' text keeps L1 with the thoracic group ("0-10 deg for T9-L1", p. 30, PDF 4), and pedicle width stays about 7 mm across the junction. L1 still has 2 of 22 pedicles under 4 mm (Table 2).
- L1 to L4: pedicle angle 10-20 deg (p. 30, PDF 4); pedicle width rises from about 7 to 11 mm; L2 has the tightest width SD (about 0.65 mm) and the tightest chord SDs (about 2.6 mm).
- L4: 23 of 24 pedicles >= 9 mm; the three width methods diverge (0 deg about 9.5 mm against about 11 mm on the axis and at 15 deg).
- L5: shortest chords (CL0 about 36, CL15 about 45 mm; 50% of CL0 in 30-35 mm, 15% in 25-30 mm, Table 1); shortest pedicle length (15.00 mm); widest pedicles (all 20 >= 9 mm; 0 deg 12.6 mm, axis about 13.85 mm, 15 deg about 15.2 mm); largest pedicle angle (about 28 deg) with the widest SD (about 9 deg).
- Other junctions (C2/C3, C7/T1, L5/S1): not covered; the study stops at T9 and L5.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Film read to 0.33 mm at 80% size; Inference: 0.33 x 1.25 = about 0.4 mm at life size. Angles to 0.5 deg. Slice thickness 4 mm; pp. 27-28 (PDF 1-2).
- CT against direct caliper measurement on 8 cadaver vertebrae (1 T12, 3 L2, 3 L3, 1 L4), three measures pooled (CL0, pedicle width perpendicular to the axis, body length): regression sectioned = 1.01 x CT + 0.7 mm, points "very close" to the y = x line; Fig. 7, p. 31 (PDF 5); p. 30 (PDF 4). No correlation coefficient, standard error, limits of agreement or per-measure breakdown is given; open and filled symbols in Fig. 7 are not explained. Inference from the plotted points: the widths span about 3 to 11 mm and scatter up to about 1.5 mm either side of the line; the chords span about 37 to 50 mm and the body lengths about 26 to 32 mm. Inference: the pooled fit implies CT reads about 0.8 mm low at 7 mm and about 1.1 mm low at 40 mm, in the direction the authors predict for pedicle width, but the fit is dominated by the long measures.
- No intra- or inter-observer repeatability, no count of observers.
- The midpedicle cut is chosen by eye as the slice where both pedicles look largest; whether the gantry was tilted to the end plates is not stated (the cadavers were potted to be positioned, and only their saw cut is said to be parallel to the end plates; p. 28, PDF 2).
- Between-subject SDs per level are in Figs. 2 to 6 (read above); the chord factors have SD 0.09 to 0.12 (p. 29, PDF 3).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication: short-segment fixation for single- or few-motion-segment problems (fracture, degeneration, spondylolisthesis, tumour); the biggest fault of the implants then available was spanning five to seven vertebrae when as few as two were diseased; p. 27 (PDF 1). Harrington distraction hooks are not rigidly attached, depend on intact soft tissue and need up to seven vertebrae spanned; Harrington or Luque sublaminar wiring still spans a long segment; Roy-Camille plates span long and their screws can toggle in the plate holes; the Magerl external fixator brings prominence and infection risk; the Dunn anterior device needs an anterior approach and lies close to the aorta; p. 27 (PDF 1). A three-dimensionally rigid grip on each vertebra is needed, and a pedicle screw provides it (the authors' own fixator, ref. 3, designed from 1981); p. 27 (PDF 1).
- Pedicle diameter and chord length are the two governing dimensions: diameter limits screw strength, and both diameter and length set the screw-bone interface strength; p. 30 (PDF 4).
- A 5-mm screw fits all but the very smallest pedicles from T9 to L5, and 6 or 7 mm or more fits most lower lumbar pedicles; screws then in use were smaller and screw breakage was a concern; p. 30 (PDF 4); conclusion 3, p. 32 (PDF 6). Pitfall: Table 2 shows that 21% of T9, 11% of T10 and 9% of L1 pedicles were under 5 mm, so a 5-mm screw does not fit every lower thoracic or thoracolumbar pedicle.
- The angle at which the width is measured hardly matters, and unintended changes in screw orientation do not much change the diameter that fits; pp. 30-31 (PDF 4-5). This holds less at L4 and L5, where the 0 deg width is clearly smaller (p. 30, PDF 4; Fig. 5).
- Chord length depends on the medial angle, a dependence the authors say they documented first: 15 deg anteromedial adds only 8 to 10 mm; fixation strength is not very sensitive to depth (ref. 3), so err slightly anteromedially for safety; a screw aimed more anterolaterally than intended risks its tip leaving the anterior cortex and damaging adjacent structures; too much medial angle makes the right and left tips collide; p. 31 (PDF 5); conclusion 6, p. 32 (PDF 6). Others recommended a 0 deg trajectory without stating a basis; p. 31 (PDF 5).
- Because chords are similar from T9 to L5 and fixation is not strongly depth dependent, only a few screw lengths are needed; p. 31 (PDF 5); conclusion 4, p. 32 (PDF 6).
- Lumbar pedicles point anteromedially: widths are slightly larger along the pedicle axis than straight ahead, and convergent right and left screws "toenail", which the authors say substantially resists posterior pull-out and lateral translation of one vertebra on the next; p. 31 (PDF 5); conclusion 7, p. 32 (PDF 6).
- The pedicle appears to grip better than the body: its trabeculae appear stronger and thicker (ref. 2), and its thick cortex lets threads often engage cortical bone; a screw should be at least 46% of the chord length to hold the whole pedicle; p. 31 (PDF 5).
- Preoperative sizing: diameter from CT; length from CT or from the lateral radiograph body depth x factor (1.32 at 0 deg to 1.60 at 15 deg); p. 29 (PDF 3), p. 32 (PDF 6).
- Pitfalls of measuring: CT pedicle width reads small (frontal-plane convexity, 4-mm partial volume; p. 31, PDF 5); frontal radiograph widths are unvalidated (p. 32, PDF 6); facet anatomy on CT is variable, hence line B (p. 28, PDF 2); metal artefact next to fixation devices (p. 27, PDF 1); the pedicle angle is the least well-defined measure (p. 31, PDF 5).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. For each vertebra on axial CT, pick the slice where both pedicles look largest and do every measurement on it (p. 27, PDF 1). Inference: on a 3D mask, reslice perpendicular to the vertebra's own axis first; the paper does not say whether its patient slices were tilted to the end plates (only the cadaver saw cut is, p. 28, PDF 2).
2. Set the AP axis so that one perpendicular bisects both the body and the canal while the transverse line runs along both transverse processes; on a mask use the instance's mirror-symmetry axis (pp. 27-28, PDF 1-2, Fig. 1A).
3. Draw line A at the canal's most anterior point and line B along the flattest part of the posterior lamina cortex, both perpendicular to the AP axis; call anterior to A body, A to B pedicle, posterior to B facet (p. 28, PDF 2).
4. Fit each pedicle's centreline, find its narrowest width and the midpoint there; measure the width perpendicular to the centreline and the transverse angle to the AP axis, positive anteromedial (p. 28, PDF 2, Fig. 1B).
5. Inference (from Fig. 6, p. 31, PDF 5): within T9 to L5, use the pedicle transverse angle across the ordered sequence, not one vertebra alone (SD 3 to 5 deg per level): about 6 deg at T9 and T10, about 0 deg at T11 and T12, about 12 deg at L1, rising to about 28 deg at L5; a rise of about 10 deg between consecutive instances after two near-zero ones suggests the thoracolumbar junction. Confirm with rib attachment, since the paper reports no rib counts.
6. Inference (from Fig. 5, p. 30, PDF 4): use transverse pedicle width to separate the lower lumbar levels: about 7 mm flat from T9 to L1, about 8.6 at L2, 9.3 at L3, 11 at L4, 14 at L5 on the pedicle axis. Do not use it to separate T9 to L1, and expect L2 and L3 to overlap.
7. Inference (from Fig. 3, p. 29, PDF 3): use the A-to-B depth (canal plus lamina) as a weak caudal cue: about 20 mm at T9, 18 to 19 mm at T10 to L3, 17 at L4, 15 at L5.
8. Do not use mid-body AP depth to tell T9 to L5 apart (flat at about 32 mm, Fig. 4, p. 29, PDF 3); Inference: use it as a size scale for the patient.
9. Expect CT-derived widths to read slightly smaller than the bone (convex cortices, 4-mm partial volume; p. 31, PDF 5). Inference from the pooled Fig. 7 fit (p. 31, PDF 5): the bias is under about 1 mm, with single-pedicle scatter up to about 1.5 mm.
10. On a lateral radiograph alone, estimate the 0 deg screw-path length as 1.32 x mid-body AP depth (1.60 x for 15 deg) (p. 29, PDF 3); correct the film for magnification first (your addition; the paper is silent on it) and allow about +/-3 to 4 mm of scatter.
11. Do not read pedicle width off an AP radiograph and treat it as the CT width; the paper gives no link between them (p. 32, PDF 6).
12. Flag pedicles narrower than 5 mm, expected in about 1 in 5 at T9 and 1 in 10 at T10 and L1, and none from T11, T12 or L2 to L5 in this sample (Table 2, p. 30, PDF 4).
13. When comparing a pedicle angle with another series, check its sign and definition first; Krag's lumbar values exceed Saillant's by about 11 to 14 deg at L1 to L3 and about 9 deg at L4 (Fig. 6, p. 31, PDF 5).
14. Treat Table 1's T12 rows as faulty (sums of 91% and 108%) and read the T12 chord from Fig. 2 instead (pp. 28-29, PDF 2-3).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none; the paper reports no transitional anomalies, rib counts or numbering method (pp. 27-32, PDF 1-6). The sample excluded fractured vertebrae and those next to hardware (p. 27, PDF 1).

(b) Measures:
- Pedicle transverse width: Krag's width perpendicular to the pedicle axis at its narrowest point on a 4-mm axial CT slice, which should be close to Panjabi's transverse pedicle width. Inference from memory: Panjabi measured cadaveric bone directly with a 3D digitiser at the narrowest point, so Krag's CT values may read lower by under about 1 mm (Fig. 7). Krag's 0 and 15 deg "diameters" are corridor widths with no Panjabi counterpart: the 0 deg width runs about 1.3 to 1.5 mm below the on-axis width at L4 and L5, while the 15 deg width equals it at L4 and runs about 1.4 mm above it at L5; do not pool them.
- Pedicle transverse angle: Krag's angle to the midsagittal plane on the midpedicle slice, anteromedial positive. Inference from memory: Panjabi reports a pedicle transverse inclination too; check its sign and reference plane before comparing values from mask-derived angles. The thoracolumbar step (T12 about 0, L1 about 12 deg) is a strong feature for the classifier if the generator holds this angle.
- Mid-body AP depth: not the same as an end-plate depth (Panjabi's upper and lower end-plate depths). Inference: the anterior body surface is concave at mid-height and line A starts at the canal, not the posterior body cortex at the end plate, so a mid-height depth from a mask will run smaller than the end-plate depths; a generator that draws end-plate depth should not be compared with Krag's 32 mm without that offset.
- Krag's "pedicle length" (line A to line B) contains the canal; Inference: it is approximately spinal canal depth plus lamina thickness, so it maps to no single Panjabi dimension; from a mask it is easy to compute (canal anterior point to posterior lamina surface along the AP axis) and could be added as a separate feature.
- Chord length (CL0, CL15) is not a Panjabi measure; it could be added, computed from the mask by ray casting as in the recipe; it is nearly flat over T9 to L5 and mainly useful as a size measure and a check.
- Not measured here: pedicle height, canal width, end-plate widths and depths, body heights, facet dimensions, anything above T9 or below L5.

(c) Measurement error to use: about 0.4 mm life-size reading resolution, 0.5 deg for angles, 4-mm slices, CT reading slightly low against caliper on 8 vertebrae (pooled fit sectioned = 1.01 x CT + 0.7 mm, Fig. 7), no repeatability data. Between-subject SDs per level (read from Figs. 2 to 6): chord 2.6 to 9.2 mm, pedicle length 1.35 to 3.1 mm, width 0.65 to 2.2 mm, angle 2.8 to 9 deg, body depth 2.3 to 3.9 mm; body-to-chord factor SD 0.09 to 0.12.

(d) Covariates: none analysed. 41 patients from Vermont, USA, 14 women and 27 men, aged 18 to 75 (mean 36, median 28); ethnicity, height, weight and bone density not reported; the sample is young and male-weighted (pp. 27-29, PDF 1-3).

## Gaps and conflicts

- Pedicle length definition conflicts within the paper: the methods say it was measured from the posterior cortex to line A and that the 5 deg values are shown (p. 28, PDF 2); Fig. 3's caption says 0 deg axis, between lines A and B, "facet corrected" (p. 29, PDF 3); Fig. 1A allows either (p. 28, PDF 2).
- Chord start point: methods say line B (p. 28, PDF 2), the Fig. 1C caption says posterior cortex, conclusion 4 says posterior cortex of the lamina (p. 32, PDF 6).
- Figure references are wrong in the discussion: Fig. 6 is cited for the three width methods (they are in Fig. 5), and "0 deg pedicle diameters in Figure 7" are cited though Fig. 7 used widths perpendicular to the pedicle axis; p. 30 (PDF 4), p. 28 (PDF 2), p. 32 (PDF 6).
- Results say the three width methods diverge at L4 and L5 (p. 30, PDF 4); the discussion says no significant difference at any level (p. 30, PDF 4). No statistical test is described anywhere, though "significant" differences from Saillant are claimed (p. 30, PDF 4).
- The corridor widths (0 and 15 deg) are defined as the largest screw that passes without breaching cortex, which should never exceed the narrowest on-axis width, yet Fig. 5 shows them above it at T10 to T12 and at L5 (15 deg); whether the tangents were drawn on the inner or outer cortex is not stated.
- The text puts L1 in the 0-10 deg angle group (p. 30, PDF 4), but Fig. 6 shows L1 at about 12 deg.
- Table 1 rows do not all sum to 100%: T12 0 deg 91%, T12 15 deg 108%, L1 0 deg 98%, T10 15 deg 99%, L3 0 deg 102%, L3 15 deg 101%, L4 0 deg 101% (p. 29, PDF 3); all but the T12 pair are rounding of counts out of 14 to 24 pedicles. Inference: the "8" printed under 65-70 mm in the T12 15 deg row (2 of 24 pedicles) probably belongs to the T12 0 deg row; moved there it makes both rows sum to 99-100 and both pedicle counts 24. The bottom "Mean %" rows are unweighted means over levels and reproduce from the printed rows within 0.1.
- Table 2's unweighted mean for 6.00-6.99 mm is printed as 15 but recomputes to 13.9 from the printed level percentages; the printed means then sum to 100.8 (p. 30, PDF 4).
- "5-mm screw fits all but the few very smallest" (p. 30, PDF 4) understates Table 2, where 21% of T9 pedicles are under 5 mm.
- The 46% share of CL0 at T12 (p. 29, PDF 3) does not follow from the plotted means (about 18.06 / 40.9 = 44%; the ratio of plotted means peaks at L1, about 45%); Inference: it may be a mean of per-vertebra ratios.
- Pedicle length "ranges between approximately 15 mm and 25 mm" (p. 29, PDF 3), while the level means run 15 to 20 mm; the 25 mm presumably counts SD bars or single values.
- Missing: level identification method, rib counts, transitional anomalies, observer count, repeatability, gantry angle, per-sex or per-age values, pedicle height, anything above T9 or below L5.
- Reference 10 (Zindrick et al.) is printed as "Spine 2:160-166, 1987"; Inference: volume 12, number 2.
- Against other papers (Inference from memory, to be checked against this set's notes): Zindrick 1987 and Panjabi's thoracic and lumbar papers also find the narrowest transverse pedicle widths in the lower thoracic and upper lumbar spine, a steep rise to L5, and a transverse angle near 0 at T11 and T12 rising through the lumbar spine; Krag's L1 to L3 angles (about 12 to 18 deg) sit well above Saillant's (about -1 to 4.5 deg) and the authors blame the loose definition. Krag's flat mid-body depth of about 32 mm from T9 to L5 contrasts with series that report end-plate depth increasing from the thoracic into the lumbar spine; the different landmark (mid-height, from the canal tangent) may explain part of it.
