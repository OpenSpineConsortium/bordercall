## What the paper is

- Design: cadaveric dry-bone morphometry, the first of a series of three (cervical and lumbar to follow), p. 888 (PDF 1). Purpose: the surface geometry of T1 to T12 as linear, angular and area dimensions, for mathematical models and clinical use. Earlier quantitative studies, the authors note, were aimed at pedicle dimensions for fixation and covered few thoracic levels, p. 888 (PDF 1); p. 898 (PDF 10).
- Specimens: 12 fresh autopsy whole spines, each C2 to L5 (no sacrum harvested; nothing is said of the ribs, and the bones were cleaned to single vertebrae), p. 888 (PDF 1); Table 1, p. 889 (PDF 2). 8 men and 4 women, aged 19 to 59, weight 54 to 84 kg, height 157 to 178 cm; Inference (my arithmetic from Table 1): means 46.3 years, 67.8 kg and 167.8 cm, the summary the later papers of the series print. Inference (my arithmetic from Table 1): men mean 50.3 years, 70.4 kg, 170.3 cm; women mean 38.5 years, 62.8 kg, 162.8 cm. Causes of death (Table 1): coronary artery disease, breast carcinoma, esophageal carcinoma, acute lymphocytic leukemia, right lung carcinoma, renal failure, pancreatic carcinoma, myocardial infarction (2), third degree burns, carcinomatosis, subdural hemorrhage: six of the twelve died of malignancy (four carcinomas, one carcinomatosis, one leukemia). Country and ethnicity are not stated. Inference: the corresponding author is at Yale (New Haven, Connecticut), so the spines are most likely from a US autopsy service; no screening for metastases, osteoporosis or deformity is reported. The specimen numbers run S3 to S24 with gaps (S3, S4, S7, S9, S10, S13, S14, S15, S20, S22, S23, S24), Table 1. Inference: the twelve were drawn from a numbered series of at least 24 by a rule the paper does not give; any exclusion is unreported.
- Denominator: 144 thoracic vertebrae (12 spines x 12 levels), p. 890 (PDF 3). Every mean is over the 12 spines at that level; the dispersion printed is the standard error of the mean (table notes, pp. 892 to 895). Inference: SD = SEM x sqrt(12) = 3.46 x SEM, assuming no missing vertebra; the paper never says whether any cell has n < 12.
- Preparation: muscles and attachments removed; ligaments, discs and remaining soft tissue softened by boiling, then bleach (0.5 h) and 75% ethanol (1 h), which cleaned the bone and separated the spine into single vertebrae, each tagged with specimen and level, p. 888 (PDF 1). Each vertebra was marked beforehand with the points to be digitised, p. 888 (PDF 1). So every measure is of cleaned bone without disc, cartilage or ligament.
- Instrument: a purpose-built three-dimensional morphometer (one linear and two rotary variable differential transformers whose axes meet at a point, a spherical coordinate system; 12-bit A/D to a DEC 11/03), p. 888 (PDF 1). Largest point-location error +/-0.5 mm, p. 889 (PDF 2).
- Pins: the text says two pointed threaded rods (3.0 mm diameter, 3.2 cm long) were screwed into each body, one roughly in the frontal and one in the mid-sagittal plane, and AP and lateral radiographs located their tips in the vertebra's frame, p. 888 (PDF 1); yet the same page speaks of "the three tips of the threaded rods" and Figure 1 (p. 889, PDF 2) draws three pins. The pin tips were re-digitised whenever the vertebra was turned over (for the lower end-plate) so the two positions could be merged, p. 889 (PDF 2).
- Local frame (p. 889, PDF 2; Figure 1): points A, B, D, E are the right superior, left superior, right inferior and left inferior edges of the posterior wall of the body, chosen because they were consistent and varied little when osteophytes were present. Their least-squares plane is the frontal plane. Origin C is the centroid of the superior end-plate. z is perpendicular to the frontal plane (anterior, Figures 1, 3B, 3C); x is parallel to AB, pointing to the left (A is right, B left, so the specimen's left); y is perpendicular to the z-x plane (cranial).
- Digitising: each structure was a set of points spanning a surface or tracing a contour, dense enough that straight lines between neighbours described it (Figure 2, p. 890, PDF 3); a rib articulation by up to 20 points (five rows of three to four), the pedicle by points around its circumference, p. 888 (PDF 1).
- Parameters (p. 889, PDF 2): linear (software extraction of "well defined" dimensions, not further specified); angular (a plane's orientation by its unit normal or its angles to the three anatomic planes, least squares where more than three points); area (the projections of the digitised part on the three anatomic planes; the area is the square root of the sum of the squares of the three projected areas).
- Statistics: single-factor ANOVA with Scheffe's F test by level, alpha 0.05; the levels from which a level differs are printed in parentheses, p. 889 (PDF 2) and table notes.
- Accuracy test: a Plexiglas piece with faces cut at 30, 45 and 60 deg, millimetre paper with drawn triangles, rectangles and circles pasted on, pins and three reference points as on a vertebra, digitised by the same protocol; linear, angular and area errors under 5% in all cases, p. 889 (PDF 2).
- Radiographic side study: end-plate angles to the transverse plane (EPItu, EPItl) on ten lateral radiographs of the thoracic spine, p. 889 (PDF 2). Whose radiographs (patients or these specimens) and which levels is not said.
- Levels: T1 to T12 only, although each spine ran C2 to L5. How levels were identified and counted is not described beyond the tag of "vertebral level" at dissection; no vertebra or specimen is reported excluded and no transitional segment is mentioned.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper reports no numerical anomaly, no transitional vertebra, no cervical or lumbar rib and no 11 or 13 rib pairs; all 12 spines are tabulated as T1 to T12 (144 vertebrae, p. 890, PDF 3). The numbering method is not stated. Inference: with the sacrum not harvested (C2 to L5 only) lumbosacral transitional status could not have been seen; a spine with 13 or 11 rib-bearing segments would either have been dropped silently (the gaps in the specimen numbers leave room for that) or numbered by a rule the paper does not give; with n = 12 the absence of reported anomalies would bound their prevalence only below about 25% (rule of three), which is useless as a prior.

What the paper does give that bears on numbering is the normal rib-articulation pattern (p. 893, PDF 5; Table 6, p. 895, PDF 7):
- T1 to T10: each body carries two costovertebral surfaces, upper and lower, on each side; the authors state that from T1 to T10 the rib articulates with its own vertebra and the one above (their example: rib 7 with T7 and T6), p. 893 (PDF 5).
- T11 and T12: "uniarticular with respect to the vertebral body", p. 893 (PDF 5); Table 6 prints dashes for the lower costovertebral areas at T11 and T12.
- Costotransverse facets: present T1 to T10 (CTAr 48.2 to 63.8, CTAl 40.0 to 65.0 mm2), small at T11 (CTAr 16.7, CTAl 11.7 mm2), absent at T12 (dash), Table 6, p. 895 (PDF 7).
- The upper costovertebral surface is largest at T11 on both sides (71.9 right, 60.0 left mm2); at T12 it is 61.0 right (second largest on the right) and 47.9 left (mid-range). At T1 (45.3 right, 56.6 left) it is 1.6 and 1.9 times T2's (29.0, 29.5); from T2 to T10 it ranges 26.7 to 56.3 right and 29.5 to 53.4 left, Table 6. Inference: T11 and T12 carry the single whole facets of ribs 11 and 12 and T1's large upper facet is rib 1's; the paper never uses the words full facet or demifacet.
- T10 still carries lower costovertebral surfaces (30.0 right, 26.9 left mm2), Table 6. Inference: by the authors' own rule these would belong to rib 11, which they call uniarticular on T11; classical anatomy has rib 10 often on T10 alone and rib 1 on T1 alone, which the paper neither confirms nor counts.
- The authors place the thoracolumbar transition where the ribs change from a complete unit at T10 to floating ribs at T11 and T12 (no ventral bony attachment), with less stiffness there, p. 897 to 898 (PDF 9 to 10).
- T12's inferior facets change from thoracic to lumbar type, which the authors give as the reason for the sudden drop in pars area at T12, p. 893 (PDF 5). This is the only statement about the T12 articular processes; the facets themselves are in a later paper of the series.

## Measures, each as a recipe

General rules for all measures below (Inference unless cited): Panjabi's frame is per vertebra and built from the posterior wall of the body, not from the global CT axes. To match his values from a SPINEPS instance mask, build the same frame for each instance: fit a plane to the posterior surface of the body subregion (or to its four posterior-wall corners A, B, D, E), take its normal as z (anterior), the line joining the two superior posterior-wall corners as x (to the patient's left), y = z x x (cranial), origin at the centroid of the superior end-plate surface. Widths are then extents along x, depths along z, heights along y; areas are vector areas of a contour (equal to the true area for a planar contour, smaller than a curved surface's true area). The specimens are cleaned bone, so the CT bone mask is the right analogue; the authors say CT "may not yield such accurate quantitative data", p. 893 (PDF 5).

Naming pitfall: in Table 2 (p. 890, PDF 3) the suffix l means lower, but in Tables 5 to 7 the same letter means left (PDWl, PDHl, PDAl, PDIsl, PDItl, CTAl, PIAl) and CVAll is lower-left. Table 2 also lists a suffix a (anterior) that no reported measure uses; no anterior body height is given.

### EPWu, EPWl: upper and lower end-plate width
- Landmarks: lateral margins of the superior (u) and inferior (l) end-plate; Figure 3A, p. 891 (PDF 4); Table 2, p. 890 (PDF 3). Inference from Figure 3A and the frame: the extent parallel to x. Instrument: morphometer contour points. Levels T1 to T12; Table 3, p. 892 (extract PDF 1).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: take the superior (inferior) end-plate rim of the body subregion, project on x, report its extent. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: AP radiograph, distance between the lateral corners of the end-plate, corrected for magnification (the paper does not treat radiographic magnification). Not from a lateral. Inference.
- Across levels: EPWu 24.5 to 24.9 mm from T1 to T5 (flat), then 26.2 (T6), 27.8, 29.5, 30.6, 31.9, 34.9, 39.0 at T12. EPWl 27.8 (T1), 27.4, dips to 25.9 at T3, then 26.0, 27.0, 28.2, 29.1, 30.5, 33.0, 35.4, 39.1, 42.1 at T12. The steepest rise is T10 to T12 for both, p. 890 (PDF 3); Figure 4A, p. 896 (PDF 8). Significance: EPWu T12 differs from T1 to T10 but not from T11; EPWl T12 from T1 to T10, Table 3.
- Within a vertebra the lower end-plate is wider than the upper at every level, by 1.0 mm (T8) to 4.2 mm (T11), Table 3 (Inference, my arithmetic).
- Across a disc: the authors say the lower end-plate of the upper vertebra is consistently wider than the upper end-plate of the vertebra below, while the depths across a disc are about equal, p. 890 (PDF 3). Inference from Table 3: the width rule holds at nine of eleven junctions and fails at T7/T8 (29.1 against 29.5) and, by 0.1 mm, at T8/T9 (30.5 against 30.6); the depths across each disc agree within 1.1 mm (largest gap T2/T3, 21.6 against 22.7).
- Sex, age, size, population: pooled; nothing by sex, age or size.
- Error: SEM 0.43 to 1.10 mm (SD about 1.5 to 3.8 mm by SEM x sqrt(12)); instrument +/-0.5 mm.

### EPDu, EPDl: upper and lower end-plate depth
- Landmarks: posterior to anterior edge of the end-plate; Figure 3B, p. 891 (PDF 4). Inference from Figure 3B: the extent parallel to z, at the end-plate. Table 3.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: extent of the end-plate rim along z, at the end-plate itself, not at mid-body. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, posterior to anterior corner of each end-plate (magnification corrected). Inference. The authors found two other studies' lower end-plate depths (Scoles, Cotterill) 4 to 5 mm below theirs and suggest, since neither article says, that those were taken at mid-body height, p. 898 (PDF 10); a hypothesis, not a measured offset.
- Across levels: EPDu 18.5 (T1), 19.6, 22.7, 23.3, 24.3, 26.0, 27.4, 27.9, 29.3, 30.5, 31.9, 32.8 (T12). EPDl 19.7 (T1), 21.6, 23.3, 24.5, 25.8, 26.9, 28.5, 29.4, 31.0, 31.6, 31.8, 33.4. The authors place the largest depth increase at T2 for the upper end-plate (the T2 to T3 step, 19.6 to 22.7) and at T1 for the lower (the T1 to T2 step, 19.7 to 21.6), p. 890 (PDF 3). Lower exceeds upper at every level except T11 (31.8 against 31.9), Table 3. EPDu T12 differs only from T1 to T7, EPDl T12 only from T1 to T6, Table 3; depth separates levels four or more apart, not neighbours.
- Error: SEM 0.41 to 1.21 mm (SD about 1.4 to 4.2 mm).

### EPWu/EPDu, EPWl/EPDl: end-plate width to depth ratio
- Derived. Table 3; Figure 4B, p. 896 (PDF 8).
- Across levels: 1.3 (T1 upper) and 1.4 (T1 lower), falling to 1.0 in the middle and rising to 1.2 (upper) and 1.3 (lower) at T12. Middle region with a ratio near 1: T4 to T10 for the upper end-plate, T5 to T8 for the lower, p. 890 (PDF 3). A size-free shape cue: T1 to T2 and T11 to T12 are wide, the middle round. Inference from Table 3: from T1 to T4 the ratio falls because the depth grows (EPDu +4.8 mm, EPDl +4.8 mm) while the upper width stays flat; from T10 to T12 it rises because the width grows faster than the depth.
- From a radiograph alone: needs both AP and lateral of the same level. Inference.
- Note: two printed ratios (T4 and T8 upper, 1.0) are lower than the ratio of the printed means (1.05, 1.06); Inference: the table may print the mean of per-specimen ratios, so this is not necessarily an error.

### EPAu, EPAl: end-plate area
- Definition: area of the digitised end-plate, the square root of the sum of squares of its three projected areas, p. 889 (PDF 2). Table 3.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: vector area of the end-plate rim contour (about the area projected on the best-fit end-plate plane). Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: not directly. The authors state that the ellipse (pi x EPW x EPD / 4) approximates the measured area within 10% and the triangle (EPW x EPD / 2) underestimates by 20% at T1 to 40% at T12, p. 900 (PDF 12); Figure 4C, p. 896 (PDF 8). Inference (my arithmetic from Table 3, and visible in Figure 4C): the ellipse is within 10% only from about T6 or T7 down (+3% to +8%); at T1 to T5 it overestimates by 11% to 19%; the triangle underestimates by 24% to 34%. So an AP width and a lateral depth give the area to about 10% in the lower thoracic spine and overestimate it by up to a fifth in the upper.
- Across levels: EPAu 300 (T1) to 954 mm2 (T12), EPAl 376 to 1024; T12 is 3.2 (upper) and 2.7 (lower) times T1, the rise steady and nearly linear, p. 890 (PDF 3). EPAu T12 differs from T1 to T10, EPAl T12 from T1 to T9, Table 3.
- Error: SEM 10.9 to 49.8 mm2.

### VBHp: posterior vertebral body height
- Landmarks: height of the posterior wall of the body between the end-plates; Figure 3B, p. 891 (PDF 4). Where across the wall (midline or lateral) is not said. Table 2 lists VBH with the suffix p; no anterior height is reported.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: extent along y of the posterior wall of the body subregion, at the midline. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, posterior-superior to posterior-inferior corner (magnification corrected). Inference.
- Across levels: 14.1 (T1), 15.6, 15.7, 16.2, 16.2, 17.4, 18.2, 18.7, 19.3, 20.2, 21.3, 22.7 mm (T12), Table 3; the text gives 14.3 at T1 and 22.7 at T12, a 60% rise, 0.8 mm per level, p. 890 (PDF 3). Figure 4D (p. 896, PDF 8) plots T12 at about 21.9, not 22.7. VBHp T12 differs from T1 to T7; T8 and T9 differ only from T1, Table 3.
- Error: SEM 0.41 to 1.04 mm (SD about 1.4 to 3.6 mm). Inference: one level's step (0.8 mm) is less than half an SD, so VBHp places a vertebra in a region, not at a level.

### EPItu, EPItl, EPIt: end-plate inclination and wedge angle
- Definition: angle of the best-fit plane of the upper (lower) end-plate to the transverse plane of the local frame (the x-z plane, perpendicular to the posterior-wall plane); the wedge angle EPIt is their sum, p. 890 (PDF 3); Figure 3B, p. 891 (PDF 4). Inference from Figure 3B: the upper end-plate slopes down anteriorly and the lower slopes up, so a positive sum is an anterior (kyphotic) wedge.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: fit planes to the superior and inferior end-plate surfaces of the body; angle of each to the plane perpendicular to the posterior wall; their sum (the angle between the two end-plates) does not depend on the frame. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph; on ten lateral films the mean angle between upper and lower end-plates was 3.9 deg against 3.8 deg from the bone, p. 890 (PDF 3), p. 898 (PDF 10); no per-level values or agreement statistic. Angles need no magnification correction (Inference).
- Across levels: EPItu 0.5 to 2.4 deg, average 1.7; EPItl 1.2 to 3.9, average 2.1 (the largest, 3.9, at T1), p. 890 (PDF 3); EPIt 5.7 (T1), 3.6, 4.5, 3.8, 4.1, 3.9, 3.8, 3.6, 2.1 (T9), 1.7 (T10), 4.5, 4.0 (T12), Table 3. The authors call the inclinations constant through the region, p. 890 (PDF 3); no level is marked significantly different. Summed over T1 to T12 the bony kyphosis is about 45.6 deg (3.8 x 12; the table's own sum is 45.3), p. 898 (PDF 10). The least wedge is at T9 and T10, the largest at T1.
- Error: SEM 0.14 to 0.87 deg; EPIt has no SEM printed.

### SCW, SCD: spinal canal width and depth
- Landmarks: lateral width and anteroposterior depth of the canal contour, drawn in the top view as the widest transverse diameter and the midline depth; Figure 3C, p. 891 (PDF 4); points in Figure 2B, p. 890 (PDF 3). The height within the canal at which the contour was traced is not stated.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the canal is the foramen enclosed by the body's posterior wall, the pedicles and the laminae; take its cross-section perpendicular to y and report the x and z extents. Which slice: not stated by the paper; Inference: mid-pedicle height, or the minimum, but record which.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference; the paper measured neither on film): SCW on the AP film approximates the interpedicular distance, which is taken between the pedicles' medial cortices and may differ from the canal's widest point; SCD on the lateral film from the posterior body wall to the spinolaminar line; at T1 to T3 the shoulders obscure the lateral view.
- Across levels: SCW 21.8 (T1), 19.5, 18.3, 17.0 (T4), 17.1, 17.3, 17.3, 17.7, 17.9, 18.2, 19.4, 22.2 (T12): a parabola, falling T1 to T4, flat at 17.4 on average over T4 to T9, rising T10 to T12, p. 891 (PDF 4); Table 4, p. 893 (PDF 5); Figure 5A, p. 896 (PDF 8). SCD is flat, 15.3 (T2) to 16.5 (T6), average 16.2, until 18.1 at T12. Only SCW T12 against T4 is marked significant; Table 4 marks no SCD difference, though the text calls the T12 depth a significant increase, p. 891 (PDF 4).
- SCW/SCD: 1.3 at T1 and T2, 1.2 at T3, 1.0 from T4 to T6, 1.1 at T7 to T9, 1.2 at T10 to T12, Table 4; Figure 5B. The canal is round mid-thoracic and transversely oval at both ends.
- Error: SCW SEM 0.34 to 1.19 mm, SCD 0.42 to 1.25 (SD about 1.2 to 4.3 mm).

### SCA: spinal canal cross-sectional area
- Definition: area of the canal contour; Table 4.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: vector area of the canal cross-section in the slice used for SCW and SCD. Inference.
- From a radiograph alone: no; the ellipse (pi x SCW x SCD / 4) always overestimates and the triangle always underestimates; the triangle is closer at T1, the ellipse at T2 to T12, p. 900 (PDF 12); Figure 5C, p. 896 (PDF 8). Inference (my arithmetic): from the means the ellipse overestimates by 9% to 32% (largest at T1 and T3) and the triangle underestimates by 16% to 31%.
- Across levels: 213 (T1), 200, 180 (T3, the smallest), 192, 201, 206, 199, 194, 200, 202, 220, 280 mm2 (T12); about 198 on average from T2 to T10, larger at T1, and a large rise from T11 to T12, p. 891 (PDF 4). No level is marked significantly different.
- Error: SEM 14.3 to 30.6 mm2; the SEMs at T1 to T4 (25.7 to 30.6) are nearly twice those at T5 to T12 (14.3 to 17.0), Table 4, so the upper thoracic canal area varies more between people.

### PDWr, PDWl, PDHr, PDHl: pedicle width and height
- Landmarks: the pedicle cross-section, perpendicular to its centreline; width is its transverse dimension, height its vertical dimension; Figure 3C inset, p. 891 (PDF 4); the pedicle was digitised by points around its circumference, p. 888 (PDF 1); the centreline is perpendicular to the section, p. 893 (PDF 5). Where along the pedicle the section lies (isthmus or otherwise) is not stated.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: find the pedicle (the bridge between the body and the posterior arch), its centreline, and the cross-section perpendicular to it. Inference: take the narrowest section, as screw-sizing studies do, and record that choice.
- From a radiograph alone: AP film, the pedicle oval (width and height), Inference; because the thoracic pedicle converges 10 to 29 deg medially and tilts 5 to 18 deg (below), the AP oval is an oblique projection, not the true section.
- Across levels (mean of right and left, Table 8, pp. 899 to 900, PDF 11 to 12; sides in Table 5, p. 894, PDF 6): PDW 8.5 (T1), 8.2, 6.8, 6.3, 6.0, 6.0, 5.9 (T7), 6.7, 7.7, 9.0, 9.8 (T11), 8.7 (T12); narrowest T4 to T7 (single sides as low as 5.5 right at T4 and 5.2 left at T7). PDH 9.6 (T1), 11.4, 11.9, 12.1, 11.3, 11.8, 12.0, 12.5, 13.9, 14.9, 17.4 (T11), 16.7. The text: height 9.6 at T1 rising fast to 11.9 at T3, level near 11.9 from T3 to T8, then about 1 mm per level to 16.6 at T12; width 8.4 at T1 falling to 6.3 on average over T3 to T8, then 0.7 mm per level to 8.7 at T12, p. 891 (PDF 4); Figure 6A, p. 897 (PDF 9). Both peak at T11 and fall slightly at T12.
- Significance (Table 5): right height at T9 differs from T1, at T10 from T1, T2 and T5, at T11 and T12 from T1 to T8; left height at T9 from T1, at T10 from T1, T2, T5 and T6, at T11 from T1 to T9, at T12 from T1 to T8; left width at T11 from T5 to T7 and at T10 from T1 only (see Gaps).
- Error: PDW SEM 0.35 to 1.06 mm (SD about 1.2 to 3.7); PDH SEM 0.23 to 0.94 mm (SD about 0.8 to 3.3).

- Table 5; Figure 6C, p. 897 (PDF 9).
- Across levels: a parabola, about 53 mm2 at T1, falling to 32 in the middle region, steepest rise from T8 to T11, 94 at T12, about threefold from T7 to T12, p. 893 (PDF 5). ### PDIsr, PDIsl: pedicle axis inclination to the sagittal plane (medial angulation)
- Definition: angle between the pedicle centreline (normal to its cross-section) and the sagittal plane, seen from above; Figure 3C, p. 891 (PDF 4); p. 893 (PDF 5). Figure 3C draws the axis converging toward the midline anteriorly; no negative (divergent) values were found here or by Scoles, unlike Berry and Zindrick, p. 898 (PDF 10).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: pedicle centreline direction projected on the x-z plane, angle to z. Inference: use the mean of right and left, which is unaffected by a small yaw of the frame; the right-left difference depends on the frame.
- From a radiograph alone: not from a plain AP or lateral film. Inference: an axial or oblique view would be needed.
- Across levels (mean, Table 8): 27.1 (T1), 28.6 (T2), 19.4, 19.5, 15.6, 16.4, 20.7, 19.6, 14.8, 12.4, 13.1, 9.8 (T12); the text: about 27 deg at T1 and T2 down to 10 at T12, with wide variation mid-thoracic, p. 893 (PDF 5); Figure 6D, p. 897 (PDF 9). The step from T2 to T3 (about 9 deg) is the largest.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- Three regions (p. 897, PDF 9): an upper cervicothoracic transition, a middle region and a lower thoracolumbar transition. The authors note that Grant, Gray, Hollingshead and Parke also describe three regions but do not agree which vertebrae belong to each. The middle region is given as T3 to T9 in the abstract (p. 888, PDF 1), T4 to T9 for the narrowest canal width (p. 891, PDF 4) and the critical vascular zone, and T4 through T9 or T10 for the narrow end-plate and canal (p. 897, PDF 9).
- Upper transition (p. 897, PDF 9): the authors characterise it by narrowing of the end-plate and canal widths from T1 to T4 with depths nearly constant, so the width-to-depth ratios fall. Inference from Tables 3 and 4: that holds for the canal (SCW 21.8 to 17.0, SCD 16.4 to 16.2) but not for the end-plate, whose upper width is flat (24.5 to 24.5) and lower width falls only 1.8 mm while both depths grow by 4.8 mm; the end-plate ratio falls because the body deepens.
- T1 (C7/T1 side): canal second widest after T12 (SCW 21.8, ratio 1.3), wide end-plates relative to depth (1.3 and 1.4), the longest transverse processes (TPW 75.3), a nearly round pedicle (PDH/PDW 1.1; PDW 8.5, PDH 9.6), medial angulation about 27 deg, transverse inclination only 7.6 deg, the largest lower end-plate inclination (3.9 deg) and wedge (5.7 deg), an upper costovertebral facet 1.6 to 1.9 times T2's (Inference: rib 1's facet) and a rib-articulation total (132.0) as large as the T6 to T10 plateau. Tables 3 to 7. Inference: almost every measure at T1 is cervical-like, which is why the authors call it transitional; this paper gives no C7 values (the cervical paper does).
- T1 to T2: sharp drop of costovertebral and total rib-articulation area (74.7 to 45.8; 132.0 to 95.9), p. 893 (PDF 5); TPW 75.3 to 69.4; SCW 21.8 to 19.5; EPDl's largest step (19.7 to 21.6); pedicle transverse inclination jumps 7.6 to 17.9 deg; pedicle shape ratio 1.1 to about 1.4.
- T2 to T3: EPDu's largest single increase (19.6 to 22.7); TPW 69.4 to 60.8; SCW 19.5 to 18.3; PDIs 28.6 to 19.4; PDIt 17.9 to 8.8. T3 holds the smallest canal area (180 mm2; the text says 189) and the smallest lower end-plate width (25.9).
- T4 to T9 (middle): canal round (ratio 1.0 at T4 to T6, 1.1 at T7 to T9), narrow (SCW about 17.4) and flat in area; end-plates round (ratio 1.0); pedicles narrowest (about 6 mm wide) and tall-oval (ratio about 1.9); costovertebral areas rising to a T6 plateau; TPW flat near 60. The authors call it the region at risk of cord impingement, stiffened by the rib articulations, p. 897 (PDF 9). Inference: in this stretch the measures change slowly and steadily, so naming one vertebra from its own measures alone will fail; the sequence and the count from a fixed landmark must carry it.
- T9 to T10: least end-plate wedge (2.1 and 1.7 deg); pedicle height and width climb about 1 and 0.7 mm per level from T8, p. 891 (PDF 4).
- T10 to T11: T11 loses the lower costovertebral facet (uniarticular) and nearly loses the costotransverse facet (CTA 62.3/49.3 to 16.7/11.7); its upper facet is the largest of the series (71.9 right, 60.0 left); TPW 58.4 to 52.2; SPL 49.3 to 45.6; pars area peaks at T11 (145 right, 179 left); end-plate widths rise steeply; pedicle height, width and moments peak at T11 and pedicle area reaches its plateau (T11 and T12 about equal); ribs 11 and 12 float, giving less stiffness, p. 897 to 898 (PDF 9 to 10).
- T11 to T12 (toward T12/L1): no costotransverse facet at T12; canal enlarges sharply (SCW 19.4 to 22.2, SCD 16.0 to 18.1, SCA 220 to 280); pars area drops (lumbar-type inferior facets); TPW 52.2 to 46.9; SPL 45.6 to 47.4; PDIs 13.1 to 9.8; PDIt 8.7 to 5.1; EPWu 34.9 to 39.0; pedicle height and width fall slightly (PDH 17.4 to 16.7, PDW 9.8 to 8.7) and so do the moments, while pedicle area is flat (right 88.4 to 90.9, left 98.3 to 97.0). The paper gives no L1 values (the lumbar paper does).
- C2/C3 and L5/S1: not covered by this paper.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Instrument: largest error in locating a point +/-0.5 mm, p. 889 (PDF 2).
- Method: linear, angular and area parameters within 5% of known values on a Plexiglas pseudo-vertebra, p. 889 (PDF 2).
- No intra- or inter-observer study, no repeat digitising, and the number of operators is not stated.
- Radiographic check of the end-plate angles only: ten lateral films gave 3.9 deg mean per vertebra against 3.8 deg from the bone, p. 890 (PDF 3); no per-level or agreement statistics, and the films' source is not given.
- Dispersion printed is SEM over 12 spines; Inference: population SD = SEM x 3.46. Typical SDs: body and canal lengths 1.2 to 4.3 mm, pedicle width 1.2 to 3.7 mm, pedicle height 0.8 to 3.3 mm, pedicle angles about 3 to 34 deg (the worst at T2), SPL 3.5 to 6.9 mm, TPW 3.1 to 13.1 mm.
- Significance marks: several are not monotone in the means and so cannot be taken at face value (Inference): EPWu at T11 (34.9) is marked different from T1 to T3 but not from T4 (24.5, the same mean as T1) or from T4 to T6, which T10 (31.9) is marked against; TPW at T12 is marked different from T5 (61.1) but not T6 (61.3); left pedicle width at T10 is marked different from T1 (8.7) only, not from T4 to T8 (5.2 to 7.0).
- Shape approximations (p. 900, PDF 12): the authors give the end-plate ellipse within 10% and the triangle -20% (T1) to -40% (T12); canal ellipse always over, triangle always under; pedicle triangle excellent at T2 to T12. Inference (my arithmetic from the tables): end-plate ellipse +11% to +19% at T1 to T5; pedicle triangle -16% to -26% at T11 and T12.
- Agreement with other studies (Table 8, pp. 899 to 900, PDF 11 to 12; p. 898, PDF 10): lower end-plate depth matches Berry but runs 4 to 5 mm above Scoles and Cotterill (the authors suspect those were taken at mid-body height); lower end-plate width closest to Scoles, several mm below Berry, slightly above Cotterill; posterior height close to Cotterill, 1 to 2 mm below Berry and Scoles; canal depth agrees with Berry and Scoles; Scoles' canal widths about 1 mm smaller; pedicle heights agree with Berry, Zindrick and Scoles; pedicle widths 1 to 2 mm wider than earlier work, and Scoles' are 2 to 3 mm below Zindrick's and 3 to 4 mm below this study's and Krag's (for example at T6: Scoles 3.5, Zindrick 5.2, Cotterill 4.1, this study 6.0); pedicle angles vary widely between studies (at T12 sagittal: this study 9.8, Zindrick -4.2, Berry -3.0, Krag 0.0, Scoles 9.5), and Berry's transverse angles (137 at T2, 110 at T7) are called grossly wrong. Inference: differences of 2 to 4 mm in pedicle width between careful studies are larger than this paper's SEMs, so the definition of the section dominates the error.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indications for the data (pp. 888, 893, 900; PDF 1, 5, 12): mathematical models of the spine (scoliosis, trauma, impact loading), spinal instability, trauma, design of pedicle screw fixation and of internal devices, functional and fixation. Canal size has been related to low-back pain and to physiologic stress in childhood (infant malnutrition), and lumbar posterior-wall concavity postulated to affect disc herniation, p. 888 (PDF 1), citing other work.
- Critical vascular zone: T4 through T9 holds the narrowest canal and corresponds almost exactly to the cord segment with the least profuse blood supply (citing Dommisse, and Rockwood and Green); many clinical articles warn that strict care is needed when surgery is contemplated there, p. 897 (PDF 9). A small canal makes the region susceptible to cord impingement, although the rib articulations add stiffness, p. 897 (PDF 9).
- Pedicle screws (Inference from Tables 5 and 8; the paper gives no screw size or safe-zone figure): mean pedicle width is about 6 mm from T4 to T7 with SD about 1.3 to 3.0 mm, so a sizable share of mid-thoracic pedicles will be narrower than 5 mm; pedicles are tall ovals (height about twice width) except at T1; medial convergence is about 27 deg at T1 and T2, 15 to 21 deg mid-thoracic, about 10 deg at T12, never divergent in this series; caudal tilt about 9 deg except about 18 at T2 and 5 at T12; pedicle stiffness rises sharply from T8 to T11.
- Thoracolumbar transition: floating ribs at T11 and T12 and smaller rib articulations go with less stiffness, coinciding with canal enlargement T10 to T12 and wider end-plates, especially the upper, p. 897 to 898 (PDF 9 to 10).
- Kyphosis: the thoracic bony wedging alone is about 45.6 deg (3.8 per vertebra), supported by 3.9 deg per vertebra on films; disc wedging was not measured, so the paper cannot apportion the curve, p. 898 (PDF 10). The authors review Aeby (1879) and Hollingshead: the thoracic concavity is primary (phylogenetic), the cervical and lumbar curves secondary and adaptive.
- Pitfall, depth level: the authors suggest that other studies' body depths ran 4 to 5 mm low because they may have been taken at mid-body height, p. 898 (PDF 10); measure at the end-plate to compare with these tables.
- Pitfall, frame: the transverse plane is defined from the posterior wall, not from the end-plates or the scanner; angles are relative to it, p. 889 (PDF 2).
- Pitfall, osteophytes: posterior-wall corners were chosen as reference points because they varied little when osteophytes were present, p. 889 (PDF 2). Inference: anterior and lateral end-plate margins are the less reliable ones in older spines.
- Pitfall, nomenclature: the suffix l is lower in Table 2 and left in Tables 5 to 7 (p. 890, PDF 3; pp. 894 to 895, PDF 6 to 7).
- Pitfall, CT: the authors held that 3D CT may not give such accurate quantitative data, p. 893 (PDF 5).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Build Panjabi's frame for every vertebral instance before measuring: plane through the four posterior-wall corners as frontal, its normal anterior as z, the superior posterior-wall corner line as x (to the left), origin at the centroid of the superior end-plate; measure widths along x, depths along z, heights along y. p. 889 (PDF 2), Figure 1. (The mask recipe is Inference.)
2. Take end-plate depth at the end-plate rim, not at mid-body; the authors attribute a 4 to 5 mm deficit in two other studies to a possible mid-height measurement. p. 898 (PDF 10).
3. Expect the inferior end-plate of a thoracic vertebra to be wider than its superior end-plate at every level (by 1.0 to 4.2 mm in the means) and usually deeper (all levels but T11). Table 3, p. 892 (extract PDF 1). Inference: the within-person difference has no printed SD, so use it as a weak check, not a rule.
4. Across each disc, expect the depths of the facing end-plates to match within about 1 mm, and the upper vertebra's lower end-plate to be at least as wide as the lower vertebra's upper end-plate (true at nine of eleven thoracic junctions in the means). p. 890 (PDF 3); Table 3. Inference: a large mismatch between consecutive instances suggests a missing or extra vertebra or a segmentation error.
5. Identify T1 by its cervical-like profile: canal width about 22 mm and wider than deep (ratio 1.3), end-plate width/depth 1.3 to 1.4, a round pedicle (H/W 1.1), the longest transverse processes (about 75 mm tip to tip), a large upper rib facet on the body, and the largest wedge (5.7 deg). Then T2 should show TPW about 69, H/W about 1.4, canal width about 19.5, and an outlying pedicle tilt (about 18 deg). Tables 3 to 7; p. 897 (PDF 9).
6. Identify T11 and T12 from the ribs where the CT shows them: T11 and T12 carry one costovertebral facet each (no lower facet), T11 a small costotransverse facet, T12 none; T10 still has lower facets and a full costotransverse facet. Read the rib head's position against the disc on CT; a vertebra mask alone does not show it (Inference). Table 6, p. 895 (PDF 7); p. 893 (PDF 5).
7. Confirm T12 by the canal jump (width about 22, depth about 18, area about 280 mm2 against about 200 above), the shortest transverse processes (about 47 mm), the smallest pedicle sagittal and transverse angles (about 10 and 5 deg), and lumbar-type inferior facets with a smaller pars than T11. Tables 4, 7, 8; p. 893 (PDF 5).
8. Do not try to name a single mid-thoracic vertebra (T4 to T9) from its own measures: neighbouring levels differ by about one SD or less in nearly every measure (VBHp 0.8 mm per level against SD about 2 mm), and the ANOVA separates body dimensions only four or more levels apart. Count from T1 or T12 and use the sequence. Table 3 parentheses; Inference for the SD comparison.
9. Use the mean of right and left for pedicle angles; in this series the right medial angle exceeded the left by 4 to 9 deg at every level T3 to T10, so a right-left difference of that size is normal. Distrust any single T2 pedicle angle (SD 21 to 34 deg). Table 5, p. 894 (PDF 6).
10. On a lateral radiograph, measure the angle between the end-plates of each vertebra; the mean on films (3.9 deg) matched bone (3.8 deg), with no per-level agreement reported; expect 1.7 to 5.7 deg per level, least at T9 and T10, largest at T1. On a lateral film use VBHp and EPD for size, on an AP film EPW, TPW and the pedicle ovals, correcting linear measures for magnification, which the paper does not treat. p. 890 (PDF 3); p. 898 (PDF 10); Table 3.
11. When checking a pedicle against its expected level, use width about 6 mm (T4 to T7) rising to about 9 to 10 mm at T10 and T11, height about 12 rising to 17 mm, with SD about 1 to 4 mm (width) and 1 to 3 mm (height). Table 5; Table 8. Inference: flag a pedicle more than 2 SD from its level's mean as either a misnamed level or an anomaly.
12. Measure SPL from the superior end-plate centroid to the inferoposterior spinous tip (about 50 mm through T10, 46 at T11), not from the canal; and TPW tip to tip. p. 893 (PDF 5); Table 7.
13. Do not treat this paper's "CVA total per rib" as a per-rib quantity; it is the sum of the upper and lower facet areas on one side of one vertebra. Table 6 (Inference by arithmetic).
14. Read the suffix l as left in the pedicle, costotransverse and pars rows and as lower in the end-plate rows. Table 2, p. 890 (PDF 3).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper reports no transitional or numerical anomaly in 12 spines and does not say how it numbered levels or whether anomalous spines were excluded (the specimen numbers S3 to S24 have gaps); take priors from other papers. What it gives the decoder is the normal T1 and T10 to T12 rib-facet pattern (instruction 6) as an emission feature where ribs are visible, the T12 lumbar-type inferior facet, and, Inference, the across-disc end-plate depth match (instruction 4) as a transition feature between consecutive instances.

(b) Measures and definitions against schwingcheck/mnemonics.py:
- SPL: this paper defines it from the centroid of the superior end-plate to the inferoposterior spinous tip (p. 893, PDF 5); mnemonics.py says from the posterior wall of the canal to the tip. A mask extractor that follows mnemonics.py will read shorter than the thoracic table, Inference: by roughly half an end-plate depth plus a canal depth along the direction of the process, of the order of 20 to 30 mm. Fix the definition (and check the cervical and lumbar papers' wording) before using SPL.
- PDW, PDH, PDA: mnemonics.py says smallest diameter and at its narrowest; the paper says only the section perpendicular to the centreline, location unstated (p. 893, PDF 5; Figure 3C). Inference: a mask extractor that takes the isthmus minimum may read lower than Panjabi if he digitised elsewhere; the 1 to 4 mm spread against other studies (Table 8) shows how much the section choice matters.
- SCW: mnemonics.py says at the level of the pedicles; the paper does not state the level. SCD in the midline is consistent with Figure 3C.
- CVA upper and lower: mnemonics.py calls them demifacets; at T11 and T12 the single facet is whole and there is no lower facet (dashes), and at T1 (Inference) the upper facet is rib 1's whole facet. The generator must model the lower CVA as absent at T11 and T12, not as zero-mean noise. "CVA total per rib" is a per-side total for one vertebra.
- CTA: absent at T12; T11 very small. A presence feature, not only an area.
- EPI and EPIt: angles to the plane perpendicular to the posterior wall; a mask extractor using the scanner axes or the next vertebra's end-plate will differ; the sum EPIt is frame-free.
- PDIs: use the side mean; the right-left gap of 4 to 9 deg at T3 to T10 is part of the source data. PDIt and PDIs at T2 have SDs of 21 to 34 deg and should get wide likelihoods.
- PDM: if used, compute from H and W with the printed formula (/64); Figure 6E's values are 16 times larger (Inference).
- Areas (EPA, SCA, PDA, PIA, CVA, CTA) are vector areas of digitised contours (p. 889, PDF 2): a mask's area of the best-fit planar section matches better than a curved surface area. The ellipse from width and depth overestimates the end-plate area by 11 to 19% at T1 to T5 and the canal area by 9 to 32% (Inference), so do not substitute it for a measured area.
- Measures not yet held that this paper offers for the thoracic spine: the rib-facet presence pattern (T10 to T12), the inferior-wider-than-superior end-plate relation, the across-disc depth match, PDH/PDW and SCW/SCD shape ratios (size-free).

(c) Measurement error to use: instrument +/-0.5 mm and under 5% on a phantom; no observer error. For the generator's between-person spread use SD = SEM x sqrt(12) per cell (Inference), noting the doubtful SEMs: CTAr 26.7 at T8 and 17.3 at T10, PIAr 1.3 and 2.8 at T3 and T4, PDIs and PDIt at T2, PIA at T11, TPW at T4.

(d) Covariates: none analysed. 8 men and 4 women, 19 to 59 years, 54 to 84 kg, 157 to 178 cm, nationality and ethnicity not stated, all pooled; the men were older, heavier and taller on average (Inference from Table 1: 50.3 against 38.5 years, 70.4 against 62.8 kg, 170.3 against 162.8 cm). The paper gives nothing on sex, age or body size effects; the size factor must come from elsewhere. From other papers, not this one: bordercall's provenance record shows the cervical (p. 861), lumbar (p. 299) and facet (p. 1299) papers of the series print the same specimen summary (46.3 years, 67.8 kg, 167.8 cm, 8 to 4), with a weight range of 54 to 85 kg where this Table 1 tops at 84; Inference: the same twelve spines, so within-person correlation across regions is real in the source data even though only means are printed.
