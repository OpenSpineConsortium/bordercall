## What the paper is

- Design: an osteometric (dry-bone) study. There are no radiographs, no CT and no living patients anywhere
  in the paper, despite its interest in lateral radiographs: every value is a caliper value on an isolated
  dry vertebra, so no magnification applies to any number in it. p. 69-72 (PDF 1-4).
- Aim: the normal range of the lumbar canal's sagittal diameter in Nigerians, for whom no data existed, and
  whether it differs from other populations. p. 69 (PDF 1).
- Population: complete sets of lumbar vertebrae of 122 adults, 79 male and 43 female, aged 23 to 60 years,
  held in the dry-bone (osteological) collection of the University of Ibadan's anatomy department (Nigeria).
  p. 69 (PDF 1) and p. 71 (PDF 3). The n of each sex is repeated in the headers of Table 3, p. 73 (PDF 5).
  The ethnic groups within Nigeria are not named; the author counts the sample among "other black
  populations" when comparing it with black South Africans (p. 74, PDF 6). How sex and age at death were
  known is not stated (Inference: from the collection's records). Per-level n is never printed; Inference:
  122 x 5 = 610 vertebrae if every set held five and none was missing.
- Background the author cites (p. 69, PDF 1): earlier normal tables of the sagittal diameter came from
  Huizinga, Heiden and Vinken (1951), Hinck et al. (1965), Sand (1970), Eisenstein (1977) and Larsen and
  Smith (1981), for Caucasian and South African groups; none for Nigerians. Of these, the reference list
  (p. 77, PDF 9) shows Hinck et al. (1965) to be on children and adults and Larsen and Smith (1981) on
  children. Inference: the author's own 1982 Nigerian study (interpedicular distance, the amonookuofi1982
  note in this set) was of living patients on AP films at Ilorin, so it is a different Nigerian sample
  from this dry-bone collection at Ibadan.
- Inclusion and exclusion: full medical histories were not available for every subject; the author made
  sure "as far as possible" that persons who died of chronic skeletal disorders or related causes were left
  out, and excluded specimens with osteophytes or other evidence of bone disease. p. 71 (PDF 3).
- Instrument: sliding vernier caliper, read to the nearest 0.1 mm; the method follows Jones and Thomson
  (1968) on osteological specimens, the technique Eisenstein (1977) also used, said to be accurate and
  reproducible. p. 69 (PDF 1), p. 72 (PDF 4).
- Measures: the mid-sagittal diameter of the canal, the antero-posterior (AP) diameter of the vertebral
  body, and the AP diameter of the inferior vertebral notch (Fig. 1a-b, p. 70, PDF 2); two derived ratios,
  canal to body (Table 4) and notch to body (Table 6). For every vertebra the author also
  recorded the shape of the canal, of the spinal nerve's groove and of the intervertebral foramen, but no result on shape is ever
  reported. p. 71-72 (PDF 3-4).
- Levels: L1 to L5 only. Nothing above L1 or in the sacrum.
- Level identification: not described beyond "complete sets of lumbar vertebrae". Transitional segments are
  never mentioned.
- Statistics: mean, SD and coefficient of variation per level and sex (Tables 1 and 5); upper and lower 95%
  limits of normal by the formula in Bradford Hill (1977) (Table 2, p. 73, PDF 5). No test of the sex
  difference or of the population difference is reported; the comparison with Eisenstein (1977) is by
  inspection, since Eisenstein printed no SDs. p. 74 (PDF 6).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, an L6, a T13,
rib counts or block vertebrae; it does not say how L1 and L5 were identified, nor whether a set with six
lumbar-type vertebrae or a transitional L5 was measured, renumbered or excluded (pp. 69-78, PDF 1-10).
Inference: "complete sets of lumbar vertebrae" implies five vertebrae per set as the collection labelled
them. The shapes of the canal, the nerve groove and the foramen were recorded for every vertebra but no
shape result is reported (p. 71, PDF 3), so the paper gives no shape prevalence (no trefoil count) either.
Inference on what an anomaly would do to the measures: an unrecognised L6 or sacralised L5 would shift
the labels on which Tables 1-6 rest; L5 is the level where the male canal re-widens and spreads most
(SD 2.4 mm, Table 1, p. 71, PDF 3), and the author ties that widening to the lumbosacral angle (p. 76,
PDF 8), so a transitional last lumbar vertebra may sit anywhere in that wide band; nothing in the paper
tests this. For anomaly priors use another paper; in this set, Eisenstein 1977, with the same caliper
technique on South African skeletons, counted a true L6 in 2 of 443 skeletons and a true
transitional lumbosacral vertebra in 1 of 443, and a trefoil canal in 66 of 443 (from the eisenstein1977
note in this set; not this paper).

## Measures, each as a recipe

### 1. Mid-sagittal diameter of the canal (sagittal diameter; Fig. 1a "i")

- Landmarks: anteriorly the posterior surface of the vertebral body in the midline; posteriorly the deep
  (anterior) surface of the canal's posterior wall in the midline, at the point where the canal is
  narrowest, which lies near the upper border of the vertebra, opposite "a slight anterior bulge in the
  deep surface of the posterior wall" (p. 72, PDF 4). The author took this landmark because Verbiest (1977)
  found it gave reproducible results. Fig. 1a (p. 70, PDF 2) draws it on the superior aspect of an L3, from
  the back of the body to the inner surface of the arch at the base of the spinous process.
  Inference: the bulge is probably the midline ridge where the two laminae meet the spinous process base;
  the paper does not name it. Inference from the eisenstein1977 note: Eisenstein's dry-bone canal landmark
  is the same (the least AP depth near the canal's cranial end, opposite a slight bulge of the posterior
  wall), so Table 3 compares like with like apart from Eisenstein's whole-millimetre rounding.
- Readings per vertebra: not stated (Inference: one).
- Plane: mid-sagittal. Instrument: sliding vernier caliper, 0.1 mm. Levels L1-L5. Dry bone.
- Values, Table 1, p. 71 (PDF 3), mean mm (SD; CV %):
  - Males (n = 79): L1 16.6 (1.0; 6.0), L2 15.8 (1.0; 6.3), L3 14.9 (1.0; 6.7), L4 15.6 (2.0; 12.8),
    L5 16.0 (2.4; 15.0).
  - Females (n = 43): L1 15.8 (1.2; 7.6), L2 15.1 (1.1; 7.3), L3 14.2 (1.1; 7.7), L4 14.1 (1.3; 9.2),
    L5 14.6 (1.2; 8.2).
  - Fig. 2 (p. 71, PDF 3) plots these means; the plotted points agree with Table 1.
- 95% tolerance range (the "95% limits of the normal values", p. 73), Table 2, p. 73 (PDF 5), mm:
  - Males: L1 14.6-18.6, L2 13.8-17.8, L3 12.9-16.9, L4 11.6-19.6, L5 11.2-20.8.
  - Females: L1 13.4-18.2, L2 12.9-17.2, L3 12.0-16.4, L4 11.5-16.7, L5 12.2-17.0.
  - Fig. 3a-b (p. 72, PDF 4) shades these limits around the mean line (the text, p. 73, PDF 5, says Fig. 3
    illustrates Table 2). Inference: every limit equals mean +/- 2 SD from Table 1 (female L2 upper limit
    computes to 17.3 against the printed 17.2).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference throughout, as the paper is dry bone. Build the vertebra's own frame
  (z normal to the end-plates, y antero-posterior through the body centre and spinous process, x across).
  Keep the mid-sagittal band of the canal (x within about one voxel of the canal's midline). For each axial
  slab in which the posterior elements close the ring behind the canal, read along y the distance from the
  posterior cortex of the body subregion to the anterior surface of the posterior-element subregion
  (lamina or spinous base) at the midline. This paper's value is the MINIMUM of that profile, taken near the
  cranial end of the ring; a mid-height slab, a mean over slabs, or a canal centroid depth reads larger.
  Use bone voxels only (the dry vertebra had no ligamentum flavum, disc or osteophyte).
- From a radiograph alone: the paper made none. Inference: lateral view only; anterior limit the posterior
  cortex of the body in the midline, posterior limit the inner cortex of the arch (spinolaminar line) in
  the upper part of the canal; divide by the film's magnification before comparing with these dry-bone
  values (Eisenstein 1977 measured 1.23 on a lateral at 110 cm tube to plate, from the eisenstein1977 note).
  Not obtainable from an AP view. The author's own suggestion for lateral films is the canal to body ratio
  (measure 3), p. 75 (PDF 7).
- Across levels: in males the diameter narrows steadily from L1 to L3 and widens again at L4 and L5
  (p. 73, PDF 5; Fig. 2); in females it narrows to L3 and L4 with the minimum at L4 (14.1 against 14.2 at
  L3) and widens a little at L5. The author calls this the "hour-glass" shape, seen in other populations,
  wider at the cephalic end than the caudal (p. 73, PDF 5; p. 76, PDF 8). The widest level is L1 in both
  sexes (p. 76, PDF 8). Steps between L1/L2 and L2/L3 are nearly 1 mm, between L4 and L5 about 0.5 mm
  (p. 73, PDF 5). Inference: adjacent steps of 0.1 mm (female L3 to L4) to 0.9 mm against SDs of 1.0-2.4 mm
  mean the measure alone separates no adjacent pair; in males L1 (16.6) and L5 (16.0) are nearly equal,
  so a single value cannot tell the two ends of the U apart; the turning point (L3 in men, L3-L4 in
  women) is the usable cue, read over several consecutive vertebrae.
- Sex, age, size, population:
  - Sex: females narrower at every level; the gap is at most 0.8 mm at L1-L3 and about 1.5 mm at L4 and L5
    (p. 73, PDF 5). Inference from Table 1: 0.8, 0.7, 0.7, 1.5, 1.4 mm. Females vary slightly more at L1-L3,
    which the author tentatively ("perhaps") puts down to greater variation of body size in women; males
    vary strikingly at L4 and L5 (SD 2.0 and 2.4) (p. 73, PDF 5). The author suggests that in a few cases
    the male canal is smaller than the female at the lower lumbar levels (p. 76, PDF 8).
  - Age: the sample spans 23-60 years but age is not analysed. The author notes that Larsen and Smith (1981)
    could not confirm the age variation Hinck et al. (1965, 1966) reported (p. 75, PDF 7).
  - Size: handled only through the canal to body ratio (measure 3).
  - Population: against Eisenstein's (1977) Zulu (108 male, 54 female) and Sotho (106 male, 62 female)
    skeletons, Table 3, p. 73 (PDF 5), mm, L1-L5: Nigerian male 16.6 15.8 14.9 15.6 16.0; Zulu male 16 15 15
    15 16; Sotho male 16 15 14 15 16; Nigerian female 15.8 15.1 14.2 14.1 14.6; Zulu female 17 16 15 16 16;
    Sotho female 16 16 15 15 16. The author reads Nigerian males as broadly larger than South African
    males and South African females as wider than Nigerian females (p. 74, PDF 6); the means of all groups
    "fall within the same range" but differ in two ways: the hour-glass is only vaguely seen in the South
    African groups, and South African females have wider canals than South African males while Nigerian
    males exceed Nigerian females (p. 76, PDF 8). He calls Nigerian female canals "markedly narrower" than
    South African ones and finds no appreciable male difference (p. 76, PDF 8). The summary concludes the
    diameter is subject to racial variation (p. 77, PDF 9), while the introduction records Eisenstein's
    caution that racial differences are subtle and probably insignificant (p. 69, PDF 1).
- Error: no repeatability or observer study; caliper resolution 0.1 mm; between-subject CV 6.0-15.0%
  (Table 1).

### 2. Antero-posterior diameter of the vertebral body (mid-waist; Fig. 1a "ii")

- Landmarks: named the "maximum anteroposterior diameter of the vertebral body", but measured "at mid-waist
  level where it was narrowest" (p. 72, PDF 4); Fig. 1a (p. 70, PDF 2) draws it in the midline from the
  posterior to the anterior surface of the body ("midsagittal diameter of the vertebral body").
  Inference: the AP length in the mid-sagittal plane at mid-height of the body, where the concave anterior
  surface makes the body narrowest; the word "maximum" conflicts with "narrowest" (see Gaps).
- Plane: mid-sagittal, mid-height. Caliper. L1-L5.
- Values, Table 4, p. 74 (PDF 6), mean mm, no SD printed: males 29.2, 30.6, 32.2, 34.0, 32.2; females 26.1,
  27.6, 29.1, 31.1, 31.3. Table 6, p. 75 (PDF 7), repeats them except male L5, printed 34.2 (see Gaps).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the vertebra's frame, the axial slab midway between the superior
  and inferior end-plates; within the mid-sagittal band, the y extent of the body subregion from anterior to
  posterior cortex; if the waist is not at exactly mid-height, the minimum AP over the middle third. This is
  not Panjabi's end-plate depth (EPDu, EPDl), taken across the end-plate rims: the waist reads smaller
  (Panjabi 1992's EPDu is 34.1-35.5 mm, from the panjabi1992 note in this set).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: lateral view, the AP width of the body at mid-height, divided by the
  magnification; not from an AP.
- Across levels: rises from L1 to L4 in both sexes (males +1.4, +1.6, +1.8 mm per level; females +1.5, +1.5,
  +2.0); at L5 it falls to 32.2 (Table 4) or stays at 34.2 (Table 6) in males and is flat in females
  (31.1 to 31.3). Inference: a monotone depth cue from L1 to L4 that fails between L4 and L5; its spread is
  unknown because no SD is printed.
- Sex, age, size, population: Inference from Table 4: females 3.1, 3.0, 3.1, 2.9 mm smaller at L1-L4
  (8.5-10.6%), and 0.9 mm (or 2.9 with 34.2) at L5. Age and size not analysed; no population comparison
  of the body is made (Table 3 compares the canal only).
- Error: none reported; no SD.

### 3. Canal to body ratio (Table 4)

- Definition: the MEAN canal sagittal diameter of a level divided by the MEAN body AP diameter of that
  level, per sex (p. 74, PDF 6). It is a ratio of group means, not a mean of per-vertebra ratios, and has no
  SD.
- Rationale given: body size should vary with the individual's build, and with it pedicle height and
  laminar width, which set the sagittal diameters of the canal and of the foramen (p. 74, PDF 6).
- Values, Table 4, p. 74 (PDF 6): 0.6 at L1 and 0.5 at L2-L5, in both sexes. The author reads this as
  confirming that the canal is more capacious at L1 than below, and suggests such ratios may be useful in practice,
  both when lateral lumbar radiographs are assessed clinically and in forensic work
  (p. 74-75, PDF 6-7).
- Inference, recomputed from the table's means to two decimals: males 0.57, 0.52, 0.46, 0.46, 0.50 (0.47
  with the L5 body of 34.2); females 0.61, 0.55, 0.49, 0.45, 0.47. The one-decimal rounding hides a steady
  fall from L1 to L4.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: measure 1 over measure 2, per vertebra.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: lateral view; both lengths lie in about the same sagittal plane at
  about the same distance from the detector, so magnification cancels to first order, which is what makes
  the ratio usable on an uncalibrated lateral film or fluoroscopy frame. Rotation does not shorten the two
  lengths equally, so a rotated lateral is still a pitfall.
- Across levels: Inference: falls from L1 to L3-L4 and is flat or rises slightly at L5. Its steps from L1 to
  L2 and L2 to L3 (about 0.05-0.06, roughly 10% of the value) are relatively larger than the canal's own
  steps (about 5%), but no SD of the ratio is given, so whether it separates levels better than the canal
  diameter cannot be judged from this paper.
- Sex, age, size, population: Inference: female ratios are higher than male at L1-L3 (0.61 vs 0.57, 0.55
  vs 0.52, 0.49 vs 0.46) and about equal at L4-L5 (0.45 vs 0.46; 0.47 vs 0.47-0.50): the canal is relatively
  larger in the smaller (female) skeleton at the upper levels. The author's own reading is only that the
  ratio holds at 0.6 and 0.5 in both sexes (p. 74, PDF 6). No age or population comparison of the ratio.
- Error: none reported.
- Not to be confused with the Spinal Index of Jones and Thomson (canal product over body product, per the
  eisenstein1977 note, not this paper), which this paper does not use; Eisenstein 1977 tested and rejected
  that index (per the eisenstein1977 note).

### 4. Antero-posterior diameter of the inferior vertebral notch (Fig. 1b "N")

- Landmarks: the greatest horizontal span from the back surface of the vertebral body's lower part
  to the deep (inner) surface of the lamina (p. 72, PDF 4); Fig. 1b (p. 70, PDF 2), a lateral view
  of an L3, draws N below the pedicle. Which side, or whether both sides were averaged, is not stated.
- Meaning: the inferior notch forms the cranial boundary and the anterior and posterior walls of the
  intervertebral foramen, so on an isolated vertebra its maximum horizontal AP width is offered as an index
  of foramen size (p. 75, PDF 7).
- Values, Table 5, p. 74 (PDF 6), mean mm (SD; CV %):
  - Males: L1 8.8 (1.2; 13.6), L2 8.4 (1.3; 15.5), L3 7.5 (1.3; 17.3), L4 7.6 (1.1; 14.5), L5 7.0 (0.9; 12.9).
  - Females: L1 8.1 (0.9; 11.1), L2 7.8 (0.7; 9.0), L3 7.5 (1.1; 14.7), L4 7.4 (1.2; 16.2), L5 7.3 (1.0; 13.7).
- Its ratio to the body AP is measure 5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In a parasagittal plane through each pedicle, below the pedicle's
  inferior border, the AP gap (along y, horizontal in the vertebra's frame) between the posterior surface
  of the body subregion and the anterior surface of the posterior-element subregion (lamina and inferior
  articular process); take the maximum over the notch's height; report each side and their mean, since the
  paper does not say which it took.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: lateral view, the AP width of the foramen's upper bay below the
  pedicle; the two sides superimpose and any rotation separates or blurs them; divide by magnification. Not
  from an AP.
- Across levels: a slight but steady fall from L1 to L5 in both sexes, with much variation (p. 75, PDF 7);
  the discussion restates that the foramen's maximum sagittal diameter falls steadily from L1 to L5 while
  the canal is hour-glass shaped (p. 76, PDF 8). Inference: Table 5's male L3 (7.5) is below L4 (7.6), so
  the fall is not strictly monotone; adjacent steps of 0.1-0.9 mm against SDs of 0.7-1.3 mm separate no
  adjacent pair alone.
- Sex, age, size, population: Inference from Table 5: females 0.7, 0.6, 0.0, 0.2 mm smaller at L1-L4 and
  0.3 mm larger at L5. Female SDs are smaller than male at L1-L2 (0.9, 0.7 vs 1.2, 1.3). Age, size and
  population not analysed for the notch.
- Error: none reported; between-subject CV 9.0-17.3%.

### 5. Notch to body ratio (Table 6)

- Definition: the mean notch AP diameter of a level divided by the mean body AP diameter of that level,
  per sex, a ratio of group means with no SD; the author computed it to see whether the notch bears a
  fixed relation to the vertebra, since the notch and the canal behave differently for a given pedicle
  height (p. 75, PDF 7).
- Values, Table 6, p. 75 (PDF 7): males 0.3 at L1-L2 and 0.2 at L3-L5; females the same except 0.3 at L3
  (text, p. 75, PDF 7). Inference to two decimals: males 0.30, 0.27, 0.23, 0.22, 0.20-0.22 (0.22 with the
  Table 4 L5 body of 32.2, 0.20 with Table 6's 34.2); females 0.31, 0.28, 0.26, 0.24, 0.23. Table 6 prints
  male notch L3 and L4 as 7.6 and 7.5, swapped against Table 5 (see Gaps); the ratios round the same.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: measure 4 over measure 2, per vertebra and side.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: lateral view; like the canal to body ratio, magnification cancels
  to first order; the author offers these ratios for lateral films and forensic work (p. 75, PDF 7).
- Across levels: Inference: falls steadily from about 0.30 at L1 to 0.20-0.23 at L5 in both sexes, a
  steadier fall than the canal to body ratio, which flattens below L3.
- Sex, age, size, population: Inference: female values 0.01-0.03 higher than male at every level; no age
  or population comparison.
- Error: none reported; one-decimal rounding (+/-0.05) is a fifth of the value.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1 (thoracolumbar junction): the widest canal in both sexes, as in the other populations studied (p. 76,
  PDF 8), canal to body ratio 0.6 (Table 4), the largest notch (Table 5). The author's explanation: Davis
  (1955, whose paper is titled "The thoracolumbar mortice joint", reference list p. 77, PDF 9) notes that in
  most people L1 coincides with the functional transition from the relatively immobile thoracic spine to
  the mobile lumbar segment; the author adds that L1 houses the lower end of the lumbar enlargement of the
  cord and the conus medullaris, so its width may reflect both the size of those contents and an
  adaptation protecting them during the complex movements of this transitional region; and that the
  curvature changes there from thoracic convexity to lumbar concavity, so the lower cord tends to be
  displaced dorsally in the erect posture and the sagittal diameter must be capacious (p. 76, PDF 8). T12
  was not measured.
- L2: intermediate; about 1 mm narrower than L1 (p. 73, PDF 5).
- L3: the narrowest male canal (14.9 mm); the hour-glass waist in males (p. 76, PDF 8).
- L4: the narrowest female canal (14.1 mm) (p. 73, PDF 5); the male SD doubles (2.0) and the sex gap widens
  to about 1.5 mm; the male 95% range is 11.6-19.6 mm (Table 2).
- L5 (lumbosacral junction): the male canal widens to 16.0 mm with the largest spread (SD 2.4; range
  11.2-20.8); the female to 14.6 mm. Greater variability at L4 and L5, with the male spread wider, confirms
  Larsen and Smith (1981) and matches the author's own 1982 finding for interpedicular distances. The
  author calls the reason for the wide variation at L5 unclear, and suggests the L5 widening is an
  adaptation to the sacral nerve roots, which would be bowstrung as the mobile lumbar segment angles
  against the fixed sacrum at the lumbosacral angulation (p. 76, PDF 8). The smallest notch
  is at L5 in both sexes (7.0 mm male, 7.3 mm female) (Table 5, p. 74, PDF 6). S1 was not measured.
- C2/C3 and C7/T1: not covered.

## Measurement error and reliability

- No intra- or inter-observer study, no repeat measurement and no technical error figure. p. 69-78
  (PDF 1-10).
- Instrument resolution: 0.1 mm (sliding vernier caliper) (p. 72, PDF 4); the canal landmark was chosen for
  reproducibility on Verbiest's (1977) authority, and the Jones and Thomson method is called accurate and
  reproducible (p. 69, PDF 1; p. 72, PDF 4).
- Between-subject spread: canal SD 1.0-2.4 mm (CV 6.0-15.0%), notch SD 0.7-1.3 mm (CV 9.0-17.3%), body AP no
  SD (Tables 1, 4, 5).
- The 95% limits are by Bradford Hill's formula (p. 73, PDF 5); Inference: mean +/- 2 SD, so they assume a
  normal distribution, which the wide male L4-L5 spread may not satisfy.
- Unquantified sources: incomplete medical histories (p. 71, PDF 3); sex and age determination not
  described; the number of readings per vertebra and the side taken for the notch are not stated.
- Rounding: the ratios of Tables 4 and 6 are printed to one decimal (+/-0.05, a tenth of the canal to
  body value), and Eisenstein's comparison means in Table 3 to whole millimetres (+/-0.5 mm); both are
  coarser than the level steps they are used to judge (Inference).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- The lumbar stenosis syndrome (Verbiest's term, 1954-1977): narrowing of the canal and foramina
  compresses the cauda equina and the emerging roots, causing back and leg pain on walking, weakness and
  paraesthesiae in the roots' distribution, classically relieved by reversing the lordosis by bending or
  crouching; it may be congenital or developmental. p. 69 (PDF 1).
- Two types: in developmental stenosis (Verbiest 1977) the transverse (interpedicular) diameters are normal
  and the sagittal reduced, by thickened laminae and articular processes and sometimes short pedicles;
  telling the types apart depends on showing which diameter is involved, hence the need for population
  baselines. p. 69 (PDF 1).
- No numeric stenosis threshold is given. Inference: the lower 95% limits of Table 2 are this paper's
  implicit lower limits of normal for Nigerian dry bone: male 14.6, 13.8, 12.9, 11.6, 11.2 mm and female
  13.4, 12.9, 12.0, 11.5, 12.2 mm at L1-L5. For comparison only (not this paper): Eisenstein 1977's skeletal
  lower limit is 13 mm and radiological 15 mm (eisenstein1977 note); Verbiest's thresholds of about 10 mm
  (absolute) and 10-12 mm (relative) are Inference from memory.
- What sets the sagittal diameter: the canal's hour-glass profile and the foramen's steady fall share
  pedicle height yet diverge, so the laminae matter more: their inclination, the interlaminar angle and
  laminar thickness are probably stronger determinants than pedicle height (p. 76-77, PDF 8-9; summary
  p. 77, PDF 9). The author presents this as amplifying Verbiest's (1977) view that developmental lumbar
  stenosis comes from disturbed growth of the pedicles, laminae and articular processes (p. 77, PDF 9).
  None of laminar angle, interlaminar angle, laminar thickness or pedicle height was measured in this
  study, so the conclusion is reasoned from the two level patterns, not shown. The author adds that this
  fits Chynn et al. (1978), who in stenosis found abnormal form and orientation of the superior articular
  process, overgrowth of the inferior articular process and bulky laminae (p. 77, PDF 9).
- Forensic and radiographic use: the canal to body and notch to body ratios are offered for lateral
  radiograph appraisal and forensic work (p. 75, PDF 7).
- Pitfall: population norms differ. The author's case is that diagnostic work needs baseline values
  (p. 69, PDF 1) and that the diameter shows racial variation (p. 75-77, PDF 7-9); Inference: use norms of
  the patient's own population where they exist, while noting Eisenstein's caution, quoted in the
  introduction, that racial differences are subtle and probably insignificant (p. 69, PDF 1).
- Pitfall: a published comparison without SDs (Eisenstein's) supports only broad conclusions (p. 74, PDF 6).
- Pitfall: the author suggests that in a few men the lower lumbar canal is smaller than in women (p. 76,
  PDF 8); Inference: canal size alone does not tell sex.
- Pitfall: the foramen (notch) and the canal follow different level patterns although pedicle height is
  common to both (p. 75-77, PDF 7-9); Inference: the size of one does not predict the other.
- Pitfall (Inference): the canal value here is a minimum near the upper border at a laminar bulge; a value
  taken elsewhere on the arch, or on a film without magnification correction, is not comparable.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Treat every value of this paper as a dry-bone caliper value (p. 69-72, PDF 1-4). Inference: before
   comparing a radiograph, divide the film length by its magnification; before comparing a CT, use bone
   voxels only.
2. Measure the canal's sagittal diameter in the mid-sagittal plane from the posterior body cortex to the
   inner surface of the arch, at the narrowest point near the vertebra's upper border, opposite the slight
   anterior bulge of the posterior wall (p. 72, PDF 4; Fig. 1a, p. 70, PDF 2). Inference for a mask: take
   the minimum over the axial slabs where the arch closes the ring, not a mid-height or mean value.
3. Read the canal diameter over the whole lumbar run, not one vertebra at a time: expect the widest at L1,
   the waist at L3 in men and L3-L4 in women, and a re-widening at L5; use the turning point, not a single
   value, to place L3 (p. 73, PDF 5; p. 76, PDF 8; Fig. 2, p. 71, PDF 3).
4. Do not use the canal diameter alone to tell L1 from L5 in a man: the means are 16.6 and 16.0 mm with SDs
   of 1.0 and 2.4 (Table 1, p. 71, PDF 3).
5. Measure the body's AP depth in the midline at mid-height (the waist), not across the end-plate rims, when
   comparing with this paper; expect it to rise from L1 to L4 by about 1.4-2.0 mm a level and to stop
   rising at L5 (p. 72, PDF 4; Table 4, p. 74, PDF 6).
6. On a lateral radiograph or fluoroscopy frame with unknown magnification, compute the canal to body ratio
   (the author suggests such ratios for lateral films, p. 75, PDF 7): expect about 0.57-0.61 at L1,
   0.52-0.55 at L2 and 0.45-0.50 at L3-L5 (Inference: two-decimal ratios of the means in Table 4, p. 74,
   PDF 6). Treat it as a weak cue: these are ratios of group means with no spread printed.
7. Measure the inferior notch in a parasagittal plane below the pedicle, from the posterior surface of the
   lower body to the deep surface of the lamina, horizontally, at its widest (p. 72, PDF 4; Fig. 1b, p. 70,
   PDF 2); Inference: do it on each side, as the paper does not say which side it used. Expect about 8-9 mm
   at L1 falling to about 7 mm at L5, and use it only as a weak, slowly falling trend (Table 5, p. 74,
   PDF 6; p. 75, PDF 7).
8. Adjust for sex: expect a woman's canal to be about 0.7-0.8 mm smaller at L1-L3 and about 1.5 mm smaller
   at L4-L5, and her body about 3 mm shallower at L1-L4 (Tables 1 and 4, p. 71 and p. 74, PDF 3 and 6).
9. Expect the widest spread at male L4 and L5 (SD 2.0 and 2.4 mm); widen the likelihood there rather than
   reading an extreme value as a wrong level (Table 1, p. 71, PDF 3; p. 76, PDF 8).
10. Inference (the paper sets no stenosis threshold): flag a canal below the Table 2 lower 95% limit for
    the level and sex (for example under 11.6 mm at a male L4, Table 2, p. 73, PDF 5) as outside the
    Nigerian dry-bone normal range; then look at the laminae (inclination, interlaminar angle, thickness)
    and the articular processes as well as the pedicles, which the author and Chynn et al. name as the
    likely causes (p. 77, PDF 9).
11. Use Nigerian norms for a Nigerian patient and say so; do not transfer them to another population without
    noting the difference, especially in women, whose canals the author finds narrower than black South
    African women's by 0.2-1.9 mm (Table 3, p. 73, PDF 5; p. 76, PDF 8). Inference: the step from the
    author's finding to a rule of practice is ours.
12. Do not draw anomaly priors (L6, transitional L5, trefoil canal) from this paper; it reports none
    (pp. 69-78, PDF 1-10).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper reports no transitional vertebra, no L6, no
    T13, no rib count and no canal shape prevalence, and does not say how levels were identified (pp. 69-78,
    PDF 1-10).

(b) Measures:
  - Canal sagittal diameter: the generator's lumbar canal depth (Panjabi's SCD) is the nearest. Definition
    difference: this paper takes the MINIMUM near the cranial border at a laminar bulge; Panjabi's SCD (per
    the panjabi1992 note in this set) runs in the midline from the body's posterior wall to the arch at the
    spinous base, digitised, with values 19.0, 18.2, 17.5, 18.6, 19.7 mm at L1-L5, which are 2.4-3.7 mm
    above this paper's male means (Inference). Both show the same U with the minimum at L3 in men. A mask
    value must choose its definition (minimum near the cranial ring for this paper, the Panjabi landmark for
    Panjabi) and never mix them; the population (Nigerian dry bone vs Panjabi's cadavers) differs too.
    Inference: this paper's landmark is the same as Eisenstein 1977's dry-bone canal AP (per the
    eisenstein1977 note), so the two can be pooled by definition, with population and rounding apart.
  - Body AP at mid-height (waist): not in Panjabi, who gives end-plate depths (EPDu, EPDl); it reads smaller.
    Could be added as a separate measure (for example "VBDm"), with these means as its only source here.
  - Inferior notch AP width: not in the generator; a candidate foramen proxy, tied to pedicle height and
    the laminae (p. 75-77, PDF 7-9).
  - Ratios canal/body and notch/body: magnification-free features for lateral radiographs and fluoroscopy
    (Inference); derive them from drawn dimensions rather than drawing them separately.
(c) Measurement error: none given. Use the per-level SDs of Tables 1 and 5 as between-subject spread only;
    instrument resolution 0.1 mm; for a CT mask the voxel size will dominate (Inference). Body AP has no SD.
(d) Covariates:
  - Sex: female canal 0.7-1.5 mm smaller, female body AP about 3 mm smaller at L1-L4, female notch 0.0-0.7 mm
    smaller at L1-L4 and 0.3 mm larger at L5 (Tables 1, 4, 5). Inference: at L1-L3 the canal's female to
    male ratio (0.95-0.96) is closer to 1 than the body's (0.89-0.90), so the canal scales less than
    proportionally with body size there; at L4 the two ratios are similar (0.90 vs 0.91).
  - Age: 23-60 years, not analysed.
  - Population: Nigerian adults (University of Ibadan collection); compared with Zulu and Sotho means in
    Table 3, p. 73 (PDF 5). A population term for the canal is supported by the author's reading (p. 76-77,
    PDF 8-9) but no test is given.
  - Size: no stature or individual-level size measure; only the ratio of group means (Table 4).

## Gaps and conflicts

- No radiographs: the paper gives no landmark, magnification or value on a lateral film; its only link to
  radiographs is the suggestion that the ratios may help appraise lateral films (p. 75, PDF 7).
- Male L5 body AP: 32.2 mm in Table 4 (p. 74, PDF 6) against 34.2 mm in Table 6 (p. 75, PDF 7).
- Male notch L3 and L4: 7.5 and 7.6 mm in Table 5 (p. 74, PDF 6) against 7.6 and 7.5 mm in Table 6 (p. 75,
  PDF 7); Table 5's CVs (17.3 and 14.5) agree with Table 5's means, so Table 6 has them swapped (Inference).
- The notch is said to fall steadily (p. 75, PDF 7) but Table 5's male L4 exceeds L3.
- The body AP is named a "maximum" diameter but measured where the waist is narrowest (p. 72, PDF 4).
- The text says the tolerance ranges "varied narrowly especially at L4 and L5" (p. 73, PDF 5), yet Table 2's
  male ranges are widest there (8.0 and 9.6 mm wide against 4.0 at L1-L3). Inference: "widely" was meant,
  or the remark refers to females.
- Female L2 upper limit 17.2 mm against mean + 2 SD = 17.3 (Table 2 vs Table 1); rounding.
- Ratios are ratios of means, rounded to one decimal, with no SD, which hides their level trend.
- Canal, groove and foramen shapes were recorded but never reported (p. 71-72, PDF 3-4).
- Per-level n, missing vertebrae, sex and age determination and the Nigerian ethnic groups are not stated.
- No significance tests for the sex or population differences; "markedly narrower" Nigerian female canals
  (p. 76, PDF 8) rests on differences of 0.2-1.9 mm (Inference from Table 3) against South African means
  printed as whole millimetres.
- The discussion says the results confirm differences in adult canal size between Nigerians and the
  samples of Hinck et al. (1966) and Eisenstein (1977) (p. 75, PDF 7), but the reference list gives Hinck
  et al. (1966) as normal interpediculate distances (p. 77, PDF 9), a transverse measure this paper did not
  take, and no Hinck value appears anywhere here. Inference: the claim rests on the author's 1982 AP-film
  comparison of interpedicular distances with Hinck's white Americans (amonookuofi1982 note), not on the
  sagittal data of this paper; or Hinck et al. (1965), the sagittal paper, was meant.
- The p. 74 (PDF 6) reading that Nigerian male canals are greater than black South African males' is at
  odds with p. 76 (PDF 8), which says the males show no appreciable difference. Inference from Table 3:
  Nigerian males exceed Zulu males by -0.1 to 0.8 mm and Sotho males by 0.0 to 0.9 mm, within
  Eisenstein's whole-millimetre rounding.
- The summary (p. 77, PDF 9) says the canal is determined primarily by the thickness and orientation of
  the lamina and less by pedicle height, but none of these was measured; the conclusion is argued from
  the differing level patterns of canal and notch (p. 76-77, PDF 8-9).
- Larsen and Smith (1981), cited as confirmed for the greater L4-L5 variability in adults (p. 76, PDF 8),
  is titled as a study of children's canals in the reference list (p. 77, PDF 9).
- The Table 3 header misspells Eisenstein once (female Zulu column, p. 73, PDF 5); no effect on values.
- Cross-paper (from the notes in this set, not this paper): Eisenstein 1977's Zulu and Sotho canal means
  match Table 3 here exactly; his Caucasoid means are higher (male 18 17 16 16 18 mm); Eisenstein judged
  the race and sex differences insignificant and his table shows females equal to or larger than males,
  whereas this paper finds Nigerian females narrower at every level and argues for racial variation.
  Panjabi 1992's SCD (17.5-19.7 mm) is 2.4-3.7 mm above the male and 3.1-5.1 mm above the female means
  here, reflecting definition (minimum vs midline arch point), method (digitiser vs caliper) and
  population together; the three cannot be separated from these papers.
