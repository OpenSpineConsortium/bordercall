## What the paper is

- Design: cadaveric dry-bone morphometry, the third of Panjabi's series after the cervical and
  thoracic papers (p. 299, PDF 1; refs 12 and 13). The methods are given in brief and referred to
  ref 13, the thoracic paper, which the text calls "the initial document" (p. 299, PDF 1); ref 13
  calls itself the first paper of the series of three (ref 13, p. 888).
- Motive (p. 299, PDF 1): textbook shapes are artists' drawings with no numbers (refs 6, 7);
  earlier lumbar series gave only pedicle-device dimensions (refs 8, 9, 17) or a subset of
  elements (Berry, ref 1; Scoles, ref 15); mathematical models of the spine need better data.
- Specimens: 12 fresh autopsy spines, each from the axis to the last lumbar vertebra (p. 299,
  PDF 1); "A total of 12 C2 to L5 spines" (p. 300, PDF 2). This report: 60 lumbar vertebrae
  (abstract p. 299, PDF 1; p. 300, PDF 2). Inference: 60 = 12 x 5, so each spine gave five lumbar
  levels; the paper never states it spine by spine.
- Subjects: mean age 46.3 years (range 19-59), mean weight 67.8 kg (range 54-85), mean height
  167.8 cm (range 157-178), male to female 8/4, so 8 men and 4 women; the specimens were
  identified by age, sex and height (p. 299, PDF 1). No country, ethnicity or cause of death is
  given in this paper. Inference: the first affiliation is Yale (New Haven, Connecticut), so a
  United States autopsy source is likely, but the paper does not say.
- Same twelve spines as ref 13 (Inference, strongly supported): ref 13's Table 1 (ref 13,
  p. 889) lists 12 specimens whose ages, sexes, heights and weights give exactly this paper's
  means and ranges except that its heaviest is 84 kg, not 85 (computed: mean age 46.3, weight
  67.8, height 167.8; 8 M, 4 F). Its causes of death: five carcinomas (breast, oesophageal, right
  lung, pancreatic, carcinomatosis), three cardiac (coronary artery disease, two myocardial
  infarctions), and one each of acute lymphocytic leukaemia, renal failure, third-degree burns
  and subdural haemorrhage.
- Screening: the specimens were radiographed "to identify any significant abnormalities"
  (p. 299, PDF 1). No abnormality, exclusion or replacement is reported, and what counted as
  significant is not said.
- Preparation: all soft tissue removed (p. 299, PDF 1). Ref 13 (p. 888) is more specific: muscles,
  ligaments and discs removed, remaining soft tissue softened by boiling, then bleach (0.5 h) and
  75% ethanol (1 h), which also separated the vertebrae. So the measured objects are cleaned dry
  bone, not embalmed and not wet specimens.
- Markers: two pointed threaded rods, printed as 2 mm in diameter and 2.5 mm long, fixed into each
  vertebral body; the two ends of one rod and the end of the other are the three marker points;
  AP and lateral radiographs located the pins in the body coordinate system, origin at the centre
  of the upper end plate (p. 299, PDF 1). (Ref 13, p. 888, gives 3.0 mm by 3.2 cm; see Gaps.)
- Instrument: a purpose-built morphometer (ref 10) of one linear (LVDT) and two rotational (RVDT)
  displacement transducers whose axes meet at one point, giving spherical coordinates (r, theta,
  phi); its tip was placed on each point and on the three markers, and coordinates went straight
  into a computer (p. 299, PDF 1).
- Digitizing: each part was a series of points spanning or circumventing it. Facets: up to 20
  points spanning the surface. End plates, spinous processes, transverse processes and pedicles:
  points circumventing the structure, enough that straight lines between adjacent points describe
  it (p. 299, PDF 1). How the canal and the pars were digitized is not stated. The facets were
  digitized but no facet result is reported in this paper (Tables 2-5 have none).
- Outputs: linear (mm), angular (deg) and area (mm2) parameters for the body, canal, pedicles,
  pars, spinous and transverse processes (Tables 2 to 5, pp. 301-304, PDF 3-6), as mean +/- SEM of
  the 12 specimens ("The results of 12 specimens were pooled", p. 300, PDF 2); Table 6 (p. 305,
  PDF 7) sets selected left-right averaged means beside six earlier studies.
- Statistics: one-way ANOVA and Scheffe's F test for pairwise comparison of levels at 95%
  (p. 300, PDF 2). A dagger in Tables 2, 4 and 5 marks P < .05 against the levels in parentheses;
  Table 3 has none.
- Levels and counting: L1 to L5 only. How L1 was identified (from C2 down, from the last rib) is
  not stated. Inference: the spines ran from C2 and the series reported 12 thoracic levels per
  spine (ref 13 abstract, p. 888: 144 thoracic vertebrae = 12 x 12), so the authors assigned
  6 cervical (C2-C7), 12 thoracic and 5 lumbar vertebrae to every spine; how T12 was told from L1
  after the ribs and soft tissue were removed is not said in either paper.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper reports no anatomical anomaly: no lumbosacral transitional vertebra, no sacralisation or
lumbarisation, no L6, no T13, no rib count, no block vertebra. The specimens stopped at "the last
lumbar vertebra" without the sacrum (p. 299, PDF 1), and the radiographic screen's findings are not
given. Inference: 60 = 12 x 5 (p. 300, PDF 2) and 144 = 12 x 12 in ref 13 mean every spine was
numbered as 5 lumbar and 12 thoracic; whether any spine with a numerical variant was excluded
before counting, or numbered into this pattern, cannot be told.

Pitfall: the abstract's "transitional" (p. 299, PDF 1) and the discussion's "Transition Zones"
(p. 304, PDF 6) are morphological regions of a normal column, not transitional anomalies: the
abstract calls L1 and L2 transitional toward the thoracic region and L4 and L5 toward the sacral
region. Do not read them as a prevalence of LSTV.

Inference: because no sacrum was kept, a partially sacralised L5 (an enlarged transverse process
or a pseudarthrosis with the ala) could not have been recognised on the specimen itself, and if
one were present its TPW and pars would be in the L5 means. The large SEM of TPW at L4 and L5 (3.78
and 3.68 mm against 1.48-2.44 above, Table 5, p. 304, PDF 6) is compatible with that but proves
nothing.

Inference: five of the twelve donors died of carcinoma (ref 13, p. 889); no vertebral metastasis
is mentioned in either paper.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### The vertebral body frame (applies to every measure below)

- Definition in this paper: four extra points were digitized on the posterior wall of the body,
  at its left and right upper and lower edges; a least-squares fit to them gave the frontal (xy),
  transverse (xz) and sagittal (yz) planes, and the centre of the upper end plate, digitized, is
  the origin (p. 299, PDF 1). Figure 1 (p. 300, PDF 2): Y points cranially, Z anteriorly (the
  side view's Z arrow points toward the body; the top view's Z arrow points from the body's
  centre toward its anterior rim), X laterally (to the figure's right in the front and top views).
- Ref 13 (p. 889, its Fig 1) spells the frame out: the four points are the right superior, left
  superior, right inferior and left inferior edges of the posterior wall; their least-squares
  plane is the frontal plane; Z is perpendicular to it; X is parallel to the line through the two
  superior points and points to the left; Y is perpendicular to the z-x plane; the origin is the
  centroid of the superior end plate. So +X is the specimen's left and X, Y, Z is right-handed,
  which this paper's three views and isometric view are consistent with (Inference for this
  paper, which does not name the side). Ref 13 chose the posterior-wall corners because they were
  consistent and varied least in the presence of osteophytes (ref 13, p. 889).
- Consequence: every width is along this vertebra's own X, every depth along its own Z, every
  height along its own Y, and every angle is to this vertebra's own planes. Nothing is measured
  against the column, the image or gravity. The frame is anchored on the posterior wall, not on
  the end plates.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): find the four corners where the posterior wall of the
  body meets the superior and inferior end plates, at the wall's left and right ends (near the
  pedicle bases); fit a plane to them (or to the posterior-wall voxels between them); its normal
  pointing away from the canal is Z; X is the direction from the right upper corner to the left
  upper corner, projected into that plane; Y = Z x X (cranial); the origin is the centroid of the
  superior end-plate surface. Rotate the mask into this frame before taking any extent. Taking
  extents along the scanner axes instead tilts with lordosis (L5 can sit 20 to 30 deg off the
  scanner's axial plane) and inflates depths and heights.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): on a true lateral, Y is along the posterior body line and Z
  perpendicular to it; on an AP, X is the line joining the upper posterior corners, which an AP
  view cannot separate from the anterior ones, so the end-plate line stands in. Rotation of the
  patient breaks the frame.

### EPWu, EPWl: upper and lower end-plate width

- Landmarks: the lateral width of each end plate (p. 301, PDF 3); in Figure 1's front view
  (p. 300, PDF 2) EPWu spans the upper end-plate rim and EPWl the lower rim, drawn out to the
  lateral lips of the lower end plate. The end plate was digitized as a loop of points around it
  (p. 299, PDF 1), and the "major and minor linear dimensions of each vertebral part were
  calculated" from the coordinates (p. 300, PDF 2). Instrument: the morphometer. Levels: L1-L5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the rim of the body's cranial (caudal) surface, its extent along the
  vertebra's X. Inference: include the ring apophysis and marginal lipping, as the dry-bone loop
  did; SPINEPS's end-plate subregions (52, 53) are thin slabs and their X extent is close to this.
- From a radiograph alone: AP view, the width of the end-plate line between the lateral cortices,
  divided by the magnification. Not from a lateral. (Inference; the paper measured no radiograph.)
- Across levels: widths grow about 14% from L1 to L5 (p. 301, PDF 3; Figure 2A, p. 303, PDF 5):
  EPWu 41.2 at L1 to 47.3 at L5; EPWl 43.3 at L1, 49.5 at L4, 49.4 at L5 (Table 2, p. 301,
  PDF 3), so the lower width plateaus at L4-L5. The lower width exceeds the upper at every level
  (by 2.1, 2.9, 3.9, 2.9 and 2.1 mm, computed from Table 2). Only L4 and L5 differ from L1 at
  P < .05 (both widths); no adjacent pair differs.
- Comparison: EPWl agreed best with Scoles, was several millimetres below Berry and above
  Cotterill (p. 304, PDF 6; Table 6, p. 305, PDF 7: Berry 49.1-54.8, Cotterill 44.3 at L3).
- Sex, age, size, population: pooled; no breakdown (p. 299, PDF 1).
- Error: the method's accuracy, under 5% (p. 300, PDF 2). SEM 0.74-1.41 mm, a population SD of
  about 2.6-4.9 mm (SEM x sqrt(12), computed, assuming n = 12 at each level, which the paper does
  not state level by level).

### EPDu, EPDl: upper and lower end-plate depth

- Landmarks: the anteroposterior depth of each end plate (p. 301, PDF 3); Figure 1's side view
  draws EPDu across the upper end plate and EPDl across the lower, anterior rim to posterior rim
  (p. 300, PDF 2). Levels L1-L5.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the end-plate rim's extent along the vertebra's Z (Inference).
- From a radiograph alone: lateral view, the length of the end-plate line from anterior to
  posterior cortex, over the magnification (Inference). Not from an AP.
- Across levels: "relatively constant" (p. 301, PDF 3): EPDu 34.1-35.5 mm; EPDl falls slowly from
  35.3 at L1 to 33.2 at L5 (Table 2, p. 301, PDF 3). No level difference is significant. Depth
  alone does not separate lumbar levels.
- Comparison: EPDl "corresponded well" with the other studies (p. 304, PDF 6).
- Error: as above; SEM 0.74-1.34 mm.

### EPWu/EPDu, EPWl/EPDl: end-plate width to depth (derived)

- Definition: the ratio of the two linear dimensions (Table 2, p. 301, PDF 3; Figure 2B, p. 303,
  PDF 5). Computed check: every printed ratio equals the ratio of the two printed means, so it is
  a ratio of means, not a mean of per-specimen ratios, and has no SEM.
- Across levels: both rise from L1 to L5, the upper by about 12% of the L1 value and the lower by
  21% (p. 301, PDF 3). Lower: 1.23, 1.30, 1.38, 1.46, 1.49; upper: 1.21, 1.23, 1.25, 1.31, 1.36
  (Table 2).
- Use: size-free, so it needs no patient-size factor; it needs both a width (AP view or mask) and a
  depth (lateral view or mask). Inference: the lower ratio rises by 0.07-0.08 per level from L1 to
  L4, the most regular level gradient among the body measures.

### EPAu, EPAl: upper and lower end-plate area

- Definition: the projected areas of the digitized end-plate loop on the frontal (xy), sagittal
  (yz) and transverse (zx) planes, each from two of the three coordinates; the total is the square
  root of the sum of their squares (p. 300, PDF 2). Figure 1's front view points EPAu and EPAl at
  the two end plates.
- Inference: that total is the magnitude of the loop's vector area, exactly the area of a flat
  loop and less than the true area of a curved one. EPA is therefore the area enclosed by the
  end-plate rim, not the area of the concave end-plate surface.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the area enclosed by the rim of the body's cranial
  (caudal) surface, projected onto the end plate's best-fit plane; equivalently the body's
  cross-sectional area just inside the end plate, taken perpendicular to the end plate's normal.
- From a radiograph alone: not measurable; pi x EPW x EPD / 4 from an AP width and a lateral
  depth overestimates it by under 10% (p. 305, PDF 7; computed from Table 2: 1.7-7.5% over).
- Across levels: L5 is 17% (upper) and 9% (lower) larger than L1 (p. 301, PDF 3); the upper area
  rises steadily and nearly linearly from L1 to L4 and holds at L5 (1057 at L1, 1239 at L4, 1237 at
  L5); the lower peaks at L3 (1290) and falls to 1218 at L5 (Table 2; Figure 2C, p. 303, PDF 5).
  No level difference is significant. The discussion places the greatest end-plate areas in the
  middle region (p. 304, PDF 6).
- Error: under 5% (p. 300, PDF 2); SEM 49-64 mm2.

### EPItu, EPItl: end-plate inclination to the transverse plane

- Definition: a least-squares plane fitted to each part's digitized points; the angles between
  that plane and each anatomic plane of the vertebra (p. 300, PDF 2); the suffix t is the
  transverse plane (Table 1, p. 301, PDF 3). Only the transverse-plane angle is tabulated; Table 1
  also defines the suffixes s and f, but no end-plate angle to the sagittal or frontal plane is
  reported.
- Inference: the transverse plane here is perpendicular to the posterior-wall plane, so EPIt is
  the end plate's departure from perpendicular to the posterior wall, in 3D (sagittal and coronal
  tilt together). An angle between two planes is unsigned, so the means (1.7-4.7 deg) are of
  magnitudes, and the paper gives no direction; the sum of the upper and lower values is not a
  signed wedge angle. No anterior body height is reported, so no wedge index can be formed.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): fit a plane to the superior (inferior) end-plate surface
  voxels; take the angle between its normal and the vertebra's Y axis.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): lateral view, the angle between the end-plate line and the
  perpendicular to the posterior body line gives the sagittal part only; the coronal part needs an
  AP view.
- Across levels: EPItu 2.7, 3.5, 1.7, 4.7, 2.2; EPItl 4.0, 2.1, 2.7, 2.7, 1.8 (Table 2) with no
  trend and no significant difference; useless for naming a lumbar level.
- Error: the accuracy object had faces at 30, 45 and 60 deg and gave errors under 5% (p. 300,
  PDF 2). Inference: a relative error tested on 30-60 deg faces says little about angles of 2-5
  deg, where the fit to a concave end plate dominates.

### VBHp: posterior vertebral body height

- Landmarks: the body height at the posterior wall, between the upper and lower end plates, drawn
  as a vertical double arrow at the posterior wall in Figure 1's side view (p. 300, PDF 2); suffix
  p, posterior (Table 1, p. 301, PDF 3). Table 1 also defines the suffix a, anterior, but no
  anterior height is tabulated. Inference: the four posterior-wall edge points that defined the
  frame give such a height (left and right); the paper does not say which points were used.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the Y-extent of the body in a thin slab at its posterior
  wall, not the body's maximum height; whether at the midline or as the mean of the left and right
  corners is not stated. bordercall's SPINEPS_MAPPING.md takes the median column height in the
  posterior 20% of label 49, which is in this spirit.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral view, the posterior wall's height from end plate to end plate,
  over the magnification (Inference).
- Across levels: flat from L1 to L4 (23.8, 24.3, 23.8, 24.1) and lowest at L5 (22.9) (Table 2);
  no significant difference. Inference: VBHp alone cannot separate L1-L4; only L5's drop shows.
- Comparison: very close to Cotterill et al and "consistently 1-2 mm less" than Berry et al,
  Scoles et al and Gilad and Nissan (p. 304, PDF 6). Table 6 (p. 305, PDF 7) bears out 1-2 mm only
  against Berry (0.2-2.3 mm); against Scoles and Gilad the gap is about 3-5 mm (for example L2
  24.3 against Gilad 28.0; L5 22.9 against Scoles 27.6).
- Error: SEM 0.95-1.10 mm.

### SCW: spinal canal width

- Landmarks: the canal's lateral width (p. 301, PDF 3); in Figure 1's top view a horizontal double
  arrow across the canal near mid-depth (p. 300, PDF 2). Where along the canal's height it was
  taken, and whether it is the widest chord or the interpedicular distance, is not stated;
  Inference from Figure 1: it lies at the level of the pedicles, between their medial walls.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the vertebral foramen, the hole enclosed by the body's
  posterior wall, the pedicles and the laminae, in a section in the vertebra's transverse plane at
  the pedicles; its extent along X. This is the bony canal of one vertebra, not the dural sac and
  not the canal at a disc level; SPINEPS's spinal-canal label (61) marks the canal's contents along
  the whole column, not one vertebra's bony foramen, and is not the same object.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): AP view, the interpedicular distance between the pedicles'
  medial cortices, over the magnification. Not from a lateral.
- Across levels: "increased steadily" from 23.7 at L1 to 27.1 at L5 (p. 301, PDF 3; Table 3,
  p. 302, PDF 4); nearly flat L1-L3 (the discussion's "relatively constant" widths of the upper
  region, p. 304, PDF 6) and rising faster L3-L5. Table 3 marks no level difference as
  significant.
- Comparison: SCW "of all studies agreed extremely well in both magnitude and trend" (p. 305,
  PDF 7); Table 6 shows Berry 0.8-3.4 mm lower (22.0 against 25.4 at L4) and Cotterill 21.5 at L3.
- Error: SEM 0.49-0.92 mm.

### SCD: spinal canal depth

- Landmarks: the canal's anteroposterior depth (p. 301, PDF 3); Figure 1's top view draws it in
  the midline from the posterior wall of the body to the anterior surface of the laminar arch at
  the base of the spinous process (p. 300, PDF 2).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the foramen's extent along Z in the midsagittal plane,
  from the body's posterior wall to the inner surface of the arch.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): lateral view, from the midpoint of the posterior body line
  to the spinolaminar line, over the magnification. Not from an AP.
- Across levels: a parabola, falling from L1 to L3 and rising to L5 (p. 301, PDF 3): 19.0, 18.2,
  17.5, 18.6, 19.7 (Table 3, p. 302, PDF 4); the narrowest is L3, 17.5 mm (p. 301, PDF 3). Above
  L1 the depth rises from T10 to T12 (p. 304, PDF 6, citing the thoracic paper). Inference: SCD
  therefore peaks near the thoracolumbar junction and bottoms out at L3, a turning point the
  decoder can use; Table 3 shows no significant level difference, so it works only as a pattern
  over several bodies.
- Comparison: slightly larger than Berry et al and Scoles et al, with the same trend (p. 304,
  PDF 6); Cotterill's L3 value is 12.1 against 17.5 here (Table 6, p. 305, PDF 7), not discussed.
- Error: SEM 0.49-0.71 mm (SD about 1.7-2.5 mm, computed).

### SCW/SCD: canal width to depth (derived)

- 1.25, 1.31, 1.39, 1.37, 1.38 (Table 3): rising from L1 to L3 and then constant to L5 (p. 301,
  PDF 3; Figure 3B, p. 303, PDF 5). Size-free; a ratio of the means (computed check); the rise
  L1-L3 is what the authors call the upper transition zone (p. 304, PDF 6).

### SCA: spinal canal area

- Definition: a cross-sectional area by the same projected-area method (p. 300, PDF 2); the canal
  outline's digitization is not described.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the area of the closed vertebral foramen in a section in
  the vertebra's transverse plane at the pedicles.
- From a radiograph alone: not measurable. pi x SCW x SCD / 4 overestimates it by 10-30% and
  SCW x SCD / 2 underestimates it by 20-30%; the ellipse fits better at the upper lumbar levels,
  the triangle at the lower (p. 305, PDF 7; Figure 3C, p. 303, PDF 5). Computed from Table 3: the
  ellipse is 10.5% over at L1 and 27-28% over at L4-L5; the triangle 30% under at L1 and 19% under
  at L4-L5. Inference: the canal turns from oval to triangular going caudally.
- Across levels: falls from L1 to L2, constant L2 to L4, rises to L5 (p. 301, PDF 3): 320, 281,
  280, 290, 330 (Table 3). The lumbosacral transition shows as a "distinct enlarging" of the canal
  area from L3 to L5 (p. 304, PDF 6).
- Error: SEM 14.6-21.2 mm2.

### PDWr, PDWl, PDHr, PDHl: pedicle width and height

- Landmarks: the pedicle's cross-sectional width (PDW) and height (PDH) (p. 301, PDF 3); each
  pedicle was digitized by points circumventing it (p. 299, PDF 1); Figure 1's top view (p. 300,
  PDF 2) shows an oval section, taller than wide, with PDW horizontal and PDH vertical. The paper
  does not say where along the pedicle the loop lay, nor that it was the narrowest section.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): SPINEPS labels no pedicle; walk from the body toward
  the arch along the pedicle axis and take the narrowest cross-section; PDW is its extent along
  the vertebra's X (within the section), PDH its extent along Y.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): AP view, the pedicle's oval gives a width and a height, but
  the beam crosses the pedicle at its convergence angle (14-25 deg here), so the oval is not the
  true section; lateral view, the pedicle's narrowest vertical dimension gives PDH with both sides
  superimposed. Divide by the magnification.
- Across levels: PDH (mean of sides) 15.9 at L1, lowest 14.4 at L3, 19.6 at L5 (p. 301, PDF 3);
  PDW 8.6 at L1, lowest 8.2 at L2, then about +3.5 mm per level to L5 (p. 301, PDF 3). Table 4
  (p. 302, PDF 4) marks PDW at L4 as different from L1-L2 (right) and L1-L3 (left), and every PDW
  and PDH at L5 as different from L1-L4: the pedicle measures give the L3/L4 separation (PDWl)
  and most of the L4/L5 separation (TPW gives the rest), the only adjacent lumbar pairs this paper
  separates at P < .05.
- Comparison: PDH agrees with Berry, Zindrick and Scoles at the upper levels, but Berry and
  Zindrick found it falling at L4 and L5 where this study and Scoles found it rising (p. 305,
  PDF 7); PDW agrees with Zindrick at all levels, while Berry, Scoles and Krag found only small
  increases at L5 (p. 305, PDF 7; at L5, Table 6: 18.6 here, 18.0 Zindrick, 10.7 Berry, 9.7
  Scoles, 13.8 Krag).
- Error: SEM 0.46-1.03 mm except PDWr at L4, 0.18 mm.

### PDH/PDW: pedicle height to width (derived)

- Right 2.00, 1.91, 1.39, 1.17, 1.09; left 1.72, 1.71, 1.44, 1.03, 1.02 (Table 4, p. 302,
  PDF 4); the average is 1.84 at L1 and L2 and approaches 1.0 at L5 (p. 302, PDF 4; Figure 4B,
  p. 303, PDF 5). Ratios of the means (computed check, within 0.013).
- Use: size-free and strongly monotonic; tall narrow pedicles at L1-L2, round at L4-L5. Needs a
  width and a height of the same pedicle (an AP view gives both, distorted; a mask gives both).

### PDAr, PDAl: pedicle cross-sectional area

- Definition: by the projected-area method (p. 300, PDF 2).
- Across levels: falls from L1 to L2 and then rises steadily to L5 (p. 302, PDF 4): right 86.4,
  83.3, 92.9, 102.8, 143.9; left 88.5, 83.6, 95.3, 112.3, 158.7 (Table 4). L5 differs from L1-L3
  (right) and L1-L4 (left).
- Shape: the ellipse (pi x PDW x PDH / 4) fits best at L1 and L2, the triangle (PDW x PDH / 2) at
  L4 and L5, with L3 about midway (p. 305, PDF 7; Figure 4C, p. 303, PDF 5). Computed from
  Table 4: at L4 the triangle is within 2.3% on both sides; at L5 both approximations exceed the
  measured area (triangle 18-23% over, ellipse 85-93% over); at L1 the ellipse is closer on the
  right (16% over against 26% under) but the triangle on the left (18% under against 29% over).
- From a radiograph alone: not measurable.

### PDIsr, PDIsl, PDItr, PDItl: pedicle inclination

- Definition: the angle of the pedicle's centre line, perpendicular to its cross-section, to the
  vertebra's sagittal plane (PDIs) and transverse plane (PDIt) (p. 302, PDF 4). Figure 1 (p. 300,
  PDF 2): PDIs in the top view, between the midline and the pedicle axis, which converges
  anteromedially; PDIt in the side view, the pedicle axis drawn with its posterior end higher.
  Inference: an angle between a line and a plane is unsigned; the paper states no sign convention.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the pedicle axis as the normal of its narrowest section
  (or the principal axis of the pedicle voxels between body and arch); PDIs is its angle to the
  vertebra's yz plane, PDIt to its xz plane.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): PDIs cannot be read from an AP or a lateral; it needs an
  axial image. PDIt can be approximated on a lateral as the pedicle's tilt from the perpendicular
  to the posterior body line.
- Across levels: left and right roughly symmetric (p. 302, PDF 4; Figure 4D). PDIs rises from
  about 14.5 deg at L1 to 24.5 deg at L5 (p. 302, PDF 4), not monotonically: L2 is lowest (14.1,
  Table 6), L3 exceeds L4 on both sides (19.8 and 17.1 against 18.4 and 14.7, Table 4), and L5 is
  8 deg above L4, the "fanning out" that marks the transition to the sacrum (p. 304, PDF 6). Only
  PDIsl at L5 differs, and only from L1-L2, at P < .05; the L4 to L5 jump is not marked
  significant. PDIt rises slightly from 2.5 deg at L1 to 5.5 deg at L5 (p. 302, PDF 4).
- Comparison: the text claims "remarkable consistency" among the studies (p. 305, PDF 7); Table 6
  shows PDIs at L1 of 14.5 here against 7.5 (Berry), at L5 24.6 against 31.5 (Berry) and 29.8
  (Zindrick), and PDIt at L5 of 5.5 here against -1.8 (Zindrick).
- Error: PDIsr at L1 has SEM 5.02 deg (SD about 17 deg, computed); the others 0.64-3.75 deg.

### PDMs, PDMt: pedicle second moments of area (derived)

- Definition: from PDH and PDW of each pedicle, treated as an ellipse: PDMs = pi x PDH^3 x PDW /
  64 and PDMt = pi x PDH x PDW^3 / 64 (printed "PDM" without the t, p. 302, PDF 4); proportional to
  the pedicle's bending stiffness in the sagittal and transverse planes (p. 302, PDF 4).
- Across levels: each roughly constant L1-L3, rising sharply to L5; sagittal always above
  transverse (p. 302, PDF 4; Figure 4E, p. 303, PDF 5). Nothing new for a classifier, as it is a
  function of PDH and PDW.

### PIAr, PIAl: pars interarticularis area

- Definition: Table 1 names it "pars interarticularis area" (p. 301, PDF 3) and the text calls it
  a cross-sectional area (p. 302, PDF 4); no landmark, section plane or digitizing rule is given.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the narrowest cross-section of the pars between the
  superior and inferior articular processes, inside SPINEPS's arch label (41), on each side.
- From a radiograph alone: not measurable (Inference).
- Across levels: a gradual increase from L1 to L5 (p. 302, PDF 4; Figure 5, p. 304, PDF 6): about
  62 mm2 at L1 and 81 mm2 at L5 by the text; the table's right and left are 92.9 and 71.7 at L5
  (Table 5, p. 304, PDF 6), a 21 mm2 side difference, and only PIAr at L5 against L1 is
  significant. Figure 5 plots PIAr at L3 at 80, where Table 5 prints 70.0 (see Gaps).
- Error: SEM 1.17-9.76 mm2, the L1 values (2.19, 1.17) far smaller than the rest (5.90-9.76).

### SPL: spinous process length

- Landmarks: from the centre of the superior end plate (the origin of the coordinate system) to
  the most inferoposterior tip of the spinous process (p. 302, PDF 4); Figure 1's side view draws
  it as a straight line from the origin to the tip (p. 300, PDF 2). It is therefore not the
  length of the process itself but the reach of the whole vertebra from its upper end-plate centre.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference for the choice of voxel): the 3D distance from the centroid
  of the superior end-plate surface to the spinous-process voxel farthest in the
  posterior-inferior direction (SPINEPS label 42).
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): lateral view; both points lie in the midsagittal plane, so
  their distance on a true lateral, over the magnification, is SPL.
- Across levels: about 68 mm at L1, 72 mm at L3, 68 mm at L5 (p. 302, PDF 4): 67.7, 71.7, 71.7,
  70.1, 68.3 (Table 5, p. 304, PDF 6); greatest at L2-L3, which the authors tie to the apex of the
  lordosis at L3 (p. 304, PDF 6). No level difference is significant.
- Error: SEM 1.17-1.77 mm.

### TPW: transverse process width

- Landmarks: from the tip of the left transverse process to the tip of the right (p. 302, PDF 4;
  Figure 1 front view, p. 300, PDF 2).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the 3D distance between the most lateral voxels of the
  two transverse (costal) processes (SPINEPS 43 and 44).
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): AP view, the tip-to-tip distance over the magnification;
  the tips lie near the frontal plane, so the projection loses little.
- Across levels: rising in general from 71 mm at L1 to 92 mm at L5 (p. 302, PDF 4), but with a
  local peak at L3 (85.7) above L4 (79.4) (Table 5, p. 304, PDF 6; Figure 6). L3 differs from L1
  only; L3 against L4 is not significant; L5 differs from L1, L2 and L4, so TPW separates L4 from
  L5.
- Error: SEM 1.48-3.78 mm, largest at L4 and L5 (SD about 13 mm, computed).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- Three regions (p. 304, PDF 6): a thoracolumbar transition, a middle region and a lumbosacral
  transition; the abstract assigns L1-L2 to the first and L4-L5 to the last (p. 299, PDF 1), and
  the discussion describes the first by changes from L1 to L3, the middle by L3, and the last by
  changes from L3 to L5 (p. 304, PDF 6). Lumbar coupling patterns of motion also differ among the
  three (ref 11, p. 304, PDF 6).
- T12/L1 and the upper zone: canal depth narrows from L1 to L3 while the width stays nearly
  constant, in contrast to its increase from T10 to T12, so the width to depth ratio rises from L1
  to L3; pedicle height falls slightly from L1 to L3, continuing a fall from T11 to T12; pedicle
  widths are very small, 8-9 mm (p. 304, PDF 6).
- L1: narrowest end plates (EPWu 41.2, EPWl 43.3), smallest TPW (71.2), deepest lumbar canal but
  one (SCD 19.0), tallest-narrowest pedicles (PDH/PDW 2.00 right, 1.72 left), smallest pars area
  (61.0, 63.2) (Tables 2-5).
- L2: the smallest pedicle width (8.2 mm by the text, p. 301, PDF 3), area (83 mm2) and
  convergence (PDIs 14.1, Table 6) and, with L3, the smallest canal area (281) (Tables 3, 4).
- L3: the middle region; the narrowest canal depth (17.5 mm) and area (280); the greatest lower
  end-plate area (1290); the longest spinous reach with L2 (71.7); the lowest pedicle height (14.4);
  a local peak of TPW (85.7) and of PDIs (Tables 2-5; p. 304, PDF 6).
- L4: the widest lower end plate (49.5), deepest upper end plate (35.5) and largest upper
  end-plate area (1239); pedicle width jumps to 14.1 (Tables 2, 4; Table 6).
- L5 and the L5/S1 junction: the canal area enlarges from L3 to L5; the pedicles grow sharply from
  L3 to L5, which the authors attribute to the larger loads of the lower levels and support by the
  rise of their moments of inertia; PDIs at L5 is 8 deg above L4, the fanning toward the sacrum
  (p. 304, PDF 6). Also at L5: the lowest posterior body height (22.9), the shallowest lower end
  plate (33.2), the widest canal (27.1), largest canal area (330) and widest TPW (92.5); pedicle
  height and width both near 19 mm, near round (Tables 2-5). The sacrum itself was not studied.
- Adjacent-level separation (Scheffe, P < .05, Tables 2-5): only L3/L4 (PDWl) and L4/L5 (PDW and
  PDH both sides, PDAl, TPW). No measure separates L1/L2 or L2/L3. Inference: the test treated the
  levels as independent groups of 12; a paired analysis within spines might separate more
  adjacent pairs, but the paper gives no within-spine differences.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Accuracy: a Plexiglas block cut at 30, 45 and 60 deg, faced with millimetre graph paper on which
  triangles, rectangles, circles and other shapes were drawn, with three markers like a
  vertebra's, digitized with the same protocol and programs; linear, angular and area values were
  all within 5% of the known values (p. 300, PDF 2).
- Point error (ref 13, p. 889, not restated here): the instrument's largest error in locating a
  point on a vertebra was +/-0.5 mm.
- Not reported: intra- or inter-observer repeatability, repeated digitizing, the number of
  observers, the per-level n or any missing data, and the error of locating anatomical landmarks
  (rims with lipping, the tips of processes) as against drawn marks.
- Dispersion: every table prints mean +/- SEM of 12 specimens (Tables 2-5); the population SD is
  SEM x sqrt(12), about 3.46 x SEM (computed).
- Odd dispersions (Inference): PDWr at L4, SEM 0.18 mm, is a quarter or less of its neighbours'
  (0.67 at L3, 1.03 at L5, 0.46 on the left at L4); PDIsr at L1, SEM 5.02 deg, is nearly three
  times the left side's 1.87 (Table 4); TPW at L4 and L5, SEM 3.78 and 3.68 mm; PIA at L1, SEM
  1.17-2.19 against 5.90-9.76 elsewhere (Table 5). Each could be a misprint or one deviant
  specimen; the paper does not comment.
- Geometric approximations: end-plate ellipse within 10% over; canal ellipse 10-30% over and
  triangle 20-30% under; pedicle ellipse best at L1-L2, triangle best at L4-L5 (p. 305, PDF 7).
- No radiographic measurement, so no magnification; dry bone measured directly in millimetres.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Canal size relates to the incidence of low-back pain (ref 14) and is affected by physiologic
  stresses in childhood (ref 2, on infant malnutrition and adult stenosis) (p. 299, PDF 1).
- The concavity of the body's posterior wall in the horizontal plane has been postulated to affect
  the frequency of disc herniation (p. 299, PDF 1; no reference given).
- Pedicle size and orientation govern the design of transpedicular fixation and its surgery
  (p. 299, PDF 1); instability, trauma and pedicle-screw design are named as the areas served,
  through more precise canal dimensions and better models (pp. 302-304, PDF 4-6); the data are
  meant to allow "more precise clinical diagnosis and surgical management" (abstract, p. 299,
  PDF 1) and to serve instability determination and the design of internal devices, functional
  and fixation, and models that predict the effects of surgery and injury mechanisms (p. 306,
  PDF 8).
- The upper lumbar pedicles are very narrow, 8-9 mm (p. 304, PDF 6), and taller than wide (ratio
  1.84 at L1-L2, p. 302, PDF 4); the lower ones are nearly round, larger and more convergent (L5
  about 8 deg more than L4, p. 304, PDF 6). Inference: screw diameter is limited by width at
  L1-L2 and a more medial trajectory is needed at L5.
- Pedicle bending stiffness is greater in the sagittal than the transverse plane at every level
  (p. 302, PDF 4).
- The pedicle section is near-elliptical at L1-L2 and near-triangular at L4-L5; the canal is
  better an ellipse above and a triangle below (p. 305, PDF 7).
- The method is cheap compared with CT and accurate (p. 304, PDF 6).
- Pitfall, definitions across studies: at L4-L5 other series disagree on the pedicle (Berry and
  Zindrick find heights falling; Berry, Scoles and Krag find much smaller L5 widths, Table 6), on
  posterior body height (3-5 mm above this study in Scoles and Gilad) and on the canal depth
  (Cotterill 12.1 at L3); a value from another method will not match Panjabi's without its
  definition (p. 305, PDF 7).
- Pitfall, wording: "transitional" in this paper means a morphological zone, not an LSTV (p. 299,
  PDF 1).
- Pitfall, frame (ref 13, p. 889): the posterior-wall corners were chosen as the reference because
  they vary least with osteophytes; a frame built on the end plates or the anterior rim inherits
  lipping and wedging.
- Not in the paper: stenosis thresholds, syndromes (Marfan, achondroplasia), sex differences,
  safe zones or screw sizes.

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Before any measure, build each vertebra's own frame from its posterior body wall: fit a plane
   to the four posterior-wall corners (left and right, upper and lower), take Z as its normal
   toward the body, X along the line through the two upper corners toward the left, Y = Z x X
   (cranial), origin at the upper end-plate centre; measure widths along X, depths along Z,
   heights along Y, never along the scanner's axes (p. 299, PDF 1; Fig 1, p. 300, PDF 2; the side
   and the corners from ref 13, p. 889).
2. Measure SPL from the centre of the superior end plate to the most inferoposterior point of the
   spinous process, as a 3D distance; do not start at the canal wall or the body centroid (p. 302,
   PDF 4).
3. Measure SCD in the midline from the body's posterior wall to the inner surface of the arch, and
   SCW across the bony foramen of the vertebra at the level of the pedicles (Inference from
   Fig 1, which is all the paper gives), not the dural sac or a disc level (p. 301, PDF 3; Fig 1,
   p. 300, PDF 2).
4. Expect SCD to fall from L1 to a minimum at L3 and rise to L5, and to rise from T10 to T12 above;
   use the turning points over several consecutive bodies, not one body, since no level
   difference in Table 3 is significant (p. 301, PDF 3; p. 304, PDF 6).
5. Use size-free ratios when the patient's size is unknown: lower end-plate width/depth rises
   1.23 to 1.49 from L1 to L5, pedicle height/width falls from about 1.84 at L1-L2 to about 1.0 at
   L5, canal width/depth rises from 1.25 at L1 to 1.39 at L3 and then holds (Tables 2-4,
   pp. 301-302, PDF 3-4). These are ratios of group means; compute the same ratio from the
   measured means of a level, not per vertebra averaged.
6. To tell L4 from L5, look first at the pedicles (L5 width and height near 19 mm, near-round) and
   at TPW (L5 widest, about 92 mm); these are the measures with a significant L4/L5 difference
   here (Tables 4-5, pp. 302-304, PDF 4-6). Use the extra pedicle convergence at L5 (about 8 deg
   more than L4, p. 304, PDF 6) only as support: Table 4 does not mark it significant.
7. Do not expect any measure of this paper to separate L1 from L2 or L2 from L3 on one body; carry
   those decisions on the sequence (Tables 2-5).
8. Treat a longer transverse span at L3 than at L4 (TPW 85.7 against 79.4, Table 5, p. 304,
   PDF 6) as a weak hint only: the difference is not significant and the L4 SEM is 3.78 mm.
9. Treat end-plate inclination (2-5 deg, no trend) and posterior body height (flat L1-L4) as
   carrying almost no level information in the lumbar spine; VBHp only drops at L5 (Table 2,
   p. 301, PDF 3).
10. Compute end-plate area as the area enclosed by the rim, not the curved surface; if only an AP
    width and a lateral depth exist, pi x W x D / 4 is within 10% (p. 300, PDF 2; p. 305, PDF 7).
11. Do not estimate canal area from an ellipse at L4-L5 (about 27-28% too large, computed); the
    triangle W x D / 2 is closer there, the ellipse at L1-L2 (p. 305, PDF 7).
12. Find the pedicle as the narrowest section between body and arch (SPINEPS has no pedicle label);
    record its width, height and the normal of that section as the pedicle axis; this paper does
    not itself say the narrowest section was used, so record which section you took (p. 299,
    PDF 1; p. 302, PDF 4).
13. Never read this paper's "transitional" as an anomaly prevalence; it gives none (p. 299,
    PDF 1). Inference: its L5 means may include an undetected partially sacralised L5, since no
    sacrum was kept.
14. When comparing a measured value with Panjabi's, convert SEM to SD (x sqrt(12)) and allow at
    least 5% for the method's own error (p. 300, PDF 2).
15. On an AP radiograph, expect the interpedicular distance (SCW) to widen caudally, slowly L1-L3
    and faster L3-L5, and the pedicle ovals to change from tall-narrow at L1-L2 to round at L4-L5
    (Tables 3-4, p. 302, PDF 4; Inference that the AP projection preserves both trends).
16. Compare a CT mask of a living patient with these values knowing they come from boiled,
    bleached dry bone with discs and ligaments removed (ref 13, p. 888); an offset of a millimetre
    or so at the rims is expected and is not by itself a level error (Inference).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper reports no LSTV, L6, T13, rib count or
block vertebra; all 12 spines are counted as five lumbar levels (60 vertebrae), with no sacrum and
no reported findings from the screening radiographs (p. 299, PDF 1; p. 300, PDF 2). Priors must come
from other papers. Inference: even read as 0 anomalies in 12 spines, the one-sided 95% upper bound
is about 22% (computed), so the paper constrains no prior.

(b) Measures and definitions:
- The generator already holds every value of Tables 2-5 (provenance). Lumbar-only facts worth
  adding: the size-free ratios (EPW/EPD, PDH/PDW, SCW/SCD) as derived checks, built as ratios of
  means like the paper's, and PDM, which is a function of PDH and PDW and adds nothing.
- Facets: this paper digitized them but reports none; lumbar facet values come from the 1993
  facets paper of the same series (Panjabi_1993_Facets_Spine_18-10_1298-1310.pdf in bordercall's
  papers folder; Inference from the set, not from this paper).
- SPL: this paper measures from the centre of the superior end plate (p. 302, PDF 4).
  schwingcheck/mnemonics.py defines SPL "from the posterior wall of the canal to the tip", and
  measures.py says Panjabi's starts at the canal's posterior wall while the archive's measurer
  (SPINEPS_MAPPING.md: "body centroid to farthest spinous voxel") starts at the body centroid. For
  the lumbar table neither is right: start at the upper end-plate centroid. The table's 68-72 mm
  only fits the whole-vertebra reach (Inference: from the canal wall it would be roughly half
  that). A body-centroid start changes it by a few mm (Inference). The cervical and thoracic
  papers' own definitions should be checked separately.
- PDW, PDH, PDA: mnemonics.py calls them the smallest diameters and the area "at its narrowest";
  this paper says only "cross-sectional" width, height and area of a circumferential loop (p. 299,
  PDF 1; p. 301, PDF 3). Inference: the L5 areas (143.9 right, 158.7 left) are smaller even than
  the triangle PDW x PDH / 2 (176 and 187, computed), which the paper calls the best fit there.
  If PDW and PDH were perpendicular chords crossing inside one convex section, that section could
  not have less area than the rhombus on them, PDW x PDH / 2; so at L5 the width, height and area
  do not describe one convex section cut square to the pedicle (a tilted or non-planar loop, whose
  vector area is smaller than its true area, or dimensions taken from different places, would
  explain it). A mask-derived L5 pedicle will not match all three at once.
- SCW: mnemonics.py says "at the level of the pedicles"; this paper gives no level (Fig 1 only
  suggests it). SPINEPS's canal label (61), which SPINEPS_MAPPING.md measures for SCW and SCD, is
  not the bony foramen Panjabi digitized.
- EPA: the area enclosed by the rim (magnitude of the vector area), not the curved end-plate
  surface (Inference from p. 300, PDF 2); a mesh surface area from a mask would read high.
- EPIt, PDIs, PDIt: angles to the vertebra's own planes, set by the posterior wall; Inference:
  unsigned magnitudes. A mask-derived signed angle, or one taken against the scanner's planes,
  will be biased; the mean of magnitudes of a near-zero angle is biased upward.
- Extents: SPINEPS_MAPPING.md takes "lateral / AP extent"; unless the mask is first rotated into
  the posterior-wall frame (X toward the left along the upper posterior corners, ref 13, p. 889),
  lordotic tilt inflates EPD and VBHp at L4-L5.
- PIA: no definition here at all; any mask recipe is an assumption. PIAr at L3 may be 80.0 rather
  than the printed 70.0 (Figure 5 against Table 5; see Gaps); the generator should not lean on it.
- Specimens: SPINEPS_MAPPING.md calls them "Twelve embalmed cadaveric spines"; this paper says
  fresh autopsy specimens (p. 299, PDF 1), and ref 13 says they were cleaned by boiling, bleach and
  ethanol (p. 888). The word embalmed should be corrected.
- Possible major/minor constraint (Inference): the paper computed "major and minor linear
  dimensions" of each part (p. 300, PDF 2); if PDH and PDW were the major and minor axes, PDH could
  not fall below PDW, which fits Table 4 (PDH/PDW >= 1.02 everywhere) and differs from Zindrick's
  L5 (PDH 14.0 below PDW 18.0, Table 6).

(c) Measurement error: the method's accuracy is under 5% for lengths, angles and areas (p. 300,
PDF 2), and the point-location error +/-0.5 mm (ref 13, p. 889); no observer error. bordercall uses
1 mm for lengths, 3 deg for angles and sqrt(pi A / 2) x 1 mm for areas (measures.py); Inference: 5%
of SPL or TPW is 3.4-4.6 mm and 5% of an end-plate area 53-65 mm2, so a flat 1 mm understates the
method's stated bound for the long measures, while two points each within 0.5 mm give a length
within 1 mm, which matches bordercall's figure for the short ones. Population SD = SEM x sqrt(12).

(d) Covariates: 8 men and 4 women, 19-59 years (mean 46.3), 54-85 kg (mean 67.8), 157-178 cm (mean
167.8) (p. 299, PDF 1); nothing by sex, age or size, no normalisation, no population named. The
size factor must come from elsewhere. Ref 13's Table 1 (p. 889) gives each specimen's age, sex,
weight and height, but no measure is reported per specimen, so no size regression can be fitted.
