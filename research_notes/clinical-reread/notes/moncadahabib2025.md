## What the paper is

- Design: retrospective, observational, single-centre CT morphometry of the lumbar pedicles and vertebral bodies, p. 2 (PDF 2). The centre is a tertiary neurosurgical centre, named in the Discussion as Mexico's National Institute of Neurology and Neurosurgery (Mexico City by the affiliations), p. 7 (PDF 7) and p. 1 (PDF 1). Ethics approval was waived because the design was retrospective and the data anonymised, p. 3 (PDF 3); the disclosures say consent was obtained or waived, p. 8 (PDF 8).
- Aim: population-specific reference values for the lumbar pedicle in adult Mexicans without structural spinal pathology; secondarily the body dimensions by level, sex and age effects, and implications for screw sizing and trajectory, p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Population: Mexican adults, living patients, whose lumbar CT was done "for non-pathological indications", p. 2 (PDF 2). The indications themselves are not named, and "Mexican" is not defined by ethnicity (mestizo, Indigenous) or birthplace. 37 patients, 54.1% men and 45.9% women, mean age 60.4 years (SD 15.8, range 26 to 88), p. 3 (PDF 3). Inference: 54.1% and 45.9% of 37 are 20 men and 17 women. How many CTs were screened and how many excluded is not reported.
- Denominators: each patient gave all five lumbar vertebrae, 37 x 5 = 185 vertebrae, p. 2 (PDF 2) and p. 3 (PDF 3); Tables 1 and 2 are headed "(n = 37)" per level, pp. 4 to 5 (PDF 4 to 5). Pedicles were measured on both sides. Inference: 370 pedicles.
- Inclusion: adults over 18 with a complete high-resolution lumbar CT done for a non-pathological indication, p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Exclusion: a history or radiological sign of spinal fracture, deformity or congenital anomaly; spinal tumour, infection or inflammatory disease; previous lumbar instrumentation or surgery; incomplete or poor-quality imaging, p. 2 (PDF 2). Degenerative change (spondylosis, osteophytes, disc collapse) is not named as an exclusion, although the mean age is 60.
- Modality and instrument: CT with 1.0 mm slices, 250 mm field of view, 120 kVp and 200 to 250 mA. Multiplanar reconstructions used a bone algorithm "to optimize cortical definition". Axial, coronal and sagittal images were read with digital calipers on PACS workstations, p. 2 (PDF 2). Scanner make and model, matrix, pixel size and patient position are not given. Every measure is a 2D caliper reading on one reformatted slice, although the Discussion calls the work "CT-based three-dimensional morphometry", p. 7 (PDF 7).
- Readers: two observers trained in spinal anatomy measured independently, and a senior spine surgeon verified the results. Differences over 2 mm or 2 deg were settled by consensus with a third reviewer, p. 3 (PDF 3). Whether the recorded value is one reader's, the mean of both, or the consensus is not stated.
- Statistics: data kept in Excel and analysed in SPSS 25: mean, SD, minimum, maximum and n per parameter and level. Sex was compared with independent-sample t-tests for each variable and age with Spearman correlations, p. 3 (PDF 3). Neither test is done per level; Tables 3 and 4 pool L1 to L5 (Inference from the t and p values; see Gaps).
- Levels covered: L1 to L5 only. Nothing is measured at the thoracic levels, the sacrum or any junction.
- How levels were identified and counted: not stated. The paper does not say how L1 or L5 was found (rib count, sacrum, counting from C2) or whether the field included the thoracolumbar junction or the whole spine. Transitional segments are not mentioned. They may fall under the "congenital anomalies" exclusion, but the paper does not say so.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts or block vertebrae, and gives no prevalence of anything. Its only related statement is that patients with "congenital anomalies" were excluded, without a count, p. 2 (PDF 2). Every patient contributes exactly five lumbar vertebrae, p. 3 (PDF 3). Inference: any six-lumbar or four-lumbar spine was either excluded under that criterion or numbered silently as L1 to L5, so a transitional L5 or a lumbarised S1 could sit unrecognised in the L5 row (L5 PW up to 18.8 mm, CW up to 63.6 mm, PH body down to 16.3 mm, Tables 1 and 2, pp. 4 to 5 (PDF 4 to 5)).

## Measures, each as a recipe

General note. The Methods name the measures but do not define them in words, p. 2 (PDF 2). The only guides are Figure 1 (CT of an L3 with numbered lines, p. 2 (PDF 2)) and Figure 2 (drawings of a lumbar vertebra with dotted lines and arrows, p. 3 (PDF 3)). Figure 2's caption does not name the measures: panel A is a sagittal drawing of the pedicle axis against the body, B an axial view of "the orientation of the pedicle and entry point", C an axial view of the pedicle axis "along its longitudinal trajectory", and D the transverse angle between the pedicle axis and the vertebral midline, p. 3 (PDF 3). Inference: the drawings fit A = pedicle height, B = pedicle width, C = pedicle length, D = transverse angle; only D is stated. The abbreviation PH stands for both pedicle height and posterior vertebral body height, p. 2 (PDF 2). Tables 1 and 3 give body dimensions in tenths of a millimetre with no unit printed: AH at L1 is printed "243.1 +/- 19.1" and the text gives 24.3 mm, p. 3 to 4 (PDF 3 to 4). Every body value below has been converted to mm. Wherever this note says "From a 3D CT instance mask" or "From a radiograph alone", the method is Inference: the paper measured only on CT slices and reformats.

Inference, common to every mask recipe. Build a local frame for each instance:
- S-I axis: the normal of planes fitted to the superior and inferior end-plate surfaces of the body subregion.
- A-P axis: from the body centroid towards the tip of the spinous process, made orthogonal to S-I.
- L-R axis: their cross product.
- Midsagittal plane: the plane through the body centroid and the spinous tip that contains S-I.
- Pedicle axial plane: the plane perpendicular to S-I that passes through the pedicles at their mid-height. Figure 1A is such a slice.

The mask includes cortex, so mask measures are outer-cortex to outer-cortex. SPINEPS labels no pedicle as far as this reader knows (Inference): the pedicle lies inside the arcus (posterior element) label, next to the body label.

### Pedicle width (PW; LPW, RPW)

- Landmarks: Inference from Figure 2B and Figure 1A: the mediolateral width of the pedicle at its narrowest point (the isthmus), between the medial and lateral cortices, on an axial slice, across the pedicle. Figure 2B draws a dotted line with arrows on the medial (canal) and lateral walls; Figure 1A draws a short line across the image-right pedicle, 9.22 mm, roughly perpendicular to the pedicle's course, p. 2 to 3 (PDF 2 to 3). The paper does not say outer or inner cortex. Inference: outer, since the line in Figure 1A runs from cortex edge to cortex edge. Which side of the image is the patient's left is not stated.
- Plane: axial CT. Instrument: PACS digital calipers. Levels: L1 to L5, both sides.
- Values (mean +/- SD, min to max, mm), Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5):
  - L1: left 7.0 +/- 1.7 (4.0 to 10.7); right 6.7 +/- 1.6 (4.0 to 9.2)
  - L2: left 7.0 +/- 1.5 (4.0 to 9.7); right 7.0 +/- 1.6 (4.0 to 9.8)
  - L3: left 8.1 +/- 1.8 (3.8 to 11.0); right 7.6 +/- 2.0 (3.9 to 12.0)
  - L4: left 9.3 +/- 1.8 (5.5 to 13.0); right 8.9 +/- 1.9 (5.3 to 12.0)
  - L5: left 11.8 +/- 3.0 (5.2 to 18.0); right 12.4 +/- 2.5 (6.0 to 18.8)
- From a 3D CT instance mask: on each side, isolate the pedicle as the bony bridge between the posterior wall of the body and the base of the superior articular and transverse processes. Fit its axis. On cross-sections perpendicular to that axis, find the narrowest section (the isthmus). PW is the extent of that section in the pedicle axial plane, perpendicular to the pedicle axis. Do not measure along the vertebra's L-R axis: at L5, where the axis runs about 22 deg from the midline here, an L-R chord reads about 1/cos(angle), some 8%, too wide (Inference). A narrowest-section rule will give values at or below a reader's single axial caliper line, because the reader works on one axial slice at one height.
- From a radiograph alone: on an AP radiograph, the mediolateral width of the pedicle's oval ring, which is the outer cortex seen nearly end-on; divide by the magnification (Inference: often 1.1 to 1.3 on a plain film; known on a calibrated C-arm). At L4 and L5 the beam crosses an obliquely running pedicle, so the oval is the projection of a tube and may overstate the isthmus (Inference). A lateral radiograph cannot give PW.
- Across levels: about the same at L1 and L2 (about 7 mm); it rises at L3 (7.6 to 8.1), at L4 (8.9 to 9.3) and steeply at L5 (11.8 to 12.4), Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5) and text p. 4 (PDF 4). The Discussion says width "nearly doubled" from cranial to caudal, p. 7 (PDF 7). Inference (difference of means over the mean SD): L1 against L2 about 0 to 0.2, L2 against L3 about 0.4 to 0.6, L3 against L4 about 0.7, L4 against L5 about 1.0 (left) to 1.6 (right). PW cannot tell L1 from L2; it separates L4 from L5 best.
- Sex, age, size, population: men have wider pedicles (Table 3, p. 6 (PDF 6), all levels pooled). Right: 8.74 +/- 2.66 in men against 7.65 +/- 2.86 in women, t -2.83, p = 0.005. Left: 8.82 +/- 2.56 against 7.76 +/- 2.65, t -2.95, p = 0.004. Age does not correlate with PW: right rho -0.10, p = 0.166; left -0.12, p = 0.113 (Table 4, p. 6 (PDF 6)). Body size was not recorded; the Introduction says sex, height and body weight influence pedicle dimensions, citing Yu 2015, p. 1 (PDF 1). Population: Mexican adults.
- Error: no ICC or repeatability is reported. Readers had to agree within 2 mm, p. 3 (PDF 3). Inference: 2 mm is 20 to 30% of an upper-lumbar PW, so the tolerance is coarse for this measure.

### Pedicle height (PH; LPH, RPH)

- Landmarks: Inference from Figure 2A, p. 3 (PDF 3): the craniocaudal height of the pedicle at its narrowest point, between the superior and inferior vertebral notches of the same vertebra (the upper and lower borders of the pedicle). Figure 2A, a lateral (sagittal) drawing, shows a vertical dotted line across the pedicle with arrows in the two notches; its caption speaks only of "the pedicle axis relative to the vertebral body". Figure 1A's caption gives "pedicle height (18.63 mm)" on an axial image, but that line runs along the pedicle and cannot be a height (see Gaps).
- Plane: sagittal (parasagittal through the pedicle), Inference from Figure 2A. Levels: L1 to L5, both sides.
- Values (mm), Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5):
  - L1: left 12.8 +/- 1.4 (11.0 to 17.0); right 13.0 +/- 1.5 (9.0 to 16.0)
  - L2: left 12.3 +/- 1.8 (8.0 to 17.0); right 12.5 +/- 1.6 (8.0 to 15.0)
  - L3: left 12.1 +/- 1.8 (8.0 to 16.0); right 12.0 +/- 1.9 (8.0 to 16.5)
  - L4: left 11.3 +/- 1.6 (9.0 to 15.0); right 10.9 +/- 1.5 (8.14 to 14.0)
  - L5: left 10.2 +/- 1.6 (7.0 to 13.0); right 10.2 +/- 1.4 (7.9 to 14.0)
- From a 3D CT instance mask: take the plane that contains the pedicle axis and the S-I axis. In it, find the minimum S-I extent of the pedicle between its superior and inferior notches; equivalently, the S-I extent of the narrowest cross-section perpendicular to the axis.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph. Measure the pedicle as the bony bar between the foramen above and the foramen below: from the floor of the superior vertebral notch to the roof of the inferior vertebral notch of the same vertebra, at its narrowest. (The inferior notch of the vertebra above and the superior notch of this one bound the foramen, not the pedicle.) The two pedicles superimpose on a true lateral and separate with rotation. The AP radiograph also shows the height of the pedicle oval. Correct for magnification.
- Across levels: falls steadily from L1 (12.8 to 13.0) to L5 (10.2), text p. 4 (PDF 4) and Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5). Steps: left 0.5, 0.2, 0.8, 1.1 mm; right 0.5, 0.5, 1.1, 0.7 mm. Inference, effect sizes: about 0.3 for L1/L2 and L2/L3, about 0.5 to 0.65 for L3/L4 and L4/L5; PH separates no adjacent pair well, only the ends (L1 against L5 about 1.8 SD). Inference, from the means: the ratio PW/PH is, right side, L1 0.52, L2 0.56, L3 0.63, L4 0.82, L5 1.22; left side 0.55, 0.57, 0.67, 0.82, 1.16. Of these five levels only the L5 pedicle is, on average, wider than it is tall. These are ratios of means; the per-vertebra spread of the ratio is not reported.
- Sex, age, size, population: no significant sex difference. Right: 11.83 +/- 1.88 in men against 11.39 +/- 1.83 in women, p = 0.097. Left: 11.9 +/- 1.97 against 11.43 +/- 1.75, p = 0.088 (Table 3, p. 6 (PDF 6)). Height falls with age: right rho -0.27, p "0.000" in the table and 0.0003 in the text; left rho -0.30, p "0.000" in the table and < 0.0001 in the text (Table 4 and text, p. 6 (PDF 6)). The authors read this as remodelling and reduced bone quality with age, consistent with degenerative change, p. 7 (PDF 7).
- Error: as for PW. No reliability statistic; agreement within 2 mm, p. 3 (PDF 3). Inference: sagittal reformats of 1.0 mm slices limit precision in the S-I direction to about 1 mm.

### Pedicle length (PL; LPL, RPL)

- Landmarks: Inference from Figure 2C, p. 3 (PDF 3): the length along the pedicle axis. Figure 2C draws a dotted line labelled "Pedicle Axis" from the anterior cortex of the body, just off the midline on the same side, to the dorsal point at the junction of the facet and the transverse process, with an arrow at each end. Inference: PL is the whole screw path from the posterior entry to the anterior cortex (a chord or axis length), not the short isthmus segment; its magnitude of about 40 mm fits only that reading. Figure 1A's caption calls a 27.97 mm line "pedicle length", but the number sits under the vertical lines drawn in the midline of the body from the anterior cortex to the posterior wall, so it looks like corpus length (see Gaps).
- Plane: axial. Levels: L1 to L5, both sides.
- Values (mm), Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5):
  - L1: left 40.4 +/- 6.2 (20.0 to 49.0); right 40.3 +/- 6.2 (20.0 to 48.0)
  - L2: left 40.6 +/- 6.5 (20.0 to 49.0); right 41.1 +/- 6.5 (21.0 to 49.0)
  - L3: left 41.3 +/- 6.8 (21.0 to 51.0); right 41.3 +/- 6.6 (21.0 to 50.0)
  - L4: left 40.5 +/- 6.0 (20.0 to 48.0); right 40.5 +/- 6.3 (20.0 to 48.0)
  - L5: left 37.8 +/- 5.5 (23.0 to 47.0); right 38.6 +/- 6.0 (22.0 to 50.0)
- From a 3D CT instance mask: fit the pedicle axis in the pedicle axial plane through the centroid of the isthmus. Two directions are possible: the pedicle's own long axis (Figure 2C), or the line from the isthmus centroid to the anterior-midline point used for the angle (Figure 2D). Cast the line both ways and measure from its dorsal exit from the posterior-element mask to its ventral exit from the body mask. Record which axis was used, because the length depends on it.
- From a radiograph alone: not directly. A lateral radiograph shows the axis foreshortened by cos(PA) and by any sagittal tilt; an AP radiograph shows it end-on. Inference: PL x cos(PA) is roughly the A-P distance on a lateral film from the dorsal entry point to the anterior body wall.
- Across levels: flat from L1 to L4 (40.3 to 41.3) and slightly shorter at L5 (37.8 left, 38.6 right), text p. 4 (PDF 4). Inference: PL separates no adjacent pair; its SD of about 6 mm swamps any difference (L4 against L5 about 0.4 SD).
- Sex, age, size, population: the largest sex difference among the pedicle measures. Right: 42.46 +/- 5.25 in men against 36.87 +/- 6.62 in women, t -6.26. Left: 42.10 +/- 5.21 against 36.87 +/- 6.65, t -5.80. Both p "0.000" (Table 3, p. 6 (PDF 6)). Age: left rho -0.17, p = 0.020 (significant); right -0.12, p = 0.092 (Table 4, p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- Error: no reliability statistic. Inference: the SD of about 6 mm and several minima of exactly 20.0 mm (L1 both sides, L2 left, L4 both sides) suggest a coarse or inconsistent endpoint.

### Pedicle transverse angle (PA; LPA, RPA)

- Landmarks: the angle in the axial plane between the pedicle axis and the vertebral midline, stated in Figure 2D's caption, p. 3 (PDF 3); Figure 2D labels the lines "Pedicle Axis" and "Median Line". In Figure 2D the vertex sits on the median line at, or just in front of, the anterior cortex of the body; the median line runs back through the spinous process and the pedicle axis runs back through the pedicle region past the transverse process. Figure 1A (23.45 deg at L3) draws the same construction: a line from the midline point of the anterior cortex back through the image-left pedicle to the junction of lamina and transverse process, against a vertical midline, p. 2 (PDF 2). This is the medial convergence a screw needs.
- Plane: axial. Levels: L1 to L5, both sides.
- Values (deg), Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5):
  - L1: left 16.8 +/- 1.9 (11.7 to 21.2); right 16.7 +/- 2.0 (11.2 to 21.0)
  - L2: left 16.5 +/- 2.3 (10.1 to 20.7); right 16.4 +/- 2.0 (11.6 to 20.7)
  - L3: left 17.0 +/- 2.4 (10.8 to 21.6); right 17.0 +/- 2.6 (11.0 to 21.2)
  - L4: left 18.6 +/- 1.9 (15.5 to 23.0); right 18.6 +/- 1.9 (14.7 to 23.0)
  - L5: left 21.9 +/- 2.8 (16.8 to 29.0); right 21.9 +/- 2.5 (16.1 to 29.0)
- From a 3D CT instance mask: in the pedicle axial plane, take the angle between the midsagittal line and the pedicle axis. Use the axis convention of PL and state it. Inference: forcing the axis through the anterior-midline point, as Figure 2D does, makes the angle depend on how far lateral the pedicle sits relative to the body's depth, and gives a different value from an axis fitted to the pedicle's own cortices.
- From a radiograph alone: not from one standard radiograph. Inference: on fluoroscopy, the C-arm's axial rotation from true AP to the en-face ("owl's eye") view of the pedicle estimates the pedicle's own axis angle, which is not this paper's anterior-vertex angle.
- Across levels: about the same at L1 to L3 (16.4 to 17.0; L2 is slightly below L1, although the text says the angle rose "steadily"), then up at L4 (18.6) and sharply at L5 (21.9), text p. 4 (PDF 4). Inference, effect sizes: L3/L4 about 0.7, L4/L5 about 1.4; it cannot tell L1, L2 and L3 apart. Inference, against other notes in this set: Panjabi 1992's PDIs runs about 14.5 deg at L1 to 24.5 at L5 and Zindrick 1987's transverse angle 10.9 to 29.8 deg, so this paper's L1 angle is higher and its L5 angle lower than both: a flatter level trend (5 deg from L1 to L5 against 10 to 19 deg), most likely from the anterior-vertex convention.
- Sex, age, size, population: no sex difference. Right: 18.33 +/- 2.96 in men against 18.10 +/- 3.05 in women, p = 0.586. Left: 18.32 +/- 2.91 against 17.94 +/- 3.15, p = 0.383 (Table 3, p. 6 (PDF 6)). The angle falls with age: right rho -0.16, p = 0.034; left -0.19, p = 0.010 (Table 4, p. 6 (PDF 6)). The authors read the caudal rise, seen across ethnic groups, as a shared adaptation to the load on the lower spine, p. 7 (PDF 7).
- Error: readers had to agree within 2 deg, p. 3 (PDF 3); no reliability statistic.

### Pedicle sagittal angle (shown, never reported)

- Landmarks: Figure 1B's caption gives a "sagittal pedicle angle" of 13.83 deg, while the number printed in the image reads 13.33 deg, p. 2 (PDF 2). On the sagittal image the vertex is at the anterior cortex of the body; one limb is horizontal in the image and the other runs up and back to the top of the pedicle and the superior articular process. The reference line (end-plate, image horizontal, posterior wall perpendicular) is not stated.
- Values: none. The Methods' list of measured parameters omits it, p. 2 (PDF 2), and no table, text or statistic gives it. Inference: measured on at least one image and then dropped.
- From a 3D CT instance mask, from a radiograph, across levels, covariates, error: nothing to take from this paper.

### Corpus length (CL): A-P depth of the vertebral body

- Landmarks: not defined in words; Table 1 calls it "vertebral body length", p. 4 (PDF 4). Inference from Figure 1A, p. 2 (PDF 2): the vertical midline line from the anterior cortex to the posterior wall of the body, on the axial slice through the pedicles (number 27.97 mm, labelled "pedicle length" in the caption), fits Table 1's CL at L3 (mean 31.7 mm, range 26.2 to 41.4 mm). CL is therefore taken to be the A-P depth of the body in the midline at the pedicle level, not the depth of an end-plate.
- Plane and instrument: axial CT (Inference from Figure 1A), PACS calipers. Levels: L1 to L5.
- Values (mm, converted from Table 1, p. 4 (PDF 4)): L1 29.4 +/- 3.2 (22.8 to 36.0); L2 30.8 +/- 3.4 (24.9 to 40.8); L3 31.7 +/- 3.1 (26.2 to 41.4); L4 31.8 +/- 2.7 (26.1 to 36.8); L5 32.4 +/- 3.6 (26.0 to 43.9).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: on the body subregion, take the A-P extent along the midsagittal line in the axial plane at the pedicle level (about mid-height). Inference: a depth taken at the end-plates, as Panjabi's EPD is, will differ, because of the concave waist and the end-plate rims and osteophytes.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, the A-P depth of the body at mid-height, corrected for magnification.
- Across levels: rises slowly from L1 to L5 (29.4 to 32.4 mm), text p. 3 (PDF 3). Steps: L1 to L2 1.4 mm, L2 to L3 0.9, L3 to L4 0.1, L4 to L5 0.6; only L1 to L2 exceeds 1 mm. Inference: CL alone separates no adjacent pair (largest step about 0.4 SD).
- Sex, age, size, population: men 32.35 +/- 2.98 mm against women 29.26 +/- 2.95 mm, t -7.63, p "0.000" (Table 3, p. 6 (PDF 6), converted). The text says there is no sex difference in body dimensions (see Gaps). Age: the text says there is no correlation, p. 6 (PDF 6); Table 4 has no row for body measures.
- Error: as for the pedicle measures (2 mm consensus tolerance, p. 3 (PDF 3)); nothing body-specific.

### Corpus width (CW): transverse width of the vertebral body

- Landmarks: Table 1 calls it "vertebral body width", p. 4 (PDF 4). Figure 1A draws a transverse line across the body on the axial slice, 39.12 mm, and the coronal Figure 1C draws a line at the body's mid-height (waist) with the same number, 39.12 mm, p. 2 (PDF 2). Inference: a mid-height width, not an end-plate width.
- Plane and instrument: axial or coronal CT, PACS calipers. Levels: L1 to L5.
- Values (mm, Table 1, p. 4 (PDF 4), converted): L1 40.3 +/- 4.5 (31.5 to 49.6); L2 42.2 +/- 4.3 (28.6 to 48.5); L3 44.0 +/- 4.5 (33.5 to 52.5); L4 45.5 +/- 4.7 (35.0 to 53.7); L5 49.5 +/- 5.7 (35.2 to 63.6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the L-R extent of the body subregion at mid-height, perpendicular to the midsagittal plane.
- From a radiograph alone: AP radiograph, the body width at mid-height (the waist), corrected for magnification. A lateral film cannot give it.
- Across levels: rises from 40.3 at L1 to 49.5 at L5, text p. 3 (PDF 3); steps 1.9, 1.9, 1.5 and 4.0 mm. Inference: the width-to-depth ratio CW/CL is 1.37 at L1 and L2, 1.39 at L3, 1.43 at L4 and 1.53 at L5, so L5 is the widest body relative to its depth; L4 against L5 separates by about 0.8 SD, other adjacent pairs by about 0.3 to 0.4.
- Sex, age, size, population: men 45.95 +/- 4.77 mm against women 41.67 +/- 5.89 mm, t -5.73, p "0.000" (Table 3, p. 6 (PDF 6)), against the text. Age: the text says no correlation; no table row.
- Error: as for CL.

### Anterior vertebral body height (AH) and posterior vertebral body height (PH, body)

- Landmarks: not defined in words, and no sagittal panel shows them. Figure 1C shows a central coronal body height of 25.15 mm at L3, p. 2 (PDF 2), which the caption calls "vertebral body height" without saying anterior or posterior. Inference: AH and PH are the S-I heights of the anterior and posterior body walls on the midsagittal reformat.
- Plane and instrument: Inference, sagittal reformat; PACS calipers. Levels: L1 to L5.
- Values (mm, Table 1, p. 4 (PDF 4), converted):
  - AH: L1 24.3 +/- 1.9 (20.0 to 24.8, as printed; see Gaps); L2 25.2 +/- 2.3 (21.2 to 31.6); L3 25.6 +/- 1.7 (22.0 to 28.5); L4 25.4 +/- 2.1 (20.6 to 29.0); L5 26.2 +/- 2.8 (20.6 to 33.0).
  - PH (body): L1 26.9 +/- 1.8 (23.6 to 30.9); L2 26.8 +/- 2.0 (22.7 to 29.5); L3 26.1 +/- 2.4 (21.3 to 31.0); L4 25.0 +/- 2.5 (20.5 to 31.9); L5 22.5 +/- 2.7 (16.3 to 31.1).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: in the midsagittal plane of the body subregion, take the S-I extent at the anterior wall and at the posterior wall, for example the maximum S-I extent within the anterior and the posterior 10% of the body's depth. The authors' landmarks are not stated.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, the anterior and posterior wall heights, corrected for magnification. Their ratio is free of magnification but not of tilt or rotation of the beam.
- Across levels: AH barely changes (24.3 to 26.2; L4 slightly below L3). PH (body) falls from 26.9 at L1 to 22.5 at L5; the authors tie this to the lumbar curvature and load bearing, text p. 3 to 4 (PDF 3 to 4). Inference, from the means: AH minus PH is -2.6 mm at L1, -1.6 at L2, -0.5 at L3, +0.4 at L4 and +3.7 at L5; AH/PH is 0.90, 0.94, 0.98, 1.02 and 1.16. The body turns from posterior-taller to anterior-taller between L3 and L4; L5 is clearly anterior-taller and L1 posterior-taller. Inference: if AH and PH varied independently, the per-vertebra SD of the ratio would be about 0.09 at L1 rising to about 0.19 at L5 (less if they vary together, as body size makes likely), so the ratio separates L1 from L5 well (about 1.8 SD) and adjacent levels poorly (L4 against L5 about 0.9 SD). The posterior height alone drops 2.5 mm from L4 to L5 (about 1 SD), the largest adjacent step of any body measure.
- Sex, age, size, population: AH is larger in men, 25.80 +/- 2.47 mm against 24.82 +/- 1.74 mm, t -3.32, p = 0.001. PH (body) does not differ: 25.58 +/- 2.79 against 25.12 +/- 2.76, p = 0.245 (Table 3, p. 6 (PDF 6), converted). Age: the text says there is no correlation, p. 6 (PDF 6); no table row.
- Error: as for CL; nothing body-specific.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1, Tables 1 and 2, p. 4 to 5 (PDF 4 to 5): the narrowest pedicles on the right (6.7 mm; left 7.0, as at L2) and the tallest (12.8 to 13.0 mm); PA 16.7 to 16.8 deg, second lowest after L2. The body is the smallest (CW 40.3, CL 29.4) and posterior-taller (AH 24.3 against PH 26.9). Minimum PW 4.0 mm on both sides.
- L2: pedicles almost the same as L1 (PW 7.0, PH 12.3 to 12.5, PA 16.4 to 16.5, the lowest mean angle). Minimum PW 4.0 mm on both sides. Body CW 42.2, CL 30.8.
- L3: PW 7.6 to 8.1, with the smallest single pedicle of the series (3.8 mm, left; right minimum 3.9), PH 12.0 to 12.1, PA 17.0. Body CW 44.0; AH 25.6 against PH 26.1, nearly equal. L3 is the level shown in Figure 1, p. 2 (PDF 2).
- L4: PW 8.9 to 9.3, PH 10.9 to 11.3, PA 18.6, the first clear step in angle. Body CW 45.5; AH and PH about equal (25.4 against 25.0).
- L5: the widest pedicles (11.8 to 12.4; up to 18.8 mm), the lowest pedicle height (10.2 mm), the most convergent (21.9 deg; up to 29 deg), and a slightly shorter PL (37.8 to 38.6). The body is the widest (CW 49.5, up to 63.6 mm) and markedly anterior-taller (AH 26.2 against PH 22.5, PH down to 16.3 mm). PW has its largest SD here on both sides (3.0 left, 2.5 right) and so does the left PA (2.8); the right PA's SD is largest at L3 (2.6 against 2.5), and PH and PL are not most variable at L5 (PL SD is largest at L3, 6.6 to 6.8).
- Junctions: the paper says nothing about T12/L1 or L5/S1, and nothing about the sacrum or the thoracic levels. Inference: the L1 posterior-taller and L5 anterior-taller body, with the jump in PW and PA and the drop in posterior body height at L5, are the cues this paper offers for the two ends of the lumbar spine; none of them was tested against a transitional segment.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Two independent readers measured. Differences over 2 mm or 2 deg went to a third reviewer for consensus, and a senior spine surgeon verified the results, p. 3 (PDF 3). Which value was recorded is not stated.
- No ICC, kappa, technical error of measurement or repeat measurement is reported. The authors list this as a limitation: inter-observer reliability "was not formally quantified", p. 7 (PDF 7).
- Acquisition: 1.0 mm slices, 250 mm field of view, bone kernel, p. 2 (PDF 2). Inference: with a usual 512 matrix the in-plane pixel is about 0.49 mm, so a single PW of 7 mm carries about 7% quantisation error, and heights read from sagittal reformats carry about 1 mm.
- The SDs mix true biological spread with reader error: about 1.5 to 3.0 mm for PW, 1.4 to 1.9 mm for PH, 5.5 to 6.8 mm for PL, 1.9 to 2.8 deg for PA, and 1.7 to 5.7 mm for the body measures (AH 1.7 to 2.8, PH body 1.8 to 2.7, CL 2.7 to 3.6, CW 4.3 to 5.7) (Tables 1 and 2, p. 4 to 5 (PDF 4 to 5)).
- Other limitations the authors admit: small sample, single centre, so limited generalisability; no intraoperative validation; the clinical impact is hypothesis-generating and needs prospective multicentre study, p. 7 (PDF 7).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication: pedicle screws are used for degenerative disease, trauma, tumour and deformity; placing them accurately needs the pedicle's size and orientation, since a wrong size or orientation endangers neurovascular structures and implant stability, p. 1 (PDF 1). The authors also cite cortical bone trajectory screws as raising the demand for 3D pedicle data, p. 1 (PDF 1).
- Width and height guide the choice of screw diameter and the avoidance of neurovascular injury, p. 7 (PDF 7). Inference: the minimum widths of 3.8 to 4.0 mm at L1 to L3 (Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5)) mean that some upper-lumbar pedicles in this population are narrower than common 5 to 6.5 mm screws.
- The trajectory converges more caudally (16.8 deg at L1 to 21.9 deg at L5), so lower lumbar screws need angulation adjustments, as earlier studies stressed, p. 7 (PDF 7).
- Sex: men have wider and longer pedicles; ignoring sex risks a suboptimal screw fit, weaker fixation, or injury to adjacent neurovascular structures, p. 7 (PDF 7).
- Age: pedicle height and transverse angle fall with age, which the authors attribute to remodelling and reduced bone quality; plan screw trajectories in older patients with care to avoid loosening and cortical breach, p. 7 (PDF 7).
- Population: even within similar ethnic groups pedicle morphology varies with BMI, genetic background and environment, so international reference values may not suit Latin American patients, p. 7 (PDF 7).
- The authors read the bodies as varying less between the sexes than the pedicles, and so put the emphasis on pedicle-specific sizing, p. 7 (PDF 7); Table 3 contradicts the premise (see Gaps).
- The authors point to CT-based 3D segmentation and reconstruction (their refs 16 and 17: a 3D method to segment lumbar vertebral bodies, and sub-millimetre 3D lumbar reconstruction from biplanar X-ray images, p. 9 (PDF 9)) and to AI-assisted analysis for planning, p. 7 to 8 (PDF 7 to 8). They state no stenosis threshold, safe zone, syndrome or screw-size rule.
- Pitfalls of reading this paper:
  - Units: Table 1 and the body rows of Table 3 are in 0.1 mm, unlabelled.
  - PH is used for two different measures.
  - The Figure 2 captions do not name PW, PH or PL; the Figure 1 caption labels do not match the lines drawn.
  - The text's sex and age statements conflict with Tables 3 and 4.
  - "Three-dimensional morphometry" in the Discussion is 2D caliper readings on reformats, p. 2 and p. 7 (PDF 2, 7).
  - Sex and age tests pool all 185 vertebrae as if independent (Inference; see Gaps).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Expect lumbar pedicle width (narrowest axial chord, perpendicular to the pedicle axis) to rise caudally: about 7 mm at L1 and L2, 8 at L3, 9 at L4 and 12 at L5, Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5). Do not use PW to tell L1 from L2. Inference: a rise of about 2.5 mm or more between consecutive instances favours the L4 to L5 step, but the paper gives only between-person SDs (2 to 3 mm at L4 and L5), not the within-spine step.
2. Expect pedicle height (lateral or parasagittal, between the superior and inferior notches of the same vertebra) to fall from about 13 mm at L1 to about 10 mm at L5, Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5). Inference, from ratios of means: PW/PH below about 0.7 suggests L1 to L3, about 0.8 L4, above 1 L5; the per-vertebra spread is not reported, so use it as soft evidence.
3. Measure the transverse angle in the axial plane against the midsagittal line, and record whether the axis passes through the anterior-midline point (this paper, Figure 2D, p. 3 (PDF 3)) or is fitted to the pedicle's own cortices. Do not compare values across the two conventions. With this paper's convention expect 16 to 17 deg at L1 to L3, about 18.6 at L4 and about 22 at L5, Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5).
4. Compute the body's AH/PH ratio on the midsagittal plane (lateral film or mask). Means are 0.90, 0.94, 0.98, 1.02 and 1.16 from L1 to L5, Table 1, p. 4 (PDF 4). Inference: use it to tell the upper from the lower lumbar spine and to flag L5 (about 1.15 or more), not to separate adjacent levels; it is magnification-free on a radiograph but sensitive to beam tilt.
5. Expect the mid-height body width to rise about 2 mm per level from L1 (40 mm) to L4 (45.5 mm), then about 4 mm to L5 (49.5 mm). Take it at mid-height (the waist), not at the end-plate, when comparing with this paper. Table 1, p. 4 (PDF 4).
6. Do not use pedicle length to tell L1 to L4 apart: it is flat at about 40 to 41 mm with an SD of about 6 mm, and only L5 is about 2 to 3 mm shorter. Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5).
7. When reading Table 1 or the body rows of Table 3, divide by 10 to get mm, as the text on p. 3 (PDF 3) shows.
8. Adjust for sex before scoring a level with PW, PL, CL, CW or AH. Men's PW is about 1.1 mm larger, PL about 5.2 to 5.6 mm, CL about 3.1 mm, CW about 4.3 mm and AH about 1.0 mm, pooled across L1 to L5 (Table 3, p. 6 (PDF 6)); the text denies the body differences, and the p-values are inflated by pooling vertebrae (Inference). PH, PA and posterior body height do not differ by sex.
9. Expect pedicle height and transverse angle to be somewhat lower in older patients (Spearman rho -0.27 to -0.30 for height, -0.16 to -0.19 for angle; width shows no age effect), Table 4, p. 6 (PDF 6). The paper gives no slope per year, so do not convert this to a number per decade.
10. Do not take this paper's data as covering transitional spines. It excluded "congenital anomalies" and says nothing about how levels were counted, p. 2 (PDF 2). Count levels independently (from C2, by ribs, by the sacrum) and treat this paper's L1 and L5 as conventional segments only.
11. Check left-right agreement within an instance. The paper's left and right means differ by at most 0.6 mm for PW (L5), 0.4 mm for PH (L4), 0.8 mm for PL (L5) and 0.1 deg for PA (Table 2, p. 5 (PDF 5)); it reports no per-person asymmetry. Inference: a large left-right gap in one instance more likely means a segmentation error, a rotated slice or a pathological pedicle than a level cue.
12. Treat 2 mm and 2 deg as the paper's reader tolerance, p. 3 (PDF 3). No better error figure exists here.
13. Measure on the vertebra's own frame, not the scanner's: the paper's lines are drawn on reformats aligned to the vertebra (Figure 1, p. 2 (PDF 2)). Inference: a chord along the scanner or vertebral L-R axis overstates an oblique pedicle's width by about 1/cos(angle).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13 or rib variants, and it excluded congenital anomalies without a count, p. 2 (PDF 2). Take priors from other papers in the set.

(b) Measures the generator holds or could add, and definition differences:
- Pedicle width and height (Panjabi PDW, PDH) at L1 to L5. Inference, from the Panjabi 1992 note in this set (cleaned dry bone, 3D morphometer): PDW about 8.6 at L1 to 18.6 at L5, PDH 15.9 at L1, lowest 14.4 at L3, 19.6 at L5. This paper is 2 to 6 mm narrower and 3 to 9 mm lower, and its PH falls at L4 and L5 where Panjabi's rises. Yu 2015 (dry bone calipers, in this set) has PW 8.5 to 18.3 and PH 15.8 falling to 13.7, Zindrick 1987 TW 8.7 to 18.0, Hou 1993 (Chinese) width 7.0 at L1 to 12.9 at L5 with height always above width. This paper's widths sit with Hou's; its heights are the lowest of all. A mask value (outer cortex, true narrowest section) should not be pooled with this paper's numbers without a calibration.
- Pedicle transverse angle (Panjabi PDIs): the convention differs. This paper puts the vertex at the anterior-midline point (Figure 2D, p. 3 (PDF 3)), giving 16.8 to 21.9 deg against about 14.5 to 24.5 in Panjabi 1992 (Inference, from that note). A mask-derived angle with a fitted pedicle axis will not match this paper's numbers.
- Body heights: AH and PH match anterior and posterior body heights in concept, landmarks unstated. The AH/PH wedge pattern (0.90 at L1 to 1.16 at L5, ratio of means) is a cheap feature to add, weak between adjacent levels.
- Body width and depth: CW and CL are mid-body (waist) measures, not Panjabi's upper and lower end-plate widths and depths (EPW, EPD). A mask end-plate width will run larger than this CW; do not pool the two.
- Canal width and depth, facets, laminae, spinous and transverse processes: not measured here.
- Pedicle length (about 40 mm at L1 to L4, 38 at L5): a screw-path length; it discriminates poorly and is not worth adding as a level feature.
- Sagittal pedicle angle: illustrated, never reported. No data.
- Population means for a Mexican prior, per level (Inference: mean of left and right): PW 6.85, 7.0, 7.85, 9.1, 12.1 mm; PH 12.9, 12.4, 12.05, 11.1, 10.2 mm; PA 16.75, 16.45, 17.0, 18.6, 21.9 deg; PL 40.35, 40.85, 41.3, 40.5, 38.2 mm; CW 40.3, 42.2, 44.0, 45.5, 49.5 mm; CL 29.4, 30.8, 31.7, 31.8, 32.4 mm; AH 24.3, 25.2, 25.6, 25.4, 26.2 mm; PH (body) 26.9, 26.8, 26.1, 25.0, 22.5 mm (Tables 1 and 2, p. 4 to 5 (PDF 4 to 5)).

(c) Measurement error to use: no reliability statistic is given. Use the readers' 2 mm or 2 deg tolerance (p. 3 (PDF 3)) as a loose upper bound on reader disagreement. Inference: half of it, about 1 mm or 1 deg, is a defensible per-reading SD for CT caliper measures of this kind. The population SDs above already include reader error.

(d) Covariates:
- Sex, pooled across levels, mean male minus female (Table 3, p. 6 (PDF 6)): PW +1.1 mm, PL +5.2 (left) to +5.6 (right) mm, CL +3.1 mm, CW +4.3 mm, AH +1.0 mm. Not significant: PH about +0.45 mm, PA about +0.2 to +0.4 deg, posterior body height +0.5 mm. Sex is not broken down by level. Inference: male/female ratio about 1.14 for PW, 1.15 for PL, 1.11 for CL, 1.10 for CW, 1.04 for AH.
- Age: Spearman rho against pedicle height -0.27 (right) and -0.30 (left); against angle -0.16 and -0.19; against left PL -0.17; against width and right PL not significant; against the body, said to be none but not shown (Table 4 and text, p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- Body size: height, weight and BMI were not recorded. The Introduction names sex, height and body weight as influences on pedicle size (citing Yu 2015), p. 1 (PDF 1), and the Discussion names BMI, p. 7 (PDF 7); no data.
- Population: Mexican adults from one Mexico City centre, mean age 60, about 54% men; no ethnic breakdown.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Sex difference in the body: the abstract (p. 1 (PDF 1)), the results text (p. 5 (PDF 5)) and the discussion (p. 7 (PDF 7)) all say the body dimensions do not differ by sex. Table 3, p. 6 (PDF 6), shows CL (p "0.000"), CW (p "0.000") and AH (p = 0.001) significantly larger in men; only posterior body height is not (p = 0.245). The table and the text cannot both be right.
- Left-side numbers in the text: the text gives left width 8.75 against 7.65 mm, p = 0.005, and left length 42.47 against 36.87 mm, p. 5 (PDF 5). These repeat the right-side figures. Table 3 gives left width 8.82 against 7.76, p = 0.004, and left length 42.10 against 36.87. The right-side figures in the text (8.75, 42.47) also differ by 0.01 from the table's (8.74, 42.46).
- Age and pedicle length: the text says no significant correlation exists between age and PW or PL, p. 6 (PDF 6). Table 4 gives left PL rho -0.17, p = 0.020.
- The abstract and discussion give a single rho range of -0.27 to -0.30 for pedicle height and angle together, p. 1 and p. 7 (PDF 1, 7). In Table 4 that range belongs to height alone; the angle rhos are -0.16 and -0.19.
- Age and the body: the abstract and the text say no correlation exists between age and the body dimensions, p. 1 and p. 6 (PDF 1, 6), but Table 4 lists no body variable, so the claim cannot be checked.
- Table 1, L1 AH: the printed range 200 to 248 (0.1 mm) cannot hold a mean of 243.1 with an SD of 19.1, p. 4 (PDF 4). Inference: the largest SD possible on that range with that mean is about 14.5 to 14.7, so the range, the mean or the SD is mistyped.
- Table 1 has no units. Its values are 10 times the mm given in the text, p. 3 to 4 (PDF 3 to 4). The body rows of Table 3 share the same scale.
- Figure 1, p. 2 (PDF 2):
  - "pedicle height (18.63 mm)" is a line drawn along the image-right pedicle on an axial slice. It is not a height, and it exceeds the L3 maximum pedicle height in Table 2 (16.0 left, 16.5 right).
  - "pedicle length (27.97 mm)" sits under the midline lines through the body, apparently corpus length.
  - The transverse angle of this L3, 23.45 deg, exceeds the L3 maximum in Table 2 (21.6 left, 21.2 right), although the caption says these values were included in the analysis.
  - The sagittal angle is 13.83 deg in the caption and 13.33 deg in the image.
  - The same width, 39.12 mm, appears on both the axial and the coronal panels.
- Figure 2's caption names only the transverse angle; which panel defines PW, PH or PL is left to the reader, p. 3 (PDF 3).
- The PH abbreviation is used for both pedicle height and posterior vertebral body height, p. 2 (PDF 2).
- "Increased steadily" for PA and "increased" for AH are not strictly true: L2's PA is below L1's, and L4's AH is below L3's, Tables 1 and 2, p. 4 to 5 (PDF 4 to 5).
- Inference, pseudo-replication: the t statistics of Table 3 fit about 185 observations (my pooled-variance t with 100 male and 85 female vertebrae: RPL 6.40 against a printed 6.26, CL 7.07 against 7.63, RPW 2.68 against 2.83). With 37 patients (20 men, 17 women) the same means and SDs give t of about 2.9 (RPL), 3.2 (CL), 2.5 (CW), 1.2 (PW) and 1.4 (AH). The p-values of Table 4 also fit n = 185: rho -0.16 gives p about 0.03 at n = 185 and about 0.34 at n = 37; even rho -0.30 gives only p about 0.06 at n = 37. Every patient's five vertebrae, which share one age and one sex, are treated as independent, so the p-values are too small; the effective n lies between 37 and 185. The sex differences in PL, CL and CW would probably survive; the PW and AH differences and every age correlation, pedicle height included, may not.
- Inference: the pooled means of Table 3 (sex-weighted 20 to 17) reproduce the level means of Table 2 within 0.1 mm for pedicle height, AH and posterior body height and within 0.1 deg for PA; for PW, CL and CW they fall about 0.3 mm below, and for PL about 0.45 mm below. A small inconsistency in n or rounding.
- Inference: female LPL and RPL are both 36.87 (Table 3, p. 6 (PDF 6)), a possible copy error. Several PL minima are exactly 20.0 mm, and the L4 right PH minimum is printed with two decimals (8.14) while the rest have one.
- Tables 3 and 4 define asterisks in their captions, but none appear in either table, and p-values are printed as "0.000", p. 6 (PDF 6).
- Citations: reference 14 (Kars et al., PNAS 2021, the genetic structure of the Turkish population, p. 9 (PDF 9)) is cited as Turkish morphometric data, p. 7 (PDF 7); it is a genetics paper. Reference 2 (Yu et al., Spine 2015, p. 8 (PDF 8)) is cited with ref 5 for South and East Asian populations, p. 7 (PDF 7); Inference, from the Yu 2015 note in this set: it is a study of American dry skeletons (Hamann-Todd collection, Cleveland), not Asian.
- Not given at all: the written definition of each measure; inner or outer cortex; level identification; whether the whole spine was imaged; any transitional anomaly; height, weight or BMI; ethnicity beyond nationality; the CT indications; scanner, matrix or pixel size; how many patients were screened or excluded; which reader's value was kept; results for the sagittal angle; sex or age effects by level; a test of asymmetry; any thoracic, sacral or junctional data; reliability statistics.
- Against other papers (Inference, from the Panjabi 1992, Zindrick 1987, Yu 2015 and Hou 1993 notes in this set): the caudal rise of PW and PA agrees with all four. The caudal fall of PH agrees with Yu 2015 and with Berry and Zindrick as Panjabi reports them, but not with Panjabi 1992 or Hou 1993, whose L5 pedicles are tallest. The absolute PH (10 to 13 mm), the L5 PW (about 12 mm, close to Hou's 12.9 but far below the 18 to 18.6 of the American series) and the flat PA range are all lower or flatter than the dry-bone series. Definition (single axial slice, anterior-vertex angle, possibly inner or slice-limited heights) may explain this as much as population does.
