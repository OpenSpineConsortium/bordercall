## What the paper is

- Design: cross-sectional (abstract, p. 175, PDF 1), observational, based on a review of CT images (p. 175, PDF 1; p. 176, PDF 2). Inference: a review of scans already done for other complaints is retrospective; the paper does not use that word.
- Site and population: "one of North India's premier tertiary care institutes", over one year (dates not given) (p. 176, PDF 2). Inference: the authors' own institute in Lucknow, named in the affiliations (p. 175, PDF 1). Living patients; ethnicity given only as Indian; no state, caste, religion or region of origin is recorded.
- Denominator: 302 patients, 174 men (57.6%) and 128 women (42.4%), aged 20 to 40 years (p. 176, PDF 2). Table 1 (p. 176, PDF 2) bins them: 20-25 y, 50 men and 44 women (94, 31.1%); 25-30 y, 47 and 35 (82, 27.2%); 30-35 y, 50 and 36 (86, 28.5%); 35-40 y, 27 and 13 (40, 13.2%). The bin edges overlap as printed; only 13 women are 35 to 40.
- Every level row in Tables 2 and 3 carries n = 174 men and 128 women, so every patient contributed every level from D12 (= T12) to S1 (pp. 177-178, PDF 3-4). Inference: no level was dropped for transitional anatomy, artifact or poor visibility, or the authors did not report it.
- Inclusion: patients having diagnostic CT for abdominal or genitourinary complaints, or sent to radiology for a region other than the spine, without vertebral column pathology or gross spinal pathology (p. 176, PDF 2). Inference: these are patients, not healthy volunteers; only gross spinal disease was excluded, so early disc degeneration and facet arthrosis were not.
- Exclusion: age under 20 or over 40; gross spinal pathology with neurological deficit from a spinal condition; major chronic systemic disease (chronic liver and chronic kidney disease named); clinically labelled dwarfism; movement artifact; metallic artifact (p. 176, PDF 2). The reason for the 20 to 40 window is not given.
- Modality: 64-slice and 128-slice multidetector CT, unenhanced, scanned from the diaphragm to the pubic symphysis with the field of view covering D12 to S1; 3 mm sections with reconstruction to 1 mm analysed; images reconstructed in "true axial, coronal, and sagittal planes" and reformatted in bone window (p. 176, PDF 2). The abstract calls the 3 mm sections thin-cut (p. 175, PDF 1). In-plane pixel size, kernel, workstation, measuring tool and whether the axial plane was angled to each vertebra are not given.
- Measuring: "Measurement was done as shown in Figure 1" (p. 176, PDF 2). Number of readers, their training, blinding and repeat measurements are not stated.
- Statistics: SPSS 17, P < .05; independent t test men against women; unpaired t test of these means against published means of other populations (p. 176, PDF 2). No correction for multiple comparisons is reported; no test of right against left is named, although right-left results are reported (p. 177, PDF 3).
- Levels covered: D12 (T12), L1 to L5, S1 (p. 176, PDF 2; Tables 2-3). How the levels were identified and counted (from the sacrum up, from the last rib down, on which view) is not stated anywhere.
- Two groups of measures (pp. 176-177, PDF 2-3): vertebral dimensions (upper and lower vertebral width, upper and lower vertebral depth, intervertebral disc height) and canal dimensions (spinal canal AP diameter, lateral recess diameter, intervertebral foramen diameter, interfacet distance). An interpedicular distance is drawn in Figure 1 but never reported.
- Aim: a normative Indian baseline for low backache (LBA) and lumbar canal stenosis, compared with other subcontinental and world populations (p. 175, PDF 1; p. 180, PDF 6). Motivation: earlier cadaveric and osteologic series, mostly western, lacked race, age and sex data (p. 176, PDF 2) and did not report sex differences (p. 175, PDF 1); the abstract adds small samples as a limitation of many of them (p. 175, PDF 1), while the introduction says they had adequate sample sizes (p. 176, PDF 2). The authors know of no reliable Indian CT source and want a large data set to set beside radiographic and osteologic techniques (p. 179, PDF 5), though they make no such comparison themselves.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts, block vertebrae or spina bifida, and gives no rule for numbering levels; it names the thoracolumbar vertebra D12 (dorsal 12). Clinically labelled dwarfism was an exclusion (p. 176, PDF 2). No anomaly prevalence can be taken from it. Inference: with no stated counting rule and every patient contributing exactly D12 to S1, any transitional segment was silently forced into the seven labels, so the S1 and L5 rows (and D12 if a thoracolumbar variant was present) may mix transitional and typical segments; the large SDs of canal depth at L5 and S1 (men 3.09 and 3.29, women 2.72 and 4.73 mm; Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3) are consistent with, but do not prove, such mixing. Inference: the scan ran from the diaphragm down, so the lowest ribs were in the field and could have named T12; the paper does not say they were used.

## Measures, each as a recipe

All definitions come from the caption and labels of Figure 1 (p. 176, PDF 2), four axial CT panels A to D; descriptions of what the panels show are this reader's reading of the image and are marked so. All values are mean +/- SD in mm. Means quoted below are for men (n = 174) unless marked; the full male and female rows are Tables 2 and 3 (pp. 177-178, PDF 3-4). Adjacent-level separations below are Inference: the difference of the two means over the root mean square of the two SDs, computed from the tables.

### Spinal canal depth (SCD), also called midsagittal diameter, canal diameter or canal AP diameter (A-B)

- Landmarks (caption): from the posterior border of the vertebra to the lamina, in the midline (Figure 1A, p. 176, PDF 2). Reading of the image: an axial slice through the pedicles in which the canal is a closed, roughly triangular ring; A sits on the posterior cortex of the body in the midline and B on the inner (anterior) cortex of the arch at the spinolaminar junction, the apex of the canal. Ring-shaped facet joints (a superior articular process cupping an inferior one) also show posterolaterally in the same slice.
- Plane: axial CT. The slice height within the vertebra is not stated in words (Inference from the image: the pedicle-lamina level, low enough that the facet joints are cut).
- Levels: D12 to S1, men and women separately (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Build the vertebra's own frame (craniocaudal axis normal to the mean end-plate plane; AP axis from the body centroid toward the spinous process). Take the axial slice through mid-pedicle height. Fill the closed ring formed by body and posterior arch; the filled-minus-bone region is the vertebral foramen. SCD is the extent of that region along the AP axis on the midsagittal line: from the posterior surface of the body subregion to the anterior surface of the posterior-element subregion. A soft-tissue spinal-canal label (SPINEPS's canal class) marks the canal contents along the column, not one vertebra's bony foramen, and is not this measure.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference (the paper discusses no radiograph). A lateral radiograph gives the analogous sagittal canal depth from the posterior body line to the spinolaminar line, divided by the magnification (Eisenstein's lateral films at 110 cm read 1.23, per the eisenstein1977 note of this set); the spinolaminar line is faint at L5-S1. Not available on an AP view.
- Across levels (men): D12 16.19 +/- 1.26, L1 15.68 +/- 1.51, L2 14.57 +/- 1.59, L3 13.45 +/- 1.75, L4 13.29 +/- 1.83, L5 14.73 +/- 3.09, S1 11.00 +/- 3.29 (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3). Women 16.33 +/- 1.52, 15.85, 14.74, 13.66, 13.40 +/- 1.68, 14.67 +/- 2.72, 11.19 +/- 4.73. Shape: falls from D12 to a floor at L3-L4, rises at L5, falls to its minimum at S1, in both sexes (p. 177, PDF 3; p. 179, PDF 5; p. 180, PDF 6). The authors read the fall as the canal changing from circular to oval cranio-caudally and say the canal changes both transversely and anteroposteriorly (p. 177, PDF 3; p. 179, PDF 5), but no transverse value is reported, so the shape claim rests on depth alone.
- Adjacent-level separation, Inference from Table 2 (men; women in brackets): D12/L1 0.37 (0.33), L1/L2 0.72 (0.79), L2/L3 0.67 (0.77), L3/L4 0.09 (0.17), L4/L5 0.57 (0.56), L5/S1 1.17 (0.90). SCD cannot tell L3 from L4; its L5 rebound (+1.4 mm in men) and S1 drop (-3.7 mm) are the usable signals.
- Sex, age, size, population: no significant sex difference (P > .05) (p. 177, PDF 3); women's means are slightly larger at six of seven levels (by 0.11 to 0.21 mm), L5 the exception (14.67 against 14.73) (Table 2). Age effect not analysed (ages 20 to 40 only). Kumar et al's Telangana CT series (61 patients) gives larger values than this paper at L1 to L5 in men and L1 to L4 in women (its women's L5 is 14.25 against 14.67 here), with women larger than men at L1 to L4 (Table 4, p. 179, PDF 5; this paper's true L1 means from Table 2); the conclusions say trends match other studies but values here are lower (p. 180, PDF 6).
- Size-free ratio, Inference computed from Tables 2 and 3: SCD over upper vertebral depth (men) is 0.60 at D12, 0.55 L1, 0.48 L2, 0.43 L3, 0.42 L4, 0.45 L5, 0.35 S1; women 0.65, 0.60, 0.52, 0.46, 0.44, 0.47, 0.38. Women have relatively deeper canals for their body size. (The reference list cites a study of the lumbar Torg ratio, ref. 13, p. 181, PDF 7, but the paper itself computes no canal-to-body ratio.)
- Error: not reported. Between-subject coefficient of variation (Inference, SD over mean, men): 8 to 14% from D12 to L4, 21% at L5, 30% at S1; the women's S1 SD (4.73 on a mean of 11.19) makes mean - 2 SD about 1.7 mm, so the S1 row is not a usable normal range. The SDs mix anatomy and measurement error and cannot be separated here.

### Spinal canal width, interpedicular distance (C-D)

- Landmarks (caption): maximum distance between the pedicles (Figure 1A, p. 176, PDF 2). Reading of the image: a transverse line across the widest part of the canal between the medial cortices of the two pedicles, in the same slice as A-B, crossing it in its anterior third.
- Values: none. The measure is defined in Figure 1 but appears in no table and no text result.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the mid-pedicle axial slice of the vertebra's frame, the largest transverse distance across the vertebral foramen between the medial surfaces of the left and right pedicles.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. AP radiograph, the classic interpedicular distance between the medial pedicle borders, divided by the magnification.
- Across levels, sex, population, error: nothing in this paper. Pearl the paper cites (p. 175, PDF 1, after Verbiest): in developmental stenosis the interpedicular distances are normal, so this width does not detect it.

### Lateral recess diameter (LRD), right and left (E-F)

- Landmarks (caption): from the posterior aspect of the vertebral body to the anteromedial point of the facet joints, on each side (Figure 1B, p. 176, PDF 2). Reading of the image: an axial slice where the body has an end-plate-like oval outline with the articular processes behind it; a transverse reference line is drawn across the whole image tangent to the anterior margins of both facet joints, and E-F on each side is the perpendicular from the posterior body surface down to that line, dropped at the lateral edge of the canal just medial to each facet joint, so it ends at the facet's anteromedial corner. In this slice the canal is not a closed ring (no pedicle joins body and arch), as at a disc or end-plate level.
- Plane: axial CT. Slice height not stated (Inference from the image: at or near an end plate or the disc, where the superior articular process lies behind the body).
- Levels: D12 to S1 (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. On the axial slice through the upper end plate of the vertebra (where its superior articular processes face the body), find the most anteromedial point of each superior articular process; draw the transverse line through the two points and, on each side, take the AP distance from the posterior surface of the body subregion to that line at the facet's anteromedial corner, which is the lateral edge of the canal. Needs articular-process subregions; with a whole-vertebra mask the anteromedial facet corner must be found as the most anterior-medial point of the posterior element lateral to the canal. The facet joint is shared with the vertebra above, so the cranial neighbour's inferior articular process may lie in the same slice.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (Inference): the lateral recess is an axial structure hidden by overlap on lateral and AP views.
- Across levels (men, right): D12 11.45 (printed 11.451) +/- 1.61, L1 11.36 +/- 0.68, L2 10.52 +/- 1.39, L3 9.24 +/- 0.91, L4 8.60 +/- 0.99, L5 7.71 +/- 0.66, S1 7.50 +/- 0.62 (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3). Men's left 11.61 (printed 11.609) to 7.61, also monotonic. Women right 9.47 (printed 9.469), 9.40, 9.22, 8.58, 8.69, 7.53, 7.45; women left 9.67, 9.60, 9.28, 8.69, 7.77, 7.62, 7.56. Described as a gradual fall from D12 to S1 in both sexes, maximum D12, minimum S1 (p. 177, PDF 3; p. 179, PDF 5); the women's right side rises from L3 to L4 (8.58 to 8.69) against that description.
- Adjacent-level separation, Inference from Table 2 (men, right): D12/L1 0.07, L1/L2 0.77, L2/L3 1.09, L3/L4 0.67, L4/L5 1.06, L5/S1 0.33. One of the better mid-lumbar separators in men, but D12/L1 and L5/S1 are not separated; in women the separations are weaker except L4/L5 on the right (1.74) and L3/L4 on the left (1.11), both made by the suspect women's L4 values (right 8.69, left 7.77).
- Sex: significantly larger in men from D12 to L5 (P < .05) (p. 177, PDF 3; p. 179, PDF 5). Inference computed from Table 2: the gap is about 2 mm (about 21%) at D12 and L1, 1.3 mm at L2, 0.6 to 0.7 mm at L3, and small caudally (right L4 -0.09, women larger; L5 0.18; S1 0.05); a t test on the printed means and SDs does not reach P < .05 for the right side at L4.
- Side: the authors report a significant right-left difference at all levels (p. 177, PDF 3; p. 179, PDF 5) and say Alam et al found the same (p. 179, PDF 5). Inference: the printed left-minus-right differences are 0.03 to 0.20 mm everywhere except women at L4 (right 8.69, left 7.77, a 0.92 mm difference); no right-left test is named in the methods, and an unpaired test on these SDs reaches P < .05 only at women's L4, so the claim is doubtful or rests on an unreported paired test.
- Population: larger than Alam et al's Pakistani CT values (49 patients) at every level and side except women's right L1 (9.4 against 9.5) (Table 5, p. 179, PDF 5, L1 to L5 only); same falling trend.
- Error: not reported. The men's right L1 SD (0.68) is less than half of the left (1.70) and of its neighbours, a likely typographic or data error (Inference).

### Intervertebral foramen diameter (IVFD), right and left

- Landmarks: not defined. Figure 1 has no panel for it and the text gives no definition (p. 176, PDF 2). Plane, direction (height or AP width), and which foramen is meant at D12 and S1 are all unstated.
- Values (men, right): D12 10.62 +/- 1.17, L1 10.54 +/- 1.32, L2 10.50 +/- 1.69, L3 9.72 +/- 1.29, L4 8.68 +/- 1.38, L5 8.32 +/- 1.48, S1 5.77 +/- 1.34 (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3). Women similar (right 10.43 at D12 to 5.80 at S1). Female left L3 is 10.30 +/- 3.43, higher than L2 (10.22) and with an SD twice its neighbours'.
- Across levels: described as a constant decrease from D12 to S1, maximum D12, minimum S1 (p. 177, PDF 3; p. 179, PDF 5); the female left L2 to L3 rise contradicts "constant". Inference from Table 2: nearly flat D12 to L2 (adjacent separations 0.06 and 0.03), then falling; the L5 to S1 drop (separation about 1.8) is the strongest step.
- Sex: no significant sex difference at any level (P > .05); right-left difference significant only at S1 (p. 177, PDF 3; p. 179, PDF 5). Inference: a t test on the printed female left L3 value (women 0.66 mm larger) would reach P < .05, against the text; the S1 right-left differences are 0.20 mm in men and 0.05 mm in women.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference only. A lumbar foramen is bounded by two adjacent vertebrae (pedicle above and below, body and disc in front, facet behind), so it cannot be taken from one instance mask; it needs the pair. At S1 the value of about 5.7 mm suggests a sacral foramen rather than the L5-S1 foramen. Do not build a generator variable on this measure until its definition is found elsewhere.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. The lumbar foramina are seen on a lateral radiograph; which dimension this paper took is unknown.
- Error: not reported; between-subject CV 11 to 24% (men, Inference from Table 2).

### Interfacet distance (IFD) (K-L)

- Landmarks (caption): distance between the anteromedial points of the facet joints (Figure 1D, p. 176, PDF 2). Reading of the image: an axial slice through the body, the closed canal and the facet joints; K-L is a transverse line across the posterior part of the canal, just in front of the laminae, between the medial margins of the left and right articular processes. Inference: so IFD is the transverse width of the bony canal at the facet level, not the distance between the joint lines themselves.
- Levels: D12 to S1 (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. On the axial slice through the facet joints, take the transverse distance between the most anteromedial points of the left and right articular processes, equivalently the canal's transverse width at that slice. Which joint is assigned to which level (the joint above the named vertebra, formed by its superior processes, or the one below) is not stated; Inference: the S1 value must come from the S1 superior articular processes, i.e. the L5-S1 joint, so the joint above each named vertebra is the likely convention.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Only approximately, on an AP radiograph where the medial margins of the articular pillars are seen (lower lumbar, sagittally oriented joints); the anteromedial corner is not a radiographic landmark. Not available on a lateral view.
- Across levels (men): D12 15.12 +/- 2.46, L1 17.27 +/- 2.02, L2 17.53 +/- 2.15, L3 17.67 +/- 2.42, L4 19.08 +/- 3.21, L5 22.69 +/- 3.91, S1 27.91 +/- 4.65 (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3); women 14.33 at D12 to 26.89 at S1. Monotonic increase, minimum D12, maximum S1 (pp. 177-178, PDF 3-4; p. 179, PDF 5).
- Adjacent-level separation, Inference from Table 2 (men): D12/L1 0.96, L1/L2 0.12, L2/L3 0.06, L3/L4 0.50, L4/L5 1.01, L5/S1 1.22 (women nearly identical). A plateau L1 to L3 bounded by two steep steps: the thoracolumbar step (T12 to L1, +2.15 mm) and the lumbosacral run (L4 +1.41, L5 +3.61, S1 +5.22 mm). Inference: IFD over upper vertebral width is flat at about 0.40 to 0.43 from D12 to L4, then 0.47 at L5 and 0.57 at S1 in men (0.47 and 0.58 in women), a size-free cue for the bottom of the column.
- Sex: significantly larger in men from D12 to L5 (p. 178, PDF 4) or at all levels (p. 179, PDF 5), the two passages disagreeing. Inference from Table 2: men larger by 0.71 to 1.06 mm (3.8 to 6.5%); a t test on the printed values just misses P < .05 at L4 (t about 1.9) and S1 (t about 1.9).
- Error: not reported; CV 12 to 17% (men, Inference).

### Upper and lower vertebral width (UVW, LVW) (I-J)

- Landmarks (caption): distance between the lateral borders of the vertebral body in the plane of the upper and of the lower end plate respectively (Figure 1C, p. 176, PDF 2). Axial CT, transverse line. Reading of the image: the example slice in panel C shows an enclosed canal and a transverse process, so it looks like a mid-body slice through the pedicles, not an end plate; the figure therefore shows the direction of the line (I-J across the widest part of the body), and the end-plate height rests on the caption alone.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Body subregion only. In the vertebra's frame take the axial slab just inside the superior (UVW) or inferior (LVW) end plate, a voxel or two in from the surface to avoid partial volume, and measure the maximum transverse extent; decide explicitly whether the ring apophysis and marginal lipping are in or out, since that moves the value by a millimetre or more. Panjabi's EPWu and EPWl are the analogous measures, digitised around the end-plate rim on dry bone (per the panjabi1992 note of this set).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. AP radiograph, width of the end-plate line between the lateral cortices, divided by the magnification; end-plate tilt and rotation shorten it.
- Across levels, UVW (men): D12 36.99 +/- 2.89, L1 40.15 +/- 3.09, L2 42.55 +/- 4.08, L3 44.34 +/- 3.17, L4 46.42 +/- 3.98, L5 48.79 +/- 5.54, S1 49.08 +/- 4.40 (Table 3, p. 178, PDF 4); women 34.62 at D12 to 46.00 at L5 and 46.17 at S1. Progressive increase D12 to S1, minimum D12, maximum S1 (p. 178, PDF 4).
- Across levels, LVW (men): D12 39.30 +/- 3.16, L1 42.26 +/- 4.50, L2 44.35 +/- 3.27, L3 46.66 +/- 3.38, L4 46.86 +/- 3.85, L5 46.93 +/- 3.36, S1 30.78 +/- 3.45 (Table 3); women 36.73 at D12 to 45.91 at L5, then 29.67 at S1. Increase D12 to L5, maximum L5, then an abrupt fall at S1 (p. 178, PDF 4). Inference: the S1 "lower end plate" is at the fused S1-S2 junction, so the S1 value is not comparable to a lumbar end plate.
- Lower minus upper width, Inference from Table 3 (men): +2.3, +2.1, +1.8, +2.3 mm from D12 to L3, +0.4 at L4, -1.9 at L5 (women +2.1 to +2.9 at D12-L3, +1.3 at L4, -0.1 at L5). The lower end plate is wider than the upper down to L3 and no wider at L5; Panjabi's dry-bone EPWl exceeds EPWu at every lumbar level including L5 (per the panjabi1992 note), so this L5 cue is not confirmed across sources.
- Adjacent-level separation, Inference from Table 3 (men): UVW D12/L1 1.06, L1/L2 0.66, L2/L3 0.49, L3/L4 0.58, L4/L5 0.49, L5/S1 0.06; LVW D12/L1 0.76, L1/L2 0.53, L2/L3 0.69, L3/L4 0.06, L4/L5 0.02, L5/S1 4.74. UVW grows 1.8 to 3.2 mm per level (largest step D12 to L1) against a between-subject SD of 2.9 to 5.5 mm, so one width alone separates neighbours weakly; LVW plateaus from L3 to L5.
- Sex: both significantly larger in men at all levels (P < .05) (p. 178, PDF 4; p. 180, PDF 6); Inference from Table 3: UVW by 2.4 to 3.2 mm (6.1 to 8.7%), LVW by 1.0 to 2.7 mm (2.2 to 7.0%, smallest at L5 and S1).
- Population (Tables 6 and 7, p. 180, PDF 6): Alam et al (Pakistan, CT, 49 patients; 33 men and 16 women in Table 7), Kang et al (Korea, 60 subjects) and Singh (Jaskaran) et al (north Indian, 20 cadavers) show the same cranio-caudal increase of UVW from L1 to L5 (p. 179, PDF 5). Inference from Table 6: men here are smaller than all three comparators at every level, but women here are larger than Kang's at L1 to L3 and than Alam's at L3 and L4; Kang's men reach 54.83 at L5 against 48.79 here. Singh's cadaveric LVW rose L1 to L2, fell at L3 and rose again (p. 180, PDF 6), a pattern not seen here; women's LVW here exceeds Alam's at every level (Table 7).
- Error: not reported; CV 7 to 11% (men, Inference). The women's L2 LVW SD (5.53) is nearly twice its neighbours'.

### Upper and lower vertebral depth (UVD, LVD) (G-H)

- Landmarks (caption): distance between the anterior and posterior ends of the vertebral body in the plane of the upper and of the lower end plate respectively (Figure 1C, p. 176, PDF 2). Axial CT; reading of the image: G-H runs in the midline from the anterior cortex to the posterior cortex of the body, stopping at the canal (same slice caveat as for width).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Body subregion, the same end-plate slabs as for width; the AP extent along the midsagittal line (not the maximum AP extent, which can fall off the midline where the posterior wall is concave).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Lateral radiograph, end-plate length from the anterior to the posterior corner, divided by the magnification; the best-transferred body measure to lateral fluoroscopy.
- Across levels, UVD (men): D12 27.09 +/- 2.59, L1 28.61 +/- 2.61, L2 30.37 +/- 3.26, L3 31.37 +/- 2.78, L4 31.56 +/- 2.27, L5 32.41 +/- 2.35, S1 31.16 +/- 3.19 (Table 3, p. 178, PDF 4); women 25.10 at D12 to 30.94 at L5, 29.47 at S1. Increase D12 to L5, maximum L5, fall at S1 (p. 178, PDF 4).
- Across levels, LVD (men): D12 27.88 +/- 2.53, L1 29.49, L2 30.60, L3 31.25, L4 32.15 +/- 3.04, L5 31.66 +/- 2.89, S1 22.78 +/- 2.66 (Table 3); women 25.82 to 30.30 at L5, 22.03 at S1. Maximum L4 in men and L5 in women; minimum S1 in both (p. 178, PDF 4; p. 180, PDF 6).
- Adjacent-level separation, Inference from Table 3 (men): UVD D12/L1 0.58, L1/L2 0.60, L2/L3 0.33, L3/L4 0.07, L4/L5 0.37, L5/S1 0.45; LVD D12/L1 0.59, L1/L2 0.40, L2/L3 0.26, L3/L4 0.33, L4/L5 0.17, L5/S1 3.20.
- Sex: both significantly larger in men at all levels (P < .05) (p. 178, PDF 4; p. 180, PDF 6); Inference from Table 3: UVD by 1.4 to 2.1 mm (4.8 to 7.9%), LVD by 0.75 to 2.2 mm (3.4 to 8.0%, smallest at S1).
- Population: Alam's Pakistani UVD values are larger at every level in men and at every level but L4 in women (30 against 30.12) (Table 8, p. 181, PDF 7). Inference, against the panjabi1992 note of this set: Panjabi's dry-bone EPDu is 34.1 to 35.5 mm and flat from L1 to L5, while UVD here is 28.6 to 32.4 mm in men and rises by level; the axial-plane CT depth here reads about 2 to 6 mm smaller than a rim-to-rim dry-bone depth.
- Error: not reported; CV 7 to 12% (men, Inference).

### Intervertebral disc height (IVDH)

- Landmarks: not defined; no panel in Figure 1 and no text on where it was taken (anterior, middle, posterior, or mean) or on which plane (p. 176, PDF 2). Inference: sagittal reformat.
- Which disc belongs to which level is not stated. Inference: the disc below the named vertebra (D12 = T12-L1 ... L5 = L5-S1, S1 = the rudimentary S1-S2 disc), since the S1 value of 3.55 mm fits a residual sacral disc and not an L5-S1 disc in adults of 20 to 40.
- Across levels (men): D12 6.45 +/- 1.27, L1 7.33 +/- 1.48, L2 8.66 +/- 1.68, L3 9.76 +/- 1.80, L4 10.60 +/- 1.76, L5 9.82 +/- 1.99, S1 3.55 +/- 0.87 (Table 3, p. 178, PDF 4); women 6.35, 7.30, 8.45, 10.17 +/- 6.87, 10.23 +/- 1.65, 9.37, 3.35. Increase to a maximum at L4, then decrease; minimum S1 (p. 178, PDF 4; p. 180, PDF 6).
- Adjacent-level separation, Inference from Table 3 (men): D12/L1 0.64, L1/L2 0.84, L2/L3 0.63, L3/L4 0.47, L4/L5 0.42, L5/S1 4.08.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. The gap along the craniocaudal axis between the inferior end plate of one body subregion and the superior end plate of the next, at the end-plate centre (or the mean over the overlapping end-plate area); needs the pair of instances. On fluoroscopy, the gap between end plates on a lateral view, divided by the magnification.
- Sex: reported as significantly larger in men at all levels (P < .05) (p. 178, PDF 4; p. 180, PDF 6). The table contradicts this at L3 (women 10.17 against men 9.76), the L1 difference is 0.03 mm, and Inference: a t test on the printed means and SDs is borderline at S1 (t about 1.97) and short of P < .05 at every other level. Treat disc height as not shown to differ by sex.
- Error: not reported; CV 17 to 25% (men, Inference). Female L3 SD is printed 6.87, about four times its neighbours; likely a typographic error.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- Thoracolumbar junction (D12 to L1): upper width jumps 3.2 mm in men (36.99 to 40.15, the largest UVW step) and interfacet distance 2.2 mm (15.12 to 17.27), while lateral recess and foramen are flat (Tables 2-3, pp. 177-178, PDF 3-4). D12 has the largest canal depth, lateral recess and foramen, the smallest upper end-plate width and depth and interfacet distance, and the smallest lower end-plate width and depth and disc height of any level above S1 (Tables 2-3, pp. 177-178, PDF 3-4); the sex gap in lateral recess is largest here (about 2 mm). Inference: IFD and UVW together are the T12/L1 cue here.
- L1 to L3: canal depth falls about 1.1 mm per level; interfacet distance is a plateau (17.27, 17.53, 17.67); widths grow 1.8 to 2.4 mm per level (Tables 2-3).
- L3 and L4: canal depth floor (13.45 and 13.29 in men; indistinguishable); LVW and UVD plateau (Tables 2-3). Disc height peaks at L4 (p. 178, PDF 4). Women's right and left lateral recess diverge at L4 (8.69 against 7.77) (Table 2); Inference: a likely data error.
- L5: canal depth rises above L4 (14.73 men, 14.67 women), with its SD rising from about 1.7-1.8 to 2.7-3.1 mm (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3); interfacet distance jumps to 22.69; UVW and UVD reach their lumbar maxima; LVW maximum (Tables 2-3); in men the lower end plate is narrower than the upper (46.93 against 48.79, Inference from Table 3).
- Lumbosacral junction (L5 to S1): canal depth to its minimum (11.00 men, 11.19 women, SD 3.3 and 4.7); IFD to its maximum (27.91, 26.89); foramen to its minimum (5.77, 5.80); lower body width and depth collapse (46.93 to 30.78; 31.66 to 22.78 in men) and the "disc" below falls to 3.55 mm (Tables 2-3, pp. 177-178, PDF 3-4). Upper width of S1 (49.08) barely exceeds L5's (48.79). Inference: the S1 lower-surface and disc values describe the fused S1-S2 junction, the strongest single sign that an instance is S1 and not a lumbar body.
- C2/C3 and C7/T1: not covered.

## Measurement error and reliability

- None reported: no intra- or inter-observer agreement, no repeat measures, no number of readers, no phantom or calibration (pp. 176-178, PDF 2-4).
- Resolution: 3 mm sections, reconstruction to 1 mm (p. 176, PDF 2); in-plane pixel not stated. Inference: with a typical abdominal field of view the in-plane pixel is about 0.7 to 0.8 mm, so values of 5 to 11 mm (foramen, lateral recess) carry a quantisation error of several percent, and the 0.001 mm precision printed for some values (11.451, 9.469, 11.609) is spurious.
- Two scanner types (64-slice and 128-slice) were pooled (p. 176, PDF 2) with no check that they measure alike; Inference: a small systematic difference between them would sit inside the tabled SDs.
- Inference: CT measurement carries no projection magnification. The authors hold CT to be more relevant and accurate than cadaveric or manual measurement (p. 180, PDF 6) and cite CT as better than x-ray and cadaver work (p. 176, PDF 2), without data of their own on it.
- The SDs in Tables 2 and 3 are between-person spread with measurement error folded in. Several SDs are implausible against their neighbours (men right LRD L1 0.68; women LVW L2 5.53; women left IVFD L3 3.43; women IVDH L3 6.87), so the tables carry transcription or data errors of their own.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Definition of lumbar canal stenosis used here: AP and lateral dimensions of the bony canal smaller than normal for the same age and sex (p. 175, PDF 1). No numeric threshold for central, lateral recess or foraminal stenosis is given anywhere in the paper.
- Inference (not in the paper): a population-specific lower limit could be taken as mean - 2 SD from Table 2, for example L4 canal depth about 9.6 mm in men and 10.0 mm in women (L3 9.9 and 10.8; L5 8.6 and 9.2); widely quoted thresholds from other work (central stenosis below about 10 mm sagittal, relative 10 to 13 mm; lateral recess depth of about 3 mm or less abnormal) are from memory and not in this paper. This paper's lateral recess values (7.5 to 11.6 mm) are larger than usual recess depths, which suggests its landmark (posterior body to the line across the facets' anterior margins, at an end-plate-like slice) is not the usual narrowest recess depth at the pedicle's upper border; Inference.
- Stenosis types (p. 175, PDF 1, after Kirkaldy-Willis and Verbiest): developmental stenosis comes from the neural arch, pedicles, laminae and articular processes, with normal interpedicular distances but short lateral sagittal diameters from thick laminae and articular processes; degenerative stenosis with facet osteoarthritis is worst opposite the disc and the articular processes, while AP and lateral diameters may be normal; combined stenosis (overall or segmental narrowing, disc protrusion, or both) gives more neurological symptoms than either alone (pp. 175-176, PDF 1-2).
- Pitfalls from the above, Inference: a normal interpedicular distance (the AP radiograph's classic measure) does not exclude developmental stenosis; a canal depth taken at pedicle level (Figure 1A) can be normal in degenerative stenosis, which narrows the canal at the disc and facet level, so measure at the disc level too when stenosis is the question.
- Presentation: lumbar spinal stenosis presents as low backache, paresthesia and bilateral lower limb pain; it comes from narrowing of the bony canal or foramina compressing central and exiting neural structures (p. 179, PDF 5). The commonest manifestation is low backache (p. 175, PDF 1); low backache is the commonest complaint sent for imaging, and canal size assessment is called one of the most essential steps in its diagnosis (p. 178, PDF 4).
- Why canal size matters for back pain, in the authors' account (p. 178, PDF 4): pathological change in the structures around the canal and foramina can disturb the canal's alignment and give low backache, and the lumbar vertebrae's sagittal curvature serves to absorb impact and reduce stiffness. They give no data of their own on alignment or curvature.
- Lumbar spondylosis is increasingly seen in young people, which the authors put down to lifestyle, and its management differs when canal stenosis accompanies it (p. 175, PDF 1).
- Population pearl: population-specific variation justifies separate norms (p. 179, PDF 5); the canal AP values here run lower than in other studies while the level trends match (p. 180, PDF 6). The abstract calls the canal differences statistically significant (p. 175, PDF 1), but no comparison table carries a P value. Body dimensions are said to follow similar trends (p. 180, PDF 6); whether they are lower depends on sex and comparator (Tables 6-8).
- Indications for the norms: evaluation of patients with low backache and lumbar canal stenosis in an Indian population (p. 175, PDF 1; p. 180, PDF 6). Screws, safe zones and syndromes other than dwarfism (an exclusion) are not discussed, although several pedicle-screw papers are cited (refs. 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, p. 181, PDF 7).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Measure canal and body dimensions on axial reformats in bone window, as the paper did (p. 176, PDF 2). Inference: align the axial plane to each vertebra's end plates; the paper says only "true axial" and does not say whether it was angled.
2. Take canal depth in the midline from the posterior body cortex to the lamina (caption, Figure 1A, p. 176, PDF 2); the image places the posterior point at the inner cortex of the spinolaminar junction on a pedicle-level slice (reading of the image). Do not expect it to separate L3 from L4 (means 13.45 and 13.29 in men, Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3).
3. Use the L5 rebound of canal depth (higher than L4 in both sexes) and the S1 drop as level cues at the bottom of the lumbar spine, but weigh them lightly: their SD is about 3 mm, 4.7 mm in women at S1 (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3), and Kumar et al's women showed no L5 rebound (Table 4, p. 179, PDF 5).
4. Use interfacet distance as a cranio-caudal gradient: a step of about 2 mm from T12 to L1, a plateau L1 to L3, then about 1.4, 3.6 and 5.2 mm steps to L4, L5 and S1 in men (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3). Take it on an axial slice through the facet joints between the anteromedial points of the two articular processes, across the back of the canal (Figure 1D, p. 176, PDF 2). Inference: divide it by the upper end-plate width (about 0.40 to 0.43 down to L4, 0.47 at L5, 0.57 at S1) to remove body size.
5. Expect upper end-plate width to grow about 1.8 to 3.2 mm per level from T12 (37 mm) to L5 (49 mm) in Indian men, and to be 2.4 to 3.2 mm smaller in women at every level (Table 3, p. 178, PDF 4); treat a single width as weak evidence and combine it with depth and with the neighbours.
6. Inference from Table 3 (p. 178, PDF 4): call an instance S1 when its lower surface is much narrower and shallower than its upper surface (about 31 by 23 mm against 49 by 31 mm in men) and the "disc" below it is about 3.5 mm. An instance whose upper surface is S1-sized but whose lower disc is lumbar-tall is a candidate lumbarised S1; this paper gives no data on it.
7. Correct for sex before comparing body dimensions with these norms: men's end-plate widths and depths are about 2 to 9% larger (Table 3, p. 178, PDF 4), lateral recess about 20% larger at T12-L1 and 14% at L2 (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3), interfacet distance about 4 to 6.5% larger. Canal depth and foramen are reported as not sex-dependent (p. 177, PDF 3), and disc height, though reported as larger in men, is not shown to differ by the printed values (Inference, Table 3).
8. Inference (computed from Tables 2-3): prefer ratios to absolute sizes when the patient's population or size is unknown; canal depth over upper body depth falls from about 0.60 at T12 to 0.42 at L4, rises to 0.45 at L5 and is lowest at S1 (0.35).
9. Inference: do not use right-left lateral recess asymmetry as a feature; the reported significance (p. 177, PDF 3) rests on differences of about 0.1 to 0.2 mm (Table 2) and no named paired test.
10. Do not use this paper's foramen diameter or disc height as a recipe: neither is defined (p. 176, PDF 2); take only their level trend (Tables 2-3).
11. Inference: do not take these norms outside ages 20 to 40 or outside a North Indian population (p. 176, PDF 2); dwarfism was excluded.
12. When stenosis is the question, compare with these Indian means and SDs rather than western cadaveric norms; the paper gives no cut-off, so report a z-score against Table 2 rather than a yes or no (p. 175, PDF 1; Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3). Remember that a normal interpedicular distance does not exclude developmental stenosis (p. 175, PDF 1).
13. Read Table 2, not Table 4, for canal depth: Table 4's L1 row carries the D12 means with the L1 SDs, and its female L3 mean is 13.60 against 13.66 (p. 179, PDF 5; p. 177, PDF 3).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper reports no transitional segment, rib count or block vertebra and no counting rule; every one of 302 patients (174 men, 128 women) is labelled D12 to S1.

(b) Measures the generator holds or could add, and definition differences:
- Canal depth (SCD) corresponds to Panjabi's spinal canal depth (midline, posterior body wall to the laminar arch at the spinous base, per the panjabi1992 note). Here it is taken on axial CT at pedicle level from body to lamina in the midline. Inference: a mask-based depth taken at a different height (end plate, disc) or to a different posterior point will differ systematically; fix the height at mid-pedicle and the posterior landmark at the inner arch cortex. Values here (13.3 to 15.7 mm, L1 to L5, men) run 3 to 5 mm below Panjabi's 17.5 to 19.7 mm with the same L3-L4 trough and L5 rise (Inference, against the panjabi1992 note), so population, living CT against dry bone, or landmark shifts the level of the curve, not its shape.
- Upper and lower end-plate width and depth (UVW, LVW, UVD, LVD) correspond to Panjabi's EPWu, EPWl, EPDu, EPDl. Here they are taken on axial CT "in the plane of" the end plate (Figure 1C), whose example slice looks mid-body. Inference: widths agree with Panjabi in size (about 40 to 49 mm), but depths here are about 2 to 6 mm smaller and rise by level where Panjabi's are flat; a mask slab placed too far inside the body picks up the waisted mid-body and reads small, and one including the ring apophysis or osteophytes reads large.
- Canal width (interpedicular distance) is defined but has no values here; use another source for the generator.
- Interfacet distance is a candidate new variable: monotonic over T12 to S1, steep at both junctions (Table 2). It is a canal width at the facet level and needs a reliable anteromedial facet point or the canal outline on the facet slice.
- Lateral recess diameter is a candidate but definition-sensitive (perpendicular from the body to the line across the facets' anterior margins, slice height unstated); values 7.5 to 11.6 mm are larger than usual recess depths, so do not mix with other papers' lateral recess values.
- Foramen diameter and disc height are undefined here and should not be generated from this paper's definitions; disc height needs two instances.
- T12 and S1 rows extend beyond the generator's C3 to L5 range; the T12 row can anchor a T12 class for the thoracolumbar transition, and the S1 rows can anchor a sacral class (Inference).

(c) Measurement error to use: none reported. Inference: treat the table SDs as an upper bound on reader error plus anatomy (between-subject CV in men: body widths and depths 7 to 12%, canal depth 8 to 14% from T12 to L4 and 21 to 30% at L5 and S1, lateral recess 6 to 15%, interfacet distance 12 to 17%, foramen 11 to 24%, disc height 17 to 25%); for a voxel-based estimate assume about +/- 0.5 to 1 mm per linear measure at the stated 1 mm reconstruction, a larger share of the small canal measures (lateral recess, foramen).

(d) Covariates:
- Sex: end-plate widths and depths larger in men by about 0.75 to 3.2 mm (2 to 9%); canal depth and foramen show no sex difference; lateral recess larger in men cranially (about 2 mm at T12-L1) and hardly caudally; interfacet distance larger in men by about 1 mm; disc height reported larger in men but not supported by the printed values (pp. 177-178, PDF 3-4; Inference for the computed sizes and tests).
- Age: restricted to 20 to 40; no age analysis.
- Size: no height, weight or BMI recorded; Inference: the patient size factor must come from the body dimensions themselves (for example mean end-plate width), since canal depth does not scale with sex the way the body does.
- Population: North Indian living adults. Inference from Tables 4-8 (pp. 179-181, PDF 5-7): canal depth below Kumar's Telangana CT values at L1 to L5 in men and L1 to L4 in women; men's upper widths below Alam (Pakistan), Kang (Korea) and Singh (north Indian cadavers) and men's upper depths below Alam; men's lower widths mostly just below Alam's; women's widths mixed, above Alam's LVW at every level; lateral recess above Alam's at almost every level. Lower in every column is not what the tables show.

## Gaps and conflicts

- No stenosis threshold anywhere, despite the title and conclusion (pp. 175, 180, PDF 1, 6).
- Interpedicular distance defined in Figure 1A but never reported, although the text says the canal changes transversely (p. 176, PDF 2; p. 177, PDF 3).
- Foramen diameter and disc height are tabulated but never defined (p. 176, PDF 2).
- The slice height for canal depth, lateral recess and interfacet distance is shown only in the figure images, not stated; the example slice for the end-plate widths and depths (Figure 1C) looks mid-body, not at an end plate; the level-to-facet-joint and level-to-disc assignment is not stated.
- Level identification and transitional anatomy: not addressed.
- No reliability data, no reader count, and no limitations section: the authors state no limitation of their own.
- Text against table and text against text:
  - Minimum left lateral recess in women at S1 is printed as 5.80 mm in the text (p. 177, PDF 3); Table 2 gives 7.56 +/- 0.60; 5.80 is the women's right foramen value at S1.
  - Table 4 (p. 179, PDF 5) labels as L1 the values 16.19 +/- 1.51 (men) and 16.33 +/- 1.43 (women): these are the D12 means of Table 2 joined to the L1 SDs; Table 2's L1 means are 15.68 and 15.85. Table 4's female L3 mean is 13.60 against 13.66 in Table 2.
  - Lateral recess "significantly more in men from the D12 to L5" (p. 177, PDF 3): at L4 right the women's mean is larger (8.69 against 8.60) (Table 2). Lateral recess "gradually decreased" (p. 177, PDF 3): the women's right side rises from L3 to L4 (Table 2).
  - Foramen "constantly decreased" (p. 177, PDF 3): the women's left rises from L2 (10.22) to L3 (10.30) (Table 2). No sex difference at any level (p. 177, PDF 3): a t test on the printed female left L3 value would say otherwise (Inference).
  - Interfacet distance larger in men "from D12 to L5" (p. 178, PDF 4) against "at all levels" (p. 179, PDF 5); the printed values just miss significance at L4 and S1 (Inference).
  - Disc height "significantly larger in men at all levels" (p. 178, PDF 4): women larger at L3 (10.17 against 9.76), a 0.03 mm gap at L1, and a t test on the printed values is borderline at S1 (t about 1.97) and short of P < .05 at every other level (Inference) (Table 3).
  - Canal depth: the text calls it the AP diameter "of the spinal cord" when saying women's values were greater (p. 177, PDF 3; p. 179, PDF 5); the measure is the bony canal, the lumbar canal holds the cauda equina (the discussion also speaks of the spinal cord traversing the lumbar canal, p. 179, PDF 5), and women are not larger at L5.
  - The abstract says vertebral and canal dimensions were in general greater in men (p. 175, PDF 1); canal depth and foramen show no sex difference (p. 177, PDF 3).
  - The conclusions say vertebral width and depth progressively increased from D12 to S1 (p. 180, PDF 6); Table 3 shows UVD, LVW and LVD falling at S1 and LVD falling at L5 in men.
  - Singh's cadaveric LVW is described as increasing from L3 to L5 (p. 180, PDF 6), but Table 7 gives 50.9 at L4 and 50.6 at L5.
  - Right-left lateral recess differences called significant at all levels with differences of 0.03 to 0.20 mm (except women's L4) and no right-left test named in the methods (p. 176, PDF 2; p. 177, PDF 3).
  - Kumar et al is described as 61 adult patients in the text (p. 179, PDF 5) while Table 4 lists n = 61 men and n = 61 women.
  - The text's maximum lateral recess values truncate the table's: men's left 11.60 and women's right 9.46 mm (p. 177, PDF 3) against 11.609 and 9.469 in Table 2, which round to 11.61 and 9.47.
  - Body dimensions: the text first says men and women differ "in most of the dimensions" (p. 178, PDF 4), then that every body dimension is larger in men at all levels (pp. 178, 180, PDF 4, 6).
  - Earlier series: "small sample size" is listed as a limitation in the abstract (p. 175, PDF 1), "adequate sample sizes" credited to them in the introduction (p. 176, PDF 2).
  - Table 3's women's row at L3 is printed "F (n=128)" beside an n column that already says 128 (p. 178, PDF 4); a typesetting slip with no effect on the values.
  - Implausible SDs: female L3 disc height 6.87 (Table 3, p. 178, PDF 4); female left L3 foramen 3.43 and male right L1 lateral recess 0.68 (Table 2, p. 177, PDF 3); female L2 lower width 5.53 (Table 3).
  - The abstract claims statistically significant population differences in the canal; no comparison table carries a P value (Tables 4-8).
- The "circular to oval" canal shape claim rests on depth alone, with no width reported (p. 177, PDF 3).
- Within the paper's own comparison, Kumar et al's Telangana CT values (Table 4, p. 179, PDF 5) show the L4 floor and L5 rise of canal depth in men (13.8 to 15.4) but not in women (14.5 to 14.25), so the L5 rebound is not universal.
- Against other notes of this set (Inference): Panjabi 1992's dry-bone canal depth (17.5 to 19.7 mm, L1 to L5) shares this paper's L3-L4 trough and L5 rise but is 3 to 5 mm larger; Panjabi's end-plate depths are flat where these rise, and his lower end plate is wider than the upper at L5 where this paper's men show the reverse. Eisenstein 1977's caliper canal depth is the least midsagittal AP near the cranial end of the canal, and his lateral-radiograph recipe puts the posterior limit on the line joining the articular-process apices; both differ from this paper's pedicle-level CT line to the lamina, so absolute canal depths should not be pooled across these papers without matching definitions, even where the level shape agrees.
- Pointers in the reference list, not used in the text: a lumbar Torg ratio study of 420 postmortem subjects (ref. 13) and Kang et al's comparison of radiographic and CT body and pedicle measurements in Koreans (ref. 16) (p. 181, PDF 7); Inference: the second is a source for how CT body widths transfer to radiographs.
