## What the paper is

- Design: retrospective cross-sectional morphometry on archived clinical MRI, plus a compilation of earlier lower lumbar disc and end-plate data (Tables 5 to 7, Figs. 8 to 10). Aims: a standard measurement protocol for transverse-section area and diameters, the error of ellipse area estimates at each level, and a comparison with the literature. p. 4117 (PDF 2). The authors' own interest was spinal biomechanics and the ergonomic assessment of work-related low back pain, which is why they chose the lower lumbar spine (p. 4117, PDF 2); the work was funded by a NIOSH pilot training grant (p. 4130, PDF 15). They claim to be the first to report both linear and planar measures of both end-plates and the disc together (p. 4122, PDF 7).
- Population: T2-weighted lower lumbar (L3 to S1) MRI from the digital archive of the University Hospital of the University of Utah, Salt Lake City, USA; living adults aged 20 to 40; 109 subjects retrieved (55 women, 54 men). Height, weight, age and sex came embedded in the scans. Ethnicity not reported; the authors call the sample a starting point for the "general US population" (p. 4129, PDF 14). The clinical reason for each scan was unknown to the authors; scans were picked because the radiology diagnosis listed no specific low back disorder. IRB approval at Auburn University and the University of Utah. pp. 4117-4118 (PDF 2-3).
- Exclusions, at two levels. Per subject (scans reviewed and the subject excluded): evidence of morphological alteration of the lumbar or thoracic spine (crushed vertebral body, trauma); known pathology likely to change geometry (scoliosis, tumour). Per disc only (the subject's other discs kept): Pfirrmann grade V discs (collapsed disc space), every disc graded by a radiologist; and missing oblique slices of the L5/S1 end-plates. A subject with a grade V L3/L4 disc kept its L4/L5 and L5/S1 data. pp. 4117-4118 (PDF 2-3). No counts are given for the subject-level exclusions, so the 109 are the subjects left after them (Inference from the order of the text).
- Denominators: 327 IVDs and 654 EPs retrieved (109 subjects x 3 levels, two end-plates per disc). 9 male discs were grade V (2 at L3/L4, 4 at L4/L5, 3 at L5/S1) and excluded. 23 subjects (13 women, 10 men) had no oblique slices of the L5/S1 end-plates (23 CrEPs and 23 CaEPs), which were excluded. Final 318 IVDs and 590 EPs. p. 4117 (PDF 2). Per level (Table 4, p. 4120, PDF 5): IVDs L3/L4 107 (55 F, 52 M), L4/L5 105 (55 F, 50 M), L5/S1 106 (55 F, 51 M); EPs per end-plate type 107, 105 and 83 (42 F, 41 M) at the same levels. Inference from the counts: the two end-plates of each excluded grade V disc were excluded with it (EP counts equal disc counts at L3/L4 and L4/L5, and 107 + 105 + 83 = 295 pairs = 590 EPs).
- Demographics (Table 2, p. 4119, PDF 4): women age 30.1 +/- 5.8 y (n 55), height 165.72 +/- 9.46 cm (n 41; heights missing from some scans), weight 74.65 +/- 19.71 kg (n 55), BMI 26.68 +/- 7.20 (n 41). Men in the L3/L4 set: age 30.5 +/- 5.3 (n 52), height 178.33 +/- 9.32 (n 38), weight 87.12 +/- 20.89 (n 52), BMI 27.36 +/- 5.53 (n 36); L4/L5 men 30.0 y, 178.00 cm (n 37), 86.71 kg (n 50), BMI 27.11 (n 35); L5/S1 men 30.6 y, 177.58 cm (n 35), 86.70 kg (n 51), BMI 27.27 (n 33). The text says men were taller and heavier (P < 0.001); Table 2 prints P 0.000 for height but 0.002 to 0.003 for weight; age (P 0.70, 0.95, 0.67) and BMI (P 0.65, 0.78, 0.70) did not differ.
- Scanner: Siemens MAGNETOM Avanto 1.5 T, head-first supine, sagittal and transverse T2; TR 3000 to 4770 ms, TE 80 to 110 ms, slice thickness 3 to 4.5 mm. In-plane pixel size, matrix and field of view are not given. p. 4117 (PDF 2).
- Measurement: OsiriX 4.1.1 (32-bit); landmarks identified by hand and traced on a workstation; measurements taken "only in oblique slices to minimize distortion". p. 4118 (PDF 3). Inference: the oblique slices are transverse slices angled parallel to each disc.
- Structures: at each level, the IVD and its two adjacent end-plates, the cranial end-plate (CrEP) and the caudal end-plate (CaEP). p. 4118 (PDF 3). Inference: at level Lx/Ly the CrEP is the inferior end-plate of Lx and the CaEP is the superior end-plate of Ly; the L5/S1 CaEP is the S1 superior end-plate. This reading agrees with the paper's statements about vertebrae (pp. 4119, 4123) and with the Table 4 means (see Level by level).
- Ellipse area estimates: formula (1) APD x FD x pi/4, attributed to Panjabi [27]; formula (2) MSD x FD x pi/4, attributed to Hutton and Adams [39]. p. 4118 (PDF 3). The introduction traces the sagittal x frontal x pi/4 method to Farfan [21], used by [20, 39], and says Seidel [29] showed it introduces systematic error (p. 4117, PDF 2).
- Statistics: split-plot factorial ANOVA (sex 2 x level 3, with interaction); independent t tests between sexes; paired t tests between end-plates of the same IVD and of the same vertebra; Tukey HSD across levels; alpha 0.05; SPSS 19. p. 4118 (PDF 3).
- Level identification and counting: not described. Nothing says how L5 or S1 were recognised, whether counting ran from the sacrum or from above, or whether transitional segments were screened.
- The Tang [31] rows of Tables 5 to 7 are this same data set: their means and SDs equal Table 4's, and ref. 31 is the first author's 2013 Auburn PhD dissertation, whose title also names "regression models" (p. 4130, PDF 15).
- The literature compilation: Tables 5 (disc), 6 (cranial end-plate) and 7 (caudal end-plate), pp. 4124-4128 (PDF 9-13), each giving per study the subjects, age, height, weight, the method in brackets and mean (SD) by level; Figs. 8 (CSA), 9 (APD/MSD) and 10 (FD), pp. 4128-4129 (PDF 13-14), plot them, cadaver series in red and in vivo in blue. Detail in the last subsection of Measures.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib count or block vertebrae, and nothing about how levels were numbered.
- The only trace of transitional anatomy is a reference title: Mahato 2011 (ref. 25, "spines with L5-S1 transitions", p. 4130, PDF 15), cited on p. 4117 (PDF 2) only as one of the CT morphometry studies; the paper draws nothing from it.
- Pfirrmann grade V (collapsed) discs: 9 of 327 discs (2.8 percent), all in men: 2 of 54 at L3/L4, 4 of 54 at L4/L5, 3 of 54 at L5/S1 (9 of 162 male discs, 5.6 percent); none reported in women (0 of 165 female discs). Living US clinical MRI, ages 20 to 40, scans chosen because the report named no specific low back disorder, so this is not a population prevalence. The paper does not say how many men these 9 discs came from. p. 4117 (PDF 2).
- The distribution of Pfirrmann grades I to IV among the kept discs is not reported, and no analysis by grade was made.
- Subjects excluded without counts: crushed vertebral body, trauma, scoliosis, tumour. p. 4117 (PDF 2). The limitations section adds that scans were scrutinised to drop obvious spinal abnormalities, pathology and severe disc degeneration (p. 4128, PDF 13).
- Inference: with no transitional screening, a lumbarised S1 or a sacralised L5 would sit in the L5/S1 row unflagged; the L5/S1 values may carry some LSTV contamination that the paper cannot measure.

## Measures, each as a recipe

Frame for all four measures: one transverse (oblique, disc-parallel) T2 MRI slice per structure; the IVD and the end-plate above and below it. Table 1 p. 4119 (PDF 4); Fig. 1 p. 4118 (PDF 3) draws the traced outline (CSA), a bounding rectangle whose AP side is the APD and whose lateral side is the FD, and a vertical mid-line chord (MSD), for an oval (a) and a kidney-shaped (b) section. All values below are mean +/- SD from Table 4 (p. 4120, PDF 5), levels in the order L3/L4, L4/L5, L5/S1. Within every level the disc is the largest of the three structures in all four measures, and the CaEP exceeds the CrEP in CSA, APD and FD (Table 4 All rows; Figs. 2 to 5, pp. 4121-4122); the one tie is the male APD at L4/L5 (CrEP and CaEP both 3.76 cm). MSD of the two end-plates is nearly equal (3.24 vs 3.27, 3.31 vs 3.31, 3.48 vs 3.52). The paper measures nothing else: no disc height, body height, canal, pedicle, facet or wedge angle.

### Cross-sectional area (CSA)

- Definition: area inside the traced actual periphery of the IVD or end-plate on the transverse slice (Table 1, p. 4119).
- Instrument: hand tracing in OsiriX on 1.5 T T2 MRI, slice 3 to 4.5 mm.
- Values (cm2):
  - CrEP: F 12.59 +/- 1.68, 12.49 +/- 1.53, 11.71 +/- 1.50; M 15.19 +/- 1.73, 15.13 +/- 1.74, 14.47 +/- 2.00; all 13.85 +/- 2.14, 13.75 +/- 2.10, 13.08 +/- 2.23 (ranges all 9.77-19.14, 9.90-19.88, 9.25-20.45).
  - IVD: F 14.76 +/- 1.90, 14.63 +/- 1.80, 13.52 +/- 1.91; M 17.71 +/- 1.96, 18.08 +/- 2.02, 17.06 +/- 2.34; all 16.19 +/- 2.43, 16.27 +/- 2.57, 15.23 +/- 2.76 (ranges 11.48-22.51, 10.63-22.80, 10.21-22.98).
  - CaEP: F 14.04 +/- 1.75, 13.95 +/- 1.56, 13.35 +/- 1.78; M 16.29 +/- 1.86, 16.47 +/- 1.95, 15.93 +/- 2.25; all 15.13 +/- 2.12, 15.15 +/- 2.16, 14.63 +/- 2.39 (ranges 10.36-20.00, 10.46-21.04, 10.21-22.46).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Build the vertebra's local frame (superior-inferior axis normal to a plane fitted to the end-plate; AP axis from the body centroid toward the canal or spinous process, projected into that plane; left-right as their cross product). Take the body subregion voxels in a slab 3 to 4.5 mm thick at the end-plate surface (to mimic one MRI slice), project them onto the end-plate plane, fill holes, and take the area of the outer outline, cortical rim included. Inferior end-plate of the upper vertebra = CrEP; superior end-plate of the lower vertebra = CaEP. For the IVD use the disc label where the segmenter gives disc instances, in a mid-height slab, or the space between the two end-plates.
- From a radiograph alone: not measurable directly. Inference from the paper's error figures: lateral depth x AP width x pi/4 comes within about 2 to 3 percent of the traced MRI area when the depth is the APD (pp. 4119, 4122), but the area carries the square of the radiographic magnification.
- Across levels: constant from L3/L4 to L4/L5, then smaller at L5/S1 by 7 percent (IVD), 6 percent (CrEP) and 5 percent (CaEP), P < 0.05 (p. 4119, PDF 4). The authors say the smallest L5/S1 disc area corroborates earlier studies [20, 29, 32, 34] (p. 4126, PDF 11). Within every level the pattern is CrEP < IVD > CaEP (Fig. 2, p. 4121, PDF 6). The IVD exceeds the CrEP by 18 percent in men and 17 percent in women, and the CaEP by 9 percent in men and 4 percent in women (men P < 0.001, women P < 0.01). The CaEP exceeds the CrEP by 12 percent in women and 9 percent in men (both P < 0.001). p. 4119 (PDF 4). Inference: L5/S1 is separated from the two upper levels only weakly (differences of about 1 cm2 against a between-subject SD of 2 to 2.8 cm2); L3/L4 is not separated from L4/L5. Inference: the pooled Table 4 means give L5/S1 decreases of about 6, 5 and 3 percent, a little smaller than the printed 7, 6 and 5 percent.
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger by 20 percent (L3/L4), 24 percent (L4/L5) and 26 percent (L5/S1) for the IVD, all P < 0.001; about 18 percent on average for the end-plates (P < 0.001); sex x level interaction P = 0.088 (p. 4119). Sex is confounded with height and weight, which the paper did not adjust for (pp. 4123, 4126, PDF 8, 11). Older cadaver series report larger areas than younger ones, which the authors tie to ageing, for example osteoporosis (p. 4128, PDF 13). Population differences come only from the compiled studies (see bordercall (d)).
- Range, all subjects (Table 4): CrEP 9.77-19.14, 9.90-19.88, 9.25-20.45; IVD 11.48-22.51, 10.63-22.80, 10.21-22.98; CaEP 10.36-20.00, 10.46-21.04, 10.21-22.46 cm2: about a twofold spread at every level, which the authors call substantial (p. 4126, PDF 11).
- Error: ICC(3,1) intra-observer 0.996 (observer I) and 0.990 (observer II), inter-observer 0.971; Pearson 0.997, 0.996 and 0.978 (Table 3, p. 4119, PDF 4).

### Antero-posterior diameter (APD)

- Definition: distance between the anterior-most and posterior-most extents of the transverse contour (Table 1, p. 4119). For a kidney-shaped section the APD is the AP side of the bounding rectangle, reaching the posterolateral lobes, not the midline (Fig. 1b, p. 4118; the caption asks the reader to note the different APD for oval and kidney shapes).
- Values (cm):
  - CrEP: F 3.43 +/- 0.22, 3.41 +/- 0.21, 3.32 +/- 0.19; M 3.77 +/- 0.22, 3.76 +/- 0.25, 3.72 +/- 0.27; all 3.59 +/- 0.28, 3.58 +/- 0.29, 3.52 +/- 0.31.
  - IVD: F 3.70 +/- 0.26, 3.68 +/- 0.23, 3.53 +/- 0.23; M 4.03 +/- 0.24, 4.06 +/- 0.26, 4.01 +/- 0.32; all 3.86 +/- 0.30, 3.86 +/- 0.31, 3.76 +/- 0.36.
  - CaEP: F 3.61 +/- 0.23, 3.50 +/- 0.21, 3.41 +/- 0.24; M 3.84 +/- 0.23, 3.76 +/- 0.23, 3.74 +/- 0.31; all 3.72 +/- 0.26, 3.63 +/- 0.25, 3.57 +/- 0.32.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the same end-plate (or disc) outline as for CSA, the extent along the local AP axis: the bounding-box depth, not the midline chord. Fig. 1 draws the box on the image axes; a mask should use the vertebra's own axes so that a rotated patient does not inflate it.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. A lateral radiograph shows the end-plate as a line from the anterior to the posterior corner; the posterior vertebral line is formed by the posterior-most points, which in a kidney-shaped body are the posterolateral lobes, so lateral end-plate depth approximates APD rather than MSD. It is magnified; the paper warns that radiographs are prone to magnification error and give larger dimensions on lateral films (p. 4126, PDF 11, citing [18, 54, 64]).
- Across levels: APD_IVD depends on sex (P < 0.001), level (P < 0.001) and their interaction (P = 0.017) (p. 4122, PDF 7; but see Gaps on Fig. 6). APD_CrEP constant from L3/L4 to L4/L5 and 2 percent smaller at L5/S1 (P < 0.05). APD_CaEP has a sex x level interaction (P = 0.044, Fig. 7, p. 4123, PDF 8): in women it falls 3.61, 3.50, 3.41, in men 3.84, 3.76, 3.74. APD_IVD (all) is 3.86 at both upper levels and 3.76 at L5/S1. So APD is flat or falls slightly from L3/L4 to L5/S1 for every structure, the opposite of MSD.
- Sex, age, size, population: APD_CrEP about 11 percent larger in men at each level (P < 0.001) (p. 4122). Inference from Table 4: APD_IVD 9 to 14 percent and APD_CaEP 6 to 10 percent larger in men, the gap widest at L5/S1. Age, height and weight not analysed; one population (see CSA).
- Range, all subjects (Table 4): CrEP 2.98-4.33, 3.07-4.57, 2.90-4.61; IVD 3.12-4.61, 3.21-4.68, 3.01-4.76; CaEP 3.12-4.41, 3.12-4.47, 2.95-4.64 cm.
- Error: ICC intra 0.993 and 0.984, inter 0.970; Pearson 0.994, 0.988, 0.971 (Table 3, p. 4119).

### Frontal diameter (FD)

- Definition: distance between the right and left lateral-most extents of the transverse contour (Table 1, p. 4119).
- Values (cm):
  - CrEP: F 4.54 +/- 0.33, 4.58 +/- 0.35, 4.61 +/- 0.33; M 5.03 +/- 0.35, 5.03 +/- 0.32, 5.05 +/- 0.39; all 4.78 +/- 0.41, 4.79 +/- 0.41, 4.83 +/- 0.42.
  - IVD: F 4.97 +/- 0.32, 5.03 +/- 0.32, 4.93 +/- 0.39; M 5.48 +/- 0.36, 5.59 +/- 0.32, 5.50 +/- 0.40; all 5.22 +/- 0.42, 5.29 +/- 0.43, 5.21 +/- 0.49.
  - CaEP: F 4.85 +/- 0.32, 5.01 +/- 0.31, 4.96 +/- 0.39; M 5.26 +/- 0.33, 5.44 +/- 0.36, 5.42 +/- 0.41; all 5.05 +/- 0.38, 5.21 +/- 0.40, 5.19 +/- 0.46.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Extent of the end-plate (or disc) outline along the local left-right axis.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. The AP radiograph gives end-plate width directly (the lateral margins of the end-plate), magnified by the radiographic factor; the paper gives no factor. Lordotic tilt of the end-plate against the beam foreshortens its depth on the AP view but not its width, so FD is the AP film's robust end-plate measure. Kang [23] (ref. title, p. 4130, PDF 15) compared radiographic and CT body transverse diameters in Koreans and is the paper to read for that conversion.
- Across levels: FD_IVD +2 percent from L3/L4 to L4/L5 and -2 percent from L4/L5 to L5/S1 (P < 0.05). FD_CrEP no level difference. FD_CaEP +3 percent from L3/L4 to L4/L5 (P < 0.05), then constant to L5/S1. p. 4122 (PDF 7). Inference: width barely separates the three levels; the one step most series share is the CaEP widening from L3/L4 to L4/L5 (the L5 superior end-plate wider than the L4 superior end-plate; see the compiled literature below).
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger by about 11 percent (IVD), 10 percent (CrEP) and 9 percent (CaEP), all P < 0.001 (p. 4122). Age, height and weight not analysed; one population (see CSA).
- Range, all subjects (Table 4): CrEP 3.95-5.80, 3.91-5.63, 3.82-5.82; IVD 4.39-6.22, 4.22-6.24, 4.13-6.22; CaEP 4.10-5.90, 4.28-6.21, 4.19-6.31 cm.
- Error: ICC intra 0.984 and 0.984, inter 0.953; Pearson 0.986, 0.988, 0.955 (Table 3, p. 4119).

### Mid-sagittal diameter (MSD)

- Definition: distance between the mid-sagittal extents of the contour (Table 1, p. 4119): the AP chord along the midline, which for a kidney-shaped section ends at the bottom of the posterior notch (Fig. 1b, p. 4118). The paper does not say how the midline was set.
- Values (cm):
  - CrEP: F 3.01 +/- 0.21, 3.09 +/- 0.19, 3.26 +/- 0.20; M 3.49 +/- 0.21, 3.55 +/- 0.23, 3.71 +/- 0.22; all 3.24 +/- 0.32, 3.31 +/- 0.31, 3.48 +/- 0.31.
  - IVD: F 3.22 +/- 0.24, 3.30 +/- 0.22, 3.46 +/- 0.26; M 3.73 +/- 0.21, 3.80 +/- 0.26, 4.00 +/- 0.32; all 3.47 +/- 0.34, 3.54 +/- 0.34, 3.72 +/- 0.40.
  - CaEP: F 3.08 +/- 0.23, 3.11 +/- 0.18, 3.32 +/- 0.20; M 3.47 +/- 0.22, 3.52 +/- 0.24, 3.73 +/- 0.24; all 3.27 +/- 0.30, 3.31 +/- 0.29, 3.52 +/- 0.32.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Find the mid-sagittal line as the line through the midpoint of the FD (or through the spinous process and canal centre) parallel to the AP axis, and take the length of the outline along it.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Not available from a lateral film, where the midline notch is hidden behind the posterolateral lobes; an axial image is needed.
- Across levels: rises from L3/L4 to L5/S1 for every structure, largest at L5/S1 (Fig. 5, p. 4122, PDF 7). The paper gives no ANOVA or post hoc result for MSD.
- Inference, from the Table 4 means: MSD/APD is about 0.88 to 0.90 at L3/L4, 0.91 to 0.93 at L4/L5 and 0.99 at L5/S1, so the posterior notch (APD - MSD) is about 0.35 to 0.45 cm at L3/L4, 0.27 to 0.32 cm at L4/L5 and 0.04 to 0.05 cm at L5/S1: the L5/S1 disc, the L5 inferior end-plate and the S1 superior end-plate are nearly oval, the levels above kidney-shaped. For the IVD the ratio is 0.87, 0.90, 0.98 in women and 0.93, 0.94, 1.00 in men, so women have the deeper notch. This is a ratio of means, not a mean of ratios; the per-subject spread is not given.
- Sex, age, size, population: no test reported. Inference from Table 4: men larger by about 12 to 16 percent, more than for APD (6 to 14 percent).
- Range, all subjects (Table 4): CrEP 2.64-4.09, 2.72-4.27, 2.85-4.25; IVD 2.77-4.23, 2.84-4.43, 2.83-4.70; CaEP 2.60-4.00, 2.77-4.22, 2.82-4.43 cm.
- Error: the least reliable measure: ICC intra 0.914 and 0.906, inter 0.938; Pearson 0.904, 0.909, 0.940 (Table 3, p. 4119).

### Ellipse-estimated area (derived from the diameters)

- Definition: APD x FD x pi/4 (formula 1) or MSD x FD x pi/4 (formula 2), compared with the traced CSA. p. 4118 (PDF 3).
- Results (p. 4119, PDF 4, continuing on p. 4122, PDF 7): with APD, the estimate is 2 percent low for IVD, CrEP and CaEP at L3/L4 and L4/L5 (P < 0.01) and 3 percent high for IVD and CrEP at L5/S1 (P < 0.001). With MSD, it is 10 percent low for all three at L3/L4 and L4/L5 (P < 0.001) and 2 percent high for CrEP (P = 0.005) and IVD (P = 0.004) at L5/S1. Mean absolute error 2 percent with APD, 9 percent with MSD. No result is given for the L5/S1 CaEP. Seidel [29] had reported an overall 9 percent underestimate (p. 4126, PDF 11). The authors stress that the error depends on the choice of minor axis (APD vs MSD) when existing data are compared [20, 21, 27, 39] (p. 4126).
- Why: Inference. The kidney shape makes MSD short at L3/L4 and L4/L5; at L5/S1 the section is oval, so both diameters give near-ellipse areas.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L3/L4 and L4/L5: no difference in CSA (IVD, CrEP, CaEP) or APD_CrEP between them (p. 4119, p. 4122). FD_IVD and FD_CaEP are 2 to 3 percent larger at L4/L5. The largest male disc area and width are at L4/L5 (18.08 cm2, 5.59 cm). Both levels have kidney-shaped sections (APD clearly above MSD; Inference from Table 4).
- L5/S1 (the lumbosacral junction): smallest IVD and end-plate areas (-5 to -7 percent), smallest APD_CrEP, largest MSD, sections nearly oval (Inference from Table 4). The authors tie the small L5/S1 load-bearing area to its theoretically highest load [62] and to its being the commonest site of pathology causing low back pain [63] (p. 4126, PDF 11). 23 of 109 subjects lacked oblique slices of the L5/S1 end-plates (p. 4117); Inference: the steep lumbosacral angle may leave the L5/S1 end-plates out of an axial stack angled for the upper discs, but the paper gives no reason. 3 of 54 men had a grade V L5/S1 disc.
- L4 vertebra: its superior end-plate (L3/L4 CaEP) is larger than its inferior end-plate (L4/L5 CrEP) in women (11 percent, P < 0.001); in men the difference is not significant (P = 0.090). pp. 4119, 4123. Inference: Table 4's male means still differ by 7 percent (16.29 vs 15.13 cm2).
- L5 vertebra: its superior end-plate (L4/L5 CaEP) is larger than its inferior end-plate (L5/S1 CrEP) in both sexes (P < 0.001; 12 percent on average, p. 4123). pp. 4119, 4123 (PDF 4, 8). Inference from Table 4 means: the inferior L5 end-plate is the smallest end-plate measured (13.08 cm2 all), and its width (4.83) is less than the L5 superior end-plate width (5.21).
- Other studies on the same vertebra comparison (p. 4123, PDF 8): Seidel [29], cadavers of unknown sex, found the same for L3 and L5 but not L4; Wang [34] showed a similar trend; Brinckmann [1] and Panjabi [27] showed a slight increase for L4, the direction not stated (Inference from Tables 6 and 7: Panjabi's L4 inferior end-plate 12.73 vs superior 12.39 cm2, Brinckmann's women 15.21 vs 14.40, men 18.16 vs 19.83); none of them tested it statistically.
- S1 superior end-plate (L5/S1 CaEP): 14.63 +/- 2.39 cm2, FD 5.19 +/- 0.46, APD 3.57 +/- 0.32, MSD 3.52 +/- 0.32 (all; Table 4). Table 7 (p. 4127, PDF 12) shows no other study with L5/S1 caudal end-plate data, and the authors note that the earlier studies giving both areas and diameters, Panjabi [27] and Wang [34], left it out (p. 4128, PDF 13).
- Craniocaudal asymmetry: the end-plate cranial to a disc is more often concave, the caudal one flatter in the lower lumbar spine [19, 34, 56, 57]; the asymmetry may follow disc degeneration [57] more than sex [56, 57] or age [19, 56]. p. 4123 (PDF 8).
- Nothing on C2/C3, C7/T1, T12/L1 or any level above L3.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Protocol: two observers measured independently a random 40 scans (20 women, 20 men), each twice, one month apart, in random order; ICC(3,1) and Pearson's r; the published values are the first observer's first round, the rest used only for reliability. p. 4118 (PDF 3).
- Table 3 (p. 4119, PDF 4), ICC / Pearson:
  - CSA: observer I 0.996 / 0.997; observer II 0.990 / 0.996; between observers 0.971 / 0.978.
  - FD: 0.984 / 0.986; 0.984 / 0.988; 0.953 / 0.955.
  - APD: 0.993 / 0.994; 0.984 / 0.988; 0.970 / 0.971.
  - MSD: 0.914 / 0.904; 0.906 / 0.909; 0.938 / 0.940.
- All above 0.90, called excellent (p. 4119); the authors credit explicit definitions and illustrated landmarks (p. 4122).
- Not given: standard error of measurement, absolute error in mm, Bland-Altman limits, pixel size, reliability per level or per structure.
- Inference (SEM = SD x sqrt(1 - ICC), with the pooled between-subject SDs of Table 4): between observers about 0.05 cm for APD, 0.09 cm for FD, 0.08 cm for MSD and 0.4 cm2 for CSA; within observer I about 0.02 cm (APD), 0.05 cm (FD), 0.10 cm (MSD), 0.15 cm2 (CSA). The 40-scan reliability subset's own SD is not reported, so these are rough.
- Systematic error of ellipse estimates: see the ellipse subsection (2 percent with APD, 9 to 10 percent with MSD).

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication: total disc arthroplasty, now a common procedure for discogenic pathology [11, 12]; variability of lower lumbar geometry is tied to its complications, and implant footprints sized to the end-plate improve prosthesis-vertebra contact and load distribution; female and L5/S1 data were lacking before this study. pp. 4116, 4117, 4122 (PDF 1, 2, 7).
- Finite element models need disc and end-plate CSAs and should carry their natural variability [6-10]. p. 4117 (PDF 2).
- End-plate area and bone mineral density together set a segment's bone mineral content, which explains much of the variance in ultimate compressive strength [1]. pp. 4116-4117 (PDF 1-2).
- The end-plate cranial to the disc (inferior end-plate of the upper vertebra) usually has higher BMD [58] and is thought stronger [34]; more vertebral fractures involve the caudal one (the superior end-plate of the lower vertebra) [1, 59, 60]. The authors speculate the concavity of the cranial end-plate may relate to its smaller load-bearing area. p. 4123 (PDF 8). Inference: ref. 60's title calls the fractured one the "cranial endplate" in the vertebra's own frame, the same naming flip as in Tang's vertebra statements.
- A small lower lumbar disc may be more vulnerable to degeneration and discogenic pain; the authors suggest diet and exercise to keep bone density in people with small spinal geometry (p. 4126, PDF 11).
- Pitfall: an ellipse from mid-sagittal depth under-reads true area by about 10 percent at L3/L4 and L4/L5; use the APD (bounding depth) if an ellipse is needed (pp. 4119, 4122, 4126).
- Pitfall: cadaver geometry may change after death (water loss [35, 54]) and with preservation (freezing [41], injected fixative [55]). p. 4126 (PDF 11).
- Pitfall: radiographs are prone to magnification error [54, 64], which may give larger dimensions on lateral films [18]. p. 4126. In Table 6 the X-ray depth values of Aydinlioglu (4.30 to 4.88 cm) and Amonoo-Kuofi (3.84 to 4.31) are at or above the top of the CT, MRI and cadaver values (2.51 to 3.86); the X-ray series of Nissan and Gilad (3.39 to 3.49) is not. p. 4125 (PDF 10). The authors put such extreme values down to small samples [21] and magnification [17, 18] (p. 4128, PDF 13); Inference: the two high series may also be disc, not end-plate, depths (see Gaps).
- Pitfall: Brinckmann [1] measured end-plate area after an ultimate compressive strength test, which may have changed it. p. 4128 (PDF 13).
- Pitfall: small samples and missing heights and weights in earlier studies prevent comparison; sex differences here are confounded with height and weight. pp. 4123, 4126, 4128.
- Pitfall: this sample came from a hospital database and may not represent a healthy population; disc degeneration correlates with end-plate changes [66-68]. Supine, minimally loaded MRI; transverse geometry may change when standing [69, 70]. pp. 4128-4129 (PDF 13-14). The authors ask for larger samples varying in age, weight, height and degeneration (p. 4130, PDF 15).
- Pitfall: studies differ in which depth they report (APD, MSD or a caliper depth), so tables mixing them (Tables 6 and 7 have one "APD/MSD" column) are not like for like (p. 4126 on the minor-axis choice; the method labels in Tables 6 and 7).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Name end-plates by vertebra, not by disc: Tang's L3/L4 CrEP is the inferior end-plate of L3 and the CaEP is the superior end-plate of L4; L5/S1 CaEP is the S1 superior end-plate. When the text speaks of a vertebra's CrEP, it means its superior end-plate. (p. 4118, PDF 3; pp. 4119, 4123; the mapping is Inference checked against Table 4.)
2. On an axial (disc-parallel) CT or MRI section, or a 3D mask resliced parallel to the end-plate, measure four things per end-plate and disc: traced area, AP bounding depth (APD), left-right width (FD) and midline depth (MSD). Keep APD and MSD apart. (Table 1, Fig. 1, pp. 4118-4119.)
3. Use the posterior notch as a soft cue to tell L5/S1 from the levels above: expect MSD/APD near 0.99 at the L5/S1 disc, L5 inferior and S1 superior end-plates, and about 0.88 to 0.93 at L3/L4 and L4/L5 (Inference from Table 4 means, p. 4120). The paper gives no per-subject ratio spread, and the notch (0.3 to 0.45 cm) is about one between-subject SD of either diameter, so never use it alone.
4. Expect the L5/S1 disc and both its end-plates to be 5 to 7 percent smaller in area than at L4/L5 and L3/L4, and L3/L4 and L4/L5 to be indistinguishable by area; do not name L3/L4 versus L4/L5 by size alone. (p. 4119, PDF 4; L5/S1 smallest also in Panjabi, Tan, Seidel and Wang, Table 6, p. 4125.)
5. Within one vertebra on MRI, expect a superior end-plate larger than the inferior one at L5 in both sexes and at L4 in women (pp. 4119, 4123), and at each disc the end-plate below it larger than the one above (p. 4119). Do not rely on the direction: the compiled series disagree (Inference from Tables 6 and 7, pp. 4125-4128, same disc compared): Brinckmann's caudal end-plates are larger in area, as in Tang; Panjabi's and Tan's cranial ones are larger in area and width and Zhou's in depth and width; Seidel, Wang, Berry and van der Houwen are mixed. Seidel [29] did not find Tang's L4 difference, and Brinckmann [1] and Panjabi [27] showed only "a slight increase for the L4 vertebra" (p. 4123); read from Tables 6 and 7, Panjabi's L4 inferior end-plate is the larger (12.73 vs 12.39 cm2; Inference). Check the modality and the definition before using it as a cue.
6. Expect the disc's transverse area to exceed the adjacent end-plates by 4 to 18 percent, most over the end-plate above the disc (p. 4119). Inference: a disc mask much smaller than its end-plates suggests a segmentation error or a collapsed disc.
7. Adjust every size expectation for sex before comparing: men about 9 to 11 percent larger in FD and APD_CrEP as the paper states (p. 4122), 6 to 16 percent across all diameters by Table 4 (Inference), and larger in areas by 20 to 26 percent for the disc and about 18 percent on average for the end-plates (p. 4119; 16 to 24 percent level by level from Table 4, Inference); prefer within-patient ratios between neighbouring levels to absolute sizes.
8. On a lateral radiograph, read end-plate depth as an APD-like (bounding) depth and correct for magnification before comparing with these MRI values; on an AP radiograph read end-plate width as FD. Never read a midline depth (MSD) from a lateral film. (Inference; p. 4126 on magnification.)
9. If you estimate end-plate area from two diameters, use APD x FD x pi/4 (error about 2 to 3 percent), never MSD x FD x pi/4 (about 10 percent low at L3/L4 and L4/L5). (pp. 4119, 4122.)
10. Before comparing any depth with a literature value, find out which depth it is (bounding, mid-sagittal or caliper) and how it was imaged: the level trend toward L5/S1 changes sign between definitions and series (Tables 6, 7, pp. 4125-4127; Inference), and X-ray series without magnification correction run up to about 1 cm deeper (p. 4125).
11. Inference: a collapsed (Pfirrmann V) disc space can occur even at age 20 to 40 (9 of 327 discs here, all men; p. 4117), so do not read an absent disc gap as a block vertebra or a missing level without checking the end-plates.
12. Do not take the L5/S1 values as LSTV-free: the paper did not screen for transitional vertebrae (whole paper; Methods pp. 4117-4118). Before using them as a reference, check that the patient's L5/S1 is a true lumbosacral junction.
13. Expect the L5/S1 end-plates to be missing from a routine axial MRI stack in about one subject in five (23 of 109, p. 4117). Inference: reslice the 3D volume parallel to the L5/S1 disc rather than reading that level off a stack angled for the discs above.

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder
- None. The paper gives no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13, rib anomalies or block vertebrae, and does not say how it numbered levels.
- Only related figure: collapsed (Pfirrmann V) disc 9 of 327 discs (2.8 percent), all men (2/54 L3/L4, 4/54 L4/L5, 3/54 L5/S1; 9 of 162 male discs), 0 of 165 female discs, US clinical MRI chosen for no specific low back diagnosis, ages 20 to 40 (p. 4117). Could inform a "disc gap absent" emission, not a numbering transition.

(b) Measures the generator holds or could add, and definition biases
- Inference mapping to Panjabi's end-plate measures: FD ~ end-plate width (EPW), APD or MSD ~ end-plate depth (EPD), CSA ~ end-plate area (EPA); L3/L4 CrEP -> L3 lower (l), L3/L4 CaEP -> L4 upper (u), L4/L5 CrEP -> L4 lower, L4/L5 CaEP -> L5 upper, L5/S1 CrEP -> L5 lower, L5/S1 CaEP -> S1 upper (no Panjabi counterpart).
- Could add: S1 superior end-plate (14.63 cm2, FD 5.19, APD 3.57, MSD 3.52); disc transverse area, depth and width at L3/L4 to L5/S1; the MSD/APD notch ratio as a shape feature; per-sex means and SDs for all of them (Table 4).
- Bias 1, depth definition: the paper attributes the APD-based ellipse to Panjabi [27] (p. 4118) and lists Panjabi in the "APD/MSD" column (Tables 6, 7). Whether Panjabi's EPD is a bounding depth or a midline chord matters by 0.3 to 0.45 cm at L3/L4 and L4/L5 (Inference: check Panjabi 1992's own landmark text before matching a mask value). Inference: Panjabi's areas are 7 to 22 percent below Tang's (Bias 4), the size of the MSD-based ellipse deficit (10 percent at L3/L4 and L4/L5) plus age and cadaver effects, which would fit a midline depth; this is a guess to test, not a finding.
- Bias 2, area definition: Panjabi's area is an ellipse estimate (Tables 6, 7 label it ellipsoid; Fig. 8 legend), and an ellipse and a traced mask area differ by about 2 percent (APD-based) or 10 percent (MSD-based) at L3/L4 and L4/L5, and by 2 to 3 percent the other way at L5/S1 (pp. 4119, 4122). A mask should compute the area the generator's definition uses, or both.
- Bias 3, upper versus lower end-plate: as compiled here, Panjabi's lower end-plates are wider than the upper ones (FD 4.80, 4.95, 4.94 for L3, L4, L5 lower vs 4.66, 4.73 for L4, L5 upper; Tables 6 and 7, pp. 4126-4127), while Tang's MRI shows the superior end-plates wider and larger (CaEP FD 5.05, 5.21, 5.19 vs CrEP 4.78, 4.79, 4.83). The other series in Tables 6 and 7 do not agree on the direction (see instruction 5). Inference: the slab chosen to represent the end-plate (rim versus concave centre) may drive this; a mask slab at the rim versus at the deepest concavity will reproduce one or the other.
- Bias 4, size: against Panjabi's 12 cadavers (aged 46.3), Tang's cranial end-plates are about as wide or narrower (FD 4.78 vs 4.80, 4.79 vs 4.95, 4.83 vs 4.94) but deeper (APD 3.59, 3.58, 3.52 vs 3.48, 3.39, 3.32); Tang's caudal end-plates are wider (5.05, 5.21 vs 4.66, 4.73) and deeper (3.72, 3.63 vs 3.55, 3.47); Tang's areas are larger by about 7 to 22 percent (cranial 13.85, 13.75, 13.08 vs 12.90, 12.73, 12.18; caudal 15.13, 15.15 vs 12.39, 12.37) (Tables 6, 7). Tang's subjects are younger (30 y) and living.
- Bias 5, slice thickness: one 3 to 4.5 mm slice averages a slab; a CT mask slab of the same thickness at the end-plate surface is the closer match (Inference).

(c) Measurement error to use
- Inter-observer ICC: CSA 0.971, FD 0.953, APD 0.970, MSD 0.938; intra-observer up to 0.996 (Table 3, p. 4119).
- Inference SEMs: APD 0.05 cm, FD 0.09 cm, MSD 0.08 to 0.10 cm, CSA 0.4 cm2 between observers. Add the ellipse definition error (2 or 10 percent) when values from different definitions are mixed.
- Between-subject SD per level and sex (Table 4) is the natural likelihood width: about 0.2 to 0.4 cm for diameters within a sex, 1.5 to 2.3 cm2 for areas.
- Inference: the SDs printed for Tan [30] (0.02 to 0.06 cm) and Panjabi [27] (0.09 to 0.14 cm, 0.52 to 0.64 cm2) are far below every other series and should not be used as population spreads (see Gaps). Multiplied by sqrt(12) = 3.46 for Panjabi's 12 specimens they become 0.31 to 0.48 cm and 1.8 to 2.2 cm2, the size of every other series' SD (Tang's FD SDs are 0.38 to 0.49 cm), which fits a standard error printed as an SD (Inference; from memory, Panjabi's morphometry papers report standard errors). If the generator took Panjabi's spreads from Panjabi's own tables, check which they are.

(d) Size, sex and population covariates
- Sex: FD and APD_CrEP 9 to 11 percent larger in men (as the paper states), all diameters 6 to 16 percent (Inference from Table 4, MSD the largest gap); areas 16 to 26 percent larger (disc 20, 24, 26 percent, widening caudally; end-plates about 18 percent on average as stated, 16 to 24 percent level by level from Table 4). Previous studies found the same sex effect on disc area [38, 39, 41], end-plate area [14] and diameters [19, 33, 35] (p. 4123, PDF 8). Men 178 cm vs women 166 cm, so the sex gap exceeds stature alone (Inference: height ratio 1.076, height-squared 1.158). pp. 4119, 4122, Table 2.
- Age: 20 to 40 only; no age effect tested. Older cadaver series are larger (p. 4128).
- Body size: height and weight recorded but not used as covariates; the authors cite evidence that anthropometry predicts lumbar geometry [20, 31, 32] (p. 4126). Inference: ref. 31, the dissertation on this same data set, names regression models in its title and may hold the height and weight regressions the paper leaves out; Colombini [20] estimated disc area from anthropometric parameters (its title, p. 4130).
- Population, from the compiled studies only: Postacchini [28] Italian (63) vs Indian (58) cadavers, caudal end-plate depth 3.2 and 3.3 vs 2.9 and 2.9 cm, width 4.7 and 4.9 vs 4.1 and 4.3 cm (L3/L4, L4/L5; Table 7, p. 4127); Tan [30], 10 Chinese Singaporean cadavers aged 65.7, the smallest areas in Tables 6 and 7 (cranial end-plate CSA 11.72, 11.99, 10.54 cm2; caudal 11.52, 11.27), though van der Houwen's patients and Postacchini's Indian group have smaller depths and widths; Kang [23], Koreans, 15 women aged 41 (161.2 cm, 53.4 kg) caudal FD 4.38 and 4.97, 35 men aged 39.8 (170.6 cm, 70.5 kg) 4.81 and 5.48 (Table 7); Berry [13] caucasian cadavers; Aydinlioglu [18], Turkish, X-ray (magnified). Population labels come from the table brackets and the reference titles (pp. 4127, 4130-4131); Postacchini's title calls both groups caucasoid ethnic groups, so his Italian versus Indian gap is not a European versus East Asian contrast.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Fig. 6 (p. 4123, PDF 8) is captioned APD_IVD and cited for the APD_IVD interaction (P = 0.017, p. 4122), but its plotted values (about 4.9 to 5.6 cm) match FD_IVD in Table 4 (women 4.97, 5.03, 4.93; men 5.48, 5.59, 5.50), not APD_IVD (3.53 to 4.06).
