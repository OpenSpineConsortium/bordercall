## What the paper is

- Design: retrospective, single-institution, consecutive chart review of CT scans; the second phase of Harianja et al. 2023 (J Bone Joint Surg Am 105(19):1512-1518), which gave normative foraminal dimensions in 600 young patients. This phase sets disc space height (DSH) beside the neural foraminal dimensions (NFD) and patient covariates. p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Institution and ethics: IRB #5220055; authors from Loma Linda University School of Medicine and Loma Linda University Health radiology, with Duke and others. Inference: the scans are Loma Linda's (Southern California, USA); the paper names no hospital, it says "single-institution" on p. 5 (PDF 5).
- Population: living patients aged 18 to 35 years who had an abdomen-pelvis CT or a lumbar CT on one scanner (GE Discovery 750 HD, 64-slice) between February 2018 and March 2023. Charts were reviewed consecutively in date order, men and women in alternation to reach equal numbers. p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Denominators: 809 screened, 109 excluded, 700 included (350 men, 350 women). Exclusions as counted: 41 poor or insufficient imaging, 37 previous spinal surgery, 11 scoliosis, 7 congenital lumbar stenosis, 6 spondylolisthesis, 7 lumbar vertebral fracture (sum 109). p. 3 (PDF 3).
- Ethnicity: 297 Hispanic (42.43%), 261 Caucasian (37.29%), 70 African-American (10.00%), 60 Asian (8.57%), 11 other (1.57%). These sum to 699 and 99.86%, not 700 (see Gaps). p. 3 (PDF 3).
- Covariates (Table 1, p. 3 (PDF 3)), mean (SD), men vs women: age 27.31 (5.21) vs 28.53 (5.06) y, p .005; BMI 28.37 (7.20) vs 28.94 (7.84) kg/m2, p .336; height 1.76 (.08) vs 1.63 (.09) m, p < .001; weight 88.52 (24.79) vs 76.69 (21.76) kg, p < .001.
- Inclusion and exclusion criteria (p. 2 (PDF 2)): excluded a history of degenerative disc disease, scoliosis (coronal deformity > 10 deg), congenital lumbar stenosis (CLS), spondylolisthesis, spinal trauma, neoplasm, spinal hardware, CT done for back pain, or previous spinal surgery. CLS was called when any L2-L5 pedicle length was below the thresholds of Singh et al. 2005 (Spine J 5(6):615-622); the thresholds themselves are not given here.
- Modality and instrument: CT, read in the IMPAX6 PACS (printed "Agfa-Gavaert", Mortsel, Belgium) with a bone window (width 2000 HU, level 500 HU); measured by four medical students (initials JR, EV, MC, DC) trained by a board-certified neuroradiologist (NW); data kept in Excel 16.58; statistics in SPSS 28. p. 2-3 (PDF 2-3).
- Levels covered: the five lumbar interspaces L1-L2, L2-L3, L3-L4, L4-L5, L5-S1, with the foramen at each interspace measured left and right. 15 DSH values (3 heights x 5 levels) and 40 NFD values (4 measures x 2 sides x 5 levels) per patient, 55 x 700 = 38,500 measurements; each DSH level mean is over 700 patients and each DSH type pooled over levels is n = 3,500 (700 patients x 5 interspaces). Pooled over the five levels the mean anterior, middle and posterior DSH were 7.98, 8.16 and 4.09 mm (no SDs given). p. 1 and p. 3 (PDF 1, 3). The thoracic and cervical spine and the sacrum below S1 are not measured.
- How levels were identified and counted: not stated anywhere. No numbering rule, no landmark (ribs, iliolumbar ligament, sacrum, T12), no field of view requirement. Transitional segments were neither named as an exclusion nor reported.
- Statistics: Levene and residual plots for homoscedasticity; Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Q-Q plots for normality; correlation called weak 0-.4, moderate .4-.7, strong .7-1; enter-method multivariate linear regression with zero-order and partial correlations; two-way ANCOVA with anthropometric covariates, estimated marginal means and type III sums of squares for sex and race/ethnicity; one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni and Tukey post hoc tests for level differences; power analyses claimed 100% power. p. 3 (PDF 3).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts or how levels were numbered; transitional cases were not stated as excluded (Inference: so any present are presumably inside the L5-S1 row, or counted as L5-S1 whatever their true number). The only conditions counted are exclusion counts among the 809 screened (CLS 7, scoliosis > 10 deg 11, spondylolisthesis 6, lumbar fracture 7), p. 3 (PDF 3); these are not population prevalences, since the screening pool and the order of exclusion rules are not described.

Inference: an LSTV with a hypoplastic or absent lowest disc would lower the mean and widen the SD of the L5-S1 middle and posterior DSH; the paper cannot tell how much of its L5-S1 decline (Table 2) comes from such segments.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### Anterior disc space height (DSH_ant)

- Definition: on the sagittal CT view, anterior and posterior DSH were defined by one phrase, "shortest distances between the planes of the anterior and posterior endplates", after Harianja et al. 2023, p. 2 (PDF 2). The sentence is ambiguous; Inference: read it as the shortest bone-to-bone distance between the upper body's inferior endplate and the lower body's superior endplate at the anterior margin of the interspace (anterior DSH) and at its posterior margin (posterior DSH), as Fig. 1 draws them. Fig. 1 (p. 2, PDF 2) places the anterior arrow at the anterior rim of the disc space, between the anterior corners of the two bodies.
- Plane: sagittal view; which sagittal slice (midsagittal or parasagittal) is not stated. Inference from Fig. 1: behind the posterior arrow lies a dark oval bounded above and below by bone, which looks like the neural foramen between two pedicles, so the illustrated slice may be parasagittal (near the foramen) rather than midsagittal; Fig. 2 shows a similar foraminal slice. Bone window 2000/500 HU. Units mm.
- Levels: L1-L2 to L5-S1.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Take the two consecutive vertebral body instances (upper, lower). Because the paper does not name the slice (and Fig. 1 may be parasagittal), compute the value on the segment's midsagittal plane and record the plane; optionally also at a parasagittal plane through the pedicles, to bracket the paper's unknown choice. Define the segment's midsagittal plane from the left-right axis of the pair (e.g. the line through the pedicle or body centroids left to right) and the body centroids. In that plane, extract the inferior endplate contour of the upper body and the superior endplate contour of the lower body (the mask voxels facing each other across the gap). DSH_ant = the minimum distance between the two contours within the anterior few millimetres of the endplates (at the anterior rim, excluding osteophytes). Use the body subregion only, never posterior elements. At 1 mm voxels expect partial-volume error of up to about one voxel per surface.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. A lateral radiograph gives it directly if the beam is parallel to the disc (each endplate a single line, not a double contour); correct for magnification with a calibration object or a known dimension. An AP radiograph cannot separate anterior from posterior height.
- Across levels (Table 2, p. 3 (PDF 3)), mean (SD) mm: L1-L2 5.68 (1.86), L2-L3 6.53 (1.90), L3-L4 7.72 (1.94), L4-L5 9.27 (2.17), L5-S1 10.76 (2.72). Rises monotonically to its maximum at L5-S1. Every pairwise level difference is significant (Table 3, p. 4 (PDF 4)): adjacent mean differences L1-L2 minus L2-L3 -.83 (95% CI -1.14 to -.52), L2-L3 minus L3-L4 -1.2 (-1.51 to -.89), L3-L4 minus L4-L5 -1.47 (-1.78 to -1.17), L4-L5 minus L5-S1 -1.56 (-1.87 to -1.25). Inference from Table 2: adjacent standardised separations are only about 0.45, 0.62, 0.75 and 0.61 pooled SD, so a single DSH_ant reading cannot name a level alone, but the monotone trend carries order information along a chain.
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger at every level, all p < .001 (Table 4, p. 4 (PDF 4)): men vs women L1-2 6.48 (1.81) vs 4.89 (1.53); L2-3 7.23 (1.83) vs 5.83 (1.71); L3-4 8.32 (1.94) vs 7.12 (1.76); L4-5 9.82 (2.16) vs 8.69 (2.02); L5-S1 11.14 (2.76) vs 10.38 (2.64). The male excess falls caudally, 1.59, 1.40, 1.20, 1.13, 0.76 mm (Inference: differences computed from Table 4; the text on p. 5 (PDF 5) says "approximately 1 mm"). By ethnicity (Table 5, p. 4 (PDF 4)), White / Hispanic / Black / Asian: L1-2 5.80 / 5.48 / 5.67 / 6.48 (p .003); L2-3 6.48 / 6.49 / 6.21 / 7.31 (p .008); L3-4 7.78 / 7.62 / 7.50 / 8.41 (p .032); L4-5 9.31 / 9.21 / 9.22 / 9.44 (p .022); L5-S1 10.68 / 10.70 / 10.46 / 11.64 (p .036). Asian largest at every level. Correlation with height .000 to .040 (none significant), with weight .130 to .354 (all significant; largest at L1-2, .354), with BMI .105 to .206 (Table 7, p. 5 (PDF 5)). Age range only 18-35, so no age trend is available.
- Error: one overall ICC of .842 for the measurements (p. 5 (PDF 5)); no per-measure, per-level, intra- or inter-observer split, and no ICC model stated.

### Middle disc space height (DSH_mid)

- Definition: the disc height measured "at the center of each interspace", after Harianja et al. p. 2 (PDF 2), Fig. 1 (middle arrow at about the AP centre of the disc space; the arrow is longer than the anterior and posterior ones in the figure). Whether the distance is the shortest one or one perpendicular to a reference line is not stated.
- Plane: sagittal view, slice not stated (see the anterior DSH plane note); mm.
- Levels: L1-L2 to L5-S1.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the same segmental midsagittal plane, take the AP midpoint of the endplates (midpoint between the anterior and posterior rims) and measure the distance between the two endplate surfaces along the line perpendicular to the mean endplate direction there. This includes the central endplate concavity, so a mask whose endplates have been smoothed, hole-filled or convex-hulled will read too small. If the segmenter gives a separate disc label, the bony gap (this measure) is what is wanted, not the disc label's own thickness.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Lateral radiograph with the beam parallel to the disc; the central endplate concavity is projected through both lateral halves of the endplate, so the radiographic middle height is a projection average and may differ from a single midsagittal CT slice. Not obtainable on AP.
- Across levels (Table 2, p. 3 (PDF 3)): L1-L2 6.61 (1.39), L2-L3 7.90 (1.59), L3-L4 8.83 (1.56), L4-L5 9.31 (1.75), L5-S1 8.14 (1.90). Peaks at L4-L5 and falls at L5-S1 to a value between L2-L3 and L3-L4. Table 3 (p. 4 (PDF 4)): L2-L3 vs L5-S1 not separable (-.24, p .062, CI -.49 to .01); L3-L4 vs L4-L5 -.49 (p < .001); L4-L5 vs L5-S1 +1.17 (CI .92 to 1.42). Middle exceeds anterior at L1-L4 (by about 0.9 to 1.4 mm), equals it at L4-L5 and is 2.6 mm below it at L5-S1 (Inference: from Table 2).
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger at L1-2 to L4-5 (p < .001): 6.86 vs 6.36, 8.19 vs 7.61, 9.13 vs 8.53, 9.62 vs 8.99 mm, a gap of 0.50 to 0.63 mm (Inference: differences computed from Table 4); at L5-S1 8.21 (1.94) vs 8.07 (1.86), p .311, no difference (Table 4, p. 4 (PDF 4)). No ethnic difference at any level (Table 5: p .310, .278, .365, .836, .158). Weight r .050 to .219 (L5-S1 .050 not significant), height .010 to .050, BMI .040 to .148 (Table 7, p. 5 (PDF 5)).
- Error: the overall ICC .842 only (p. 5 (PDF 5)).

### Posterior disc space height (DSH_post)

- Definition: the shortest distance between the endplates at the posterior margin of the interspace, same sentence as DSH_ant (Inference: same reading of an ambiguous sentence). p. 2 (PDF 2), Fig. 1, where the posterior arrow sits at the posterior rim of the disc space, just in front of the dark foraminal oval.
- Plane: sagittal view, slice not stated; mm.
- Levels: L1-L2 to L5-S1.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. As DSH_ant, at the posterior rim of the two body endplates (just anterior to the canal), body subregion only. The posterior rim is the edge closest to the pedicles and the canal; exclude posterior osteophytes and ring apophysis irregularity.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. Lateral radiograph, beam parallel to the disc; the posterior corners are often partly hidden by the pedicles and facets, so error is larger than anteriorly. Not on AP.
- Across levels (Table 2, p. 3 (PDF 3)): L1-L2 3.11 (1.15), L2-L3 4.02 (1.24), L3-L4 4.66 (1.38), L4-L5 4.88 (1.45), L5-S1 3.80 (1.41). Peaks at L4-L5; L3-L4 vs L4-L5 not separable (-.17, p .174, CI -.36 to .03); L5-S1 sits between L1-L2 and L2-L3 (L1-L2 minus L5-S1 -.66; L2-L3 minus L5-S1 +.24, p .004). Table 3, p. 4 (PDF 4).
- Sex, age, size, population: men larger at L1-2 to L4-5 (p < .001, .004 at L4-5): 3.25 vs 2.98, 4.21 vs 3.82, 4.88 vs 4.44, 5.04 vs 4.73 mm, a gap of 0.27 to 0.44 mm (Inference: differences computed from Table 4); L5-S1 3.83 (1.45) vs 3.77 (1.36), p .606 (Table 4, p. 4 (PDF 4)). Asian largest at every level (Table 5): L1-2 3.64 vs White 3.09 (p .007); L2-3 4.69 vs 3.80 (p < .001); L3-4 5.29 vs 4.40 (p < .001); L4-5 5.09 vs 4.70 (p .036); L5-S1 4.24 vs 3.61 (p .002). Hispanic values run up to about 0.4 mm above White (L2-3 4.10, L3-4 4.79) and Black values near White; the text says the three non-Asian groups did not differ (p. 3 (PDF 3)). Weight r .128 to .307, height .000 to .030, BMI .060 to .251 (Table 7).
- Error: the overall ICC .842 only.

### Derived: anterior minus posterior DSH (disc wedging)

Not reported by the paper. Inference from Table 2: DSH_ant minus DSH_post is 2.57, 2.51, 3.06, 4.39 and 6.96 mm at L1-L2 to L5-S1, and DSH_ant / DSH_post is 1.83, 1.62, 1.66, 1.90 and 2.83. The L5-S1 value is about 2.6 mm beyond L4-L5; together with anterior minus middle DSH (-0.04 mm at L4-L5, +2.62 mm at L5-S1, a step of 2.66 mm) these are the two largest level-to-level steps derivable from Table 2, so the anterior-heavy wedge marks the lumbosacral junction in these population means. These are differences of group means; Inference: per-patient wedging SDs are not reported, so the per-patient separation is unknown. Dividing the difference by the endplate AP depth gives the tangent of the disc wedge angle; endplate depth is not reported here.

### Sagittal AP foraminal width

- Definition: the maximal distance from the posterior margin of the upper ("superior") vertebral body to the anterior margin of the inferior articular facet, measured on the sagittal view (Fig. 2, line CD, drawn roughly horizontal at the level of the disc space across the foramen). p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Plane: sagittal view through the foramen (Fig. 2); the exact parasagittal position is not stated. mm.
- Levels and sides: L1-L2 to L5-S1, left and right.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In a parasagittal plane through the foramen (lateral to the canal, at the pedicles), take the posterior surface of the upper body instance and the anterior surface of the facet region bounding the foramen posteriorly; the longest AP chord between them. The paper names the inferior articular facet; anatomically the posterior foraminal wall is the facet joint (the superior articular process of the lower vertebra with the inferior process of the upper), so a mask recipe must choose one and state it.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. The lateral radiograph superimposes both foramina; an approximate width can be read only where they overlap well. Not on AP.
- Across levels (Table 6, p. 5 (PDF 5)), left / right mean (SD) mm: L1-L2 9.52 (2.12) / 9.69 (2.06); L2-L3 9.24 (2.03) / 9.43 (1.99); L3-L4 9.18 (2.19) / 9.20 (2.14); L4-L5 8.68 (2.13) / 8.68 (2.23); L5-S1 8.81 (2.52) / 9.03 (2.44). Narrows from L1-L2 to L4-L5, then rises slightly at L5-S1 on both sides.
- Covariates: correlation with DSH at most .191 on the left and .184 on the right, both with L4-5 anterior DSH (Table 7, p. 5 (PDF 5)). No sex or ethnicity breakdown of NFD in this paper.
- Error: overall ICC .842.

### Axial AP foraminal width

- Definition: as the sagittal AP width (posterior margin of the upper body to the anterior margin of the inferior articular facet, maximal distance) but measured on the axial view. p. 2 (PDF 2), Fig. 2 caption. Which axial slice (disc level or pedicle level) is not stated.
- Levels and sides: L1-L2 to L5-S1, left and right.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the axial plane through the foramen (between the pedicles of the two vertebrae), the longest AP distance from the posterior body margin to the anterior facet margin, per side.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (axial plane).
- Across levels (Table 6), left / right: L1-L2 9.34 (2.21) / 9.30 (2.21); L2-L3 9.28 (2.32) / 9.31 (2.22); L3-L4 8.99 (2.29) / 9.07 (2.25); L4-L5 8.58 (2.22) / 8.60 (2.27); L5-S1 8.72 (2.60) / 8.97 (2.53). Same shape as the sagittal width: narrowest at L4-L5, a slight rise at L5-S1; on the right L1-L2 and L2-L3 are equal (9.30, 9.31).
- Covariates: correlation with DSH at most .199 on the left and .194 on the right, both with L4-5 anterior DSH (Table 7, p. 5 (PDF 5)).
- Error: overall ICC .842.

### Neuroforaminal height (NFH)

- Definition: the maximum distance from the inferior notch of the pedicle of the upper vertebra to the superior notch of the pedicle of the lower vertebra (Fig. 2, line AB, roughly craniocaudal across the foramen), sagittal view. p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Levels and sides: L1-L2 to L5-S1, left and right; mm.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In a parasagittal plane through the pedicles, the longest craniocaudal distance between the inferior surface of the upper instance's pedicle and the superior surface of the lower instance's pedicle. Needs the pedicle region of each instance (the posterior-element subregion where it joins the body), on each side.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference. A lateral radiograph shows the pedicles and the foramen in profile (both sides superimposed), so NFH can be approximated as the pedicle-to-pedicle gap, magnification corrected. Not on AP.
- Across levels (Table 6, p. 5 (PDF 5)), left / right: L1-L2 17.31 (2.41) / 17.26 (2.27); L2-L3 18.38 (2.26) / 18.77 (3.68); L3-L4 18.42 (2.48) / 18.49 (3.90); L4-L5 17.81 (2.61) / 17.64 (2.40); L5-S1 15.33 (2.26) / 15.14 (2.41). Unimodal: highest at L3-L4 on the left and L2-L3 on the right, lowest at L5-S1 (about 2.3 to 2.5 mm below L4-L5). The right L2-L3 and L3-L4 SDs (3.68, 3.90) are much larger than the others (about 2.3 to 2.6).
- Covariates: NFH is the one NFD weakly tied to DSH (Table 7, p. 5 (PDF 5)): left NFH r .060 to .301, right NFH .010 to .308; strongest at L4-5 posterior DSH (.301 left, .308 right) and L4-5 anterior (.287 left); weakest on the left at L5-S1 anterior (.060), on the right at L2-3 middle (.010) and L2-3 anterior (.050), with L5-S1 anterior .070. All below .4, so "weak" by the paper's scale; the results text says NFH was weakly but significantly tied to all three DSH measures (p. 3 (PDF 3)).
- Error: overall ICC .842.

### Neuroforaminal area

- Definition: the area of the foramen traced on the sagittal view with the IMPAX6 freeform tracing tool (Fig. 2, the region inside the hatched line). p. 2 (PDF 2).
- Levels and sides: L1-L2 to L5-S1, left and right.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the parasagittal plane through the foramen, the area of the background region enclosed by the upper and lower instances' pedicles, the posterior body margins and disc gap, and the facet region; the plane must be fixed (for example the plane of the narrowest cross-section, or the pedicle plane), since the paper does not say which plane it used.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: only roughly on a lateral film, both sides superimposed.
- Across levels (Table 6), left / right (the table caption gives the unit as mm; Inference: an area must be mm2): L1-L2 132.47 (35.18) / 132.04 (34.20); L2-L3 140.54 (37.26) / 142.53 (38.06); L3-L4 143.83 (40.16) / 143.89 (41.09); L4-L5 138.03 (38.92) / 137.10 (36.92); L5-S1 124.38 (36.95) / 124.93 (34.03). Peak at L3-L4, smallest at L5-S1.
- Covariates: correlation with DSH .000 to .251 (Table 7).
- Error: overall ICC .842.

### Pedicle length for congenital lumbar stenosis (used, not reported)

Pedicle length at L2-L5 against Singh et al. 2005's thresholds was used only to exclude CLS (7 of 809 screened); no pedicle values or thresholds are given. p. 2-3 (PDF 2-3).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1-L2: smallest DSH on all three heights (5.68 / 6.61 / 3.11 mm); the widest foramen in AP (9.52 / 9.69 sagittal) and second-lowest NFH and area. The largest sex difference in DSH_ant (6.48 vs 4.89 mm, men vs women) and the strongest DSH-weight correlation (.354, L1-2 anterior). Tables 2, 4, 6, 7, pp. 3-5 (PDF 3-5). The paper says nothing about T12/L1.
- L2-L3: DSH 6.53 / 7.90 / 4.02; right NFH peaks here (18.77) with an inflated SD (3.68). Middle DSH not separable from L5-S1 (p .062). Tables 2, 3, 6.
- L3-L4: the foraminal peak (area 143.83 / 143.89 mm2, left NFH 18.42); DSH 7.72 / 8.83 / 4.66; posterior DSH not separable from L4-L5 (p .174). Tables 2, 3, 6; abstract p. 1 (PDF 1).
- L4-L5: the tallest middle (9.31) and posterior (4.88) DSH; the paper ties this to the region's greater mobility (citing Tibrewal and Pearcy 1985, Demir 2018), p. 4 (PDF 4). The narrowest foraminal AP width (8.68 both sides sagittal; 8.58 left, 8.60 right axial). The strongest NFH-DSH correlations (about .30). Tables 2, 6, 7.
- L5-S1 (lumbosacral junction): the tallest anterior DSH (10.76) but middle and posterior DSH drop (8.14, 3.80), so this disc is the most wedge-shaped. The paper attributes the drop to the L5-S1 disc shape, its interface with the sacrum and greater sustained axial loads (citing Humzah and Soames 1988, Urquhart 2014, Schmitt 2004, Ozturk 2008), p. 4 (PDF 4). Smallest NFH (15.33 / 15.14) and area (124.38 / 124.93). No sex difference in middle or posterior DSH here (p .311, .606). DSH-NFD correlations near zero here except right NFH vs posterior DSH (.236). Tables 2, 4, 6, 7.
- C2/C3, C7/T1 and T12/L1: not studied.

## Measurement error and reliability

- One ICC, .842, called acceptable, after a "well-defined" protocol and training; no ICC model, no raters per image, no number of images in the reliability sample, no split by measure, level, side or intra versus inter-observer. p. 5 (PDF 5).
- Four student raters trained by one neuroradiologist; the paper names reviewer subjectivity as a limitation. p. 2, p. 5 (PDF 2, 5).
- Instrument: PACS calipers on reformatted CT; slice thickness, reconstruction kernel and reformat orientation are not reported.
- Inference: if the .842 ICC holds for each DSH measure within one level, the implied measurement error SD is SD x sqrt(1 - .842), about 0.76 mm for DSH_ant (SD about 1.9), 0.64 mm for DSH_mid (SD about 1.6) and 0.52 mm for DSH_post (SD about 1.3). If instead the single ICC was computed over values pooled across measures and levels (the paper does not say), the between-measure and between-level spread inflates it, and the true within-level error is larger than these figures.
- Inference: the level comparison used one-way ANOVA (p. 3 (PDF 3)), which treats the five levels of one patient as independent groups; the true within-patient level differences are probably estimated with less noise than Table 3's intervals suggest.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indications named for DSH and NFD normative values: device design, understanding direct and indirect decompression, sagittal and coronal balance correction, restoration of disc height, and interbody cage selection that avoids distractive or compressive endplate loads. p. 1-2 (PDF 1-2). The authors point to a paucity of literature on implant size selection. p. 5 (PDF 5).
- DSH is at most weakly related to foraminal size (all r < .4); NFH is the only NFD weakly related. The authors argue that foraminal size is set more by vertebral morphometry such as pedicle length and width, facet orientation and ligaments than by disc height, so restoring disc height alone should not be assumed to open the foramen proportionally. p. 3-4 (PDF 3-4). The introduction had expected DSH to correlate with NFH, citing six earlier studies (p. 2 (PDF 2)); the authors call the absence of even a moderate DSH-NFD association surprising (p. 3 (PDF 3)).
- DSH differs by sex (men larger, chiefly anteriorly and in the upper lumbar spine) and by ethnicity (Asian subgroup taller anterior and posterior DSH); the discussion says height and weight are not associated with DSH, while the results and Table 7 give weight a consistent weak correlation (p. 3, p. 5 (PDF 3, 5)). The authors suggest using the values as frames of reference adjusted to the patient. p. 5 (PDF 5).
- The male excess in anterior DSH agrees with Bach et al. 2018 (240 CT scans), but values for the second and third decades differ from Bach's; the authors put this down to their adjustment for demographic and anthropometric confounders and their larger sample. They cite Bach's CT findings as agreeing on no association of DSH with height or weight, and note that most earlier radiographic DSH studies did not account for anthropometric or demographic characteristics. p. 5 (PDF 5).
- The authors explain the middle DSH not rising caudally as in the anterior and posterior ones by the more elliptical middle cross-section of the disc compared with the more rectangular anterior and posterior ones. p. 4-5 (PDF 4-5).
- The paper says its population is the first to give DSH and NFD in Hispanic and African-American cohorts; earlier work covered Korean, Pakistani, Nepalese and Iranian groups. p. 5 (PDF 5).
- CT shows bone sharply and has none of MRI's ligament signal or perineural fat; it cannot show disc herniation or ligamentous infiltration of the foramen, which the authors judge a minor concern in this young sample. p. 5 (PDF 5).
- Pitfalls they name: single institution; ages 18-35 only; "normal" is defined by chart and imaging history, so undocumented asymptomatic pathology may be included; reviewer subjectivity. p. 5 (PDF 5). Their conclusions apply only to young asymptomatic patients; older and symptomatic patients are planned. p. 6 (PDF 6).
- Congenital lumbar stenosis was screened by short L2-L5 pedicle length (Singh 2005); 7 of 809 screened were excluded for it. p. 2-3 (PDF 2-3).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Measure disc space height between the bony endplates of two consecutive vertebral bodies on a sagittal plane, at three points: the anterior rim, the AP midpoint and the posterior rim (p. 2 (PDF 2), Fig. 1). Use the body part of each instance mask only. The paper does not name the sagittal slice and its Fig. 1 may be parasagittal; Inference: use the segment's midsagittal plane and record the plane with the value.
2. Expect anterior DSH to rise at every step from L1-L2 (about 5.7 mm) to L5-S1 (about 10.8 mm); use the monotone rise as an ordering cue along the chain, not as a per-level label, since adjacent levels overlap by most of a SD (Table 2, Table 3, p. 3-4 (PDF 3-4)).
3. Find the lumbosacral interspace as the disc with the largest anterior DSH and the largest anterior minus posterior difference (about 7 mm, against about 4.4 at L4-L5), with middle and posterior DSH falling below those of L4-L5 (Table 2, p. 3 (PDF 3); the results text on p. 3 states the L5-S1 pattern; the difference is Inference from the table). These are means of 700 patients; Inference: weigh the cue in the chain decoder rather than thresholding one disc, since the paper does not say how transitional lowest discs were handled.
4. Do not try to separate L3-L4 from L4-L5 by posterior DSH (difference .17 mm, p .174), nor L2-L3 from L5-S1 by middle DSH (p .062) (Table 3, p. 4 (PDF 4)).
5. Before comparing a measured DSH with these norms, condition on sex: women's anterior DSH is about 1.6 mm lower at L1-2, shrinking to about 0.8 mm at L5-S1; middle and posterior differences are about 0.5 and 0.3 mm and vanish at L5-S1 (Table 4, p. 4 (PDF 4)).
6. Allow taller anterior and posterior DSH (up to about 1 mm over White) in patients the paper would group as Asian, if ethnicity is known; do not adjust middle DSH for ethnicity (Table 5, p. 4 (PDF 4)). The Asian subgroup is 60 US patients whose origin the paper does not give.
7. Within one sex, do not scale DSH by body height (r <= .05 in Table 7, p. 5 (PDF 5), whose caption says the correlations account for sex, ethnicity and the anthropometric factors); a weak scaling with weight is allowed (r up to .35 at L1-2 anterior). Inference: men are 13 cm taller and have larger DSH (Tables 1 and 4), so across both sexes together stature and DSH will still move together through sex.
8. Read neuroforaminal height on the sagittal view through the foramen, from the inferior notch of the upper pedicle to the superior notch of the lower pedicle (p. 2 (PDF 2), Fig. 2); expect about 17 to 19 mm at L1-L2 to L4-L5 and about 15 mm at L5-S1 (Table 6, p. 5 (PDF 5)). Inference: a drop of NFH by about 2.5 mm at the last mobile level supports the L5-S1 call, given the SD of about 2.3 to 2.6 mm it is a weak cue for one patient.
9. Do not infer foraminal stenosis or foraminal size from disc height alone; the correlation is weak at best (Table 7, p. 5 (PDF 5)).
10. Read these norms as supine CT values of 18-35-year-olds; for older patients, standing films or degenerated discs, expect lower DSH and do not use these SDs unchanged (p. 5-6 (PDF 5-6); the standing versus supine point is Inference).
11. On a lateral radiograph or fluoroscopy frame, take DSH only where the endplates project as single lines (beam parallel to the disc) and after magnification correction; the paper gives no radiographic recipe (Inference).
12. Record how the levels were counted (from C2, from T12 and the last rib, or from the sacrum) beside every DSH reading, because this paper's L5-S1 row may contain transitional segments it did not identify (Inference; the paper is silent, p. 2 (PDF 2)).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13, rib variants or block vertebrae, and no numbering rule. Exclusion counts among 809 screened 18-35-year-olds (CLS 7, scoliosis 11, spondylolisthesis 6, fracture 7) are not usable as priors.

(b) Measures:
- Disc space height is not one of Panjabi's vertebral dimensions (Inference from memory: Panjabi's lumbar paper measured isolated vertebrae, not discs). It is a between-instance quantity: the generator could add per-interspace DSH_ant, DSH_mid and DSH_post for L1-L2 to L4-L5 (and L5-S1 as the junction), with means and SDs by sex from Table 4 and by ethnicity from Table 5, p. 4 (PDF 4).
- Bias from a mask: middle DSH includes the central endplate concavity; a mask that fills or smooths the endplate will read low. Anterior and posterior DSH are "shortest" distances near the rims; a mask that includes osteophytes or the ring apophysis edge, or a slice off the midline, changes them. The paper does not state the slice and its Fig. 1 may show a parasagittal (foraminal) slice; Inference: use the segment's midsagittal plane and expect a possible offset against these norms, since a lateral slice crosses a different part of the domed endplate.
- A disc label from a segmenter measures disc tissue; this paper's values are bone-to-bone on a bone window and the two should not be mixed.
- NFH needs the pedicle region; SPINEPS-style posterior-element subregions may not isolate the pedicle (Inference), so define the pedicle as the posterior-element voxels within the body's lateral extent between the body and the articular processes, and say so.
- The foraminal AP width definition (posterior body margin to the anterior margin of the "inferior articular facet") differs from definitions that use the superior articular process of the lower vertebra; state which surface the mask recipe uses.

(c) Measurement error: one ICC .842 (p. 5 (PDF 5)). Inference: error SD about 0.5 to 0.8 mm for DSH, larger for the foraminal area (no figure given). Instrument resolution and slice thickness are not reported.

(d) Covariates: sex (Table 4, men larger, the gap shrinking caudally and vanishing for middle and posterior DSH at L5-S1); ethnicity (Table 5, Asian larger anterior and posterior DSH, no middle effect; White, Hispanic and Black alike); body height none within sex (r <= .05, partial on sex and ethnicity per the caption); weight weak (r .05 to .35); BMI weak (.04 to .25) (Table 7, p. 5 (PDF 5)). Inference: the sex effect (Table 4, reported as persisting after adjustment for height, weight and BMI) is the size-like covariate to carry; a generator size factor tied to stature should not raise DSH within one sex. Age: no information beyond 18-35, and age is not among the stated covariates although women were 1.2 years older (p .005, Table 1, p. 3 (PDF 3)). Population: a US single-institution cohort, 42% Hispanic. The paper gives no patient size factor that DSH scales with; Inference: do not tie DSH to the generator's size factor through stature.

- Abstract (p. 1 (PDF 1)) says DSH rose linearly from L1-L2 to L5-S1; Table 2 and the results text (p. 3 (PDF 3)) show middle and posterior DSH falling at L5-S1. The conclusion says L1-L5 (p. 6 (PDF 6)).
- The results text (p. 3 (PDF 3)) says all DSH measures rose significantly from L1-L2 to L4-L5; Table 3 gives posterior L3-L4 vs L4-L5 as not significant (p .174).
