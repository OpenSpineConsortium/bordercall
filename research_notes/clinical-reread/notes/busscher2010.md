## What the paper is

- Design: descriptive cadaver CT morphometry of human and porcine vertebrae measured by one protocol, to judge whether the pig can stand in for the human spine in implant and surgical-technique testing, p. 1104 (PDF 1), p. 1105 (PDF 2). A second stated use: computational models of the spine need precise geometry, p. 1105 (PDF 2). The biomechanics of the same specimens were published separately (ref. 11, Busscher et al. 2009, Spine 34(26)), p. 1111 (PDF 8), p. 1113 (PDF 10).
- Human population: fresh frozen spines of 6 male cadavers, aged 55-84 at death (mean 72), standing 175-192 cm (mean 182 cm), supplied by the anatomy department of the Groningen university medical centre (the Netherlands), p. 1105 (PDF 2). No women; ethnicity not stated (Inference: Dutch, presumably white European). Inference: 182 cm is about the Dutch male average and tall by the standard of most other populations.
- Porcine population: 6 domestic Landrace pigs, 4 months old, mean weight 40 kg (range 37-44), from a local abattoir, p. 1105 (PDF 2). Pigs this age are not fully grown, p. 1112 (PDF 9).
- Specimens: every spine dissected from C3 to S1; only musculature removed, ligaments and discs left intact; about 3 cm of rib kept on both sides with the costotransverse and costovertebral joints, p. 1105 (PDF 2). C1, C2 and the sacrum below S1 are not in the specimens, so no C2 value and no C2/C3 junction exists in this paper; S1 is in the specimen but no S1 dimension is reported.
- Counts: every human spine had 7 cervical, 12 thoracic and 5 lumbar vertebrae; every pig had 7 cervical, 15 thoracic and 6 lumbar, p. 1105 (PDF 2). Human denominator (my count): 6 spines x 22 vertebrae (C3-L5) = 132 vertebrae; porcine 6 x 26 (C3-L6) = 156. Every human level value is a mean of 6 vertebrae of 6 men.
- Modality: CT of the dissected spines on a Siemens scanner printed as "Somaton Sensation 64" (sic), the same protocol for both species, slice thickness 1.00 mm, in-plane resolution 0.39 x 0.39 mm, p. 1105 (PDF 2). CTs were made at two university radiology departments, p. 1113 (PDF 10).
- Measurement: three independent observers (initials IB, JP, PL; PL is Pieter Lammers, M.D., thanked for measuring in the acknowledgments, p. 1113, PDF 10) measured 16 dimensions on every vertebra on 2D multiplanar views in AquariusNET (TeraRecon); for each measurement the plane was set in line with the vertebra's own anatomical landmarks as seen in the other views, by placing the axes in the frontal and sagittal views, p. 1105 (PDF 2), Fig. 2 caption p. 1106 (PDF 3). Inference: this is a double-oblique reformat per vertebra. Not stated: whether the reported values are the mean of the three observers or one observer's readings.
- Statistics: means and SD in Excel 2003; intraclass and interclass correlations in SPSS; human against porcine compared per vertebra with the Mann-Whitney U test. A region was "comparable" in absolute value when the majority (methods, p. 1106) or "half or more" (Table 4 footnote, p. 1108) of its vertebrae were "significantly equal" (p > 0.05); a pattern was comparable when the human/porcine ratio of each vertebra varied by less than 20% across the vertebrae of the region, p. 1105-1106 (PDF 2-3), Tables 4 and 5 footnotes p. 1108 (PDF 5). Several dimensions were also divided by an "appropriate standard" (end-plate width or depth, central body height) per vertebra, p. 1106 (PDF 3). Four regions: cervical, high thoracic, low thoracic, lumbar; the boundary between high and low thoracic is never given.
- Inclusion and exclusion: none stated beyond availability; only six human spines could be obtained, p. 1112 (PDF 9). The CTs showed "mild degeneration" in the human specimens and no other pathology in either species, p. 1106 (PDF 3); degeneration is not graded.
- How levels were identified: not stated. Inference: counted on the dissected specimen from the cut end at C3 and from S1, with the preserved rib stumps marking the thoracic vertebrae; no human spine is described as transitional.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

- The paper reports no human transitional anomaly: all 6 human spines had 7 cervical, 12 thoracic and 5 lumbar vertebrae, p. 1105 (PDF 2). Denominator 6 elderly Dutch men; this is no prevalence estimate. How the count was checked (ribs, sacral fusion) is not said.
- The porcine spines had 15 thoracic and 6 lumbar vertebrae (rows T13, T14, T15 and L6 in Table 3, p. 1107, PDF 4). The authors handled the extra segments by listing them as additional rows, by measuring kyphosis T1 to T15 and lordosis L1 to L6 in the pig (Table 2 footnote, p. 1107, PDF 4), and by giving porcine total length without the 4 extra vertebrae, 471.7 mm against 569.5 mm with them, p. 1107 (PDF 4). Because the counts differ, they refused to normalise dimensions to total spine length, p. 1113 (PDF 10).
- Inference: for the human decoder the paper gives nothing on L6, T13, rib anomalies or LSTV. Its one lesson is that equal C3-to-caudal length can hide a different count: the pig matches the human length with four more vertebrae, mostly because porcine discs are 2-4 mm against human 4.4-12.7 mm (Table 3), so total length alone cannot fix a count.

## Measures, each as a recipe

General conventions, p. 1105 (PDF 2), p. 1107 (PDF 4), Fig. 1 p. 1105 (PDF 2): "width" is side-to-side, "depth" is dorsal-to-ventral. Pedicle height, width and angle are the mean of left and right. Central body height and spinous process length are taken in the sagittal midline. Disc height is taken at the central midpoint of the disc. Each measurement plane was aligned to the vertebra's own anatomy, not to the scanner axes (Fig. 2, p. 1106, PDF 3). All human values are means of 6 men; SD in parentheses. "Intra-CC" and "Inter-CC" below are the paper's Table 1 columns (intraclass and interclass correlation of the measuring method, p. 1106, PDF 3); the paper describes no repeated reading by one observer, so taking them as intra- and inter-observer reliability is an Inference.

Sex, age, size, population, for every measure below: 6 men, 55-84 years (mean 72), 175-192 cm tall (mean 182), Dutch cadavers with mild degeneration, p. 1105 (PDF 2), p. 1106 (PDF 3); no women, no subgroup or regression on age or height, so the paper gives no sex, age or size effect for any measure.

General recipe for a 3D CT instance mask (Inference, applies to every measure below): build a per-vertebra frame first, as the authors did with their multiplanar alignment. Superior-inferior axis = the normal of a plane fitted to the body's two end-plate surfaces (or the body subregion's long principal axis); anterior-posterior axis = in the mid-sagittal plane, from the posterior body wall centre to the anterior body wall centre; left-right axis = their cross product. Mid-sagittal plane = the plane through the body centroid and the spinous process tip that contains the superior-inferior axis. Then measure in that frame.

### Vertebral body height anterior, central, posterior (VBHa, VBHc, VBHp)

- Landmarks: three superior-inferior heights of the body in the lateral view, at the anterior margin, at the centre of the end-plates, and at the posterior margin (Fig. 1 lower right sketch, p. 1105, PDF 2). VBHc is stated to be in the sagittal midline, p. 1105 (PDF 2).
- Plane: sagittal reformat aligned to the vertebra. Instrument: CT MPR, 1 mm slices. Levels: C3-L5 human (Table 3, p. 1107, PDF 4).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: in the mid-sagittal slice of the body subregion, the distance along the superior-inferior axis between the superior and inferior bone surfaces at the anterior cortex (VBHa), at the end-plate centre (VBHc), and at the posterior cortex (VBHp). Inference: VBHc must be taken through the end-plate concavity, so it needs the true bony surface, not a convex hull of the body; a mask that fills the end-plate concavity overstates VBHc.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: a lateral radiograph gives all three, as anterior, mid and posterior heights of the body outline; VBHc on a lateral film is the distance between the deepest points of the two concave end-plate lines, blurred by end-plate obliquity to the beam. Correct for magnification; the paper is CT and gives none. An AP film does not give them reliably (Inference).
- Across levels (human, Table 3, p. 1107, PDF 4):
  - VBHa: C3 14.2 (0.7), C4 14.5 (1.3), C5 13.4 (1.1, the cervical minimum), C6 14.0 (0.5), C7 15.7 (0.5), T1 17.3 (0.8), T6 19.1 (3.0), T12 25.6 (1.6), L1 25.5 (2.5), L2 27.3 (2.8), L3 28.7 (1.9), L4 27.8 (2.4), L5 29.5 (1.4, the maximum).
  - VBHc: C3 12.6 (0.7), C5 12.1 (2.1, the minimum), C7 14.3 (2.0), T1 16.1 (0.5), T6 18.1 (2.5), T12 25.5 (1.3), L1 24.5 (4.8), L2 24.7 (3.6), L3 25.5 (0.8), L4 24.1 (3.4), L5 25.3 (1.8). VBHc reaches its plateau at T12 and stays at 24.1-25.5 mm through the lumbar spine.
  - VBHp: C3 15.1 (1.1), C6 13.9 (0.8, the minimum), C7 15.9 (0.7), T1 18.8 (1.0), T6 21.7 (2.0), T12 28.5 (0.9), L1 28.5 (2.5), L2 29.8 (1.2) and L3 29.8 (1.0) (maximum), L4 28.0 (2.1), L5 24.9 (3.8).
  - The text says all body heights generally rise from the cervical spine to L3, p. 1107 (PDF 4).
  - Shape: the authors state that central height was smaller than anterior and posterior height in all vertebrae, i.e. the human body is "saddle-shaped" with concave end-plates while the porcine body is more "cubical", p. 1108 (PDF 5); Table 3 has two exceptions (see Gaps). Inference (computed from Table 3): VBHc / mean(VBHa, VBHp) is about 0.85-0.88 in C3-C6 and L2-L4 and about 0.91-0.95 in T7-T12, so end-plate concavity is deepest in the mid-cervical and mid-lumbar spine.
  - Wedging, Inference (computed from Table 3): VBHa/VBHp is 0.94-1.01 in C3-C7, 0.92-0.99 in T1-T4, 0.88-0.92 from T5 to L2 (the thoracic kyphotic wedge, deepest T6-T8 at 0.88-0.89), 0.96 at L3, 0.99 at L4 and 1.18 at L5. Only L5 is clearly taller in front (C6, 1.01, is the only other value above 1): its posterior height drops 3.1 mm from L4 (28.0 to 24.9) while its anterior height rises 1.7 mm (27.8 to 29.5). The ratio separates L5 from L4 sharply, but it does not separate C3-C7 from T3-T4 or L3-L4, which share values of 0.96-0.99.
  - Junction steps (Table 3): C7 to T1 adds 1.6 (VBHa), 1.8 (VBHc) and 2.9 mm (VBHp); T12 to L1 changes almost nothing (VBHa 25.6 to 25.5, VBHc 25.5 to 24.5, VBHp 28.5 to 28.5), so body height does not mark the thoracolumbar junction.
- Error: Intra-CC VBHa 0.98 (95% CI 0.56-1.00), VBHc 0.99 (0.88-1.00), VBHp 0.99 (0.78-1.00); Inter-CC 0.98 (0.93-0.99), 0.99 (0.92-1.00), 0.98 (0.84-0.99), Table 1 p. 1106 (PDF 3). No error in mm. Between-specimen SD 0.4-4.8 mm (Table 3); the largest are VBHc at L1 (4.8), VBHp at L5 (3.8), VBHc at L2 (3.6) and L4 (3.4), and VBHa at T5 (3.2) and T6 (3.0). The 1 mm slice bounds the superior-inferior resolution.

### Upper and lower end-plate width (UEPW, LEPW)

- Landmarks: side-to-side width of the upper (or lower) end-plate, drawn as the horizontal arm of a crosshair whose other arm is the midline depth line (Fig. 1 upper right sketch, p. 1105, PDF 2; Fig. 2 shows LEPW and LEPD on the transverse view of a human L3, p. 1106, PDF 3).
- Plane: transverse (axial) reformat aligned to the end-plate.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: take the body subregion's superior (or inferior) surface layer, project it onto the end-plate plane, and measure its left-right extent along the line through the end-plate centroid parallel to the left-right axis. Inference: measure through the centroid, not the maximum width, to match the crosshair; exclude osteophytes, and at T1-T12 exclude rib heads, which were kept on these specimens (p. 1105, PDF 2) and which a whole-vertebra mask may also touch.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: an AP radiograph gives the body width at the end-plate line, magnified; it is not the same as an axial width when the end-plate is tilted to the beam, and in the cervical spine the AP outline includes the uncinate processes.
- Across levels (human): the text gives UEPW "ranging from" 27.7 (C3) to 54.0 mm (L5), p. 1108 (PDF 5); the rise is not monotone. Approx., Fig. 3a p. 1109 (PDF 6): C3 27.6, C4 26.4, C5 27.6, C6 30.3, C7 31.5, T1 28.8, T2 26.8 (the minimum), T3 27.2, T4 27.1, T5 29.3, T6 31.6, T7 33.7, T8 35.2, T9 38.0, T10 39.9, T11 44.1, T12 46.5, L1 48.7, L2 50.3, L3 51.8, L4 53.8, L5 53.8. LEPW, approx., Fig. 3b p. 1109 (PDF 6): C3 about 24 and C4 about 23 (partly hidden), C5 27.6, C6 28.0, C7 30.7, T1 35.4, T2 35.0, T3 33.9, T4 33.5, T5 34.2, T6 35.0, T7 35.9, T8 38.2, T9 39.4, T10 43.3, T11 46.5, T12 48.9, L1 50.4, L2 51.2, L3 53.9, L4 53.2, L5 53.9.
  - Inference from the figure: at C3-C4 the upper end-plate is wider than the lower one (about 26-28 against 23-24 mm; Inference: the uncinate processes widen the upper cervical end-plate); at C7 the two are about equal (31.5 against 30.7); from T1 to T5 the lower end-plate is 5-8 mm wider than the upper end-plate of the same vertebra (T1 35.4 against 28.8); the gap narrows to 2-4 mm in T6-T12 and to within about 2 mm in L1-L5. Across the C7/T1 boundary UEPW falls (31.5 to 28.8) while LEPW rises (30.7 to 35.4).
  - Width rises fastest from T8 to L1, about 2.5-3 mm per level on average and about 4 mm from T10 to T11.
- Shape: end-plates are oval in the cervical spine, more circular in the thoracic spine and oval again in the lumbar spine, p. 1108 (PDF 5).
- Sex, age, size, population: see the general line; the authors attribute the caudal widening to the upright posture, which demands relatively larger caudal bodies for the higher axial loads, p. 1111 (PDF 8).
- Error: UEPW Intra-CC 0.99 (0.98-1.00), Inter-CC 0.99 (0.97-0.99); LEPW Intra-CC 0.99 (0.98-1.00), Inter-CC 0.96 (0.89-0.99), Table 1 p. 1106 (PDF 3). Between-specimen SD from the positive error bars of Fig. 3a about 1-5 mm, largest (about 4-5 mm) at T1, T9-T10 and L2 for UEPW.

### Upper and lower end-plate depth (UEPD, LEPD)

- Landmarks: dorsal-to-ventral depth of the end-plate along the midline, the vertical arm of the crosshair (Fig. 1 upper right sketch, p. 1105, PDF 2; Fig. 2, p. 1106, PDF 3).
- Plane: transverse reformat aligned to the end-plate; Inference: equivalently the end-plate length in the mid-sagittal plane.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the anterior-posterior extent of the body's superior (or inferior) surface layer along the mid-sagittal line through its centroid. Inference: stop at the posterior cortex of the body; do not let the pedicle bases or a mask's body-to-pedicle bridge extend the posterior end.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: a lateral radiograph gives end-plate depth as the antero-posterior length of the end-plate line, magnified.
- Across levels (human): the text gives UEPD "ranging from" 16.5 (C3) to 36.7 mm (L5), p. 1108 (PDF 5); L5 is not the maximum. Approx., Fig. 3a p. 1109 (PDF 6): C3 16.5, C4 about 17 and C5 about 17 (partly hidden), C6 19.5, C7 19.9, T1 18.8, T2 19.9, T3 22.2, T4 24.1, T5 26.4, T6 28.7, T7 30.3, T8 32.2, T9 34.5, T10 33.7, T11 35.2, T12 35.6, L1 36.4, L2 39.1, L3 39.5 (maximum), L4 37.9, L5 36.7. LEPD, approx., Fig. 3b: C3-C4 about 17-18 (mostly hidden), C5 19.4, C6 20.4, C7 18.5, T1 20.0, T2 21.6, T3 23.5, T4 about 25.7, T5 27.6, T6 29.9, T7 31.8, T8 33.5, T9 34.2, T10 35.0, T11 35.8, T12 about 34.8, L1 36.2, L2 and L3 about 37.5, L4 37.7, L5 36.2.
  - Inference: depth grows mostly in the mid-thoracic spine (T2 to T9, about 2 mm per level), then rises slowly from about 34 at T10 to a peak of about 39.5 at L2-L3 and falls at L4-L5. UEPW/UEPD: about 1.55-1.65 in C3-C7, about 1.1 at T6-T8 (the round thoracic end-plate), about 1.3-1.45 in L1-L5.
- Sex, age, size, population: see the general line.
- Error: UEPD Intra-CC 0.97 (0.89-0.99), Inter-CC 0.95 (0.85-0.98); LEPD Intra-CC 0.99 (0.93-1.00), Inter-CC 0.98 (0.94-0.99), Table 1 p. 1106 (PDF 3).

### Intervertebral disc height (IDH)

- Landmarks: disc height at the central midpoint of the disc, p. 1105 (PDF 2).
- Plane: Inference: sagittal midline, from "central mid-point".
- Which disc: the paper lists one IDH per vertebra (C3 to L5) without saying whether it is the disc above or below. Inference: it is the disc below. The specimens start at C3 with C2 removed, so a C2/C3 disc height between two end-plates cannot be measured, and S1 is present; and the 22 discs summed with the 22 central body heights give the printed total length (my sum of the Table 3 means is 569.7 mm against the printed 569.4). So "C3" = C3/C4 and "L5" = L5/S1.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the superior-inferior gap, along the local axis, between the inferior surface of one body mask and the superior surface of the next body mask, at the centre of the end-plates. Inference: it needs two consecutive instances, so it is a pair feature, not a single-vertebra feature.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: a lateral radiograph gives mid-disc height between the concave end-plates, magnified.
- Across levels (human, Table 3, p. 1107, PDF 4), labelled by the upper vertebra: C3 6.1 (0.5), C4 5.8 (1.5), C5 4.5 (1.3), C6 4.4 (1.0, the minimum), C7 4.7 (0.7), T1 5.0 (0.8), T2-T9 4.5-5.3, T10 5.9 (1.5), T11 6.1 (1.8), T12 9.3 (2.1), L1 10.3 (3.2), L2 11.5 (2.3), L3 11.8 (2.1), L4 12.7 (2.6, the maximum), L5 8.8 (3.2). The human disc height is largest in the low thoracic and lumbar regions, p. 1108-1109 (PDF 5-6).
  - Junctions: the T12 disc (Inference: T12/L1) jumps 3.2 mm over T11; the L5 disc (Inference: L5/S1) is 3.9 mm lower than L4's at its centre.
  - IDH/UEPW (approx., Fig. 4 p. 1109, PDF 6): 0.22 at C3-C4, about 0.15 at C5-C7, about 0.16-0.17 at T1-T6, falling to 0.125 at T9 (minimum), 0.14-0.145 at T10-T11, 0.20 at T12, 0.21-0.235 at L1-L4, 0.16 at L5. The ratio was comparable with the pig in the cervical and lower thoracic regions, p. 1109 (PDF 6).
- Sex, age, size, population: see the general line; Inference: disc height is age-sensitive and these discs are those of men of 55-84 years with mild degeneration. Human lumbar discs are up to about four times as high as the porcine ones, p. 1111 (PDF 8); the authors relate the larger human discs to upright loading, p. 1111 (PDF 8).
- Error: Intra-CC 0.94 (0.86-0.97), Inter-CC 0.93 (0.86-0.97), Table 1 p. 1106 (PDF 3); between-specimen SD 2.1-3.2 mm from T12 to L5 (Table 3).

### Total spine length

- Definition: sum of all central body heights and disc heights from C3 to L5 (pig: L6), Table 2 footnote and text, p. 1105 (PDF 2), p. 1107 (PDF 4).
- Human 569.4 mm (SD 17.67); porcine 569.5 mm (16.19); porcine without its 4 extra vertebrae 471.7 mm, p. 1107 (PDF 4).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: sum VBHc of every body plus every IDH along the chain. From a radiograph alone: Inference: only if the whole C3-S1 span is on one lateral film, with magnification corrected.
- Error: none given; it inherits the error of 22 VBHc and 22 IDH readings.

### Sagittal curvatures

- Definitions (Table 2 footnote, p. 1107, PDF 4; p. 1105, PDF 2): cervical lordosis = the angle from C3's upper end-plate to C7's lower end-plate; thoracic kyphosis = upper end-plate of T1 to lower end-plate of T12; lumbar lordosis = upper end-plate of L1 to lower end-plate of L5.
- Human: cervical lordosis 20.1 deg (10.0), thoracic kyphosis 34.5 deg (12.8), lumbar lordosis 29.2 deg (7.6); porcine 43.8 (9.5), 15.6 (5.3), 7.9 (5.7), Table 2 p. 1107 (PDF 4).
- From a mask or a lateral radiograph: Inference: the angle between the two end-plate planes; a lateral radiograph alone suffices.
- Pitfall: measured on excised specimens with the muscles removed; the authors warn that loss of muscle tone after death and release of soft tissue on excision may change these angles, though both species were handled alike, p. 1112 (PDF 9). Inference: lumbar lordosis here stops at the L5 lower end-plate, so it omits the L5/S1 disc and is smaller than an L1-S1 angle.
- Error: none given.

### Spinal canal width (SCW)

- Landmarks: side-to-side width of the vertebral foramen, the horizontal arm of the crosshair drawn in the canal (Fig. 1 upper left sketch, p. 1105, PDF 2).
- Plane: transverse reformat. The slice height (pedicle level, narrowest, widest) is not stated.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: the canal is the enclosed background hole of the vertebral ring (posterior body wall, pedicles, laminae) in axial slices of the vertebra's own frame; SCW = its left-right extent through the canal centroid. Inference: take it in the slice through the pedicle mid-height to match the sketch; report also the minimum over the pedicle span to see the sensitivity.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: an AP radiograph gives the interpedicular distance, the classic surrogate for canal width, but it is not this paper's measure; magnified.
- Across levels (human): canal width is largest in the cervical and lumbar regions, p. 1109 (PDF 6). Approx., Fig. 5a p. 1110 (PDF 7): C3 25.9, C4 27.8, C5 28.3, C6 28.3, C7 27.3, T1 23.9, T2 20.7, T3 19.2, T4-T9 about 17.7-18.3 (partly hidden behind the depth markers; minimum near T4-T7), T10 about 19.3, T11 21.5, T12 24.9, L1 26.7, L2 27.3, L3 27.5, L4 29.0, L5 31.9 (error bar to about 34.6).
  - Inference: the canal narrows abruptly over C7-T3 (about 27.3 to 19.2 mm) and widens again over T10-T12; L5 is the widest canal of the series.
  - SCW/UEPW (approx., Fig. 5b p. 1110, PDF 7): 0.94 at C3, about 1.0-1.04 at C4-C5 (canal as wide as the body), 0.93 at C6, 0.86 at C7, falling to about 0.65 at T4 and about 0.48 at T9-T11, then about 0.53-0.55 at T12-L4 and 0.59 at L5.
- Shape: triangular in the cervical spine, circular in the thoracic spine, triangular again in the lumbar spine, p. 1110 (PDF 7).
- Sex, age, size, population: see the general line.
- Error: Intra-CC 0.90 (0.60-0.98), Inter-CC 0.91 (0.81-0.96), Table 1 p. 1106 (PDF 3).

### Spinal canal depth (SCD)

- Landmarks: dorsal-to-ventral depth of the vertebral foramen in the midline, the vertical arm of the canal crosshair, from the posterior body wall to the inner surface of the posterior arch (Fig. 1 upper left sketch, p. 1105, PDF 2).
- Plane: transverse reformat; slice height not stated.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: anterior-posterior extent of the canal hole along the mid-sagittal line, posterior body cortex to the anterior lamina cortex at the spinolaminar junction (Inference on the posterior landmark).
- From a radiograph alone: Inference (the standard lateral-film method; the paper does not discuss radiographs): a lateral radiograph gives the sagittal canal diameter from the posterior body wall to the spinolaminar line; this is the canal measure a lateral film alone can give; magnified.
- Across levels (human): more fluctuating than in the pig, with similar relative size differences, p. 1109 (PDF 6). Approx., Fig. 5a p. 1110 (PDF 7): C3 15.3, C4 15.0, C5 15.2, C6 16.1, C7 15.6, T1 16.1, T2 16.6, T3 16.3, T4-T8 about 16.3-16.8, T9 16.1, T10 17.6, T11 18.4, T12 19.7, L1 19.7, L2 18.6, L3 17.1 (lumbar minimum), L4 17.9, L5 21.8 (largest SD, error bar to about 25.7).
  - SCD/UEPD (approx., Fig. 5b): 0.93 at C3, 0.82-0.91 at C4-T2, 0.71 at T4, falling to about 0.47 at T9, about 0.52-0.55 at T10-L1, 0.48 at L2, 0.43 at L3 (minimum), 0.47 at L4, 0.59 at L5.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication of the work: fresh frozen human cadaver spines are scarce, especially young ones, and vary widely in geometry and biomechanics through shape, size, bone mineral density, degeneration and age, so animal spines are used for testing implants and procedures, p. 1104 (PDF 1); the pig is said to be the most representative animal model, p. 1105 (PDF 2), p. 1111 (PDF 8). Precise geometry is also needed for computational models, p. 1105 (PDF 2).
- Pedicle screws and fusion: porcine pedicle width and height resemble human, so the pig suits instrumentation involving the pedicles and pedicle screw fixation, p. 1111 (PDF 8), and implants using pedicle screws and spinal fusion, p. 1113 (PDF 10). McLain et al. saw the same in L4 of immature 60 kg Landrace pigs, p. 1111 (PDF 8).
- Pig age matters: Dath et al. used older pigs (18-24 months) and found larger pedicle values, with all their lumbar dimensions 1.3-1.5 times those of this study's pigs, p. 1111-1112 (PDF 8-9). Bozkus et al. measured the thoracic spine of 30 kg pigs, and the dimensions measured the same way agreed with this study's porcine values within less than 10% on average, p. 1112 (PDF 9).
- Disc: the largest species difference is the disc; the pig is not the model for disc implants or disc procedures, p. 1113 (PDF 10).
- Scaling: differences in size must be taken into account when testing implants, p. 1113 (PDF 10).
- Normalisation pitfall: normalising to total spine length was rejected because the two species have the same total length but different vertebral counts, p. 1113 (PDF 10). Inference: the same pitfall applies to a human with an extra or missing segment.
- Curvature pitfall: excised specimens lose muscle tone and soft-tissue restraint, so specimen lordosis and kyphosis may not equal in vivo values; both species were handled alike, p. 1112 (PDF 9).
- Definition pitfall: spinous process length here is not Panjabi's (to the middle of the body), p. 1112 (PDF 9).
- Comparison with Panjabi: striking resemblance in pedicle width and height, canal width and depth and transverse process length in all regions, p. 1112 (PDF 9).
- Rib pitfall, Inference: about 3 cm of rib with the costovertebral joints was kept (p. 1105, PDF 2); the 7-8 mm mismatch between the lower end-plate of T1 (about 35) and the upper end-plate of T2 (about 27) across one disc (Fig. 3) suggests that upper thoracic end-plate widths may be affected by the costal facets or rib heads.
- Statistical pitfall (Inference): calling a non-significant Mann-Whitney test with 6 per group "significantly equal" mistakes absence of evidence for equivalence; "comparable" regions are favoured by the small sample.
- No stenosis threshold, syndrome, screw size or safe zone is given.
- Limitations stated: small sample (6 per species) and the age gap (human 55-84 years, pigs 4 months and not fully grown); younger human cadavers cannot be obtained and older pigs are hard to obtain because of food-industry rules, pigs being slaughtered at about 4 months, so many in vitro tests use that age, p. 1112-1113 (PDF 9-10).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Before measuring any vertebra, reslice it into its own frame (end-plate-aligned axial, mid-sagittal, coronal), as the authors set each plane by the vertebra's landmarks in the other views; never measure on scanner axes, p. 1105 (PDF 2), Fig. 2 p. 1106 (PDF 3).
2. Measure VBHa, VBHc and VBHp in the mid-sagittal slice at the anterior cortex, end-plate centre and posterior cortex; keep VBHc on the true concave end-plate surface, since the human body is saddle-shaped and VBHc is smaller than the other two, p. 1108 (PDF 5), Table 3 p. 1107 (PDF 4).
3. Compute VBHa/VBHp: a value near 1.18 with VBHp about 3 mm below that of the vertebra above marks L5; 0.88-0.92 marks T5-L2; 0.94-1.01 is shared by C3-C7, T3-T4 and L3-L4, so use it there only with other features, Table 3 p. 1107 (PDF 4) (ratios are my computation).
4. Measure TPL as the left-to-right tip-to-tip span on the aligned axial slice, excluding rib; expect about 54 mm at T12 and about 79 mm at L1, and a jump from about 64 (C6) to 75 (C7) and 81 (T1); use the T12/L1 step as the strongest thoracolumbar marker this paper offers, Fig. 7a p. 1111 (PDF 8).
5. Measure PedA on the axial slice as the angle at the anterior midline point of the body between the midline and the line to the pedicle (Fig. 1 sketch, p. 1105, PDF 2); expect about 0 deg at T12, 11-15 deg at L1-L4 and about 35 deg at L5, and a near-monotone decline from about 50 deg at C4-C5 to near 0 at T11-T12, Fig. 8b p. 1112 (PDF 9). A pedicle-axis angle may differ (Inference).
6. Measure canal width and depth in the vertebral foramen on the axial slice, and report the slice used; expect a wide triangular cervical canal (SCW about 26-28, SCD about 15-16), a small round mid-thoracic canal (about 18 by 16.5) and a triangular lumbar canal widening to L5 (about 32 by 22), Fig. 5a p. 1110 (PDF 7), p. 1110 (PDF 7).
7. On a lateral radiograph alone, take SCD as posterior body wall to spinolaminar line, body heights, disc heights, pedicle height, SPL and SPA; on an AP radiograph alone, take end-plate width, TPL and pedicle width; do not try PedA on either (Inference; the paper's measures are CT, p. 1105, PDF 2). Correct radiograph magnification before comparing with these CT values.
8. When comparing a measured SPL with Panjabi-derived norms, add the base-to-mid-body offset or use only the level pattern, since Panjabi measured to the middle of the body, p. 1112 (PDF 9).
9. Treat disc height as a pair feature between consecutive body instances; expect a step up at the T12/L1 disc (6.1 to 9.3 mm), a maximum at L4/L5 (12.7) and a drop at L5/S1 (8.8), assuming the disc listed under a vertebra is the one below it (Inference), Table 3 p. 1107 (PDF 4).
10. Use size-free ratios (SCW/UEPW, TPL/UEPW, PedW/UEPW, PedH/VBHc, IDH/UEPW) when the patient's size is unknown; TPL/UEPW bottoms at T12 (about 1.15) and peaks at T1-T2 (about 2.8); IDH/UEPW bottoms at T9 (about 0.125), Figs. 4, 5b, 7b, 8c pp. 1109-1112 (PDF 6-9).
11. Do not trust a single reading of spinous process angle, pedicle height or canal depth near a decision boundary: these have the lowest coefficients (SPA Inter-CC 0.83, PedH Inter-CC 0.89, SCD Intra-CC 0.83 and Inter-CC 0.86), Table 1 p. 1106 (PDF 3).
12. Expect these norms to run large: they come from 6 tall (mean 182 cm), elderly Dutch men with mild degeneration; scale for a smaller patient and for women, for whom the paper has no data, p. 1105 (PDF 2), p. 1106 (PDF 3).
13. Do not read specimen lordosis or kyphosis (20.1, 34.5, 29.2 deg) as in vivo norms; they come from excised spines, p. 1112 (PDF 9).
14. At the thoracolumbar junction combine the TPL jump, the PedA jump, the SPA fall and the disc-height step; do not use body heights, which are equal at T12 and L1, Table 3 p. 1107 (PDF 4), Figs. 6-8 pp. 1110-1112 (PDF 7-9).
15. At C7/T1 look for the LEPW rise against a falling UEPW, the PedH step (about 7.5 to 9.6 mm) and the T1 peaks of TPL and PedW; check end-plate widths at T1-T5 for rib-head contamination (Inference), Figs. 3, 7a, 8a pp. 1109-1112 (PDF 6-9).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: the paper gives none. All 6 human spines were 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar (p. 1105, PDF 2); 0 anomalies in 6 is not a usable prior. The porcine 15 thoracic and 6 lumbar is a different species.

(b) Measures held or addable, and definition differences:
- Same set as the generator: end-plate widths and depths (UEPW, UEPD, LEPW, LEPD), body heights (VBHa, VBHp, plus central VBHc), canal width and depth, pedicle width, height and transverse angle, spinous process length, transverse process tip-to-tip span. Inference: one cohort covers exactly C3-L5, the generator's range, with one protocol, which Panjabi's three separate regional papers do not.
- Addable: VBHc (end-plate concavity), IDH (a pair feature between instances), spinous process angle, and the size-free ratios of Tables 4-5 and Figs. 4, 5b, 7b, 8c.
- Definition differences that would bias a mask-derived value:
  - SPL: base (the reference line at the posterior arch) to tip here, to mid-body in Panjabi, p. 1112 (PDF 9). Inference: a generator built on Panjabi's SPL will exceed a mask SPL taken base to tip by roughly the body half-depth plus canal depth plus lamina; match the definition.
  - PedA: Inference from the Fig. 1 sketch: here the angle is taken at the anterior midline point of the body to the line through the pedicle; Panjabi's transverse pedicle angle is the angle of the pedicle axis (Inference, from memory); the two can differ by several degrees.
  - End-plate width: crosshair through the end-plate centre here; Inference: a mask's maximum left-right extent of the body will be larger, and osteophytes of degenerate end-plates, cervical uncinate processes and thoracic rib heads inflate it further.
  - Canal width and depth: slice level not stated here; the mask code must fix one (Inference).
  - Pedicle width and height: drawn at the pedicle's junction with the posterior body wall in Fig. 1 (p. 1105, PDF 2), not stated as the isthmus; take the junction section to match this paper and the isthmus as a second feature (Inference).
  - TPL: tip-to-tip over both sides; in the thoracic spine exclude rib and costotransverse joint voxels; in the cervical spine include both tubercles (Inference).
  - IDH listed per vertebra: Inference that it is the disc below; check before pairing it with a level.
- Full per-level human means and SDs for VBHa, VBHc, VBHp and IDH are in Table 3, p. 1107 (PDF 4). All other human values exist only as plotted points (Figs. 3-8) with positive SD bars; the digitised readings in this note are the best available, about +/-0.3 mm where the marker is visible.

(c) Measurement error to use: only correlation coefficients (0.83-1.00, Table 1 p. 1106, PDF 3); no millimetre error. Inference: use at least one voxel as the floor (0.39 mm in-plane, 1 mm superior-inferior) and inflate SCD, SPA, PedH and PedA error relative to the others, since their coefficients are lowest. Use the Table 3 SDs (and the figure error bars) as between-subject spread, not as measurement error.

(d) Size, sex and population covariates: men only, 55-84 years (mean 72), body height 175-192 cm (mean 182), Dutch cadavers with mild degeneration, n = 6 (p. 1105, PDF 2; p. 1106, PDF 3). No regression on height or age, no women. Total C3-L5 length (sum of VBHc and IDH) 569.4 mm (SD 17.67) is a size anchor for a tall male, Table 2 p. 1107 (PDF 4). The ratio approach of the paper (dimension over UEPW, UEPD or VBHc) is a ready-made way to factor out the patient size factor.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Not stated: the boundary between high and low thoracic regions; the axial level of the canal measurements; whether pedicle width and height are at the isthmus; whether the disc listed under a vertebra is above or below it; how regions were compared when the pig had extra vertebrae; the ICC model, what the Intra-CC column measures and which vertebrae entered it; whether reported values are means of the three observers; whether ratios are means of per-specimen ratios; how levels were identified on the specimens; the grade of degeneration; per-specimen ages and heights.
- Text against tables:
  - The methods call a region comparable when the "majority" of its vertebrae are equal, p. 1106 (PDF 3); the Table 4 footnote says "half or more", p. 1108 (PDF 5).
  - The text says absolute pedicle height and its pattern were comparable in the cervical and lower thoracic regions, p. 1111 (PDF 8); Table 4 marks PedH comparable in the cervical and lumbar regions, not the lower thoracic, p. 1108 (PDF 5); Table 5 marks cervical and low thoracic.
  - The text says central body height was smaller than anterior and posterior "in all" vertebrae, p. 1108 (PDF 5); in Table 3 human L5 VBHc 25.3 exceeds VBHp 24.9 and T11 VBHc equals VBHa (24.2), p. 1107 (PDF 4).
  - The text says body heights rise from the cervical spine to L3, p. 1107 (PDF 4); VBHa is highest at L5 (29.5), Table 3.
  - The text's end-plate ranges (UEPW 27.7 to 54.0, UEPD 16.5 to 36.7, p. 1108, PDF 5) are the C3 and L5 values, not the minimum and maximum: Fig. 3a puts UEPW at C4 and T2-T4 below 27.7 and UEPD at L2-L4 above 36.7.
  - The abstract says regional trends were comparable for all dimensions except canal depth and spinous process angle, p. 1104 (PDF 1); Table 5 marks IDH not comparable in every region and several other dimensions not comparable in one or more regions, p. 1108 (PDF 5).
  - The text says human lumbar discs are up to four times the porcine height, p. 1111 (PDF 8); Table 3 gives L4 12.7 against 2.7 mm, about 4.7 times (my computation).
  - The text puts the spinous process angle maximum at T6 (62.2 deg), p. 1110 (PDF 7); in Fig. 6 T7 plots at the same height.
  - Table 5 shows an undefined code "CN" for SCD, SPL and SPL/UEPD in the lumbar column, p. 1108 (PDF 5); the legend defines only C and NC.
  - Tables 4 and 5 label the last ratio PedH/VBH; Fig. 8c calls it PedH/VBHc, p. 1112 (PDF 9).
  - Correlation coefficients are called "Kappa values" in the text, p. 1107 (PDF 4).
  - Porcine check: the sum of the porcine Table 3 means of VBHc and IDH is 570.0 mm against the printed 569.5, and 472.1 without T13-T15 and L6 against the printed 471.7 (my computation; within rounding).
- Consistency check that holds: the sum of the Table 3 human means of VBHc and IDH from C3 to L5 is 569.7 mm against the printed total length 569.4 mm (Table 2), consistent within rounding (my computation).
- Inference (anatomy against the figure): the lower end-plate of T1 (about 35.4 mm) is 7-8 mm wider than the upper end-plate of T2 (about 26.8 mm) across one disc, Fig. 3, p. 1109 (PDF 6); adjacent end-plates rarely differ so much, so upper thoracic widths may include costal facets or rib heads.
- Against other papers: the authors report close agreement with Panjabi's pedicle, canal and transverse process values and a different SPL definition, p. 1112 (PDF 9). Inference (from memory, to be checked against the Panjabi notes in this set): this cohort's lumbar end-plate widths (about 54 mm at L4-L5) sit at or above the upper end of Panjabi's lumbar values, which fits a tall male cohort; the human L1-L2 pedicle widths read from Fig. 8a (about 7.1-7.3 mm) are smaller than commonly quoted CT values for the L1-L2 isthmus in men; a junction-level landmark would read wider, not narrower, so the cause (observer convention, window setting or cohort) is unexplained.
