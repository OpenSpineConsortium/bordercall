## What the paper is

- Purpose: shape data on the lumbar endplates and the disc space for designing artificial intervertebral discs (AIDs) that fit the endplate rather than requiring it to be milled flat; implant migration and dislocation are the problems named, worst in osteoporotic patients, p. 33 (PDF 1).
- Design: retrospective CT morphometry, p. 34 (PDF 2). Patients who had a thoracic or abdominal spinal CT were pulled from the hospital PACS. They had varied spinal problems (hernias and fractures are the examples); only intact vertebrae above and below the problem area were measured.
- Population: 77 patients, 46 men and 31 women, aged 21 to 86 years (mean 49.8), p. 34 (PDF 2). Living patients, supine. Country: the Netherlands (Groningen; the acknowledgments thank the hospital's radiology department for the CT scans, p. 40 (PDF 8)). Ethnicity not stated.
- Denominator per measure: the stated goal was 25 measured samples per endplate, which is why 77 patients were needed, p. 34 (PDF 2); the method then counts 25 patients per endplate, 10 points x 12 endplates x 25 = 3,000 coordinates, p. 35 (PDF 3). So each per-level mean in Figure 2 rests on 25 endplates of 25 patients (the paper never confirms the goal was met at every level). The paper does not say which of the 77 patients gave which levels, nor the sex split per level. Inference: 12 x 25 = 300 endplates from 77 patients is about 4 endplates per patient, so different levels come partly from different people and level-to-level differences carry between-person sampling noise. The L4-L5 wedge mean rests on 24 angles after one was dropped, p. 38 (PDF 6).
- Exclusions, p. 34 (PDF 2): implanted devices; visible osteoporotic deformation of the vertebral body; vertebrae with visible axial rotation relative to the scanner axis; spinal columns with a large lateral slant (Inference: scoliotic spines are excluded).
- Modality and instrument, p. 34 (PDF 2): 16-detector MDCT (Siemens), 120 kV, 250 mAs, 0.75 s rotation, 16 x 0.75 mm collimation, 0.75 mm reconstructed slice thickness, 0.4 mm reconstruction index, B80s sharp kernel. Analysis in Vitrea2 (Vital Images) on average-intensity projections 3.13 mm thick.
- Levels covered: the 12 endplates from T12 inferior to S1 superior, i.e. the six disc spaces T12-L1 to L5-S1, p. 34-35 (PDF 2-3).
- Level identification: not described. The levels are simply named T12inf to S1sup; there is no statement of how T12 or S1 was recognised, no rib count and no mention of transitional anatomy.
- Statistics, p. 36 (PDF 4): multilevel linear mixed-effects model (S-Plus LME, REML, general covariance for random effects) because several vertebrae come from one person; paired t-test for the two middle points; significance at p < 0.02. The sex comparisons of Table 2 are medians with Mann-Whitney p-values, p. 39 (PDF 7); the S-on-T fits are per-endplate linear regressions, p. 38 (PDF 6).
- Nomenclature, p. 34-35 (PDF 2-3): "inferior" endplate = the caudal endplate of a body, "superior" = the cranial endplate. The variable location numbers the endplates 1 = S1sup, 2 = L5inf, 3 = L5sup, 4 = L4inf, 5 = L4sup, 6 = L3inf, 7 = L3sup, 8 = L2inf, 9 = L2sup, 10 = L1inf, 11 = L1sup, 12 = T12inf (the text says "numbered 1-11" but lists 12).

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib counts or block vertebrae; it gives no prevalence and does not say whether transitional spines were excluded. Fractures and hernias were present in the cohort but only intact vertebrae were measured (p. 34 (PDF 2)), and the authors suspect fractures near T12-L1 may have influenced the T12inf values (p. 38 (PDF 6)). Numbering: the endplates are named T12inf to S1sup and coded 1 (S1sup) to 12 (T12inf), counted upward from S1, p. 34-35 (PDF 2-3). Inference: the labels assume five lumbar vertebrae and a recognisable S1; in a 77-patient hospital series some transitional spines would be expected, and any such spine was either excluded silently or numbered by an unstated rule, so the S1sup and L5 rows may mix true S1 with a sacralised L5 or a lumbarised S1.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### Reference construction (shared by all measures)

- Three reference planes were set in oblique mode with bone window settings, p. 35 (PDF 3): a sagittal plane through the most anterior point of the vertebral body and the "posterior niche" (Inference: the midline notch of the posterior body wall); a frontal plane through the most lateral points of the body; an axial plane parallel to the endplate. The text then says measurements were made in orthogonal mode, so the scanner's sagittal plane "is not necessarily the actual mid-sagittal" section, p. 35 (PDF 3).
- In the sagittal section a line is drawn tangent to the endplate; equidistant marks along it are projected onto the endplate surface to give points 1 to 5. The frontal section gives points 6 to 10 in the same way. The frontal plane crosses the sagittal plane at point 3 (arbitrary choice), so points 3 and 8 are both "middle" but do not coincide exactly, p. 34 (PDF 2).
- Points 1, 5, 6 and 10 are where the tangent line meets the body rims, p. 34 (PDF 2). From the equations and verification text: point 5 is the anterior rim, 4 anterior quarter, 3 middle, 2 posterior quarter, 1 posterior rim; point 6 right rim, 7 right, 8 middle, 9 left, 10 left rim (pp. 36, 38 (PDF 4, 6)).
- Bone edges were found by the maximum drop in Hounsfield units next to a manually located HU maximum (Waarsing's procedure), p. 35 (PDF 3).
- A least-squares plane is fitted through the four rim points 1, 5, 6 and 10: this is the endplate reference plane, p. 35 (PDF 3).
- Figure 1, p. 35 (PDF 3), draws the scheme: a 3D rendering with the sagittal, frontal and transverse planes and the wedge angle between two adjacent endplate planes; a frontal cut with points 6 to 10 on the superior endplate; a sagittal cut with points 1 to 5; and an axial view of a lumbar vertebra in which the sagittal plane runs through the midline, the frontal plane crosses the body at about mid-depth, and the sagittal and transverse diameters are drawn across the body. Figure 1 shows the superior endplate's points, p. 34 (PDF 2).

### Endplate depth (concavity), the paper's "endplate depth"

- Definition: perpendicular distance from each surface point (2, 3, 4 in the sagittal section; 7, 8, 9 in the frontal) to the rim best-fit plane, p. 35 (PDF 3). Positive = concave in the text's convention (a convex L2sup was reported as -1.3 mm, p. 36 (PDF 4)).
- Projection: CT, oblique reformats on 3.13 mm average-intensity slabs; sagittal and frontal sections through the endplate centre.
- Levels: all 12 endplates T12inf to S1sup.
- Results, Figure 2, p. 37 (PDF 5); means of 25 per endplate, SD shown only as error bars (read from the bars of Figures 2 and 5: about 0.5 to 1.0 mm for the interior points, the widest about 0.9 to 1.0 mm at the L4inf middle and posterior points; approximate).
  - Range over individuals: 5.3 mm in the middle of a deep L4inf to -1.3 mm (convex) at the anterior point of an L2sup, p. 36 (PDF 4).
  - Shape across the endplate: smallest anteriorly, then the lateral points, largest in the middle or posteriorly; left and right similar; points 3 and 8 not significantly different, p. 36 (PDF 4). Maximum posterior for T12inf and L1inf; maximum in the middle for L3inf, L4inf, L5inf, p. 36 (PDF 4).
  - Anterior point [4] means: superior endplates 0.33 (L5sup) to 1.15 mm (S1sup); inferior endplates 0.85 (L1inf) to 1.77 mm (L5inf). Posterior point [2]: superior 0.98 (L2sup) to 1.62 mm (S1sup); inferior 1.75 (L1inf) to 2.64 mm (L5inf). Fig. 2, p. 37 (PDF 5).
  - Every inferior endplate is deeper on average than the superior endplate across the same disc, p. 37 (PDF 5) and p. 38 (PDF 6). Inference (computed from Fig. 2): this holds at every one of the six interior points at all six discs, not only on average.
  - Superior endplates (Inference, computed from Fig. 2): the sagittal maximum is posterior at L1sup (1.15) and S1sup (1.62), middle at L3sup (1.40) and L4sup (1.40), and middle and posterior nearly tied at L2sup and L5sup.
  - Left-right symmetry: the paper says left and right means are similar, p. 36 (PDF 4). Inference (computed from Fig. 2): the largest asymmetry is S1sup, right [7] 1.00 against left [9] 0.69 mm; L1inf (1.26 vs 1.54) and L5sup (0.89 vs 1.14) differ by about 0.25 mm; elsewhere within about 0.1 mm.
- Regression on level (inferior endplates, all points, p < 0.0001), p. 36 (PDF 4), depth in mm, location 2 (L5inf) to 12 (T12inf):
  - right [7] 2.46 - 0.10 x location; middle [8] 3.06 - 0.14 x location; left [9] 2.36 - 0.08 x location; anterior [4] 1.56 - 0.09 x location + 0.008 x age; middle [3] 3.17 - 0.15 x location; posterior [2] 2.75 - 0.09 x location.
  - Inference: each caudal step of one vertebra (location -2) adds about 0.3 mm of central concavity on the inferior endplate.
- Regression on level and age (superior endplates), p. 38 (PDF 6), location 1 (S1sup) to 11 (L1sup), age in years:
  - right [7] 0.46 + 0.0006 x location + 0.012 x age; middle [8] 0.88 - 0.03 x location + 0.010 x age; left [9] 0.46 + 0.007 x location + 0.011 x age; anterior [4] 0.35 - 0.05 x location + 0.010 x age; middle [3] 0.96 - 0.05 x location + 0.010 x age; posterior [2] 0.89 - 0.03 x location + 0.010 x age.
  - The text says the location trend on superior endplates held only for the anterior and posterior points and the sagittal middle point (p = 0.01) and not for the frontal points, so the listed equations are "only valid under these limited circumstances", p. 36-37 (PDF 4-5). The conclusions add that superior endplate depth also rose toward the lower lumbar levels in the sagittal plane, p. 40 (PDF 8).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: take the vertebral-body subregion; find its cranial and caudal surfaces; build the mid-sagittal plane through the most anterior body point and the posterior-wall midline notch, and the frontal plane through the widest lateral points; on each endplate surface take the rim points where those planes leave the endplate (anterior, posterior, right, left), fit a least-squares plane through these four, and report the perpendicular distance of the surface at the quarter and middle points (or the maximum over the surface). Average over a slab about 3 mm thick to match the paper. Use the surface of the mask facing the disc, on the cranio-caudal axis of the body, not the scanner z axis. Concavities are 1 to 3 mm, so voxels coarser than about 1 mm, or a mask that fills the endplate dip, will bias depth toward zero.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: a true lateral shows the sagittal profile (anterior to posterior) of the endplate as the central dip below the line joining the anterior and posterior rim corners; an AP shows the frontal profile. Superimposed lateral rims, tilt and magnification blur 1 to 3 mm concavities; the paper gives no radiographic method. Eijkelkamp's values cited for comparison came from MRI and X-ray (Table 1, p. 34 (PDF 2)).
- Across levels: inferior concavity increases monotonically L1inf to L5inf (middle [3]: 1.46, 1.86, 2.32, 2.61, 2.96 mm); T12inf (1.59) breaks the trend and is larger than L1inf. Superior concavity is flatter and not monotonic (middle [3]: L1sup 0.83, L2sup 1.00, L3sup 1.40, L4sup 1.40, L5sup 1.03, S1sup 1.42). Fig. 2, p. 37 (PDF 5). Inference (computed from Fig. 2): the inferior-to-superior concavity ratio across a disc is largest at L4-L5 (2.5) and L5-S1 (2.1), smallest at L2-L3 (1.3); within one vertebra the caudal endplate is 1.7 to 1.9 times as deep as the cranial for L1 to L4 and 2.9 times for L5.
- Sex, age, size, population: no sex effect on depth, p. 38 (PDF 6) and p. 39 (PDF 7). Superior-endplate depth rises about 0.01 mm per year of age at all points (p < 0.02); the inferior endplate showed no significant age relation, p. 37-38 (PDF 5-6), though the inferior anterior-point equation carries +0.008 x age, p. 36 (PDF 4). Inference (computed): across the cohort's ages, 21 to 86 years, the age term moves a cranial-endplate depth by about 0.65 mm, comparable to a whole between-subject SD and about half or more of the mean superior depth (0.83 to 1.42 mm at the middle point). No body height or weight was available, p. 39 (PDF 7). Population: one Dutch hospital series, ethnicity unstated.
- Error: not measured for depth itself. CT method repeatability and CT-versus-caliper agreement were measured on pig vertebrae for linear dimensions only (see Measurement error).
- Comparison with the literature, Figure 5, p. 39 (PDF 7), bars read approximately: middle, anterior and posterior depths of this study beside Eijkelkamp's (MRI and X-ray) for all 12 endplates and Lee's (a patent) for the middle point only, at L3inf to S1sup (Lee about 1.8 L3inf, 1.1 L4sup, 1.85 L4inf, 1.0 L5sup, 1.9 L5inf, 0.75 S1sup mm; no Lee bar at T12inf to L3sup). On the superior endplates L1sup to L5sup this study and Eijkelkamp agree within about 0.4 mm; on the inferior endplates this study is deeper, by about 1 to 1.7 mm posteriorly at T12inf, L1inf, L4inf and L5inf (for example L4inf posterior about 0.95 Eijkelkamp vs 2.6 here) and by about 1.4 to 1.5 mm anteriorly at L4inf and L5inf; at S1sup Eijkelkamp is about 0 (middle and posterior) and -0.35 mm (anterior) against 1.2 to 1.6 mm here. The authors read only the inferior-deeper pattern from Figure 5 and Eijkelkamp's L5-S1 exception, p. 38 (PDF 6). Inference: concavity values depend strongly on modality and method; MRI or radiograph-based depths of the caudal endplates may run 1 mm or more below sharp-kernel CT values.

### Endplate sagittal diameter (S), rim to rim

- Definition: distance between points 1 and 5, the posterior and anterior rim points on the sagittal tangent line, p. 35 (PDF 3). The authors stress it is a rim-to-rim length and differs from the outer body dimensions usually reported, p. 35 (PDF 3) and p. 38 (PDF 6).
- Projection: CT, sagittal section as above.
- Values (means, Fig. 2, p. 37 (PDF 5)): T12inf 27.2, L1sup 27.3, L1inf 28.0, L2sup 28.0, L2inf 27.9, L3sup 28.0, L3inf 28.0, L4sup 27.3, L4inf 28.8, L5sup 27.5, L5inf 26.4, S1sup 28.4 mm. SD error bars about 2 to 3 mm (read from the bars; approximate).
- Sex medians (Table 2, p. 39 (PDF 7), labelled "median endplate depth", but the title calls them diameters): women 24.8 to 28.4 mm, men 27.4 to 30.6 mm; men larger at every level except L5sup (27.7 F vs 27.6 M, p = 0.803); not significant also at L4inf (p = 0.101) and S1sup (p = 0.495), and under the paper's own p < 0.02 rule also not at L1sup (0.031), L2sup (0.040) and L5inf (0.049). Inference (computed): female/male ratio averages 0.91.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: on the body subregion, the anteroposterior extent of the cranial or caudal endplate face measured in the mid-sagittal plane between the rim edges, along the line in the endplate plane. The mask's full extent (osteophytes, cortical shell, lip of the ring apophysis) gives an outer value closer to Panjabi's endplate depth (EPD), larger than this rim-to-rim value.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: lateral radiograph, anterior to posterior endplate corners, divided by the magnification factor; matches a sagittal rim-to-rim chord if the beam is tangent to the endplate.
- Across levels: nearly constant (27 to 28 mm, p. 38 (PDF 6)); it does not separate lumbar levels. The discussion gives a range of 27.1 (T12inf) to 29.1 mm (L4inf), p. 38 (PDF 6), which does not match Fig. 2 (see Gaps).
- Error: see Measurement error.

### Endplate transverse diameter (T), rim to rim

- Definition: distance between points 6 and 10, the right and left rim points in the frontal section, p. 35 (PDF 3).
- Values (means, Fig. 2, p. 37 (PDF 5)): T12inf 36.9, L1sup 36.3, L1inf 37.3, L2sup 36.2, L2inf 37.4, L3sup 37.2, L3inf 39.7, L4sup 40.0, L4inf 44.6, L5sup 41.7, L5inf 44.1, S1sup 41.2 mm. SD error bars about 2.5 to 4 mm (read; approximate).
- Sex medians (Table 2, p. 39 (PDF 7), labelled "median endplate width"): women 32.7 to 41.8 mm, men 38.0 to 48.9 mm; men larger at all levels, p < 0.05 except S1sup (p = 0.125), and under the paper's p < 0.02 rule also not at L1inf (0.040) and L5sup (0.020); largest gaps L2inf (32.7 vs 41.1) and L4inf (41.1 vs 48.9). Inference (computed): female/male ratio averages 0.88.
- Across levels: rises from thorax to lumbar, with the step at the L3/L4 level, p. 38 (PDF 6). Inference (computed from Fig. 2): it is flat at about 36 to 37 mm from T12inf to L3sup, rises at L3inf and L4sup (about 40), peaks at L4inf and L5inf (about 44), and S1sup (41.2) is narrower than L5inf. The caudal endplate is wider than the cranial endplate across the same disc at every disc but L3-L4 (L3inf 39.7 vs L4sup 40.0), by 2.9 mm at L4-L5 and L5-S1, p. 38-39 (PDF 6-7) for the statement. Within one vertebra the caudal endplate is the wider, most at L4 (44.6 vs 40.0, ratio 1.12).
- Inference (computed): T/S ratio about 1.29 to 1.36 at T12-L3, 1.42 to 1.47 at L3inf-L4sup, 1.52 to 1.67 at L4inf to L5inf, 1.45 at S1sup: a scale-free index that separates upper from lower lumbar levels.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: left-right extent of the endplate face at the frontal plane through the widest points of the body, rim edge to rim edge.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: AP radiograph, endplate width between the lateral rim corners, corrected for magnification. Not available from a lateral.
- Error: see Measurement error.

### Sagittal diameter as a function of transverse diameter

- Linear regression S = a + b x T per endplate, p. 38 (PDF 6); the text says it was significant for 8 surfaces. Printed: S1sup 17.583 + 0.263 T (p = 0.022); L5inf 16.495 + 0.220 T (0.04); L5sup 24.838 + 0.0063 T (0.510); L4inf 19.151 + 0.222 T (0.08); L4sup 9.950 + 0.433 T (0.01); L3inf 16.207 + 0.297 T (0.02); L3sup 11.213 + 0.449 T (0.02); L2inf 17.200 + 0.286 T (0.07); L2sup 7.537 + 0.566 T (0.00); L1inf 10.993 + 0.092 T (0.00); L1sup 13.451 + 0.383 T (0.10); T12inf 15.180 + 0.323 T (0.018).
- Inference: these slopes (0.2 to 0.6) express a shared size factor between the two diameters. Two printed equations do not reproduce their own Fig. 2 means: L1inf gives S = 14.4 mm at T = 37.3 against a mean of 28.0 (an implied slope of about 0.455), and L5sup gives 25.1 against 27.5 (implied slope about 0.063, a likely misplaced decimal). Use those two only with caution.

### Wedge angle (disc)

- Definition: the angle between the planes of the two endplates facing a disc (the rim best-fit planes), computed by the "heights method" of Eijkelkamp, p. 35 (PDF 3). Inference: probably the arctangent of the anterior-minus-posterior disc height over the sagittal diameter. Measured supine (Fig. 3 caption, p. 39 (PDF 7)).
- Values (Fig. 3, p. 39 (PDF 7); read from the bars, approximate): T12-L1 about 5 deg, L1-L2 about 6.7, L2-L3 about 6, L3-L4 about 7, L4-L5 about 9.5, L5-S1 about 15.4 deg; SD bars about 1.7 (L1-L2) to 3.8 deg (L3-L4), about 5 deg at L5-S1. One L4-L5 angle was dropped as negative and more than 2 SD from the mean, its underlying measurements kept, p. 38 (PDF 6).
- Comparison: Eijkelkamp (MRI and X-ray) about 5, 5.8, 6.2, 6.9, 10.1, 13.2 deg; Nissan (X-ray, angle derived by the authors from anterior and posterior disc heights and sagittal diameters) about 3.7, 5.1, 5.5, 11.0, 15.4 deg from L1-L2 to L5-S1, Fig. 3, p. 39 (PDF 7); the agreement is taken as validation of the points, p. 40 (PDF 8).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference: angle between the rim planes fitted to the caudal endplate of one body and the cranial endplate of the next, projected onto the mid-sagittal plane for a lordotic angle.
- From a radiograph alone: Inference: lateral radiograph, angle between the lines joining anterior and posterior rim corners of the two endplates; posture (standing vs supine) changes it.
- Across levels: "generally increases from T12 to S1", p. 38 (PDF 6), abstract p. 33 (PDF 1), Fig. 3 p. 39 (PDF 7); the steep rise is at L4-L5 and L5-S1. Not strictly monotonic: L2-L3 (about 6.0) sits below L1-L2 (about 6.7), read from Fig. 3. Inference: only L4-L5 and especially L5-S1 stand out from the upper lumbar discs, whose means (5 to 7 deg) lie within one SD of each other.
- Error: no repeatability for the angle; the authors take agreement with Eijkelkamp and Nissan as validation of the point placement, p. 35 (PDF 3) and p. 40 (PDF 8).

### Intervertebral disc height (IDH)

- Definition: distance between points of the same number on the two endplates facing a disc, p. 35 (PDF 3). Patient age and sex were recorded.
- No IDH value is reported anywhere in the paper; Figure 2 sets the two depth profiles of each disc one above the other as the "disc contour" without heights. The literature values the authors cite (Table 1, p. 34 (PDF 2)): Nissan (X-ray) anterior IDH 7.8 mm at T12-L1 to 10.6 mm at L5-S1; Aharinejad (cadaver/CT/MRI) IDH 8.5 mm at L1-L2 to 10.3 mm at L5-S1.

### Verification dimensions (pig only)

- Defined on pig vertebrae for method checks, not reported for humans, p. 36 (PDF 4): outer superior and inferior sagittal and transverse endplate diameters; right and left body heights (points 6 to 6 and 10 to 10); anterior body height (5 to 5) and posterior body height (1 to 1); spinous process length, defined as printed from the foramen's "anterior wall" to the spinous process's "anterior point" in the transverse plane (Inference: read literally this spans the vertebral canal from its anterior wall to the base of the spinous process, closer to a canal sagittal depth than to a process length; a posterior tip may have been meant); transverse process width (between the most lateral aspects of the two transverse processes). The nine repeatability dimensions are the four outer endplate diameters, right and left body heights, anterior body height, spinous process length and transverse process width; the caliper comparison used six per vertebra, the four outer endplate diameters and the anterior and posterior body heights (points 5 to 5 and 1 to 1), on two vertebrae, 12 in all.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- T12inf (T12-L1 junction): concavity deepest posteriorly ([2] 1.82 mm); larger than L1inf, breaking the caudal trend. The authors offer two explanations: the shallow depth makes measurement error weigh more, or T12-L1 is a common fracture level and fractures nearby may have affected values despite care, p. 38 (PDF 6). Transverse 36.9, sagittal 27.2 mm. Wedge about 5 deg.
- L1sup: the shallowest superior endplate (middle [3] 0.83 mm), deepest posteriorly ([2] 1.15 mm).
- L1inf: deepest posteriorly ([2] 1.75 mm), p. 36 (PDF 4).
- L2sup: the individual minimum of the study, -1.3 mm (convex) at the anterior point; mean anterior [4] 0.41 mm, p. 36 (PDF 4) and Fig. 2.
- L2inf, L3sup, L3inf: concavity grows; transverse diameter begins its rise at L3inf (39.7).
- L3-L4: the one disc where the caudal endplate of the upper body (L3inf 39.7) is not wider than the cranial endplate of the lower (L4sup 40.0), Fig. 2.
- L4inf: the deepest single endplate, 5.3 mm in the middle, p. 36 (PDF 4); mean middle 2.61 mm, frontal middle 2.62 mm; widest transverse diameter (44.6 mm) and widest sagittal (28.8 mm). Largest sex gap in width (41.1 F vs 48.9 M median), Table 2, p. 39 (PDF 7).
- L5sup: shallow (middle 1.03 mm, anterior 0.33 mm, the smallest anterior mean), no sex difference in sagittal diameter, Table 2.
- L5inf: deepest mean concavity (middle [3] 2.96 mm), maximum in the middle; transverse 44.1, sagittal 26.4 mm (the smallest sagittal mean).
- S1sup (L5-S1 junction): concave on average (middle 1.42, posterior 1.62 mm, deepest posteriorly; frontal right 1.00 vs left 0.69 mm, the most asymmetric endplate, Inference computed from Fig. 2), unlike Eijkelkamp's finding that S1 is flat (Table 1, p. 34 (PDF 2); Fig. 5, p. 39 (PDF 7), where Eijkelkamp's S1sup bars are near 0). Transverse 41.2 mm, narrower than L5inf; no significant sex difference in either diameter. Eijkelkamp found inferior-superior depth differences mainly at L5-S1, p. 38 (PDF 6). Largest wedge, about 15 deg.
- Hall (cited, Table 1, p. 34 (PDF 2)): the endplate outline is printed as "cartoid" at L4 (Inference: cardioid, heart-shaped) and becomes more elliptical toward S1.

## Measurement error and reliability

- Repeatability, p. 36 (PDF 4) and p. 38 (PDF 6): one person measured nine dimensions (four outer endplate diameters, right, left and anterior body heights, spinous process length, transverse process width) three times on one pig vertebra by CT and by vernier caliper; mean of the nine SDs 0.22 mm for the CT maximum-gradient method and 0.18 mm for the caliper (N = 9 each). For the CT repeats the best image plane was chosen afresh each time.
- Accuracy, p. 36 (PDF 4) and p. 38 (PDF 6), Fig. 4 (Bland-Altman), p. 39 (PDF 7): two pig vertebrae scanned with the human protocol, then explanted, cleaned with a scalpel and measured with a Mitutoyo vernier caliper (accuracy +/-0.05 mm); 12 measurements (outer superior and inferior sagittal and transverse endplate diameters, anterior and posterior body heights, per vertebra). Mean difference caliper minus CT -0.18 mm, SD 0.66 mm, interval -1.47 to 1.12 mm (called a 95% confidence interval; Inference: it is mean +/- 1.96 SD, i.e. limits of agreement). In Fig. 4 the measured sizes lie at about 29 to 55 mm, in two clusters (four points at about 29 to 31 mm, eight at about 46 to 55 mm), and the differences lie between about -1.2 and +0.8 mm, with no visible trend against size. Inference: the four small values are the body heights and the eight large ones the endplate diameters (2 vertebrae x 2 heights, 2 x 4 diameters). The conclusion that CT sizes are accurate rests on these 12 pig measurements, p. 39 (PDF 7) and p. 40 (PDF 8).
- What was not measured: no inter-observer study, no human repeat, no repeatability of endplate concavity depth (the paper's main measure) and none of the wedge angle. The verification used outer diameters while the human study used rim-to-rim diameters, and pig, not human, bone.
- Inference: a limit of agreement of about +/-1.3 mm on a single linear dimension is as large as the mean concavity of most superior endplates (0.3 to 1.6 mm), so a single depth reading by this method may carry an error of the same order as the quantity, if the depth points share the diameter error; the paper does not test this. The authors themselves offer measurement error, weighing more on a shallow endplate, as one explanation of the T12inf anomaly, p. 38 (PDF 6).
- Between-subject spread (read from the error bars of Figs. 2, 3 and 5; approximate): depths about 0.5 to 1.0 mm SD, diameters about 2 to 4 mm SD, wedge angles about 1.7 to 3.8 deg SD and about 5 deg at L5-S1.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indication: AID design. Current AIDs need the endplates milled flat, which weakens the vertebral shell; the authors argue for implants that follow the native endplate shape, and ultimately custom implants planned from CT, p. 33 (PDF 1) and p. 40 (PDF 8).
- Implant migration and dislocation are the main post-operative problems of AIDs, especially in osteoporotic patients, p. 33 (PDF 1).
- Load-bearing logic: the caudal endplate of the upper body is deeper (and wider) than the cranial endplate of the lower body across the same disc; the authors find this hard to explain biomechanically, since disc pressure acts equally on both; Lee (a patent) found the same and Panjabi et al. found it in cervical endplates, p. 38 (PDF 6).
- The inferior endplate's larger transverse diameter is only partly explained by the extra body mass carried caudally, p. 38-39 (PDF 6-7).
- Depth is independent of sex, so the authors conclude concavity is partly independent of the body-size-related (and so sex-related) geometry, p. 39 (PDF 7).
- Pitfall: the scanner's orthogonal sagittal plane is not the true mid-sagittal section of a vertebra, p. 35 (PDF 3).
- Pitfall: rim-to-rim diameters are smaller than the outer diameters reported elsewhere, p. 35 (PDF 3) and p. 38 (PDF 6).
- Pitfall: rotation, lateral slant, implants and osteoporotic deformity were excluded; fractures near T12-L1 may still bias T12inf, p. 34 (PDF 2) and p. 38 (PDF 6).
- Pitfall: endplates are not always concave; an anterior L2sup point reached -1.3 mm (convex), p. 36 (PDF 4).
- Pitfall: "endplate depth" means concavity in the text and Figure 2 but the anteroposterior diameter in Table 2, p. 37 (PDF 5) and p. 39 (PDF 7).
- Pitfall: wedge angles here are supine CT values (Fig. 3 caption, p. 39 (PDF 7)); the comparison values come from MRI and radiographs (Table 1, p. 34 (PDF 2)).
- Pitfall (Inference, from Fig. 5, p. 39 (PDF 7)): concavity values differ by modality and method by up to about 1.7 mm at the caudal lumbar endplates, more than the between-subject SD, so values from different studies are not interchangeable.
- The cited literature (Table 1, p. 34 (PDF 2)): cortical shell thickness superior 0.25 to 0.26 mm, inferior 0.29 to 0.52 mm (Silva); ventral shell 0.308 mm, dorsal 0.272 mm (Ritzel); body concavity increases with age (Twomey, who also computed an endplate index of concavity, p. 33 (PDF 1)); posterior body height falls from L3 to L5, 29.9 to 28.4 mm (Zhou, CT); all dimensions lower in a Chinese (Singaporean) group (Tan); Eijkelkamp's mean lumbar endplate depth 1.2 mm (range -1.1 to 3.6 mm).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Build the endplate reference as a least-squares plane through four rim points (anterior, posterior, right, left), not through the whole surface, and measure concavity perpendicular to it (p. 35 (PDF 3)).
2. Set the sagittal plane through the most anterior point of the body and the posterior-wall midline notch, and the frontal plane through the widest lateral points; do not trust the scanner's orthogonal sagittal plane as mid-sagittal (p. 35 (PDF 3)).
3. Find bone edges by the steepest HU drop next to the local HU maximum, not by a fixed threshold (p. 35 (PDF 3)). Inference: on a mask, use the boundary of the body subregion facing the disc, and check it against the HU profile where the CT is at hand, since a mask that fills the endplate dip flattens the concavity.
4. On every lumbar disc, expect the caudal endplate of the upper body to be deeper than the cranial endplate of the lower body (p. 37 (PDF 5)); Inference: use this to confirm the cranio-caudal orientation of a mask sequence, but not as a level marker by itself, since it holds at every disc.
5. Expect inferior-endplate central concavity to grow steadily from L1 (about 1.5 mm) to L5 (about 3.0 mm) (Fig. 2, p. 37 (PDF 5); equations p. 36 (PDF 4)), about 0.3 mm per level. Inference: with a between-subject SD of about 0.5 to 1.0 mm, one vertebra's caudal concavity only shifts the odds between upper (L1, L2) and lower (L4, L5) lumbar levels and cannot tell adjacent levels apart. Do not expect T12inf to be shallower than L1inf: it was larger in this series (p. 36 (PDF 4), p. 38 (PDF 6)).
6. Use the transverse endplate diameter, not the sagittal diameter, to separate upper from lower lumbar levels: the sagittal diameter stays at 27 to 28 mm (p. 38 (PDF 6)), the transverse rises at the L3/L4 level (p. 38 (PDF 6)) and peaks at L4inf and L5inf (Fig. 2, p. 37 (PDF 5)). Inference (computed from Fig. 2): the transverse-to-sagittal ratio, about 1.3 at T12inf to L3sup and 1.5 to 1.7 at L4inf to L5inf, does the same without a size factor.
7. At the lumbosacral junction, expect S1sup (41.2 mm) to be narrower than L5inf (44.1 mm) (Fig. 2, p. 37 (PDF 5)) and the L5-S1 wedge to be the largest (about 15 deg supine, SD about 5 deg; Fig. 3, p. 39 (PDF 7)). Inference: a disc with a wedge near 15 deg whose upper endplate is one of the two widest caudal endplates (L4inf 44.6, L5inf 44.1, too close to tell apart) and whose lower endplate is narrower is the L5-S1 candidate; the L4-L5 wedge (about 9.5 +/- 2 deg) overlaps it within its SD, and the paper covers no transitional spine, so check rib count and sacral shape before naming it.
8. Correct diameters for sex (women's medians about 9 percent smaller sagittally and 12 percent smaller transversely, computed from Table 2, p. 39 (PDF 7)) but not concavity depth (p. 38 (PDF 6)).
9. Add about 0.01 mm per year of age to the expected cranial-endplate concavity (p. 37-38 (PDF 5-6)); do not age-correct the caudal endplate except perhaps its anterior point (p. 36 (PDF 4)).
10. Accept a convex endplate point (down to about -1.3 mm) as normal variation, not as a segmentation error (p. 36 (PDF 4)).
11. Skip or down-weight fractured, osteoporotic, rotated or laterally tilted vertebrae, and treat T12-L1 values with caution (p. 34 (PDF 2), p. 38 (PDF 6)).
12. When reading Figure 2 values, take the magnitude as the concavity: the printed signs are negative for inferior and positive for superior endplates while the plots draw them the other way (p. 37 (PDF 5)).
13. When a value is labelled "endplate depth", check which meaning is meant: concavity in this paper's text and Fig. 2, but anteroposterior diameter in Table 2 (p. 39 (PDF 7)) and in Panjabi 1992's EPD (Inference, from this set's panjabi1992 note).
14. On a lateral radiograph, measure the sagittal endplate diameter and the disc wedge between rim-corner lines, dividing lengths by magnification; take the transverse diameter only from an AP view (Inference; the paper gives no radiographic method).
15. Compare a measured concavity only with values taken by the same modality and method: the inferior endplate depths here run about 1 to 1.7 mm above Eijkelkamp's MRI and X-ray values at T12inf, L1inf, L4inf and L5inf, and S1sup is about 1.4 mm here against about 0 there (Fig. 5, p. 39 (PDF 7), bars read approximately; the paper notes only the S1 difference, Table 1 p. 34 (PDF 2), p. 38 (PDF 6)).
16. Expect the caudal endplate of each lumbar vertebra to be wider than its cranial endplate, by about 1 to 2.5 mm at L1 to L3 and L5 and 4.6 mm at L4 (Inference, computed from Fig. 2, p. 37 (PDF 5)); Panjabi 1992 shows the same pattern (this set's panjabi1992 note).
17. Treat wedge angles from supine CT as supine values (Fig. 3 caption, p. 39 (PDF 7)); Inference: a standing radiograph or an intraoperative position will change them, so use the ordering (L5-S1 largest, then L4-L5) rather than the absolute degrees.

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of any transitional or numbering anomaly and does not say how levels were counted. What it does give the decoder are junction cues with no anomaly in view: the L5-S1 wedge is the largest (about 15 deg supine), S1sup is narrower than L5inf, and T12inf is deeper than L1inf (Figs. 2 and 3, pp. 37 and 39 (PDF 5 and 7)).

(b) Measures:
- Panjabi's endplate width (EPW) and endplate depth (EPD, anteroposterior) for L1 to L5 correspond to this paper's transverse and sagittal rim-to-rim diameters, and the paper adds T12inf and S1sup. Bias warning: these are rim-to-rim chords on one sagittal and one frontal section, smaller than outer extremes. Inference (from this set's panjabi1992 note, Panjabi 1992 Table 2): Panjabi's EPDu is 34.1 to 35.5 mm and EPDl 35.3 at L1 to 33.2 at L5, about 6 to 8 mm above this paper's sagittal chords (26.4 to 28.8 mm); Panjabi's EPWu is 41.2 at L1 to 47.3 at L5 and EPWl 43.3 at L1 to 49.5 at L4, about 5 mm above the transverse chords (36.2 to 44.6 mm). The patterns agree (depth flat, width rising to L4-L5, the lower width above the upper in each vertebra), but this paper's width-to-depth ratios run about 0.04 to 0.18 higher than Panjabi's at the same endplate (Panjabi 1.21 to 1.36 upper, 1.23 to 1.49 lower; here 1.29 to 1.52 superior, 1.33 to 1.67 inferior, computed), because the sagittal chord falls further short of the outer depth than the transverse chord does of the outer width. A CT mask's full extent sits closer to Panjabi's outer values than to these chords, so do not pool the two without an offset.
- Candidate additions: (1) central concavity of the cranial and caudal endplates per level, with the caudal deeper and rising L1 to L5; (2) disc wedge angle per disc, T12-L1 to L5-S1, rising steeply at L4-L5 and L5-S1; (3) T/S ratio per endplate as a size-free shape index; (4) the within-vertebra caudal-to-cranial width difference, largest at L4. The generator's level range (C3 to L5) lacks S1sup; this paper supplies S1sup values for the junction state.
- A mask-derived concavity is sensitive to voxel size and to whether the mask fills the endplate dip; the paper's values come from 3.13 mm average-intensity slabs on 0.75 mm slices with a sharp kernel.

(c) Measurement error: CT method repeatability SD 0.22 mm (one observer, three repeats of nine dimensions on one pig vertebra); CT versus caliper bias -0.18 mm, SD 0.66 mm, limits about -1.47 to 1.12 mm, on 12 dimensions of two pig vertebrae of about 29 to 55 mm (p. 36 and p. 38 (PDF 4 and 6), Fig. 4 p. 39 (PDF 7)). No error for concavity depth or wedge angle; Inference: for a mask-derived concavity use at least the 0.66 mm SD as a floor, since depths of 0.3 to 3 mm are of that order. Between-subject SD (read from bars, approximate): depth about 0.5 to 1.0 mm, diameters about 2 to 4 mm, wedge about 1.7 to 3.8 deg and about 5 deg at L5-S1.

(d) Covariates: sex acts on diameters (female/male median ratio about 0.91 sagittal and 0.88 transverse, computed from Table 2, p. 39 (PDF 7); no significant sex difference at S1sup or for the L5sup sagittal) and not on concavity; age raises cranial-endplate concavity about 0.01 mm per year; no height or weight. Population: Dutch hospital patients aged 21 to 86 with spinal problems, ethnicity unstated, 25 endplates per level; a cited study found smaller dimensions in Chinese Singaporeans (Table 1, p. 34 (PDF 2)).

## Gaps and conflicts

- No IDH values despite the title and a defined method (p. 35 (PDF 3)); the "intervertebral volume" is shown only as paired depth profiles.
- No account of how levels were identified, and nothing on transitional anatomy, rib counts or LSTV.
- Per-level sample is 25 from 77 patients with no per-level patient or sex breakdown; Table 2's medians by sex rest on unknown subgroup sizes.
- The location variable is said to run 1 to 11 but 12 positions are listed (p. 34-35 (PDF 2-3)).
- The text says the superior location trend held for "anterior and posterior points [1] and [5]" (p. 36 (PDF 4)), but 1 and 5 are rim points with zero depth by construction; Inference: points [2] and [4] are meant.
- The text says the superior frontal points showed no location relation (p. 37 (PDF 5)), yet the superior age-and-location table still prints location terms for [7], [8] and [9] (p. 38 (PDF 6)); those for [7] (+0.0006) and [9] (+0.007) are near zero and positive, consistent with no effect, while [8] (-0.03) matches the sagittal points. No p-value or standard error is given for any coefficient.
- The text says inferior depth has no significant age relation (p. 38 (PDF 6)), yet the inferior anterior [4] equation carries +0.008 x age and the results say an age relation was found at point [4] (p. 36 (PDF 4)).
- The S-on-T regression is called significant for 8 surfaces, but under the paper's own p < 0.02 rule only 4 (L4sup, L2sup, L1inf, T12inf) qualify; 8 matches a p <= 0.05 rule. Two equations (L1inf, L5sup) do not reproduce their Fig. 2 means (Inference, computed).
- Table 2 is titled endplate diameters but labels the sagittal diameter as "depth", colliding with the paper's use of depth for concavity.
- Discussion gives sagittal diameters from 27.1 (T12inf) to 29.1 mm (L4inf) (p. 38 (PDF 6)), while Fig. 2 gives 27.2 for T12inf, 28.8 for L4inf and a minimum of 26.4 at L5inf.
- Figure 2 prints inferior depths as negative and superior as positive but plots them in the opposite direction; the text uses positive for concave.
- Table 1 cites Panjabi ref. 6 (lumbar 1992) for the cervical cortical shell finding, which belongs to ref. 7; Figure 5's caption cites ref. 5 (Nissan), whose data do not appear in Figure 5; Lee's comparison data come from a patent.
- The method says reference planes were set in oblique mode but measurements made in orthogonal mode (p. 35 (PDF 3)); how points were taken off the oblique planes is not fully clear.
- Verification used pig vertebrae and outer diameters only; no human or inter-observer reliability and none for the concavity.
- The spinous process length of the verification is defined from the foramen's anterior wall to the spinous process's anterior point (p. 36 (PDF 4)), which read literally measures across the canal; Inference: a posterior tip may have been meant.
- Figure 5's "This study" bars at L4inf (about 2.7 middle and 2.6 posterior, read from the bars) sit 0.1 to 0.2 mm above Figure 2's printed values (2.61 and 2.39); the other levels match within reading error (Fig. 2 p. 37 (PDF 5), Fig. 5 p. 39 (PDF 7)). Figure 5 also spells Eijkelkamp as "Eikelkamp".
- Table 1 prints Hall's endplate shape as "cartoid" at L4 (p. 34 (PDF 2)); Inference: cardioid. Lee's patent reference, US2008046082, is dated 2003 in the list (p. 40 (PDF 8)); Inference: the number reads as a 2008 publication.
- Fig. 5 shows this study's inferior endplates 1 to 1.7 mm deeper than Eijkelkamp's at several levels (read approximately), a disagreement the discussion does not address beyond L5-S1 (p. 38 (PDF 6)).
- Conflict with other sources: Eijkelkamp reports a flat S1 endplate, this paper a concave S1sup (about 1.4 mm middle). Inference (from this set's panjabi1992 note): Panjabi's 1992 lumbar endplate depths and widths are outer dimensions from a morphometer's digitized rim loop on cadaveric vertebrae and run about 5 to 8 mm larger than these rim-to-rim CT chords; any pooled prior must reconcile the definitions.
