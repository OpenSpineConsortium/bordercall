## What the paper is

- Design: cadaveric dry-bone morphometry of the articular facets, which the paper calls the final
  stage of a series of anatomic studies (cervical 1991, thoracic 1991, lumbar 1992; refs 24-26).
  Methods are given in brief and referred to the thoracic paper, "the initial document" (ref 25)
  (p. 1298, PDF 1). The paper does not say in words that the twelve spines are the same as the
  earlier papers'; it prints the same twelve-specimen summary (below), and bordercall's provenance
  treats all four papers as one sample (Inference from the identical summaries).
- Nomenclature (Table 1, p. 1299, PDF 2): linear FCH (facet height), FCW (facet width), IFH
  (interfacet height), IFW (interfacet width); areal FCA (facet area); angular CAX and CAY (card
  angles about the X and Y axes), S (sagittal plane angle), T (transverse plane angle); suffixes
  s superior, i inferior, r right, l left (FCWsr = superior right facet width).
- Specimens: 12 fresh autopsy spines, "axis to last lumbar vertebra", radiographed to identify
  "any significant abnormalities" (pp. 1298-1299, PDF 1-2); no abnormality, exclusion or
  replacement is reported. Disarticulated into 276 vertebrae (p. 1299, PDF 2; abstract p. 1298),
  which is 12 spines x 23 levels (C2-C7, T1-T12, L1-L5). Data pooled from "the 12 vertebrae at
  each level" (p. 1299, PDF 2); "A total of 12 C2 to L5 spines" (p. 1300, PDF 3).
- Subjects: mean age 46.3 years (range 19-59), mean weight 67.8 kg (range 54-85), mean height
  167.8 cm (range 157-178), male:female 8:4 (p. 1299, PDF 2). No country, ethnicity or cause of
  death in this paper. Inference: the first department is Yale (New Haven, Connecticut), so a
  United States autopsy source is likely but not stated. bordercall's provenance holds these
  twelve to the thoracic paper's Table 1, whose heaviest specimen is 84 kg, not 85; that table
  (ref 25, p. 889, read on its page image) also gives the causes of death, six of the twelve
  malignant (four carcinomas, carcinomatosis, acute lymphocytic leukaemia), which may bear on bone
  quality (Inference that these are the same twelve).
- Preparation: all soft tissue removed (whether articular cartilage went with it is not said);
  two pointed threaded rods, 2 mm in diameter and at least 2.5 cm long, fixed in each body; both
  ends of one rod and one end of the other are the three marker points; AP and lateral
  radiographs placed the markers in the body coordinate system (p. 1299, PDF 2).
- Instrument: a purpose-built morphometer (ref 22) with one linear and two rotational
  displacement transducers whose axes meet at one point, giving spherical coordinates (latitude,
  longitude, radius); its tip was placed on each point and on the three markers, giving 3D
  coordinates in a computer (p. 1299, PDF 2).
- Facet digitising: each facet surface was digitised as a set of points covering it,
  enough that straight lines between adjacent points described its geometry
  (p. 1299, PDF 2). The number of points per facet is not given.
- Statistics: means and standard errors of the mean (SEM), n = 12 at every level and side
  (p. 1299, PDF 2; Tables 2-7). Table 8 (card angles) prints means only. No test is named,
  although the results call some changes "significant" (p. 1300, PDF 3; p. 1304, PDF 7).
- Levels: C2 to L5, superior and inferior facets, right and left. The C1 facets, the sacral
  facets and the occipital condyles were not measured.
- The abstract's summary ranges (width 9.6-16.3 mm, height 10.2-18.4, area 72.3-211.9 mm2,
  interfacet width 20.8-40.6, interfacet height 12.2-33.0, T 41.0-86.0, S 67.4-154.8, X-card
  41.0-86.0, Y-card 5.8-66.1) are stated for the facets of C3 to L5, with the superior C2 facet
  left out (p. 1298, PDF 1); three of their minima are not the tables' (Gaps).
- Why it was done, as the introduction puts it: vertebral shapes in atlases are often artists'
  drawings from inspection (refs 15, 16); before this paper the facets had been quantified only
  regionally, Francis for the cervical facets (ref 12), Malmivaara for the thoracolumbar junction
  (ref 20), Horwitz and Smith, Skipor et al and Ahmed et al for lumbar facet orientation or
  geometry (refs 17, 31, 3), while the studies of other vertebral elements (refs 5, 13, 18, 19,
  24-26, 28, 34) did not focus on the facets (p. 1298, PDF 1); the discussion lists the regional
  facet studies as cervical (ref 12) and thoracolumbar (refs 3, 5, 8, 17, 20, 28, 31, 33), none as
  comprehensive (p. 1308, PDF 11).
- Method source: this paper gives its method "in brief" and points to the thoracic paper (ref 25)
  for the full description (p. 1298, PDF 1). That paper (Panjabi et al 1991, Spine 16:888-901,
  p. 889, Figure 1; read here only for the axes) defines Z as the normal to the posterior-wall
  plane and draws it pointing anteriorly, X parallel to the line from the right to the left
  superior corner of the posterior wall and pointing left, Y perpendicular to the Z-X plane
  (cranial); it gives the instrument's largest point-location error as +/-0.5 mm and says some
  planes were fitted to up to 20 points. Inference: the facets paper used the same frame, since it
  cites ref 25 for its method and prints the same four corner points.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper reports no anatomical anomaly: no lumbosacral transitional vertebra, no
sacralisation or lumbarisation, no L6, no T13, no rib count, no block vertebra. How thoracic and
lumbar levels were identified is not said. The count 276 = 12 x 23 (p. 1299, PDF 2) means every
spine contributed exactly C2-C7, T1-T12 and L1-L5. Inference: either none of the twelve had a
numerical variant, or such a spine was not reported; the phrase "last lumbar vertebra" (p. 1298,
PDF 1) leaves open which vertebra was called last, and no sacrum was kept, so a sacralised L5 or a
lumbarised S1 could not have been recognised on the specimen.

The one transition the paper describes is the normal thoracolumbar change of facet orientation:
on average it happens at the T12-L1 joint, between the inferior facets of T12 and the superior
facets of L1 (p. 1304, PDF 7), and the paper uses it to identify T12 (p. 1307, PDF 10). It also
names the cervicothoracic and C2-C3 changes as regional boundaries (below). The SEMs of the
sagittal-plane angle are 0.9-2.8 deg at every facet from T1 to T10, but 4.7-5.5 at the T11-T12
joint (inferior T11 5.3/4.7, superior T12 5.5/5.1), 8.2-9.5 at the T12-L1 joint (inferior T12
9.5/8.8, superior L1 8.9/8.2) and 3-9 through the lumbar spine (Tables 6, 7, p. 1303, PDF 6); as
spreads (SEM x sqrt(12)) that is about 3-10 deg in the mid-thoracic spine against 16-33 deg at the
junction. Inference: this is consistent with the switch falling at a different joint in some of
the twelve spines (one higher or one lower), with partly transitional facets, or with unstable
plane fits to curved facets; the T11 inferior and T12 superior means (72.2/70.5, 72.5/67.4) sit a
little below the thoracic plateau rather than above it, so the outliers there are not simply
lumbar-type facets on the 0-180 scale. The paper does not report the spines one by one, so how
often the switch is not at T12-L1 cannot be read from it. Malmivaara's superior T12 angle of 98
deg beside Panjabi's 70 deg (Table 10, p. 1308, PDF 11) also says the T12 facet type differs
between samples.

For the decoder: this paper gives no anomaly prevalence at all. Inference: even read as 0 numerical
variants in 12 spines, the one-sided 95% upper bound is about 22% (1 - 0.05^(1/12)), so no prior
can be drawn from it; the screening radiographs' findings are not reported (pp. 1298-1299,
PDF 1-2).

## Measures, each as a recipe

### The vertebral frame and the facet plane (apply to every measure below)

- Frame: four extra points on the posterior wall of the body (its left and right, upper and lower
  edges) defined, by least squares, the frontal (XY), transverse (XZ) and sagittal (YZ) planes;
  the digitised centre of the upper end-plate is the origin (p. 1299, PDF 2; Figure 1, p. 1299,
  PDF 2, an anterior view of a lumbar vertebra with X to the figure's right and Y up). The text
  says the left facet's sagittal angle is positive "since it was in the direction of the positive
  X-axis" (p. 1300, PDF 3), so +X is the vertebra's left. This paper draws Z (Figure 2, p. 1300,
  PDF 3) but does not name its direction; the thoracic paper it cites for the method draws Z
  anterior and X to the left (ref 25, p. 889, Figure 1), which makes X, Y, Z right-handed. The
  card normals implied by Table 8 point up and toward -Z, that is up and back, as a superior
  facet's articular surface faces (Inference: consistent with Z anterior).
- Consequence: every facet angle is to this vertebra's own planes, anchored on the posterior wall
  of its body, not to the column, the image or gravity. Lordosis, kyphosis and scoliosis do not
  enter the angles.
- Facet plane: the facet surface was approximated by a least-squares best-fit plane through its
  digitised points (p. 1300, PDF 3; Figure 2A). The authors say the facets are in fact curved,
  "especially in the lumbar region", and chose the plane as the simplest useful description
  (pp. 1306-1307, PDF 9-10).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): build the frame from the body's posterior wall (fit a
  plane to the body voxels facing the canal, between the pedicle roots; its normal away from the
  canal is +Z; Y runs from the wall's caudal edge midpoint to its cranial edge midpoint; X = Y x Z
  points left; origin at the centroid of the upper end-plate). Rotate the instance into this frame
  before any extent or angle. The facet surface is the face of the articular process that meets
  its partner across the joint: for the superior facet of vertebra k, the boundary voxels of k's
  superior articular process (SPINEPS subregion masks give left and right superior and inferior
  articular processes, as far as I know) that lie within about 2-3 mm of the inferior articular
  process of k-1 on the same side; for the inferior facet, the mirror case with k+1. Without
  subregions, take the boundary voxels of instance k posterior to the canal that lie within that
  distance of instance k-1 or k+1. Fit the plane by principal components of those surface points
  (the normal is the least-variance axis). The C2 superior facet's partner is C1 and the L5
  inferior facet's is S1, so those need C1 and the sacrum in the segmentation.
- From a radiograph alone: see each measure. The paper's own statement is that facet angles to the
  transverse plane can be read from lateral radiographs accurately only where the sagittal-plane
  angle is near 90 deg, the cervical and thoracic regions, and the CT angle to the sagittal plane
  only where the transverse-plane angle is near 90 deg, the lumbar region (p. 1309, PDF 12).
- Sex, age, size, population (all measures): none. Eight men and four women aged 19-59 are pooled;
  no subgroup, no correlation with height or weight, no scaling (p. 1299, PDF 2).
- Error (all measures): a Plexiglas "pseudovertebra" with faces cut at 30, 45 and 60 deg, each
  carrying millimetre graph paper with drawn shapes (triangles, rectangles, circles), three marker
  points and parts of known size and inclination, was digitised and computed with the same
  protocol and programs; the error was under 5% in every case, for linear, area and angular
  parameters (p. 1300, PDF 3). No intra- or inter-observer repeat, no repeat digitising of a real
  facet, no instrument resolution is given in this paper; the thoracic paper gives the
  morphometer's largest point-location error as +/-0.5 mm (ref 25, p. 889).

### FCW (FCWsr, FCWsl, FCWir, FCWil): facet width

- Definition: a linear dimension of the facet seen along its normal ("Facet oriented to provide
  normal view", Figure 1, p. 1299, PDF 2), drawn across the facet (horizontal in the figure).
  The methods say software computed the "major and minor linear dimensions" (p. 1299, PDF 2),
  but the width-to-height ratio exceeds 1 at C5-T1 and elsewhere (Table 2, p. 1301, PDF 4), so
  width is not always the minor dimension. Inference: width is the in-plane extent across the
  facet, roughly mediolateral (or anteroposterior-oblique for the lumbar facets), and height the
  in-plane extent at right angles to it, roughly craniocaudal; the exact algorithm is not given.
- Units mm; dry bone; morphometer; C2-L5, four facets per vertebra (Tables 2, 3, p. 1301, PDF 4).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): project the facet surface points onto the fitted plane;
  take the extent along the in-plane direction closest to the vertebra's X axis (or the second
  principal axis if width and height are taken as minor and major). Never take the extent along
  the vertebra's X axis without first laying the facet flat: an inclined facet's projected extent
  is foreshortened by the cosine of its tilt.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): not reliably. Where the facet is seen edge-on (cervical and
  thoracic joints on a lateral view, S near 90 deg) its width lies along the beam and is lost.
- Across levels (superior, Fig 3A p. 1305, PDF 8; Table 2): largest at C2 (16.8 right, 16.0 left),
  then L4 and L5; a sharp fall from C2 to C3 (11.4, 10.8); C3 to T1 larger than mid-thoracic,
  a local peak at T1 (13.3, 14.0); smallest T6-T7 (9.6 right); slight rise toward the
  thoracolumbar junction; a large rise through L3 to L5 (16.3, 14.9) (p. 1300, PDF 3).
  Inferior (Fig 3B; Table 3): same pattern, rising C3 to C7, smallest mid-thoracic, rising in the
  lumbar spine; at C2 the inferior facet is the size of the subjacent levels' (11.4, 10.4), not
  large like C2's superior (p. 1301, PDF 4). The smallest width of the whole column is the right
  inferior facet of T12 (9.5), at the transitional joint, against 12.4 at L1 (Table 3); on the
  left, T12's inferior width (9.8) is not the minimum, which is 9.7 at T5 and T6 (L1 left 10.7).
- Error: width SEMs 0.2-1.0 mm in Tables 2 and 3 (SD = SEM x sqrt(12), about 0.7-3.5 mm; the
  largest, 1.0, at superior right T10 and L4, superior left C4 and L2).

### FCH (FCHsr, FCHsl, FCHir, FCHil): facet height

- Definition: the facet's other in-plane dimension, drawn along it (vertical in Figure 1,
  p. 1299, PDF 2); same caveats as FCW.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): extent of the in-plane projected surface along the
  in-plane direction closest to the vertebra's Y axis. Taking the extent along Y itself
  understates it by about sin(T): at T near 45 deg (C3-C6) a craniocaudal extent is about 0.7 of
  the facet height.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): on a true lateral view of the cervical and thoracic spine
  the joint is seen nearly edge-on and the length of the joint line approximates FCH; in the lumbar
  spine the joint is oblique to a lateral beam and FCH cannot be read; magnification applies.
- Across levels: superior (Table 2) C2 18.0/17.7 (largest), C3-C7 10.2-12.5, T1-T11 10.8-13.0,
  T12 13.4/12.5, L1 12.7/12.2, then a jump to L2 14.6/14.6 and on to L5 17.4/17.5. Inferior
  (Table 3) C2 13.0/11.8, C3-C7 10.8-13.9, T2-T8 10.3-11.8, T9-T12 11.7-13.3, then a jump at L1
  (15.3/15.7) and on to L5 18.4/17.3. The jumps sit on either side of the L1-L2 joint (inferior L1
  with superior L2): by size, the lumbar-type joint starts at L1-L2, while by orientation it
  starts at T12-L1 (Inference from Tables 2, 3, 6, 7).
- Error: height SEMs 0.2-1.2 mm (SD about 0.7-4.2 mm; the largest, 1.2, at superior right C4,
  superior left T1 and inferior left C3, Tables 2, 3).

### FCW/FCH: facet width to height (derived, per side)

- Definition: FCW/FCH, printed per facet and side (Tables 2, 3).
- Across levels (Fig 3C, p. 1305, PDF 8): rises from C2 to C7, falls to T7, rises to T10, falls
  into the lumbar region (p. 1301, PDF 4); the same for superior and inferior (p. 1302, PDF 5).
  Peaks above 1 at C5-T1 (superior right C6 1.14, left C7 1.20); lowest at L1-L2 (inferior left
  L1 0.68; superior right L2 0.76). In the discussion the ratio averaged over superior and
  inferior is smallest at C2, about equal at C3-C4 and higher at C5-C7 (p. 1307, PDF 10).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the ratio of the two in-plane extents above; it is
  size-free, but inherits the facet-boundary error of both.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (Inference; the facet is never seen face-on).
- Sex, age, size, population: none; as a ratio of lengths it should not scale with size
  (Inference).
- Error: inherits FCW's and FCH's; bordercall's provenance finds 2 of the 23 superior right ratios
  of Table 2 not reproducible from their own means (C4 printed 0.96 against 12.0/12.3 = 0.976, C5
  1.04 against 12.3/12.0 = 1.025), the other three columns all reproducible.

### FCA (FCAsr, FCAsl, FCAir, FCAil): facet surface area

- Definition: the digitised facet outline was projected onto the frontal (XY), sagittal (YZ) and
  transverse (ZX) planes by dropping one coordinate; the total area is the "euclidean sum" (square
  root of the sum of squares) of the three projected areas (p. 1299, PDF 2). Units mm2.
- Inference: for a flat facet this is exactly its true area (the length of the area vector); for a
  curved facet it is the area of its flat "shadow" along the mean normal and understates the
  curved area. A meshed CT surface area would be larger than Panjabi's on curved lumbar facets.
- Elliptical approximation: area = pi x FCW x FCH / 4, drawn in Figure 4 (p. 1303, PDF 6; Fig 4
  p. 1305, PDF 8); it "slightly overestimated" the measured area for both facets (p. 1309,
  PDF 12). Inference, computed from the means of Tables 2-4 over all 92 facet cells: the ellipse
  exceeds the measured area at 90 cells, by a median of about 17% (interquartile about 10-21%),
  up to 46% (superior right C7, 116.9 against 79.9; inferior left T1 46%, superior right T2 40%);
  at superior L2 it falls short instead (right -8%, 127.3 against 138.0; left -12%, 130.7 against
  148.2). So the overestimate is neither "slight" everywhere nor universal.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): compute the vector area of the triangulated facet
  surface (sum of triangle area vectors, then its length), which is Panjabi's formula; or the area
  of the outline projected onto the fitted plane. Do not use the mesh's scalar area on lumbar
  facets.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible from one projection (Inference).
- Across levels (Fig 4; Table 4, p. 1302, PDF 5): superior largest at C2 (211.3, 205.1) and in the
  lumbar region (L5 211.9, 199.1); "remarkably constant" from C3 to T12 (p. 1302, PDF 5), about
  73-115; inferior mimics superior except at C2, whose inferior area (94.6, 85.0) is that of the
  lower cervical levels (pp. 1302-1303, PDF 5-6). The superior lumbar rise is at L2 (138.0, 148.2
  against 96.7, 99.2 at L1); the inferior rise is at L1 (127.9, 125.9 against 90.8, 90.0 at T12).
  A local superior peak at T1 (102.9, 114.8).
- Error: SEMs 2.0-21.8 mm2 (SD about 7-76 mm2); the cervical areas are the noisiest (C3-C5
  superior SEM 8.5-19.2 and inferior left C3 19.3, coefficients of variation of about 35-73%).

### IFWs, IFWi: interfacet width, superior and inferior

- Definition: the distance between the centroids of the two superior facets (IFWs) and of the two
  inferior facets (IFWi) (p. 1300, PDF 3); "the lateral distance between superior or inferior
  facets" (p. 1303, PDF 6); Figure 1 draws IFWs across the top and IFWi across the bottom. Whether
  it is the 3D distance between centroids or its X component is not said; left and right centroids
  should differ little in Y and Z, so the two should be close (Inference).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): centroid of each fitted facet surface; distance between
  left and right.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): an AP view (beam along Z) shows the X separation of the
  facet centres directly, times magnification, which makes IFW the natural AP facet measure;
  Fawcett's frontal shapes (below) are drawn on the same projection.
- Across levels (Fig 5A, p. 1305, PDF 8; Table 5, p. 1302, PDF 5): largest in the cervical region
  (IFWs peaks 39.9 at C6, IFWi 40.5 at C5), falling sharply through the upper thoracic spine to a
  minimum at T5 (20.8 both), rising gradually to L1 and more steeply to L5 (IFWs 35.0; IFWi 40.6,
  the largest interfacet width of the column) (p. 1304, PDF 7). C2's IFWs (30.3) is below C3's
  (35.9). Inference: IFWi of level k matches IFWs of level k+1, as it must for one joint (C2 35.9 /
  C3 35.9; C7 33.6 / T1 33.2; L4 34.8 / L5 35.0), a congruence check between neighbouring
  instances; from the C2-C3 joint to the T10-T11 joint the pairs agree within 0.6 mm, but from the
  T11-T12 joint to the L3-L4 joint they part by 1.3-2.3 mm, the lower vertebra's IFWs always the
  larger (T11 IFWi 24.6 / T12 IFWs 25.9; T12 24.9 / L1 26.2; L1 24.8 / L2 26.4; L2 26.6 / L3 28.6;
  L3 29.1 / L4 31.4), while L4-L5 agrees again (0.2); allow about 2.5 mm there (Table 5).
- Error: SEMs 0.4-2.1 mm (smallest IFWi at T12, 0.4; largest at L3-L5, 1.5-2.1; Table 5).

### IFHr, IFHl: interfacet height, right and left

- Definition: the distance between the superior and inferior facets of one side, "the vertical
  distance between left or right facets" (pp. 1303-1304, PDF 6-7; p. 1300, PDF 3 says only that
  the heights were between the left and right facets); Figure 1 draws IFHr on the
  vertebra's right. Whether centroid to centroid, and whether the whole distance or its Y
  component, is not said explicitly. Inference: centroid to centroid, as for the width.
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): distance (or Y component) from the superior facet
  surface centroid to the inferior facet surface centroid on the same side.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): on a lateral view, the vertical distance between the centre
  of the superior and inferior joint of one articular column; left and right superimpose, so a
  lateral view gives about their mean; on an AP view, the same distance along each facet column.
- Across levels (Fig 5A; Table 5): lowest in the cervical region (C3 12.2/13.0, C5 13.3/12.7),
  rising gradually through the thoracic and upper lumbar spine to a peak at L2 (33.0/32.4), then
  falling at L4 (28.5/28.3) and L5 (26.1/26.4) (p. 1304, PDF 7). C2 is high (20.1/20.2) against
  C3. C4 is out of line (15.8/15.9 with SEM 3.6/3.3, a spread of about 12 mm, where its
  neighbours' SEMs are 0.4-0.8 at C3 and C5).
- Error: SEMs 0.4-1.8 mm except C4 (3.6, 3.3). Inference: one aberrant spine or one misdigitised
  facet at C4; treat the C4 IFH spread as suspect.

### IFWs/IFWi and IFW/IFH: interfacet ratios (derived)

- Definitions: IFWs/IFWi; IFW/IFH = mean of IFWs and IFWi over mean of IFHr and IFHl (Figure 5C
  caption, p. 1305, PDF 8; p. 1304, PDF 7; Table 5).
- IFWs/IFWi across levels (Fig 5B): below 1 at C2 (0.84) and C3-C5, rising above 1 from the
  middle to the lower cervical spine (C6 1.03, C7 1.15) to a peak at T1 (1.18), falling to T9
  (0.93; inferior width now the larger), rising to L1 (1.06), then falling to L5 (0.86)
  (p. 1304, PDF 7). The discussion calls its fall from 1.15 at T1 to 0.95 at T9 the most
  significant difference between individual thoracic vertebrae (p. 1307, PDF 10); Table 5 prints
  1.18 at T1 and 0.93 at T9 (1.15 is C7's value), so the discussion's figures are loose.
- IFW/IFH across levels (Fig 5C): between 1.5 and 3 in the cervical region (C2 1.64, C3 2.93,
  C4 2.38, C5 3.04, C6 2.88, C7 2.09), about 1 in the thoracic and lumbar regions (T5-L3
  0.79-0.98, lowest L1 0.79), rising again at L4 (1.16) and L5 (1.44) (p. 1304, PDF 7).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): the two ratios of the centroid distances above; on the
  mask they need all four facet surfaces of the instance, so both neighbours must be segmented.
- From a radiograph alone (Inference): IFW/IFH is magnification-free and needs only an AP view
  (widths and heights of the facet columns), which makes it the most usable facet number for
  fluoroscopy. IFWs/IFWi is also magnification-free on an AP view, but its thoracic range
  (0.93-1.18) is a few millimetres of difference at 20-30 mm widths, near what a radiograph
  resolves.
- Sex, age, size, population: none (as for every measure here). Inference: a ratio of two
  lengths should not scale with body size, which makes these ratios the facet numbers least
  affected by the missing size data.
- Error: inherits the SEMs of Table 5; no spread is printed for the ratios themselves. bordercall's
  provenance reproduces every printed IFWs/IFWi from its own means and 22 of 23 IFW/IFH (at T4
  the printed 1.04 against 1.054 from the means).

### T (Tsr, Tsl, Tir, Til): facet angle to the transverse plane

- Definition: the angle between the facet's best-fit plane and the vertebra's transverse (XZ)
  plane (p. 1300, PDF 3; Figure 2A). All printed values are positive on both sides (Tables 6, 7,
  p. 1303, PDF 6). Inference: with the unit facet normal n in the vertebra's frame, T =
  arccos(|n_y|).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): as above, after rotating into the posterior-wall frame.
- From a radiograph alone: on a lateral view the facet joint line shows the angle to the transverse
  plane accurately only where the sagittal-plane angle is near 90 deg, that is the cervical and
  thoracic spine (p. 1309, PDF 12). Measure the joint line against the perpendicular to the
  posterior body line, not against the image horizontal (Inference). Inference, by projection
  geometry: the lateral-view angle is arccos(cos T / sin S'), with S' the acute angle to the
  sagittal plane; from the left-right means of Table 6 it is within 2 deg of T from C3 to T12,
  0.6-3.7 deg low in the lumbar spine, and about 10 deg low (27 against 37) for C2's superior
  facet; from Table 7 (inferior facets) it is within 2.1 deg from C3 to T11, 4.4 deg low at C2
  (29 against 34) and 1.4-10.7 deg low from T12 to L5 (71 against 82 at L1); and in the lumbar
  spine no sharp joint line is seen on a lateral view anyway.
- Across levels (Fig 6B, p. 1306, PDF 9, which plots the mean of the absolute left and right
  values): rises gradually from about 35 deg at C2 to about 75 at T4; about constant T4 to T12;
  slightly larger, about 80, in the lumbar spine (p. 1304, PDF 7). Superior means: C2 40.7/33.5,
  C3-C6 45-55, C7 59.4/51.6, T1 62.2/57.7, T4-T12 72.7-78.2, L1-L5 81.2-86.0. Inferior: C2
  31.4/36.1 (smallest of the column), C3-C7 41-55, T1 66.3/62.2, T3-T12 72.7-78.4, L1-L2
  78.7-82.8, L3 75.6/77.8, L4 70.5/80.3, L5 71.0/81.6.
- Pitfall (Inference): the joint is the same surface seen from both sides, yet the inferior L4
  and superior L5 means differ (inferior L4 70.5/80.3 against superior L5 86.0/85.1), and so do
  inferior C7 (49.8/49.1) and superior T1 (62.2/57.7). Planes fitted to the convex and concave
  sides of a curved joint differ; do not demand exact congruence.
- Error: SEMs 0.4-6.7 deg (largest inferior L4 right 6.7 and C3 right 6.5; smallest T9 superior
  right 0.4).

### S (Ssr, Ssl, Sir, Sil): facet angle to the sagittal plane

- Definition: the angle between the facet plane and the vertebra's sagittal (YZ) plane, signed:
  positive when oriented toward the positive axis perpendicular to the plane, so left facets
  positive and right facets negative (p. 1300, PDF 3; Tables 6, 7). The magnitude runs from 0 to
  180 deg, not 0 to 90: about 67-86 in the cervical (C3-C7, and C2's inferior facet) and thoracic
  spine, 113-155 in the lumbar spine (from T12's inferior facet) and at C2's superior facet
  (114.1/118.5). How the 0-180 branch is chosen is not stated.
- Inference, worked out from Tables 6-8: take the facet normal n that points up and back (n_y > 0,
  n_z < 0, the normal of Panjabi's card) and its component n_lat along the facet's own lateral
  direction (-n_x on the right, +n_x on the left, +X being left). Then (i) the acute angle to the
  sagittal plane is arccos(|n_lat|) everywhere; (ii) from the C4-C5 joint down (inferior C4,
  superior C5 and every facet below) the printed |S| is arccos(n_lat), and for the joints above it
  (superior C2, C3, C4; inferior C2, C3) it is 180 minus that; checked at all 92 cells, the card
  normal reproduces the printed |S| within 0.1 deg at 89, within 0.9 deg at superior right L1 and
  within 1.5 deg at inferior right T6 (S -73.5 against 72.0 from CAY 18.4), and at superior left
  T1 only once CAX is corrected (below); (iii) the sign of n_lat says whether the facet faces
  laterally or medially, and it is what Table 8's CAY sign encodes (medial where CAY has the same
  sign as S). By that reading the
  superior facets face laterally at C2 and at C5-T12 and medially at C3-C4 and L1-L5 (and the
  joints with them), which fits the known lateral slope of C2's superior facets and the medial
  facing of lumbar superior facets. The card angles were computed by the authors from the plane
  angles, so the facing comes from their internal convention, not from a separate measurement;
  if instead the card signs were wrong at C2-C4, C2's superior facets would face medially, which
  they do not. So the printed S mixes two conventions; the safe pair of numbers is the acute
  angle and the facing.
- Acute angle to the sagittal plane, min(|S|, 180 - |S|), from the left-right means of Table 6:
  superior C2 64, C3-C7 74-85, T1-T12 70-86, L1 43, L2 42, L3 48, L4 58, L5 61 deg. For the lumbar
  spine it is the usual "facet angle to the midsagittal plane".
- Frontal-plane angle: not printed; "complementary" to T in the cervical and thoracic regions and
  to S in the lumbar region (p. 1304, PDF 7). Inference: exactly, cos F = sqrt(1 - cos^2 S -
  cos^2 T).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): with the unit facet normal n of the fitted plane,
  oriented up and back, in the posterior-wall frame: acute angle to the sagittal plane =
  arccos(|n_x|); facing = lateral when n_x points toward the facet's own side (n_x < 0 on the
  right, > 0 on the left, +X being left), medial otherwise. To reproduce Panjabi's printed number,
  |S| = arccos(n_lat) from the C4-C5 joint down and 180 minus that above it (the inference
  above), with the right side negative; this needs the level first, so score the acute angle and
  the facing.
- From a radiograph alone: an axial CT slice gives S accurately only where T is near 90 deg, the
  lumbar spine (p. 1309, PDF 12). Inference, computed from the left-right means: the axial-slice
  trace angle falls short of the true acute angle by 0.1-0.6 deg for the lumbar superior facets,
  0.3-1.4 deg in the thoracic spine (superior; 0.3-1.8 for the inferior facets T1-T12), 0.8-1.3
  deg for the lumbar inferior facets, 1.5-4.4 deg at C3-C7 superior (4.7-6.3 at C3-C5 inferior),
  about 15 deg at C2's inferior facet (57 against 73) and about 21 deg at C2's superior facet (43
  against 64), so axial CT facet angles in the neck are biased toward the sagittal plane. Not readable on a plain AP or lateral view (Inference); on
  AP,
  sagittally oriented upper lumbar joints are seen end-on as joint spaces and the more coronal
  lower ones are not (Inference, general radiographic knowledge, not this paper).
- Across levels (Fig 6A, p. 1306, PDF 9, means of absolute values): superior falls from about
  116 at C2 to 75 at C3; rises from 75 at C3 to 80 at C7; constant about 70-75 from T2 to T11; a
  "dramatic change" at the thoracolumbar junction, located at the inferior facets of T12 and the
  superior facets of L1, from about 70 at T12 to 137 at L1; then falls from about 137 at L1 to 118
  at L5 (p. 1304, PDF 7). Inferior: C2-C7 70.8-83.1, T1-T11 70.5-76.8, T12 139.7/138.8, L1
  152.1/154.8 (largest of the column), L2 142.5/136.7, L3 130.6/123.3, L4 112.7/114.7, L5
  127.6/125.1 (Table 7).
- Error: SEMs 0.9-9.5 deg: 0.9-2.8 at every facet from T1 to T10; up to 5.3 in the cervical spine
  (superior left C3 5.3, inferior left C4 5.0); 4.7-5.5 at the T11-T12 joint; 8.2-9.5 at the
  T12-L1 joint; 2.9-9.2 in the lumbar spine (Table 6, Table 7). Inference: the junctional and
  lumbar values are the least stable, from curvature, from where the switch falls in each spine,
  or both.

### CAX, CAY (CAXsr ... CAYil): card angles

- Definition: a flat card starting in the transverse plane is rotated first about the X axis by
  CAX, then about the Y axis by CAY, to lie in the facet plane (p. 1300, PDF 3; Figure 2B). By the
  sagittal mirror symmetry CAX should be about equal on the left and right and CAY about equal in
  size and opposite in sign (p. 1300, PDF 3). Card angles were computed from the average planar
  angles, not per specimen (pp. 1304-1305, PDF 7-8), so Table 8 (p. 1304, PDF 7) has no SEM.
- Inference, from the geometry and checked on the tables: CAX = -T. Checked on the page images at
  all 92 cells, it holds exactly at 87, to 0.1 deg at four (inferior right C3 -44.1 against 44.2,
  inferior right T7 -77.5 against 77.6, inferior right L1 -81.3 against 81.4, inferior left L1
  -81.7 against 81.8; rounding), and fails at superior left T1 (CAX -47.7 against T 57.7);
  bordercall's provenance, with a 0.1 deg tolerance, counts 91 of 92. The T1 misprint is in CAX,
  not T: CAY -5.6 with T 57.7 gives back the printed S of 85.3 (85.27), while with 47.7 it gives
  85.9. The second rotation must be about the vertebra's fixed Y axis (a rotation about the
  card's own rotated Y axis, which is its normal, would not move the plane). The card's normal is
  then n = (sin CAX sin CAY, cos CAX, sin CAX cos CAY), which points up and back, and |cos S| =
  sin T x |sin CAY|; over all 92 cells this reproduces |S| within 0.1 deg at 89 (worst inferior
  right T6, 1.5 deg, and superior right L1, 0.9 deg). So CAX carries nothing beyond T, and the
  size of CAY nothing beyond T and |S|; the sign of CAY adds one thing the printed S does not,
  whether the facet faces laterally or medially (see S above).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference): with the unit facet normal oriented up and back,
  CAX = -arccos(n_y) and CAY = atan2(-n_x, -n_z); these formulas give back Table 8's values,
  signs included, from the normals the table implies.
- From a radiograph alone: not directly.
- Across levels (Fig 7, p. 1306, PDF 9): superior CAX goes almost linearly from about -41 at C2 to
  -86 at L5, with no abrupt change (p. 1305, PDF 8). CAY reverses direction at three places: C2 to
  C3, C4 to C5, and T12 to L1 (p. 1305, PDF 8). Superior right CAY: +38.9 at C2, -17.6 at C3, +9.2
  at C5, positive and slowly rising from C5 to T12 with a dip at T1 (+4.1), +18.0 at T12, -50.5 at
  L1, then shrinking in size to -27.7 at L5 (p. 1306, PDF 9). Thoracic CAY is about 16 deg
  throughout, like the mid and lower cervical spine and unlike the lumbar (p. 1307, PDF 10).
  Inferior card angles equal the superior ones of the vertebra below, shifted one level up (right
  inferior C6: -55.0, 10.3; right superior C7: -59.4, 11.4) (p. 1306, PDF 9). Figure 8 (p. 1307,
  PDF 10) draws the right superior cards of C5 (+9.8 label, -45.8), T6 (+18.2, -73.5) and L3
  (-42.0, -81.9); its caption notes the gradual rise of CAX from C5 to L3 and the sudden reversal
  of CAY in the lumbar region (p. 1307, PDF 10).
- The authors chose cards over the alternatives they name, the direction cosines of the facet's
  unit normal or its angles to the three anatomic planes, as the easiest to visualise; the card
  idea was earlier used only qualitatively (ref 27) (pp. 1306-1307, PDF 9-10). Inference: a mask
  pipeline should store the unit normal (direction cosines) and derive T, S and the cards from it.
- Sex, age, size, population: none; angles should not scale with size (Inference).
- Error: none printed (means only).

## Level by level and at the junctions

- C2: the superior facets are the largest of the column (width 16.8/16.0, height 18.0/17.7, area
  211.3/205.1; Tables 2, 4) and are oriented unlike any other cervical facet (S 114.1/118.5, T
  40.7/33.5; Table 6). Its inferior facets are the size of the lower cervical ones (pp. 1301,
  1303, PDF 4, 6). IFWs 30.3 is less than IFWi 35.9 (ratio 0.84, the column's lowest); IFH
  20.1/20.2; IFW/IFH 1.64 (Table 5). The paper treats C2 as its own cervical subregion (p. 1307,
  PDF 10). Its superior facets and L5's inferior facets break the rule that inferior facets copy
  the superior facets below (p. 1306, PDF 9).
- C2/C3: superior facet size falls sharply (p. 1300, PDF 3); superior S falls from about 116 to 75
  (p. 1304, PDF 7); superior CAY changes sign (p. 1305, PDF 8); IFH falls from 20 to 12-13 and
  IFW/IFH rises from 1.64 to 2.93 (Table 5).
- C3-C4 against C5-C7: a CAY sign change between C4 and C5 (superior right -8.4 to +9.2; Table 8)
  and a higher FCW/FCH at C5-C7 make three cervical subregions, C2, C3-C4 and C5-C7 (p. 1307,
  PDF 10).
- C7/T1: superior T rises (C7 59.4/51.6, T1 62.2/57.7, T2 66.5/66.6); interfacet widths fall
  sharply (IFWs 38.6 at C7, 33.2 at T1, 28.2 at T2); IFWs/IFWi peaks (1.15 at C7, 1.18 at T1,
  1.16 at T2); IFW/IFH falls 2.09, 1.68, 1.25 (C7, T1, T2); IFH rises 17.6/17.0 at C7 to 18.0/18.6
  at T1 (Table 5). Superior CAY dips at T1 (+4.1, right). Inference: by the paper's own cervical
  rule (IFW/IFH above 1.5), T1 (1.68) would be called cervical; the threshold falls between T1 and
  T2, not between C7 and T1.
- Mid-thoracic (T3-T8): the smallest facets and interfacet widths (T5 IFW 20.8 both); nearly
  uniform linear and areal dimensions, which makes single thoracic levels hard to tell apart
  (p. 1307, PDF 10). The discussion calls T part of a "uniform increase" from 35 deg at C2 to 80
  at L5 and offers CAX (that is -T), falling nearly linearly from C2 to L5, to tell the thoracic
  region from the others; it gives CAY as about 16 deg throughout the thoracic spine, like the
  middle and lower cervical spine and unlike the lumbar (p. 1307, PDF 10). Inference from Table 8:
  the superior thoracic |CAY| averages about 15 on the right (bordercall provenance: 15.24 for
  T1-T12), but C5-C7's superior |CAY| is 5.8-17.9 (mean about 11), so "similar" is loose.
- T12/L1: the orientation switch, at the inferior facets of T12 and the superior facets of L1
  (p. 1304, PDF 7): superior S 72.5/67.4 at T12 against 139.1/134.2 at L1; inferior S 72.2/70.5 at
  T11 against 139.7/138.8 at T12 (Tables 6, 7). T12 is easily identified by the abrupt change of S
  and CAY between its own superior and inferior facets (p. 1307, PDF 10). The lumbar paragraph says
  the superior right CAY "first changes signs from T12 to L1" (p. 1308, PDF 11), meaning the
  first change below the neck; it changes sign at C2-C3 and C4-C5 too (p. 1305, PDF 8). By size,
  the T12-L1 joint is still small
  (inferior T12 width 9.5/9.8, the right the column's smallest; area 90.8/90.0) and the jump comes
  at the L1-L2 joint (Tables 3, 4). IFW/IFH is at its lowest at T12-L2 (0.79-0.81).
- L1-L2: at L1 the superior interfacet width exceeds the inferior (1.06); at L2 it does not
  (0.99) (Table 5). Inferior L1 has the column's largest S (152.1/154.8).
- L3-L5 (Fawcett's scheme, p. 1308, PDF 11): the frontal-plane outline of the facet tips goes
  from taller-than-wide at L3, through square at L4, to wider-than-tall at L5; Panjabi's
  IFW/IFH of 0.9, 1.16 and 1.44 is offered as support. IFWs/IFWi falls from L1 to L5 (Fig 5B).
- L5/S1: the L5 inferior facets articulate with the sacrum and do not copy any measured superior
  facet (p. 1306, PDF 9). L5 has the column's largest inferior interfacet width (40.6) and its
  superior facet area (211.9 right) matches C2's (211.3); IFH falls at L4 and L5 (Table 5).
  The L5 inferior facets (the L5-S1 joint) stand closer to the sagittal plane than the L4
  inferior facets: |S| 127.6/125.1 at L5 against 112.7/114.7 at L4 (Table 7, p. 1303, PDF 6),
  acute angles about 54 against 66 deg (Inference, computed), while the superior facets keep
  turning coronal to L5 (acute 61). Inference: the inferior lumbar trend is not monotonic at the
  last joint; the paper does not remark on it, and S1's facets, which would check it, were not
  measured. Inferior L4 and L5 also carry the largest right-left gap in T (70.5 against 80.3,
  71.0 against 81.6; Table 7).

## Measurement error and reliability

- Instrument check only: under 5% error for linear, area and angular values on a Plexiglas
  pseudovertebra with faces at 30, 45 and 60 deg (p. 1300, PDF 3). Inference: 5% of 30-60 deg is
  1.5-3 deg; 5% of a 10-17 mm facet dimension is 0.5-0.9 mm.
- No observer reliability (intra or inter), no repeat digitising, no description of how the facet
  margin was judged on bone.
- Model error: the planar approximation of curved facets, acknowledged for the lumbar spine
  (p. 1306, PDF 9). Card angles are functions of mean plane angles and have no spread.
- Spread across specimens: SD = SEM x sqrt(12) = 3.46 x SEM. Facet linear SEMs 0.2-1.2 mm;
  interfacet SEMs 0.4-2.1 mm (C4 IFH 3.6/3.3 the outlier); area SEMs 2.0-21.8 mm2; T SEMs
  0.4-6.7 deg; S SEMs 0.9-9.5 deg, the largest at T12-L3.
- The paper's own inconsistencies (see Gaps) are of the order of a few tenths of a millimetre or a
  degree, except the abstract minima and the CAXsl T1 cell.
- Internal consistency (Inference, computed): CAX = -T at 87 of 92 cells exactly and 4 more to
  0.1 deg; the card normal gives back the printed |S| within 0.1 deg at 89 of 92 cells; the
  printed facet width-to-height ratios match their own means at 21 of 23 (superior right) and all
  23 of the other three columns, IFWs/IFWi at all 23 and IFW/IFH at 22 of 23 (bordercall
  provenance, its tolerance). Rechecked here with each printed mean allowed its own rounding of
  +/-0.05 and the ratio its +/-0.005, exactly those three cells (superior right C4 and C5, IFW/IFH
  at T4) cannot be reproduced, and all 90 other printed ratios can. So the angular tables are
  internally coherent and their printed digits can be trusted to about 0.1 deg.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Why facets matter, as the paper states: they have been proposed as a frequent source of low back
  pain (refs 4, 21, 29), their orientation as a cause of rotational coupling in the lumbar spine
  (ref 9), and their role in segment kinematics is much studied (refs 1, 2, 10) (p. 1298, PDF 1).
  Spinal instability and trauma "may benefit" from the data, and mathematical models built on them
  can predict the effects of surgery and injury mechanisms (pp. 1306, 1309, PDF 9, 12).
- The paper gives no stenosis threshold, no syndrome, no screw or implant sizing, no safe zone and
  no tropism threshold.
- Regional signature (pp. 1307-1308, PDF 10-11): cervical facets lie at small angles to the
  transverse plane (stated as 30-50 deg) and large angles to the sagittal plane; thoracic facets
  are uniform; lumbar facets lie 120-150 deg from the sagittal plane against 60-90 for cervical and
  thoracic facets, CAX about 90 deg in size and about the same at every lumbar level, superior right
  CAY falling in size from about 50 at L1 to 28 at L5, and large facet widths, heights and areas;
  to tell lumbar levels apart "a combination of parameters" is advised (p. 1308, PDF 11).
- Pitfall, angle definitions: the CT angle of earlier studies is the angle between the lines in
  which the facet and sagittal planes cut the axial slice, not the true dihedral angle; it equals
  the true one only when T is near 90, and deviation makes the CT angle smaller than the true one
  (p. 1309, PDF 12). Likewise a lateral radiograph gives T only where S is near 90 (p. 1309,
  PDF 12). Inference, computed from the left-right means: at the lumbar superior T of 81-86 deg
  the CT trace angle differs from the true angle by 0.1-0.6 deg, so the definition cannot explain
  the 11-21 deg gaps between Panjabi and the CT studies of Table 10; in the neck, by contrast, the
  trace angle falls 1.5-6.3 deg short at C3-C7, about 15 deg short at C2's inferior facet and
  about 21 deg short at C2's superior facet, so axial CT angles of cervical facets are biased
  toward the sagittal plane.
- Pitfall, the sign and range of S: right facets are negative, and S passes 90 deg at the
  thoracolumbar switch and at C2's superior facet. Averaging right and left without mirroring
  gives zero. The 0-180 branch is not one rule over the column (Inference, from Tables 6-8: it
  changes at the C4-C5 joint), so a mask-side angle must be compared as an acute angle with a
  facing, not as Panjabi's S.
- Pitfall, curvature: the facets are curved, especially the lumbar ones, and a full description of
  the curved surfaces is left to later work (pp. 1306-1307, PDF 9-10). Inference: a plane fit, an
  area and an angle then all depend on how much of the surface is taken.
- Pitfall, symmetry assumed: the card representation is introduced with the expectation that, by
  sagittal mirror symmetry, CAX is about equal and CAY about equal and opposite on the two sides
  (p. 1300, PDF 3); the means break it most at C2's superior facet (CAY +38.9 right against -59.9
  left, a 21 deg gap; CAX -40.7 against -33.5; Table 8, p. 1304, PDF 7), which follows from the
  plane angles of Table 6 (Inference, computed). Tropism is never discussed.
- Pitfall, C2: by S alone (over 90) C2's superior facet looks lumbar; its T (33-41) does not.
- Pitfall, ellipse areas: pi W H / 4 overestimates the measured area (p. 1309, PDF 12), by a
  median of about 17% and up to 46%, but underestimates it at superior L2 by 8-12% (Inference,
  computed from Tables 2-4).
- Skipor et al reported an average forward tilt of the lumbar inferior facets of 18 deg (range -28
  to 53); Panjabi observed 19.5 to 8.7 deg (p. 1309, PDF 12). Inference: the tilt is 90 - T of the
  inferior facets (Table 7: 90 - 70.5 = 19.5 at L4 right); 8.7 is 90 minus inferior right L1's
  CAX (-81.3; its T is printed 81.4), and the range leaves out inferior right L2 (T 82.8, a tilt
  of 7.2), so the quoted range is not the full span of Table 7's lumbar inferior cells.
- The abstract says the plane angles are similar to the angles seen on the traditional
  radiographic views and CT, and that the card angles visualise orientation better (p. 1298,
  PDF 1); the discussion narrows the first claim to lateral views of the cervical and thoracic
  spine and axial CT of the lumbar spine (p. 1309, PDF 12).
- Indication for the data, as the introduction puts it: the geometry fed into mathematical models
  of the spine (used for responses to external forces and to instrumentation, refs 14, 30) is of
  much lower quality than the models themselves (p. 1298, PDF 1).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. Before any facet angle, build each vertebra's own frame from the posterior wall of its body
   (least-squares planes through its four corner points) and the centre of its upper end-plate,
   +X to the left, Y cranial, Z anterior (this paper names only +X; Z anterior is from the
   thoracic paper it cites for the method, ref 25, p. 889); never use scanner or image axes
   (p. 1299, PDF 2; p. 1300, PDF 3).
2. Take each facet as the joint face of its articular process, fit a least-squares plane, orient
   its normal up and back, and compute T = arccos(n_y), the acute sagittal angle arccos(|n_x|)
   and the facing (lateral when the normal's x component points to the facet's own side). Compare
   with Tables 6 and 7 as acute angle min(|S|, 180 - |S|) and with Table 8's CAY sign as facing,
   never with S as printed (p. 1300, PDF 3; Inference for the conversion).
3. Measure facet width and height in the facet's own plane (laid flat, seen along its normal),
   not along the vertebra's axes (Figure 1, p. 1299, PDF 2).
4. Compute facet area as the vector area (root sum of squares of the three projected areas), not
   as a curved mesh area and not as an ellipse (p. 1299, PDF 2; p. 1309, PDF 12).
5. Treat IFW/IFH above 1.5 as cervical, as the paper does (p. 1307, PDF 10), but know that the
   table's T1 (1.68) passes it too and C2 (1.64) only just, so the rule alone calls T1 cervical
   and the cut falls between T1 (1.68) and T2 (1.25) (Table 5; Inference). Separate C7 from T1
   with T (superior 59.4/51.6 against 62.2/57.7, a weak cue), IFWs (38.6 against 33.2), IFW/IFH
   (2.09 against 1.68) and, better, the first rib (Inference, not from this paper) (Tables 5, 6).
6. Name C2 by its superior facets: the largest of the column and with S above 90 but T of only
   33-41 deg; its inferior facets are ordinary (Tables 2, 4, 6; pp. 1300-1303, PDF 3-6).
7. Split the cervical spine into C2, C3-C4 and C5-C7 by the sign of the superior CAY, that is by
   whether the superior facet faces laterally (C2, C5-C7) or medially (C3-C4) (Inference for the
   facing), and by FCW/FCH rising at C5-C7 (p. 1307, PDF 10; Table 8). At C4, C5, left C7 and T1
   the superior facet plane stands 83-86 deg from the sagittal plane (C4 83.9/85.2, C5 83.4/83.7,
   C7 left 85.5, T1 86.4/85.3; Table 6), so its normal leans only 4-7 deg toward either side and
   a fitting error of that size flips the facing sign there (Inference).
8. Find the thoracolumbar switch: the vertebra whose superior facets are thoracic-type (S about
   67-77) and whose inferior facets are lumbar-type (S about 139) is T12 on average (p. 1304,
   PDF 7; p. 1307, PDF 10). Do not hard-code it: the spread of S from the T11-T12 joint down says
   the junction varies between spines or fits poorly, and Malmivaara's sample put T12's superior
   facets at 98 deg (Tables 6, 7, 10, pp. 1303, 1308, PDF 6, 11). Inference, not from this paper:
   check it against the rib count and the lumbar count before naming.
9. Expect lumbar-sized facets to start one joint below the orientation switch, at L1-L2 (inferior
   L1 and superior L2 jump in height and area) (Tables 2-4).
10. Within the thoracic spine, use IFWs/IFWi (1.18 at T1 falling to 0.93 at T8-T9, back to 1.04
    at T12) as the most level-specific facet cue (p. 1307, PDF 10; Table 5); expect the rest to be
    nearly flat from T3 to T9.
11. Within the lumbar spine, use the AP outline of the four facet tips: superior wider than
    inferior at L1 only (1.06), IFW/IFH 0.90 at L3, 1.16 at L4, 1.44 at L5 (Fawcett's upright
    rectangle, square, horizontal rectangle) (p. 1308, PDF 11; Table 5), together with lumbar S
    falling from about 137 at L1-L2 to 118-122 at L4-L5 (Table 6).
12. On a lateral radiograph or fluoroscopic frame, read facet inclination only in the cervical and
    thoracic spine (p. 1309, PDF 12), against the perpendicular to the posterior body line
    (Inference); expect T of about 41-55 deg at C3-C6, 49-66 deg at C7-T1 and 72-78 deg at T4-T12
    (Tables 6, 7). At C2 the true T is 31-41 deg, but by projection (Inference) its joints should
    read lower on a lateral view, about 27 deg superior and 29 deg inferior.
13. On axial CT, read the lumbar facet-to-sagittal angle and compare it with 180 - |S| (42-61 deg
    superior, L1 to L5), knowing that the CT studies printed in Table 10 sit 11-21 deg more
    sagittal and Horwitz and Smith's dissections 3-18 deg more (Table 10, p. 1308, PDF 11).
    Expect the angle to grow (the facet to turn more coronal) from L1-L2 to L5, the trend all five
    studies share (p. 1309, PDF 12).
14. Use joint congruence as a sequence check: the inferior facets and IFWi of vertebra k should
    match the superior facets and IFWs of k+1 (p. 1306, PDF 9; Table 5), except C2's superior and
    L5's inferior facets. Inference from the table means: allow plane mismatches of about 12 deg
    at C7-T1 (T 49.8/49.1 against 62.2/57.7) and 15-17 deg at L1-L2 (S 152.1/154.8 against
    135.4/139.7) and L4-L5 (right T 70.5 against 86.0), and about 2.5 mm of interfacet-width
    mismatch from T11-T12 to L3-L4.
15. Use the transverse-plane angle (equivalently CAX) as a regional cue, as the authors propose
    for telling thoracic from other vertebrae (p. 1307, PDF 10): superior T 33-59 deg at C2-C7,
    58-78 at T1-T12, 81-86 at L1-L5 (Table 6). It separates cervical from thoracic well but
    thoracic from lumbar only by a few degrees (T12 77.1/78.2 against L1 82.9/81.3).
16. Call a vertebra lumbar when its facets stand 120-150 deg from the sagittal plane on Panjabi's
    scale (acute 30-60 deg) against 60-90 for cervical and thoracic facets, together with large
    facets (p. 1307, PDF 10); to tell lumbar levels apart the authors advise a combination of
    parameters (p. 1308, PDF 11).
17. Treat interfacet height at C4 and every lumbar S as noisy (Table 5; Tables 6, 7) and weight
    them down.
18. Never trust the abstract's ranges as bounds: they are stated for C3 to L5 without C2's
    superior facet, and even there the tables go below them (width 9.5, area 69.4, Y-card 4.1)
    (p. 1298, PDF 1; Tables 3, 4, 8).
19. Do not extrapolate the lumbar inferior-facet trend to the L5-S1 joint: L5's inferior facets
    are more sagittal than L4's (|S| 127.6/125.1 against 112.7/114.7, Table 7), and S1 was not
    measured (Inference from Table 7; the paper does not comment). On a mask, measure the L5
    inferior facet against the sacrum's superior facet directly.

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper names no transitional vertebra, no
L6, no T13 and no rib anomaly, and all 12 spines had 23 levels C2-L5 (p. 1299, PDF 2). The only
usable fact is qualitative: the thoracolumbar facet switch is at T12-L1 on average, with a spread
from the T11-T12 joint down that may mean some spines switch one joint off (Inference, Tables 6,
7). A decoder should not use the switch alone as the T12 label; it is a feature with its own
level uncertainty.

(b) Measures and definitions:
- The generator holds FCW, FCH, FCA (superior and inferior), IFWs, IFWi, IFH, T and S (superior
  and inferior), and leaves the card angles out for want of a spread. Keep their magnitudes out
  for good: CAX = -T and |cos S| = sin T |sin CAY| (Inference, checked). The sign of CAY is the
  one new thing, the facet's lateral or medial facing; it could enter as a per-level category
  (superior facets lateral at C2 and C5-T12, medial at C3-C4 and L1-L5), with an error rate where
  the facet normal lies nearly in the sagittal plane, so that its side tilt is only 4-7 deg (C4,
  C5, left C7, T1) (Inference).
- Frame bias: Panjabi's angles are to the vertebra's posterior-wall frame. Angles taken from scanner
  axes, or from a principal-axes frame of the whole instance, tilt T and S by the vertebra's
  sagittal and axial rotation (Inference).
- Width and height must be taken in the facet plane; extents along the vertebra's axes are
  foreshortened by about sin T or sin S (Inference). schwingcheck's mnemonics describe FCWs as
  the "transverse extent" and FCHs as the "craniocaudal extent" of the facet; Panjabi's are
  in-plane dimensions of a facet "oriented to provide normal view" (Figure 1, p. 1299, PDF 2), so
  a mask measurer written to the mnemonic's words would read FCH low by up to 30% at C3-C6
  (Inference, sin T at T near 45 deg).
- Area must be the vector (flat) area; a CT mesh's scalar area of a curved lumbar facet exceeds it
  (Inference).
- Facet surface: Panjabi digitised the articular surface of cleaned bone; a CT mask's articular
  face is the subchondral bone plus whatever partial-volume shell the segmenter draws; at 1 mm
  voxels a 10-16 mm facet is 10-16 voxels across, so widths carry about a voxel of error and the
  joint gap of 1-2 mm may be bridged by the mask (Inference).
- S scale: the printed S is on a 0-180 scale whose branch changes at the C4-C5 joint (Inference,
  above); a mask-side extractor cannot know which branch a level uses before it knows the level.
  Draw and score the acute angle min(|S|, 180 - |S|), mirrored, and the facing separately; on the
  acute scale the thoracic (70-86) and lumbar (42-61) superior facets still separate well
  (Inference).
- IFW: whether the 3D centroid distance or its X component is not stated; they should agree within
  a millimetre (Inference). IFH may be the Y component; on lumbar levels the centroids also differ
  in Z, so the two readings can differ more (Inference).
- Table 9's "present study" values for FCHs at C4-C7 and FCWs at C4 and C7 are not the means of
  Table 2's sides (bordercall provenance); draw from Tables 2-8 only.

(c) Measurement error to use: the paper gives only the instrument's under-5% error (p. 1300,
PDF 3). The generator's kind errors (1 mm for a length, sqrt(pi A / 2) for an area, 3 deg for an
angle) are of the right order for CT-mask extraction and above the morphometer's (Inference). Use
SD = 3.46 x SEM for the between-person spread. Flag C4 IFH's spread (SEM 3.6/3.3) as probably one
outlier rather than a true spread; schwingcheck's README already counts the interfacet height at
C4 among the values its generator's floor at 0.5 touches most, which a spread of about 12 mm on a
mean of 16 mm produces (bordercall schwingcheck/README.md). Treat S at the thoracolumbar joint
(SEM 8.2-9.5) and in the lumbar spine (SEM 2.9-9.2, spreads of 10-32 deg against level-to-level steps of 1-14 deg) as
weakly informative for telling one lumbar level from the next.

(d) Size, sex and population covariates: none. Twelve spines, 8 men and 4 women, 19-59 years,
height 157-178 cm, weight 54-85 kg as printed (p. 1299, PDF 2; the thoracic paper's Table 1,
ref 25 p. 889, lists the twelve with a heaviest of 84 kg); no scaling with height or sex is
given. Inference: lengths should scale with size (power 1), areas with its square and angles not
at all, as the generator already assumes; the paper cannot test it. The four Panjabi papers
print the same twelve-specimen summary and this one calls itself the last of the series, so
facet, body, canal and pedicle values very likely come from one sample (Inference; bordercall's
provenance treats them so) and could in principle be correlated, but no correlation is
published.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Not given: the number of points per facet; whether cartilage was removed; how width and height
  were computed (the "major and minor" wording conflicts with ratios above 1); whether IFW and IFH
  are full distances or components; how the 0-180 branch of the sagittal angle is chosen (by the
  tables it changes at the C4-C5 joint, Inference above); the direction of Z (named only in the
  thoracic paper, ref 25); per-specimen left-right asymmetry (tropism) and per-specimen location
  of the thoracolumbar switch; any test behind "significant" (the thoracic paper used ANOVA with
  Scheffe's test, ref 25 p. 889; this paper names none); specimen ethnicity and origin.
- Tropism: the paper prints right and left means separately but never compares them per spine and
  never uses the word; it assumes mirror symmetry when it introduces the card angles (p. 1300,
  PDF 3). The largest right-left differences of means are inferior T at L5 (71.0 against 81.6)
  and L4 (70.5 against 80.3), superior T at C7 (59.4 against 51.6) and C2 (40.7 against 33.5),
  and inferior S at L3 (130.6 against 123.3) (Tables 6, 7); superior CAY at C2 (+38.9 against
  -59.9, a 21 deg gap in size; Table 8); in size, the areas of inferior C7 (94.3 against 114.6),
  superior C5 (107.0 against 87.2), inferior C4 (105.3 against 85.7) and superior C3 (101.3
  against 81.9), the inferior C7 height (11.4 against 13.9) and the inferior L1 width (12.4
  against 10.7) (Tables 2-4). Inference: differences of means are not tropism, and a 10 deg mean
  difference in 12 spines at L4-L5 is either a real side bias or a digitising bias; the paper does
  not say which.
- Abstract against tables (p. 1298, PDF 1): minimum facet width 9.6 against 9.5 (inferior right
  T12, Table 3); minimum area 72.3 against 69.4 (inferior right T6, Table 4); minimum Y-card angle
  5.8 against 4.1 (superior right T1, Table 8). All three are also in bordercall's
  CONTRADICTIONS.md.
- Table 8 against Tables 6 and 7: CAXsl at T1 is -47.7 where Tsl is 57.7 (bordercall provenance).
  Inference: the misprint is in Table 8, since CAYsl -5.6 reproduces the printed Ssl 85.3 only
  with T 57.7 (85.27 against 85.9 with 47.7), and Figure 6B's T1 point (about 60, the mean of 62.2
  and 57.7) agrees with Table 6.
- Figure 8 labels C5's superior right CAY +9.8; Table 8 and p. 1306 give +9.2 (p. 1307, PDF 10).
- The text quotes CAX at C2 as about -41 (p. 1305, PDF 8), the right side only; the left is -33.5.
- Wrong cross-references: the card angles are said to come from the average planar angles "shown
