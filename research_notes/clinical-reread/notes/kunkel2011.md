## What the paper is

- Design: cadaveric morphometric study of thoracic intervertebral discs (TIVD) and their neighbouring vertebral bodies, measured twice: directly on mid-sagittal saw-microtome sections (anatomical) and on lateral contact radiographs of the same segments (radiographic). Aim: give anatomical disc heights for every thoracic level, quantify the radiographic error, and predict disc heights from vertebral body heights by regression. p. 375 (PDF 1), p. 376 (PDF 2).
- Denominators: 72 isolated motion segments (two vertebrae with the disc between) from 30 human spines; six segments per disc level, C7-T1 to T11-T12 (12 levels x 6 = 72). 37 segments from 15 women (mean age 58.67 +/- 10.74 years, range 43-80) and 35 from 15 men (56.20 +/- 11.65, range 37-79); overall age 57.43 +/- 11.27. p. 375 (PDF 1), p. 377 (PDF 3). Inference: since a spine has one disc per level, the six segments at a level come from six different donors; the sex split per level is not given.
- Population: human body donors; the paper names no country or ethnicity (the authors work at the University of Ulm, Germany; funding German Research Foundation, p. 387, PDF 13). No stature or weight is given.
- Ages and sexes were not analysed: the discs "were not classified into age or gender groups". p. 377 (PDF 3).
- Inclusion: only segments graded mild to moderate degeneration (grades ii and iii of a four-grade scheme, below) on anatomical and radiographic inspection were selected. p. 379 (PDF 5). Radiographs with overlay from other structures or poor film quality were excluded. p. 377 (PDF 3).
- Levels: vertebral bodies C7 to T12 (13 levels; Table 2, p. 379, PDF 5); discs C7-T1 to T11-T12 (12 levels; Table 1, p. 378, PDF 4). C7-T1 was included on purpose to see the cervicothoracic transition from the mobile cervical to the stiff thoracic spine. p. 377 (PDF 3). Nothing below T11-T12: no T12-L1 disc, no lumbar level.
- How levels were identified: not stated. The ribs were sawn off before the segments were stored at -28 deg C. p. 377 (PDF 3). Transitional segments are not mentioned.
- Radiography: Faxitron automatic X-ray machine (Hewlett Packard); each segment in a neutral standard position; film-focus distance 60 cm, 60 s, 46.5 kV; beam centred on the disc; off-centre positioning error examined and a magnification correction factor calculated; lateral and AP radiographs taken, but only lateral films were measured (Fig. 1 caption). Each film placed on a viewing table; eight landmarks (the four corners, anterior and posterior, of the end-plates of each of the two vertebrae) marked by Farfan's method (1973); heights measured with an electronic digital caliper (Mitutoyo Absolute Digimatic, accuracy +/- 0.05 mm); films calibrated by a scale on the rig close to the specimen. p. 377 (PDF 3); Fig. 1, p. 376 (PDF 2).
- Anatomical: frozen segments cut horizontally through the upper and lower vertebral bodies with a high-precision saw; posterior elements removed, the disc kept with thick slices of both bodies; two rods (3.0 mm diameter, 20 mm long) set into each body to mark the frontal and median sagittal plane; segment frozen in an ice block and cut sagittally on a saw microtome (Leica SP4000) along the rods; disc heights read with a sliding vernier caliper (Mitutoyo), including the cartilaginous end-plates, at the anterior and posterior landmarks and at two mid-vertebral points on the superior and inferior end-plates for MDH. p. 377 (PDF 3). Fig. 1b shows annulus, nucleus, cartilaginous end-plate, body, anterior and posterior longitudinal ligaments on a T9-10 section of a 57-year-old woman, p. 376 (PDF 2).
- Parameters: five measured (ADH, MDH, PDH, VBHA, VBHP) and three dimensionless indices (I1, I2, I3) taken from methods for lumbar discs (Twomey and Taylor 1987; Amonoo-Kuofi 1991). p. 377 (PDF 3); Fig. 1, p. 376 (PDF 2). Methods say all five were taken both on films and on sections, but the Fig. 1 caption says only the three disc heights were taken on sections, MDH was never taken on film (Table 1 footnote), and the Table 4 caption says the vertebral parameters are radiographic. So: VBHA and VBHP are radiographic only; ADH and PDH are both; MDH is anatomical only.
- Degeneration grading (their own four-grade scheme, from lumbar schemes): grades i none, ii mild, iii moderate, iv strong, each described for nucleus (elastic and clearly separate from annulus, to fibrous brittle and partly scar), annulus (regular lamellae, to ruptures and fissures), cartilaginous end-plate (even hyaline, to complete destruction) and body (rounded margins; small projections; first marginal osteophytes under 2 mm; osteophytes over 2 mm on the anterior vertices). pp. 377-379 (PDF 3-5).
- Reliability design: two observers measured every radiograph and specimen (inter-observer); one observer repeated measurements on 10 radiographs from 10 specimens at five spinal levels, minutes apart (intra-observer); precision given as a "coefficient of variation". p. 377 (PDF 3).
- Statistics: linear regression of radiographic on anatomical ADH and PDH; least-squares linear and non-linear (polynomial up to cubic) fits of disc heights and body heights against ADH, VBHA and VBHP, chosen as predictors because they can be taken from films with acceptable accuracy (MDH and PDH excluded as predictors); ANOVA, P < 0.05; external check against Todd and Pyle (1928). p. 377 (PDF 3). Inference: the fits are across the 12 or 13 level means (Fig. 2 caption says "mean values from each disc level"; Figs 6-9 plot one point per level), so they describe how one dimension follows another along the column, not how a larger person's disc scales with a larger body.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about transitional vertebrae, rib counts (11 or 13 pairs), T13, cervical ribs, block vertebrae or how levels were counted; ribs were sawn away before storage (p. 377, PDF 3). No anomaly prevalence can be taken from it.

## Measures, each as a recipe

Abbreviations as the paper uses them (Fig. 1, p. 376, PDF 2). All values in mm.

### VBHA: vertebral body height anterior

- Landmarks: anterior superior and anterior inferior corners of the body, the extreme anterior margins of its two end-plates, marked by Farfan's method; distance between them. Fig. 1a, p. 376 (PDF 2); p. 377 (PDF 3).
- Plane or projection: lateral radiograph of the isolated segment, magnification corrected by a scale on the rig. Cartilaginous end-plates not included (p. 384, PDF 10).
- Instrument: electronic digital caliper on the film, +/- 0.05 mm.
- Levels: C7 to T12, Table 2, p. 379 (PDF 5).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Use the body subregion. Set a local frame: cranio-caudal axis as the mean normal of the superior and inferior end-plate surfaces (or the line through the centroids of the neighbouring bodies), antero-posterior axis in the median plane through the body centroid towards the spinous process. In a mid-sagittal slab (a few mm thick), take the most anterior points of the superior and inferior end-plate rims (the corners) and measure their distance along the cranio-caudal axis. A lateral DRR silhouette gives the radiographic version: the corner is then the outermost projected rim across the whole width, not the mid-sagittal point, and reads slightly differently when the end-plate is tilted to the beam.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, corner to corner; needs a magnification correction (the paper used a calibration scale and a 60 cm focus-film distance). Not taken on AP.
- Across levels (means, Table 2, p. 379, PDF 5): C7 13.89, T1 14.49, T2 15.01, T3 15.65, T4 15.42, T5 15.84, T6 16.04, T7 15.94, T8 16.99, T9 18.26, T10 18.98, T11 19.60, T12 20.80. Smallest at C7, largest at T12, a rise of about 50 percent. Flat from T3 to T7 (15.4-16.0, with dips at T4 and T7); steeper from T7 to T12. Inference: steps of 0.6 mm per level against SDs of 1.1-2.1 mm, so VBHA separates regions several levels apart, never neighbours.
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed. Fig. 5a (p. 382, PDF 8): Berry 1987, Todd and Pyle 1928, Scoles 1988 and Cotterill 1986 lie a little above this study at most levels (read from the figure, roughly 1-4 mm above, the largest gap at T11-T12); the authors call the agreement very good (p. 384, PDF 10).
- Error: SD 1.07 (T5) to 2.12 (T9); inter-observer "CV" 0.95-0.98 (high repeatability); intra-observer error 1.7-5.1 percent (given jointly for VBHA and VBHP). p. 380 (PDF 6); Table 2, p. 379 (PDF 5).

### VBHP: vertebral body height posterior

- Landmarks: posterior superior and posterior inferior corners of the body, as for VBHA. Fig. 1a, p. 376 (PDF 2).
- Plane, instrument, levels: as VBHA (lateral radiograph, caliper, C7-T12).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. As VBHA at the posterior wall of the body subregion; take the corners, not the mid-wall, since the posterior wall carries the basivertebral notch and the pedicle roots join it; keep the posterior elements out of the body subregion.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph; the posterior corners are harder to see where many shadows overlap, especially in the upper thoracic spine (p. 383, PDF 9; stated for PDH, the same corners).
- Across levels (means, Table 2, p. 379, PDF 5): C7 14.38, T1 15.28, T2 16.11, T3 17.41, T4 18.15, T5 17.33, T6 18.22, T7 18.67, T8 20.05, T9 20.25, T10 20.35, T11 22.67, T12 23.12. A rise of about 60 percent from C7 to T12; a dip at T5 below T4 and a plateau T8-T10.
- VBHP exceeds VBHA at every level, so every body from C7 to T12 is wedged with the anterior wall shorter (Table 2). Inference, derived from Table 2: VBHP/VBHA = C7 1.035, T1 1.055, T2 1.073, T3 1.112, T4 1.177, T5 1.094, T6 1.136, T7 1.171, T8 1.180, T9 1.109, T10 1.072, T11 1.157, T12 1.112. Smallest at the cervicothoracic junction, largest in the mid-thoracic kyphosis (T4, T7, T8).
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed. Fig. 5b (p. 382, PDF 8) compares Todd and Pyle 1928, Berry 1987 and Panjabi 1991a/b; Panjabi's values sit at or a little below this study's.
- Error: SD 1.12 (T3) to 2.44 (T9); inter-observer "CV" 0.95-0.98; intra-observer 1.7-5.1 percent. p. 380 (PDF 6).

### ADH: anterior disc height

- Landmarks: anatomical, the distance between the anterior margins of the two bony end-plates at the anterior edge of the disc on the mid-sagittal section, including the cartilaginous end-plates; radiographic, the distance between the anterior inferior corner of the upper body and the anterior superior corner of the lower body. Fig. 1, p. 376 (PDF 2); p. 377 (PDF 3).
- Plane or projection: mid-sagittal saw section (anatomical); lateral radiograph (radiographic).
- Instrument: vernier caliper on the section; digital caliper on the film.
- Levels: C7-T1 to T11-T12.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. A pair feature between consecutive body instances: in the shared mid-sagittal slab, distance along the local cranio-caudal axis from the anterior inferior corner of the upper body mask to the anterior superior corner of the lower body mask. A bone mask gap includes the radiolucent cartilaginous end-plates, so in principle it matches the anatomical definition better than the radiographic one. At 1 mm voxels a 3-7 mm disc is 3-7 voxels, so one voxel of threshold or partial-volume error is 15-30 percent of the value; use a sub-voxel surface.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, corner to corner; magnification corrected. Radiographic ADH reads on average about 9 percent shorter than anatomical (abstract p. 375, PDF 1; p. 380, PDF 6).
- Across levels (anatomical means, Table 1, p. 378, PDF 4): C7-T1 4.5, T1-2 4.5, T2-3 3.4, T3-4 3.3, T4-5 3.0, T5-6 3.5, T6-7 4.1, T7-8 4.2, T8-9 5.6, T9-10 5.4, T10-11 7.2, T11-12 6.0. The text: about 4.5 at C7-T1, falling to about 3 at T4-5, rising to about 7.2 at T10-11 and back to about 6 at T11-12 (p. 380, PDF 6). Radiographic means run from 2.85 (T4-5) to 5.91 (T10-11), C7-T1 3.96 (Table 1).
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed. Fig. 4a (p. 381, PDF 7): Todd and Pyle's anatomical ADH (male cadavers) agrees, but runs lower at T4-5 to T6-7 and at T10-11 (read from the figure, about 2.1 at T4-5 and 4.9 at T10-11). Fig. 4b: radiographic ADH agrees with Manns and with Giles and Singer (2000) (p. 383, PDF 9).
- Error: anatomical SD 0.30 (T3-4) to 1.74 (T9-10); radiographic SD 0.22 to 1.03; radiographic shortfall per level 1.67 percent (T11-12) to 19.22 percent (T8-9), absolute 0.10-1.29 mm (Table 1); R^2 of radiographic on anatomical level means 0.921 (P = 7.98E-07, Fig. 2a, p. 380, PDF 6); intra-observer error 1.7-6.1 percent (p. 380, PDF 6).

### PDH: posterior disc height

- Landmarks: as ADH at the posterior margins (posterior inferior corner of the upper body to posterior superior corner of the lower body on film; posterior edge of the disc on the section, including cartilaginous end-plates). Fig. 1, p. 376 (PDF 2).
- Plane, instrument, levels: as ADH.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. As ADH at the posterior corners in the mid-sagittal slab; the posterior corner region is where body and pedicle roots meet, so restrict to the body subregion.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, but poor: radiographic PDH reads on average about 37 percent shorter than anatomical (abstract p. 375, PDF 1; p. 380, PDF 6), 28.91 percent (C7-T1) to 50.12 percent (T8-9) per level (Table 1), and its landmarks are hidden by overlapping shadows in the upper thoracic spine (p. 383, PDF 9).
- Across levels (anatomical means, Table 1): C7-T1 4.5, T1-2 4.3, T2-3 3.5, T3-4 3.2, T4-5 3.3, T5-6 3.6, T6-7 4.1, T7-8 3.6, T8-9 5.0, T9-10 4.2, T10-11 5.8, T11-12 4.8. Lowest at T3-4, highest at T10-11; the text gives a trend like ADH with an increase of about 21 percent from T7-8 on (p. 380, PDF 6). Radiographic means 1.95 (T2-3) to 3.66 (T10-11).
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed. Fig. 4c (p. 381, PDF 7): Giles and Singer's radiographic PDH runs much higher than this study's (read from the figure, about 3.7-4.2 from T1-2 to T9-10, about 5.0 at T10-11 and 5.8 at T11-12, against 2.0-3.7 here); the authors attribute the radiographic differences to magnification bias, positioning errors and parallax (pp. 383-384, PDF 9-10).
- Error: anatomical SD 0.45 (T4-5) to 1.26 (T11-12); radiographic SD 0.21 to 0.70; R^2 radiographic on anatomical level means 0.690 (P = 0.0008, Fig. 2b); inter-observer "CV" for radiographic PDH 0.49, the lowest of all; intra-observer error 17-26.1 percent. p. 380 (PDF 6).

### MDH: middle disc height

- Landmarks: distance between two mid-vertebral points, the middle of the superior and of the inferior end-plate bounding the disc, on the mid-sagittal section, including cartilaginous end-plates. p. 377 (PDF 3); Fig. 1b.
- Plane or projection: mid-sagittal section only.
- Instrument: vernier caliper.
- Levels: C7-T1 to T11-T12.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the mid-sagittal slab, the gap along the cranio-caudal axis between the centre of the inferior end-plate surface of the upper body mask and the centre of the superior end-plate surface of the lower body mask (centre = midpoint of the end-plate's AP depth). It includes the bony end-plate concavities, which is why it exceeds the rim heights.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible. The midline distance between the oval images of the two end-plates cannot be identified on a film (p. 383, PDF 9, citing Edmondston 1999). Inference: the concave end-plate projects as an oval or double contour on a lateral film.
- Across levels (anatomical means, Table 1): C7-T1 5.6, T1-2 5.7, T2-3 5.8, T3-4 4.4, T4-5 5.0, T5-6 4.7, T6-7 5.5, T7-8 5.3, T8-9 6.1, T9-10 5.5, T10-11 6.7, T11-12 6.5. Lowest at T3-4, highest at T10-11. MDH is on average about 22.7 percent above the average disc height (p. 380, PDF 6; abstract).
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed. No literature comparison exists (p. 383, PDF 9).
- Error: SD 0.45 (T6-7) to 1.18 (T10-11) (Table 1); no repeatability figure given separately.

### Average disc height

- Definition: mean of anatomical ADH and PDH at each level (Table 1 footnote, p. 378, PDF 4).
- Across levels (Table 1): C7-T1 4.5, T1-2 4.4, T2-3 3.5, T3-4 3.3, T4-5 3.2, T5-6 3.5, T6-7 4.1, T7-8 3.9, T8-9 5.3, T9-10 4.8, T10-11 6.5, T11-12 5.4. Range 3.2 to 6.5, mean 4.3 +/- 1 (p. 380, PDF 6). The thinnest disc is T4-5 (Fig. 3, asterisk, p. 380, PDF 6).
- From a CT mask or radiograph: as ADH and PDH.

### Average vertebral body height

- Definition: mean of VBHA and VBHP (Table 2 footnote). Range 14.14 (C7) to 21.96 (T12) (Table 2; the text says 14.2 to 21.96, p. 380, PDF 6); it rises at every step except T4 to T5 (16.79 to 16.59). Inference, derived: the ratio of each body's average height to the one above is 0.99 (T5/T4) to 1.075 (T11/T10).

### I1: relative disc height index (and the disc : body height ratio)

- Definition: I1 = (ADH + PDH) / (VBHA + VBHP), after Amonoo-Kuofi 1991; disc : body ratio = 1 / I1. Table 3 footnotes, p. 379 (PDF 5); Fig. 1. Disc heights are anatomical, body heights radiographic (Table 4 caption, p. 382, PDF 8). Inference, from recomputing Table 3 from Tables 1 and 2: the body used is the upper (cranial) vertebra of each segment (C7 for C7-T1, T11 for T11-T12); the cranial body reproduces every printed I1, the caudal or the mean of both do not.
- Size-free and level-specific: the most useful number in the paper for naming without a scale.
- Across levels (I1, Table 3, p. 379, PDF 5): C7-T1 0.32, T1-2 0.30, T2-3 0.22, T3-4 0.20, T4-5 0.19, T5-6 0.21, T6-7 0.24, T7-8 0.23, T8-9 0.29, T9-10 0.25, T10-11 0.33, T11-12 0.26. The printed disc : body ratio is 1/I1 from the unrounded index, from 1:3.0 (T10-11) and 1:3.1 (C7-T1) to 1:5.3 (T4-5). A U shape: relatively tall discs at the cervicothoracic junction, the relatively thinnest at T3-4 and T4-5, tall again at T10-11. Overall about 1:4.1 (p. 380, PDF 6); by region 1:4.0 upper, 1:4.7 middle, 1:3.8 lower (Fig. 3, p. 380, PDF 6).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. ADH and PDH from the gap to the next body down, VBHA and VBHP of the upper body; all four from bone masks, so the mask I1 will sit between the paper's anatomical I1 and the radiographic version below.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph; but with radiographic disc heights the index runs lower. Inference, derived from Table 1 radiographic means over Table 2 cranial bodies: C7-T1 0.253, T1-2 0.226, T2-3 0.166, T3-4 0.156, T4-5 0.144, T5-6 0.165, T6-7 0.172, T7-8 0.189, T8-9 0.189, T9-10 0.204, T10-11 0.243, T11-12 0.216; the same U shape, minimum at T4-5.
- Error: no SD per level is printed for any index; with n = 6 per level and disc SDs up to 1.7 mm, the zig-zags (T7-8 below T6-7 and T8-9; T9-10 below T8-9; T11-12 below T10-11) may be sampling noise (Inference).

### I2: disc convexity index

- Definition: I2 = MDH / (ADH + PDH), after Twomey and Taylor 1987 (Table 3 footnote). Inference: 0.5 means the middle is as tall as the rims' average; above 0.5 the disc is biconvex.
- Across levels (Table 3): C7-T1 0.63, T1-2 0.65, T2-3 0.83, T3-4 0.67, T4-5 0.80, T5-6 0.67, T6-7 0.68, T7-8 0.68, T8-9 0.57, T9-10 0.58, T10-11 0.52, T11-12 0.60. No steady trend down the column; most convex at T2-3, least at T10-11 (p. 380, PDF 6).
- Companion column "% MDH to average disc height" (Table 3): 20-27 percent at most upper and middle levels, peaks of 39.65 at T2-3 and 37.50 at T4-5, 12.56-16.77 at T8-9, T9-10 and T11-12, and 3.50 at T10-11; region means 27.12 upper, 29.68 middle, 14.66 lower (Fig. 3). Inference, from recomputation: this column is (MDH - average disc height) / MDH, a percentage of MDH; by the usual (MDH / average - 1) the excess would be larger (about 3 to 66 percent, mean about 30).
- From a CT mask: Inference. MDH and the rim heights as above; on a bone mask I2 rises with end-plate concavity.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (no MDH on film).

### I3: disc antero-posterior wedging index

- Definition: I3 = ADH / PDH (Table 3 footnote). Above 1 the disc is taller in front; the authors call that a "posterior wedge" (p. 380, PDF 6), the opposite of some authors' naming.
- Across levels (Table 3, anatomical): C7-T1 1.00, T1-2 1.06, T2-3 0.97, T3-4 1.03, T4-5 0.89, T5-6 0.97, T6-7 1.01, T7-8 1.16, T8-9 1.13, T9-10 1.29, T10-11 1.23, T11-12 1.25. C7-T1 is the only unwedged disc; from T7-8 down ADH exceeds PDH by about 21.25 percent; T1-2 to T6-7 near parallel (p. 380, PDF 6). Fig. 3 regions: upper ADH >= PDH, middle ADH <= PDH, lower ADH > PDH.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral film, but radiographic I3 is systematically larger because radiographic PDH is short. Inference, derived from Table 1 radiographic means: C7-T1 1.24, T1-2 1.21, T2-3 1.66, T3-4 1.47, T4-5 1.43, T5-6 1.60, T6-7 1.70, T7-8 1.64, T8-9 1.82, T9-10 1.64, T10-11 1.61, T11-12 1.83. Never compare a film I3 with the anatomical I3.
- From a CT mask: Inference. ADH / PDH of the gap between consecutive body masks; depends on segment posture (neutral, unloaded specimens here).

### Prediction equations (Table 4, p. 382, PDF 8)

- Form y = C1 + C2 x + C3 x^2 + C4 x^3, x the predictor at the level (VBHA and VBHP radiographic, disc heights anatomical). R^2, P and SE (mm) as printed:
  - ADH to MDH: linear, R^2 0.726, P 0.0004, SE 0.37, C1 3.500, C2 0.453.
  - ADH to PDH: R^2 0.906, P 0.0002, SE 0.27, coefficients -2.149, 3.067, -0.538, 0.037.
  - VBHA to ADH: R^2 0.835, P 0.0017, SE 0.60, coefficients 544.560, -97.473, 5.795, -0.113.
  - VBHA to MDH: R^2 0.576, P 0.0640, not significant, no equation.
  - VBHA to PDH: R^2 0.667, P 0.0260, SE 0.52, coefficients 360.580, -63.935, 3.783, -0.074.
  - VBHA to VBHP: R^2 0.953, P 2.7E-06, SE 0.66, coefficients -180.00, 31.073, -1.646, 0.030.
  - VBHP to ADH: R^2 0.780, P 0.0053, SE 0.66, coefficients 298.43, -46.993, 2.561, -0.045.
  - VBHP to MDH: R^2 0.571, P 0.0676, not significant.
  - VBHP to PDH: R^2 0.659, P 0.0282, SE 0.53, coefficients 201.190, -32.029, 1.705, -0.030.
  - VBHP to VBHA: R^2 0.943, P 6.5E-06, SE 0.58, coefficients 44.199, -5.305, 0.286, -0.004.
- Text: disc heights correlate with body heights at R^2 0.659-0.835, P < 0.005; MDH not (R^2 < 0.6, P > 0.05). p. 381 (PDF 7).
- Mean percent errors over 10 percent marked on the figures (read from the figures): ADH to MDH +14.9 (T3-4), -12.21 (T2-3); ADH to PDH +10.51 (T7-8) (Fig. 6, p. 383, PDF 9). VBHA to ADH -12.95 (T1-2), +14.84 (T3-4), +24.04 (T4-5), +13.35 (T5-6), +21.32 (T9-10); VBHA to PDH +14.01 (T3-4), -11.75 (T8-9), +24.06 (T9-10); VBHA to VBHP none (Fig. 7, p. 384, PDF 10). VBHP to ADH +10.55 (C7-T1), -20.68 (T1-2), -11.14 (T2-3), +26.26 (T4-5), -21.16 (T10-11); VBHP to PDH +15.09 (T3-4), +20.74 (T4-5), +20 (T7-8), +25.52 (T9-10), +13.28 (T11-12); VBHP to VBHA -7.07 at T10, R^2 printed 0.942 (Fig. 8, p. 385, PDF 11).
- Check against Todd and Pyle (Fig. 9, p. 386, PDF 12): PDH predicted from radiographic ADH, errors -13.37 (C7-T1), -12.83 (T1-2), -16.58 (T8-9), -16.98 (T10-11); the text gives about -13 percent upper and -17 percent lower (pp. 381-382, PDF 7-8). VBHP from VBHA on Todd and Pyle's data +11.65 (T2), +11.46 (T3), +17.04 (T12); VBHA from VBHP -11.47 (T2), -12.37 (T3); mean errors under 17 percent (p. 382, PDF 8).
- Discussion summary: ADH predicted from VBHA or VBHP with largest error about 26 percent; MDH predicted significantly only from ADH, largest error about 15 percent; PDH from body heights with about 26 percent error, less than the radiographic PDH error at every level; VBHA and VBHP predict each other within 10 percent. p. 385 (PDF 11).
- Inference, from my own evaluation of the printed coefficients at the Table 2 means: the equations with ADH as predictor and VBHA to VBHP reproduce the level means within about 1 mm, but the cubics in VBHA or VBHP for ADH and PDH do not: VBHA to ADH gives 5.0-9.5 against observed 3.0-7.2 and against the plotted predictions of Fig. 7a (about 3.6-6.5); VBHP to ADH gives 17.8-25.0 mm, impossible for a disc; VBHP to PDH gives 1.8-4.0 against 3.2-5.8; VBHP to VBHA runs up to 4 mm high. With x near 14-23 mm, x^3 is several thousand, so three-decimal coefficients cannot carry the fit, and some coefficients may be misprinted. Do not use these cubics as printed; refit from the level means of Tables 1 and 2 if wanted.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- C7-T1 (cervicothoracic junction): included on purpose to observe the transition from mobile cervical to stiff thoracic spine (p. 377, PDF 3). ADH = PDH = 4.5, I3 1.00, the only disc not wedged (p. 380, PDF 6; Table 3). Relatively tall (I1 0.32, 1:3.1) with convexity 0.63. C7 has the smallest body (VBHA 13.89, VBHP 14.38) and, by derivation, the least body wedge (VBHP/VBHA 1.035). Radiographic ADH 3.96 with the smallest SD (0.22).
- T1-2: ADH 4.5, PDH 4.3, MDH 5.7, I1 0.30; upper thoracic posterior corners hidden by overlapping shadows on film (p. 383, PDF 9).
- T2-3: the most convex disc, MDH almost 40 percent above the average disc height, I2 0.83 (p. 380, PDF 6; Table 3); radiographic PDH smallest (1.95).
- T3-4: lowest PDH (3.2) and MDH (4.4); I1 0.20.
- T4-5: the thinnest disc (average 3.2, ADH 3.0; Fig. 3 asterisk, p. 380, PDF 6), lowest I1 (0.19, 1:5.3), I3 0.89 (taller behind). T4 VBHA (15.42) is below T3 (15.65) and T5 VBHP (17.33) is below T4 (18.15) (Table 2).
- Regions (p. 381, PDF 7; Fig. 3, p. 380, PDF 6), an approximation of Panjabi 1991a's thoracic regions: upper C7-T1 to T3-4, ADH and PDH falling caudally to the thinnest disc at T4-5, slight posterior wedging; middle T4-5 to T6-7, slight anterior wedging (ADH <= PDH); lower T7-8 to T11-12, "the apex of the thoracic spine" at T7-8 onwards, discs clearly taller in front (ADH > PDH).
- T7-8: start of the lower-region wedge (I3 1.16).
- T8-9: largest radiographic PDH shortfall (50.12 percent) and largest ADH shortfall (19.22 percent).
- T10-11: the tallest disc (ADH 7.2, average 6.5, MDH 6.7, PDH 5.8), least convex (I2 0.52), highest I1 (0.33, 1:3.0).
- T11-12 (approaching the thoracolumbar junction): ADH 6.0, PDH 4.8, I3 1.25; T12 the largest body (VBHA 20.80, VBHP 23.12). The paper stops here; T12-L1 not studied.
- Mobility: the disc : body ratio of the lower region (1:3.8) goes with more sagittal motion where the rib cage constrains less; the thoracic spine is the least mobile region (p. 384, PDF 10, citing Kapandji 1985 and White and Panjabi 1990).

## Measurement error and reliability

- Inter-observer: anatomical disc heights "CV" 0.79-0.93; radiographic 0.49-0.82; radiographic PDH 0.49; radiographic VBHA and VBHP 0.95-0.98. p. 380 (PDF 6). Inference: these "CV" values rise with better repeatability, so they behave like a reliability coefficient, not a conventional coefficient of variation; the paper does not define the statistic further.
- Intra-observer: errors 1.7-6.1 percent for ADH, 17-26.1 percent for PDH, 1.7-5.1 percent for VBHA and VBHP; reproducibility from repeat radiographs "generally 15% lower" (p. 380, PDF 6). The text calls these "anatomical measurements" though the methods say intra-observer work used 10 radiographs (p. 377, PDF 3) and VBHA/VBHP were taken on film only.
- Radiographic against anatomical: ADH about 9 percent shorter (1.67-19.22 percent by level), PDH about 37 percent shorter (28.91-50.12 percent); linear R^2 0.921 for ADH, 0.690 for PDH across level means (Table 1; Fig. 2; p. 380, PDF 6). The authors' explanation: the anatomical caliper included the cartilaginous end-plates, which films do not show (p. 383, PDF 9). Inference: since cartilaginous end-plates are radiolucent and lie inside the radiographic disc space, that explanation is doubtful; end-plate rims projecting into the disc space when not parallel to the beam, and corner placement on the film, are likelier causes.
- Instrument: digital caliper +/- 0.05 mm; magnification correction by a scale on the rig; 60 cm focus-film distance (p. 377, PDF 3).
- Remaining radiographic error sources despite controlled positioning: disc orientation to the central beam and observer differences (handled by Pope 1977 and Andersson 1981 recommendations), overlapping shadows, magnification bias, positioning errors, parallax (pp. 383-384, PDF 9-10).
- Biological spread: per-level SDs in Tables 1 and 2 (above); n = 6 per level.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indications for the data: the thoracic spine is the commonest site of deformity (kyphosis, scoliosis), disc data are needed for implants and for mathematical models, and disc height is a diagnostic tool in orthopaedics. pp. 375, 382, 386 (PDF 1, 8, 12).
- A disc has no single height: the planes bounding it are not parallel, so ADH, MDH and PDH differ within one disc and between regions (pp. 382-383, PDF 8-9), against Oliver and Middleditch's "nearly uniform" thickness.
- Disc height falls with age, scoliosis and herniation; post-mortem change and degeneration add error (p. 383, PDF 9). This sample is only mild to moderately degenerated; a grade iv disc is outside these norms (p. 379, PDF 5).
- Cadaveric discs carry no body weight: these values should be slightly larger than radiographic values in a living person sitting or standing, which matters for arthroplasty and models (pp. 384-385, PDF 10-11).
- MDH cannot be taken from a film; radiographic PDH is unreliable, worst in the upper thoracic spine where shadows overlap (p. 383, PDF 9).
- Errors in radiographic body heights pass on into disc heights predicted from them (p. 385, PDF 11); the equations are valid only under the paper's controlled conditions and need checking on patient data (pp. 385-386, PDF 11-12).
- Earlier work had gaps: Hurxthal 1968 and Manns measured ADH on films of women from T5-6 to T11-12 only; Todd and Pyle 1928 only ADH in male cadavers; Pooni 1986 few cadavers aged 73-85, as a percentage of spine height; Goh 1999 and Giles and Singer 2000 gave segmental trends, not ADH and PDH values; earlier radiographic work lacked standard positioning, control of film, specimen and focus distances, landmark visibility, magnification correction and reliability testing. pp. 375-376 (PDF 1-2).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. On a lateral radiograph or a lateral DRR, mark the four end-plate corners of each thoracic body (Farfan's method) and take VBHA and VBHP corner to corner after a magnification correction. p. 377 (PDF 3); Fig. 1a, p. 376 (PDF 2).
2. Expect VBHP > VBHA at every level from C7 to T12; a body with VBHA >= VBHP in a thoracic run is unusual (fracture, measurement error, or not thoracic). Ratio about 1.03 at C7, 1.07-1.18 from T3 to T12 (derived from Table 2, p. 379, PDF 5).
3. Do not name a single thoracic vertebra from its body height: neighbours differ by about 0.6 mm against SDs of 1-2.4 mm, and T4/T5 and T6/T7 reverse the trend. Fit the rising trend (C7 about 14 mm to T12 about 22 mm average height) over a run of four or more consecutive bodies and anchor it to the junctions. Table 2, p. 379 (PDF 5).
4. Use disc-to-body ratios to place a run without a scale: I1 = (ADH + PDH) / (VBHA + VBHP of the body above) is about 0.30-0.32 at C7-T1 and T1-2, falls to 0.19-0.20 at T3-4 and T4-5, and rises to 0.25-0.33 from T8-9 to T11-12. Look for the U-shaped minimum to find T3 to T5. Use the anatomical values for CT masks and the lower radiographic values (about 0.14-0.25) for films. Table 3, p. 379 (PDF 5); derived from Table 1.
5. Use disc wedging as a regional marker: the cervicothoracic disc is parallel (I3 1.00), T1-2 to T6-7 near parallel (0.89-1.06), T7-8 to T11-12 taller in front (1.13-1.29). Compare like with like: on a film I3 is inflated (about 1.2-1.8) because radiographic PDH is short. Table 3; p. 380 (PDF 6).
6. Never measure the middle disc height on a radiograph; take it only from CT or a section. p. 383 (PDF 9).
7. Treat radiographic posterior disc height as about 37 percent short and the least repeatable measure; prefer anterior disc height and body heights on films, and distrust posterior corners from T1 to T4 where shadows overlap. p. 380 (PDF 6); p. 383 (PDF 9).
8. Expect the thinnest thoracic disc at T4-5 (about 3.2 mm mean) and the tallest at T10-11 (about 6.5 mm); a sudden jump in disc height below T11-12 signals the thoracolumbar junction, which this paper does not cover. Table 1, p. 378 (PDF 4); Fig. 3, p. 380 (PDF 6).
9. On a CT mask, measure disc heights as gaps between consecutive body masks along the local cranio-caudal axis in the mid-sagittal slab, at the anterior corners (ADH), posterior corners (PDH) and end-plate centres (MDH); use sub-voxel surfaces, since one voxel is 15-30 percent of a thoracic disc. Inference from the definitions on p. 377 (PDF 3).
10. Do not use the Table 4 cubics in VBHA or VBHP for disc heights as printed; evaluate any equation at the table means first and refit if it fails. Never extrapolate a cubic beyond the fitted range (VBHA 13.9-20.8 mm, VBHP 14.4-23.1 mm): the 17 percent error at Todd and Pyle's larger T12 shows how fast it diverges. Table 4, p. 382 (PDF 8); Fig. 9b, p. 386 (PDF 12).
11. Expect living, loaded discs on standing or sitting films to be somewhat thinner than these cadaveric values, and severely degenerated discs to fall outside them. pp. 379, 384-385 (PDF 5, 10-11).
12. Before using a radiographic disc height, check that the end-plates are seen edge-on (no double contour); an oblique beam shortens the projected disc space. pp. 383-384 (PDF 9-10); the mechanism is Inference.

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of transitional vertebrae, rib anomalies or numbering variants and does not say how levels were counted (p. 377, PDF 3).

(b) Measures:
- Body heights: the generator's posterior body height (Panjabi's VBHp, dry bone, 3D digitiser) matches this paper's VBHP in landmarks. Inference: this paper's radiographic VBHP runs about 0.2-2.0 mm (mean about 1 mm) above Panjabi 1991's thoracic VBHp level by level (Panjabi T1 14.1 to T12 22.7, from the panjabi1991t note in this set, against 15.28 to 23.12 here); a corner-to-corner projected height on film reads larger than a mid-sagittal digitiser height. Panjabi's frame is built on the posterior wall of the body with heights along its cranial axis (panjabi1991t note); a CT mask measured in that frame matches VBHp, while a lateral-film corner height does not exactly. VBHA (anterior body height) is not one of Panjabi's thoracic measures as far as this set's notes show (Fig. 5b compares Panjabi only for VBHP); this paper offers it for C7-T12 and it could be added, with VBHP/VBHA as a size-free wedge ratio.
- Disc heights: the generator holds none (Panjabi measured dry bone). This paper gives ADH, MDH, PDH for C7-T1 to T11-T12 with SDs, plus I1, I2, I3. These are pair features between consecutive instances and suit an HMM emission on transitions.
- Definitions that bias a mask value: the anatomical heights include cartilaginous end-plates (bone to bone on a fresh section), which a CT bone-mask gap also includes (Inference), so mask ADH/PDH/MDH should be compared with the anatomical columns, not the radiographic ones. Mask segmentation thresholds that eat into the end-plate cortex will lengthen the gap; end-plate osteophytes (grade iii-iv) will shorten ADH. I1 here uses the cranial body only (Inference from recomputation): compute it the same way.
- Size-free features worth carrying (all from Tables 1-3 or derived): I1 by level, I3 by level, I2 by level, VBHP/VBHA by level, ratio of consecutive body heights (derived: caudal over cranial average height 0.99-1.075 per step, Inference). None comes with a per-level SD; take the variance from the component SDs or from other data.

(c) Measurement error to use: radiographic ADH bias about -9 percent (level range -1.7 to -19.2), PDH about -37 percent (-28.9 to -50.1); intra-observer about 2-6 percent for ADH and body heights, 17-26 percent for PDH; between-specimen SDs ADH 0.30-1.74, PDH 0.45-1.26, MDH 0.45-1.18, VBHA 1.07-2.12, VBHP 1.12-2.44 mm, each from 6 specimens. Add a voxel-size term for CT (Inference).

(d) Covariates: none. No sex, age, stature or population effect is analysed; the sample is 15 women and 15 men aged 37-80 of unstated origin. The regressions are across level means, so their R^2 is not a size-scaling coefficient and must not be used as a patient-size factor (Inference).

## Gaps and conflicts

- Gaps: no levels above C7 or below T12 (no T12-L1); n = 6 per level; no individual data; no SDs for the indices; no sex, age or body-size analysis; no stature or weight; no country or ethnicity; no anomaly or level-counting information; no AP measurements though AP films were taken; no in vivo or loaded data; no CT.
- Abstract against tables: the abstract says disc thickness varied from 4.5 to 7.2 mm (p. 375, PDF 1), but Table 1 gives average disc height 3.2-6.5, ADH 3.0-7.2 and MDH 4.4-6.7, and the results text says 3.2 to 6.5 (p. 380, PDF 6).
- Text against Table 3: from T1-2 to T6-7 the text gives a maximum wedge difference of about 2.77 percent (p. 380, PDF 6), but Table 3 has I3 0.89 at T4-5 and 1.06 at T1-2.
- Methods against figures: methods say all five parameters were taken on films and sections (p. 377, PDF 3); Fig. 1 caption, Table 1 footnote and Table 4 caption say MDH was anatomical only and body heights radiographic only. Figs 7c and 8c label the body heights "Anatomical data" though they are radiographic.
- Intra-observer: methods describe repeats on 10 radiographs; results call the reproduced measurements anatomical (pp. 377, 380, PDF 3, 6).
- Fig. 2: Inference, from comparison with Table 1: the axis labels look swapped; the values on the axis labelled anatomical match the radiographic means (ADH 2.85-5.91, PDH 1.95-3.66) and those on the radiographic axis match the anatomical means. p. 380 (PDF 6).
- Fig. 8c prints R^2 0.942, P 6.52E-06 for VBHP to VBHA; Table 4 prints 0.943, 6.5E-06 (p. 385, PDF 11; p. 382, PDF 8).
- Manns et al. is dated 1996 in the Fig. 4b legend (p. 381, PDF 7) and 1986 in the text (p. 383, PDF 9) and the reference list (p. 387, PDF 13). Inference (from memory): Clin Radiol volume 51 is 1996, so the 1986 is likely the misprint.
- "% MDH to average disc height" (Table 3) is computed relative to MDH (Inference from recomputation), so "MDH 22.7 percent greater" understates the conventional excess.
- Table 4 cubic coefficients in VBHA/VBHP for ADH and PDH do not reproduce the paper's own means or plotted predictions (Inference, my computation; see Measures).
- The explanation that radiographic disc heights are short because cartilaginous end-plates are invisible is doubtful (Inference; see Measurement error).
- Against other papers in this set (Inference, from the other notes): Busscher 2010's CT central disc heights in six men (T2-T9 4.5-5.3, T10 5.9, T11 6.1 mm, from the busscher2010 note) agree with this paper's MDH (4.4-6.7). Twomey and Taylor's lumbar convexity index uses the same definition as I2, so lumbar I2 values from twomey1985 continue this series below T12. Giles and Singer's radiographic PDH disagrees (Fig. 4c). Kunkel 2010 and 2011 (prediction of thoracic and lumbar vertebral and facet morphometry from radiographic body heights) are the authors' companion papers (p. 376, PDF 2).
