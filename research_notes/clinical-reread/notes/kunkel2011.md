## What the paper is

- Design: cadaveric morphometric study of thoracic intervertebral discs (TIVD) and their neighbouring vertebral bodies, measured twice: directly on mid-sagittal saw-microtome sections (anatomical) and on lateral contact radiographs of the same segments (radiographic). Aims: anatomical disc heights for every thoracic level, the size of the radiographic error, and prediction of disc heights from vertebral body heights by regression. p. 375 (PDF 1), p. 376 (PDF 2).
- Denominators: 72 isolated motion segments (two vertebrae with the disc between) from 30 human spines; six segments per disc level, C7-T1 to T11-T12 (12 levels x 6 = 72). 37 segments came from 15 women (mean age 58.67 +/- 10.74 years, range 43-80) and 35 from 15 men (56.20 +/- 11.65, range 37-79); all donors 57.43 +/- 11.27 years. p. 375 (PDF 1), p. 377 (PDF 3). The sex split per level is not given. Inference: 72 segments from 30 spines is about 2.4 segments per spine; since neighbouring segments share a vertebra, one spine can hardly give two adjacent segments with whole bodies, so each spine probably gave non-adjacent segments and the six segments at a level come from six different donors.
- How many bodies stand behind each Table 2 row is not printed. Inference: C7 occurs only in the C7-T1 segments and T12 only in the T11-T12 segments (n = 6 each), while T1 to T11 occur in two segment types (n up to 12).
- Population: human body donors; the paper names no country or ethnicity (the authors work at the University of Ulm, Germany; funding German Research Foundation, Wi-1352/12-1, p. 387, PDF 13). No stature or body weight is given.
- Ages and sexes were not analysed: the discs "were not classified into age or gender groups". p. 377 (PDF 3).
- Inclusion: only segments with mild or moderate degeneration (grades ii and iii of a four-grade scheme, below) on anatomical and radiographic inspection were selected. p. 379 (PDF 5). Radiographs with overlay from other structures or poor film quality were excluded. p. 377 (PDF 3). No other inclusion or exclusion rule (deformity, fracture, surgery, cause of death) is stated.
- Levels: vertebral bodies C7 to T12 (13 levels; Table 2, p. 379, PDF 5); discs C7-T1 to T11-T12 (12 levels; Table 1, p. 378, PDF 4). C7-T1 was included on purpose to see the change from the mobile cervical to the stiff thoracic spine. p. 377 (PDF 3). Nothing below T11-T12: no T12-L1 disc, no lumbar level.
- How levels were identified: not stated. The ribs were sawn off before the segments were stored at -28 deg C. p. 377 (PDF 3). Transitional segments are not mentioned.
- Radiography: Faxitron automatic X-ray machine (Hewlett Packard, McMinnville, USA); each segment in a neutral standard position; film-focus distance 60 cm, 60 s, 46.5 kV; beam centred on the disc; the error of off-centre positioning was examined and a magnification correction factor calculated. Lateral and AP films were taken, but no AP measurement is reported; the Fig. 1 caption says all parameters except MDH were measured on the lateral films. Each film was laid on a viewing table; eight landmarks (the anterior and posterior corners of both end-plates of each of the two vertebrae) were marked by Farfan's method (1973); heights were measured with an electronic digital caliper (Mitutoyo Absolute Digimatic, accuracy +/- 0.05 mm); films were calibrated by a scale on the rig close to the specimen. p. 377 (PDF 3); Fig. 1, p. 376 (PDF 2).
- Anatomical: the frozen segments were cut horizontally through the upper and lower vertebral bodies with a high-precision saw; posterior elements removed, the disc kept with thick slices of both bodies; two rods (3.0 mm diameter, 20 mm long) set into each body to mark the frontal and median sagittal planes; the segment frozen in an ice block and cut sagittally on a saw microtome (Leica SP4000) along the rods; disc heights read with a sliding vernier caliper (Mitutoyo), including the cartilaginous end-plates, at the anterior and posterior landmarks and, for MDH, between two mid-vertebral points on the superior and inferior end-plates. p. 377 (PDF 3). Fig. 1b shows annulus, nucleus, cartilaginous end-plate, body and both longitudinal ligaments on a T9-10 section of a 57-year-old woman, p. 376 (PDF 2).
- Parameters: five measured (ADH, MDH, PDH, VBHA, VBHP) and three dimensionless indices (I1, I2, I3) taken from methods for lumbar discs (Twomey and Taylor 1987; Amonoo-Kuofi 1991). p. 377 (PDF 3); Fig. 1, p. 376 (PDF 2). The methods say all five were taken both on films and on sections, but the Fig. 1 caption says only the three disc heights were taken on sections, the Table 1 footnote says MDH was never taken on film, and the Table 4 caption says the vertebral parameters are radiographic. So: VBHA and VBHP are radiographic only; ADH and PDH are both; MDH is anatomical only.
- Degeneration grading (the authors' own four-grade scheme, built from lumbar schemes because none existed for thoracic discs): grades i none, ii mild, iii moderate, iv strong, each described for the nucleus (from elastic and clearly set off from the annulus, through slightly fibrotic, to fibrous, dry, fissured and discoloured with the annulus-nucleus boundary lost, to brittle and partly scar), the annulus (from regular concentric lamellae, through drier lamellae, to disordered lamellae with vessel ingrowth, to ruptures and fissures), the cartilaginous end-plate (even hyaline, irregular thickness, local defects, complete destruction) and the body (rounded margins; small marginal projections; first marginal osteophytes under 2 mm; osteophytes over 2 mm on the anterior corners). pp. 377-379 (PDF 3-5).
- Reliability design: two observers measured every radiograph and specimen (inter-observer); one observer repeated measurements on 10 radiographs from 10 specimens at five spinal levels, minutes apart (intra-observer); precision given as a "coefficient of variation" (CV). p. 377 (PDF 3). Whether the tabled means are one observer's or the mean of both is not stated.
- Statistics: linear regression of radiographic on anatomical ADH and PDH; least-squares linear and non-linear (polynomial up to cubic) fits of disc heights and body heights against ADH, VBHA and VBHP, chosen as predictors because they can be taken from films with acceptable accuracy (MDH and PDH excluded as predictors); ANOVA, P < 0.05; the equations tried on Todd and Pyle's (1928) data. p. 377 (PDF 3). Inference, checked by recomputation: every regression is across level means, not specimens. The printed P values are reproduced exactly by an F test on 12 disc-level means (the eight disc equations and the two radiographic-anatomical fits of Fig. 2) and on 13 vertebral-level means (VBHA and VBHP on each other); with 72 specimens they would be smaller by many orders. A cubic refit on the Table 1 and Table 2 means, with the cranial body of each segment as x, reproduces the printed R^2 and SE (see Prediction equations). So the R^2 values describe how one dimension follows another along the column, not how a larger person's disc scales with a larger body.
- The four tables, exactly:
  - Table 1 (p. 378, PDF 4, printed rotated): "Anatomical and radiographic measurements" of thoracic disc heights. 12 rows, one per disc level C7-T1 to T11-T12. 17 value columns: for ADH, anatomical mean and SD, radiographic mean and SD, and under the heading "Anatomical-radiographic ratio" a Difference (mm) and a % (printed negative); the same six columns for PDH; MDH anatomical mean and SD only; and Average disc height (no SD). Units mm. Anatomical means printed to 0.1 mm, radiographic means and all SDs to 0.01 mm (MDH SD at T3-4 printed as 0.8). Footnotes: abbreviations as Fig. 1; all in mm; no radiographic MDH; average disc height is the mean of anatomical ADH and PDH at each level; the last footnote ends with a stray "o e digital". Inference, recomputed: Difference = anatomical minus radiographic mean, and % = Difference / anatomical mean x 100 with a minus sign; the column headed "ratio" therefore holds a difference and a percentage, not a ratio. No n, no confidence interval.
  - Table 2 (p. 379, PDF 5): "Anterior and posterior human thoracic vertebral body heights". 13 rows, C7 to T12. Columns: VBHA mean and SD, VBHP mean and SD, average vertebral body height (mean of VBHA and VBHP, no SD). mm, to 0.01. Radiographic (Table 4 caption). No n.
  - Table 3 (p. 379, PDF 5): "Morphometric indices" for the 12 disc levels. Columns: I1 (relative disc height index, 2 decimals), disc : vertebral body height ratio (printed 1:x.x), I2 (disc convexity index), "% MDH to average disc height" (2 decimals), I3 (disc antero-posterior wedging index). Footnotes give I1 = (ADH + PDH)/(VBHA + VBHP) after Amonoo-Kuofi 1991, I2 = MDH/(ADH + PDH) after Twomey and Taylor 1987, I3 = ADH/PDH, and the ratio as 1 divided by I1. No SD, no n.
  - Table 4 (p. 382, PDF 8): polynomial prediction equations y = C1 + C2 x + C3 x^2 + C4 x^3. 12 rows: predictor ADH (predicting MDH, PDH), VBHA (ADH, MDH, PDH, VBHP), VBHP (ADH, MDH, PDH, VBHA). Columns: R^2, P-value, SE (mm), C1, C2, C3, C4. The two MDH rows from body heights carry only R^2 and P (not significant); ADH to MDH is linear (C3, C4 dashes); the other nine are cubic, so ten equations in all (the abstract's "10 prediction equations"). Caption: levels C7-T1 to T11-T12; vertebral parameters radiographic, disc parameters anatomical.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about transitional vertebrae, rib counts (11 or 13 pairs), T13, cervical ribs, block vertebrae or how levels were counted; the ribs were sawn away before storage (p. 377, PDF 3), and the segments were isolated, so no specimen could be counted from C1 or S1. No anomaly prevalence can be taken from it.

## Measures, each as a recipe

Abbreviations as the paper uses them (Fig. 1, p. 376, PDF 2). All values in mm unless stated. Every level value below is a mean of six segments (discs) or of an unstated number of bodies (Table 2).

### VBHA: vertebral body height anterior

- Landmarks: anterior superior and anterior inferior corners of the body, the extreme anterior margins of its two end-plates, marked by Farfan's method; distance between them. Fig. 1a, p. 376 (PDF 2); p. 377 (PDF 3).
- Plane or projection: lateral radiograph of the isolated segment, magnification corrected by a scale on the rig. The cartilaginous end-plates are not counted in it (p. 384, PDF 10).
- Instrument: electronic digital caliper on the film, +/- 0.05 mm.
- Levels: C7 to T12, Table 2, p. 379 (PDF 5).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. Use the body subregion. Set a local frame: cranio-caudal axis as the mean normal of the superior and inferior end-plate surfaces (or the line through the centroids of the neighbouring bodies), antero-posterior axis in the median plane through the body centroid towards the spinous process. In a mid-sagittal slab (a few mm thick), take the most anterior points of the superior and inferior end-plate rims (the corners) and measure their distance along the cranio-caudal axis. A lateral DRR silhouette gives the radiographic version: the corner is then the outermost projected rim across the whole width, not the mid-sagittal point, and reads slightly differently when the end-plate is tilted to the beam.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, corner to corner; needs a magnification correction (the paper used a calibration scale and a 60 cm focus-film distance). Not taken on AP.
- Across levels (means, Table 2, p. 379, PDF 5): C7 13.89, T1 14.49, T2 15.01, T3 15.65, T4 15.42, T5 15.84, T6 16.04, T7 15.94, T8 16.99, T9 18.26, T10 18.98, T11 19.60, T12 20.80. Smallest at C7, largest at T12, a rise of about 50 percent. Nearly flat from T3 to T7 (15.4-16.0, with falls at T4 below T3 and T7 below T6); steeper from T7 to T12. Inference: the mean step is about 0.6 mm per level against SDs of 1.1-2.1 mm, so VBHA separates regions several levels apart, never neighbours.
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed. Fig. 5a (p. 382, PDF 8) sets Berry 1987, Todd and Pyle 1928, Scoles 1988 and Cotterill 1986 beside this study: read from the figure, the published points lie about 1.5-3 mm above this study's means from T1 to T11 and up to about 4 mm above at T12, mostly at or above this study's upper SD band; none is plotted at C7. The authors nonetheless call the agreement very good (p. 384, PDF 10).
- Error: between-specimen SD 1.07 (T5) to 2.12 (T9) (Table 2); inter-observer "CV" 0.95-0.98 (high repeatability); intra-observer error 1.7-5.1 percent (given jointly for VBHA and VBHP). p. 380 (PDF 6).

### VBHP: vertebral body height posterior

- Landmarks: posterior superior and posterior inferior corners of the body, as for VBHA. Fig. 1a, p. 376 (PDF 2).
- Plane, instrument, levels: as VBHA (lateral radiograph, digital caliper, C7-T12).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. As VBHA at the posterior wall of the body subregion; take the corners, not the mid-wall, since the posterior wall carries the basivertebral notch and the pedicle roots join it; keep the posterior elements out of the body subregion.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph; the authors say bony landmarks were hard to find where many shadows overlap, their example being PDH in the upper thoracic spine (p. 383, PDF 9); the posterior body corners are the same points.
- Across levels (means, Table 2, p. 379, PDF 5): C7 14.38, T1 15.28, T2 16.11, T3 17.41, T4 18.15, T5 17.33, T6 18.22, T7 18.67, T8 20.05, T9 20.25, T10 20.35, T11 22.67, T12 23.12. A rise of about 60 percent from C7 to T12; a fall at T5 below T4 and a plateau T8-T10.
- VBHP exceeds VBHA at every level, so every body from C7 to T12 is wedged with the anterior wall shorter (Table 2). Inference, derived from Table 2: VBHP/VBHA = C7 1.035, T1 1.055, T2 1.073, T3 1.112, T4 1.177, T5 1.094, T6 1.136, T7 1.171, T8 1.180, T9 1.109, T10 1.072, T11 1.157, T12 1.112. Smallest at the cervicothoracic junction, largest in the mid-thoracic kyphosis (T4, T7, T8).
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed. Fig. 5b (p. 382, PDF 8) compares Todd and Pyle 1928, Berry 1987 and Panjabi 1991a/b. Read from the figure: Panjabi's points sit about 0.2-2 mm below this study's at most levels, Todd and Pyle's about 1.5-3 mm above (largest at T12), so this study lies between the two.
- Error: between-specimen SD 1.12 (T3) to 2.44 (T9), with 2.22 at C7 (Table 2); inter-observer "CV" 0.95-0.98; intra-observer 1.7-5.1 percent. p. 380 (PDF 6).

### ADH: anterior disc height

- Landmarks: anatomical, the distance between the anterior margins of the two bony end-plates at the anterior edge of the disc on the mid-sagittal section, the cartilaginous end-plates included; radiographic, the distance between the anterior inferior corner of the upper body and the anterior superior corner of the lower body. Fig. 1, p. 376 (PDF 2); p. 377 (PDF 3).
- Plane or projection: mid-sagittal saw section (anatomical); lateral radiograph (radiographic).
- Instrument: vernier caliper on the section; digital caliper on the film.
- Levels: C7-T1 to T11-T12.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. A pair feature between consecutive body instances: in the shared mid-sagittal slab, distance along the local cranio-caudal axis from the anterior inferior corner of the upper body mask to the anterior superior corner of the lower body mask. A bone-mask gap includes the radiolucent cartilaginous end-plates, so in principle it matches the anatomical definition better than the radiographic one. At 1 mm voxels a 3-7 mm disc is 3-7 voxels, so one voxel of threshold or partial-volume error is 15-30 percent of the value; use a sub-voxel surface.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, corner to corner; magnification corrected. Radiographic ADH reads on average about 9 percent shorter than anatomical (abstract p. 375, PDF 1; p. 380, PDF 6).
- Across levels (anatomical means, Table 1, p. 378, PDF 4): C7-T1 4.5, T1-2 4.5, T2-3 3.4, T3-4 3.3, T4-5 3.0, T5-6 3.5, T6-7 4.1, T7-8 4.2, T8-9 5.6, T9-10 5.4, T10-11 7.2, T11-12 6.0. The text: about 4.5 at C7-T1, falling to about 3 at T4-5, rising to about 7.2 at T10-11 and back to about 6 at T11-12 (p. 380, PDF 6). Radiographic means (Table 1): 3.96, 3.69, 3.23, 3.07, 2.85, 3.36, 3.70, 4.06, 4.52, 4.88, 5.91, 5.90, from 2.85 (T4-5) to 5.91 (T10-11).
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed. Fig. 4a (p. 381, PDF 7) compares Todd and Pyle's anatomical ADH (male cadavers): read from the figure, their values match this study at C7-T1 and T1-2, lie lower at every level from T2-3 to T10-11 (about 2.1 against 3.0 at T4-5, about 4.3 against 5.6 at T8-9 and about 4.9 against 7.2 at T10-11, several points below this study's lower SD band) and slightly higher at T11-T12 (about 6.4 against 6.0). Fig. 4b: radiographic ADH set beside Manns (plotted only from T5-6 to T11-12) and Giles and Singer (2000), whose values run about 0.3-1 mm lower than this study's from T2-3 to T10-11 (read from the figure). The authors call both comparisons good agreement (p. 383, PDF 9).
- Error: anatomical SD 0.30 (T3-4) to 1.74 (T9-10); radiographic SD 0.22 (C7-T1) to 1.03 (T9-10); radiographic shortfall per level 1.67 percent (T11-12) to 19.22 percent (T8-9), absolute 0.10-1.29 mm (Table 1); R^2 of radiographic on anatomical level means 0.921 (P = 7.98E-07, Fig. 2a, p. 380, PDF 6); inter-observer "CV" for disc heights 0.79-0.93 on sections, 0.49-0.82 on films; intra-observer error 1.7-6.1 percent (p. 380, PDF 6).

### PDH: posterior disc height

- Landmarks: as ADH at the posterior margins (posterior inferior corner of the upper body to posterior superior corner of the lower body on film; posterior edge of the disc on the section, cartilaginous end-plates included). Fig. 1, p. 376 (PDF 2).
- Plane, instrument, levels: as ADH.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. As ADH at the posterior corners in the mid-sagittal slab; the posterior corner region is where body and pedicle roots meet, so restrict to the body subregion.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, but poor: radiographic PDH reads on average about 37 percent shorter than anatomical (abstract p. 375, PDF 1; p. 380, PDF 6), 28.91 percent (C7-T1) to 50.12 percent (T8-9) per level (Table 1), and its landmarks are hidden by overlapping shadows in the upper thoracic spine (p. 383, PDF 9). Predicting PDH from ADH (Table 4) gives a smaller error than measuring it on film (Fig. 9a; pp. 385-386, PDF 11-12; see Prediction equations).
- Across levels (anatomical means, Table 1): C7-T1 4.5, T1-2 4.3, T2-3 3.5, T3-4 3.2, T4-5 3.3, T5-6 3.6, T6-7 4.1, T7-8 3.6, T8-9 5.0, T9-10 4.2, T10-11 5.8, T11-12 4.8. Lowest at T3-4, highest at T10-11; the text gives a course like ADH's with values about 21 percent higher from T7-8 on (p. 380, PDF 6). Radiographic means (Table 1): 3.20, 3.04, 1.95, 2.09, 2.00, 2.10, 2.18, 2.47, 2.49, 2.97, 3.66, 3.23, from 1.95 (T2-3) to 3.66 (T10-11).
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed. Fig. 4c (p. 381, PDF 7): Giles and Singer's radiographic PDH runs much higher than this study's radiographic PDH (read from the figure, about 3.7-4.2 from T1-2 to T9-10, about 5.0 at T10-11 and 5.8 at T11-12, against 2.0-3.7 here; none plotted at C7-T1). The authors attribute the small differences between radiographic studies to magnification bias, positioning errors and parallax (pp. 383-384, PDF 9-10). Inference: Giles and Singer's radiographic PDH lies within about 1 mm of this study's anatomical PDH at every level, so it is this study's radiographic PDH, not theirs, that looks short.
- Error: anatomical SD 0.45 (T4-5) to 1.26 (T11-12); radiographic SD 0.21 (T4-5) to 0.70 (T10-11); R^2 radiographic on anatomical level means 0.690 (P = 0.0008, Fig. 2b); inter-observer "CV" for radiographic PDH 0.49, the lowest of all; intra-observer error 17-26.1 percent. p. 380 (PDF 6).

### MDH: middle disc height

- Landmarks: distance between two mid-vertebral points, the middle of the superior and of the inferior end-plate bounding the disc, on the mid-sagittal section, cartilaginous end-plates included. p. 377 (PDF 3); Fig. 1b.
- Plane or projection: mid-sagittal section only.
- Instrument: vernier caliper.
- Levels: C7-T1 to T11-T12.
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. In the mid-sagittal slab, the gap along the cranio-caudal axis between the centre of the inferior end-plate surface of the upper body mask and the centre of the superior end-plate surface of the lower body mask (centre = midpoint of the end-plate's AP depth). It includes the bony end-plate concavities, which is why it exceeds the rim heights.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible. The midline distance between the oval images of the two end-plates cannot be identified on a film (p. 383, PDF 9, citing Edmondston 1999). Inference: the concave end-plate projects as an oval or double contour on a lateral film.
- Across levels (anatomical means, Table 1): C7-T1 5.6, T1-2 5.7, T2-3 5.8, T3-4 4.4, T4-5 5.0, T5-6 4.7, T6-7 5.5, T7-8 5.3, T8-9 6.1, T9-10 5.5, T10-11 6.7, T11-12 6.5. Lowest at T3-4, highest at T10-11. The text and abstract say MDH is on average 22.7 percent higher than the average disc height (p. 375, PDF 1; p. 380, PDF 6); that figure is the mean of Table 3's "% MDH" column (Inference, recomputed: 22.57), which is a share of MDH (see I2).
- Sex, age, size, population: not analysed. The authors found no published MDH to compare with (p. 383, PDF 9).
- Error: SD 0.45 (T6-7) to 1.18 (T10-11) (Table 1); no repeatability figure is given for MDH by itself.

### Average disc height

- Definition: mean of anatomical ADH and PDH at each level (Table 1 footnote, p. 378, PDF 4).
- Across levels (Table 1): C7-T1 4.5, T1-2 4.4, T2-3 3.5, T3-4 3.3, T4-5 3.2, T5-6 3.5, T6-7 4.1, T7-8 3.9, T8-9 5.3, T9-10 4.8, T10-11 6.5, T11-12 5.4. Range 3.2 to 6.5, mean 4.3 +/- 1 (p. 380, PDF 6). The thinnest disc is T4-5 (Fig. 3, asterisk, p. 380, PDF 6).
- From a CT mask or radiograph: as ADH and PDH.

### Average vertebral body height

- Definition: mean of VBHA and VBHP (Table 2 footnote). Range 14.14 (C7) to 21.96 (T12) (Table 2; the text says 14.2 to 21.96, p. 380, PDF 6); it rises at every step except T4 to T5 (16.79 to 16.59). Inference, derived: the ratio of each body's average height to the one above is 0.988 (T5/T4) to 1.075 (T11/T10).

### I1: relative disc height index (and the disc : body height ratio)

- Definition: I1 = (ADH + PDH) / (VBHA + VBHP), after Amonoo-Kuofi 1991; disc : body ratio = 1 / I1. Table 3 footnotes, p. 379 (PDF 5); Fig. 1. The disc heights are anatomical and the body heights the only ones measured, the radiographic Table 2 values. Inference, from recomputing Table 3 from Tables 1 and 2: the body used is the upper (cranial) vertebra of each segment (C7 for C7-T1, T11 for T11-T12); the cranial body reproduces every printed I1, the caudal body or the mean of both do not (C7-T1: 0.318 cranial, 0.302 caudal, printed 0.32).
- Size-free and level-specific: the most useful number in the paper for naming without a scale.
- Across levels (I1, Table 3, p. 379, PDF 5): C7-T1 0.32, T1-2 0.30, T2-3 0.22, T3-4 0.20, T4-5 0.19, T5-6 0.21, T6-7 0.24, T7-8 0.23, T8-9 0.29, T9-10 0.25, T10-11 0.33, T11-12 0.26. Ratio (Table 3): 1:3.1, 1:3.4, 1:4.5, 1:5.1, 1:5.3, 1:4.7, 1:4.2, 1:4.4, 1:3.5, 1:4.0, 1:3.0, 1:3.9, from 1:3.0 (T10-11) to 1:5.3 (T4-5). A U shape: relatively tall discs at the cervicothoracic junction, the relatively thinnest at T3-4 and T4-5, tall again at T10-11. Overall about 1:4.1 (p. 380, PDF 6); by region 1:4.0 upper, 1:4.7 middle, 1:3.8 lower (Fig. 3, p. 380, PDF 6). Inference, recomputed: these region and overall figures are means of the per-level ratios (4.03, 4.73, 3.76; 4.09).
- From a 3D CT instance mask: Inference. ADH and PDH from the gap to the next body down, VBHA and VBHP of the upper body; all four from bone masks, so the mask I1 should sit between the paper's anatomical I1 and the radiographic version below.
- From a radiograph alone: lateral radiograph, but with radiographic disc heights the index runs lower. Inference, derived from Table 1 radiographic means over Table 2 cranial bodies: C7-T1 0.253, T1-2 0.226, T2-3 0.166, T3-4 0.156, T4-5 0.144, T5-6 0.165, T6-7 0.172, T7-8 0.189, T8-9 0.189, T9-10 0.204, T10-11 0.243, T11-12 0.216; the same U shape, minimum at T4-5.
- Error: no SD per level is printed for any index; with n = 6 per level and disc SDs up to 1.7 mm, the zig-zags (T7-8 below T6-7 and T8-9; T9-10 below T8-9; T11-12 below T10-11) may be sampling noise (Inference).

### I2: disc convexity index

- Definition: I2 = MDH / (ADH + PDH), after Twomey and Taylor 1987 (Table 3 footnote). Inference: 0.5 means the middle is as tall as the mean of the rims; above 0.5 the disc is biconvex (the end-plates are concave).
- Across levels (Table 3): C7-T1 0.63, T1-2 0.65, T2-3 0.83, T3-4 0.67, T4-5 0.80, T5-6 0.67, T6-7 0.68, T7-8 0.68, T8-9 0.57, T9-10 0.58, T10-11 0.52, T11-12 0.60. The authors say the convexity follows no steady trend down the column and is most pronounced at T2-3 (p. 380, PDF 6); Table 3 has the least at T10-11.
- Companion column "% MDH to average disc height" (Table 3): C7-T1 20.55, T1-2 23.11, T2-3 39.65, T3-4 25.17, T4-5 37.50, T5-6 25.58, T6-7 25.95, T7-8 26.75, T8-9 12.56, T9-10 13.74, T10-11 3.50, T11-12 16.77. Fig. 3's column headed "Disc convexity" gives 27.12 percent upper, 29.68 middle, 14.66 lower; Inference, recomputed: these are the region means of this column, not of I2. Inference, also recomputed: the column is (MDH - average disc height) / MDH, a percentage of MDH; by the usual (MDH / average - 1) the excess would be about 3 to 68 percent, mean about 31.
- From a CT mask: Inference. MDH and the rim heights as above; on a bone mask I2 rises with end-plate concavity.
- From a radiograph alone: not possible (no MDH on film).

### I3: disc antero-posterior wedging index

- Definition: I3 = ADH / PDH (Table 3 footnote). Above 1 the disc is taller in front; the authors call that a "posterior wedge" configuration (p. 380, PDF 6), the opposite of some authors' naming.
- Across levels (Table 3, anatomical): C7-T1 1.00, T1-2 1.06, T2-3 0.97, T3-4 1.03, T4-5 0.89, T5-6 0.97, T6-7 1.01, T7-8 1.16, T8-9 1.13, T9-10 1.29, T10-11 1.23, T11-12 1.25. Apart from C7-T1 every disc is wedged; from T7-8 down ADH exceeds PDH by about 21.25 percent (the mean of the five I3 values, Inference); T1-2 to T6-7 show only small wedges (p. 380, PDF 6). Fig. 3 labels the regions upper ADH >= PDH, middle ADH <= PDH, lower ADH > PDH; T2-3 (0.97) in the upper and T6-7 (1.01) in the middle region break those labels (Table 3).
- From a radiograph alone: lateral film, but radiographic I3 is systematically larger because radiographic PDH is short. Inference, derived from Table 1 radiographic means: C7-T1 1.24, T1-2 1.21, T2-3 1.66, T3-4 1.47, T4-5 1.43, T5-6 1.60, T6-7 1.70, T7-8 1.64, T8-9 1.82, T9-10 1.64, T10-11 1.61, T11-12 1.83. Never compare a film I3 with the anatomical I3.
- From a CT mask: Inference. ADH / PDH of the gap between consecutive body masks; depends on segment posture (neutral, unloaded specimens here).

## Measurement error and reliability

- Inter-observer: anatomical disc heights "CV" 0.79-0.93; radiographic 0.49-0.82; radiographic PDH 0.49; radiographic VBHA and VBHP 0.95-0.98. p. 380 (PDF 6). Inference: these "CV" values rise with better repeatability, so they behave like a reliability coefficient, not a conventional coefficient of variation; the paper does not define the statistic further.
- Intra-observer: errors 1.7-6.1 percent for ADH, 17-26.1 percent for PDH, 1.7-5.1 percent for VBHA and VBHP; reproducibility from repeat radiographs "generally 15% lower" (p. 380, PDF 6). The text calls these "anatomical measurements" though the methods say the intra-observer work used 10 radiographs (p. 377, PDF 3) and VBHA and VBHP were taken on film only. No intra-observer figure for MDH.
- Radiographic against anatomical: ADH about 9 percent shorter (1.67-19.22 percent by level; the mean of the 12 levels is 9.37), PDH about 37 percent shorter (28.91-50.12 percent; mean 37.13); linear R^2 0.921 for ADH, 0.690 for PDH across level means (Table 1; Fig. 2; p. 380, PDF 6). The authors' explanation: the anatomical caliper included the cartilaginous end-plates, which films do not show (p. 383, PDF 9). Inference: since cartilaginous end-plates are radiolucent and lie inside the radiographic disc space, that explanation is doubtful; end-plate rims projecting into the disc space when not parallel to the beam, and corner placement on the film, are likelier causes.
- Instrument: digital caliper +/- 0.05 mm; magnification correction by a scale on the rig; 60 cm focus-film distance (p. 377, PDF 3). Anatomical means are printed only to 0.1 mm (Table 1).
- Remaining radiographic error sources despite controlled positioning: the two main ambiguities, disc orientation to the central beam and differences between observers, were handled by Pope 1977's and Andersson 1981's recommendations; landmark identification stayed hard where shadows overlap; magnification bias, positioning errors and parallax may explain differences between radiographic studies (pp. 383-384, PDF 9-10).
- Biological spread: per-level SDs in Tables 1 and 2 (above); n = 6 discs per level. The SE column of Table 4 (0.27-0.66 mm) is the residual of a fit to level means (Inference, see What the paper is), not the error of predicting one specimen.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Indications for the data: the thoracic spine is the commonest site of deformity (kyphosis, scoliosis), disc data are needed for implants and for mathematical models, and disc height is used as a diagnostic tool in orthopaedics. pp. 375, 382, 386 (PDF 1, 8, 12). The data are offered as a reference for models and for anatomical and biomechanical studies in the mid-sagittal plane (p. 386, PDF 12).
- A disc has no single height: the planes bounding it are not parallel, so ADH, MDH and PDH differ within one disc and between regions (pp. 382-383, PDF 8-9), against Oliver and Middleditch's "nearly uniform" thickness. The authors say this may reflect the donors' age, or loss of disc height in scoliosis or disc herniation, and that post-mortem change and degeneration may add error (p. 383, PDF 9).
- The sample is only mild to moderately degenerated (grades ii and iii); a grade iv disc or a body with anterior osteophytes over 2 mm is outside these norms, and grade iii bodies already carry marginal osteophytes under 2 mm at the corners (p. 379, PDF 5).
- Cadaveric discs carry no body weight: the authors expect these values to be slightly larger than radiographic values in a living person sitting or standing, which matters for arthroplasty and for models (pp. 384-385, PDF 10-11).
- MDH cannot be taken from a film; radiographic PDH is unreliable, worst in the upper thoracic spine where shadows overlap (p. 383, PDF 9).
- On a film, PDH predicted from ADH carried smaller errors (-13 to -17 percent, Fig. 9a) than PDH measured directly (-29 to -50 percent, Table 1); the authors conclude that the equations can reduce the radiographic error at least for PDH (p. 375, PDF 1; p. 386, PDF 12).
- Errors in radiographic body heights pass on into disc heights predicted from them (p. 385, PDF 11); the equations hold only under the paper's controlled conditions and need testing on patient data (pp. 385-386, PDF 11-12).
- Earlier work had gaps: Hurxthal 1968 and Manns measured ADH on films of women from T5-6 to T11-12 only; Todd and Pyle 1928 only ADH, in male cadavers of unreported age; Pooni 1986 few cadavers aged 73-85, reported as a percentage of spine height; Goh 1999 and Giles and Singer 2000 gave segmental trends for kyphosis, not ADH and PDH values; earlier radiographic work lacked standard positioning, control of film, specimen and focus distances, landmark visibility, magnification correction and reliability testing. pp. 375-376 (PDF 1-2).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. On a lateral radiograph or a lateral DRR, mark the four end-plate corners of each thoracic body (Farfan's method) and take VBHA and VBHP corner to corner after a magnification correction. p. 377 (PDF 3); Fig. 1a, p. 376 (PDF 2).
2. Expect VBHP > VBHA at every level from C7 to T12, the ratio about 1.03 at C7 and 1.07-1.18 from T2 to T12 (derived from Table 2, p. 379, PDF 5). Inference: a thoracic body with VBHA >= VBHP is unusual and points to a fracture, a measurement error or a body that is not thoracic.
3. Do not name a single thoracic vertebra from its body height: neighbours differ by about 0.6 mm against SDs of 1-2.4 mm, and the means fall at T4 (VBHA), T5 (VBHP and average) and T7 (VBHA) (Table 2, p. 379, PDF 5). Inference: fit the rising trend (average height about 14 mm at C7 to about 22 mm at T12) over a run of four or more consecutive bodies and anchor it to a junction found by other means.
4. Use disc-to-body ratios to place a run without a scale: I1 = (ADH + PDH) / (VBHA + VBHP of the body above) is 0.30-0.32 at C7-T1 and T1-2, 0.19-0.20 at T3-4 and T4-5, and 0.25-0.33 from T8-9 to T11-12 (Table 3, p. 379, PDF 5). Use the anatomical values for CT masks and the lower radiographic values (about 0.14-0.25, derived from Table 1) for films. Inference: the U-shaped minimum marks T3 to T5.
5. Use disc wedging as a regional marker: the cervicothoracic disc is parallel (I3 1.00), T1-2 to T6-7 near parallel (0.89-1.06), T7-8 to T11-12 taller in front (1.13-1.29). Compare like with like: on a film I3 is inflated (about 1.2-1.8, derived) because radiographic PDH is short. Table 3; p. 380 (PDF 6).
6. Never measure the middle disc height on a radiograph; take it only from CT or a section. p. 383 (PDF 9).
7. Treat radiographic posterior disc height as about 37 percent short and the least repeatable measure; prefer anterior disc height and body heights on films, and distrust posterior corners in the upper thoracic spine, where shadows overlap. p. 380 (PDF 6); p. 383 (PDF 9).
8. When a film's posterior disc height is needed, estimate it from the film's anterior disc height with the ADH to PDH cubic of Table 4 instead of measuring it; expect it about 13-17 percent low, since the equation was fitted to anatomical ADH and the film's ADH is short. Fig. 9a, p. 386 (PDF 12); Table 4, p. 382 (PDF 8).
9. Expect the thinnest thoracic disc at T4-5 (about 3.2 mm mean) and the tallest at T10-11 (about 6.5 mm). Table 1, p. 378 (PDF 4); Fig. 3, p. 380 (PDF 6). Inference, from other papers in this set: a further jump in disc height at T12-L1 marks the thoracolumbar junction, which this paper does not cover.
10. On a CT mask, measure disc heights as gaps between consecutive body masks along the local cranio-caudal axis in the mid-sagittal slab, at the anterior corners (ADH), posterior corners (PDH) and end-plate centres (MDH); use sub-voxel surfaces, since one voxel is 15-30 percent of a thoracic disc. Inference from the definitions on p. 377 (PDF 3).
11. Do not use the Table 4 cubics in VBHA or VBHP for disc heights or for the other body height as printed; their three-decimal coefficients cannot carry a cubic in x near 20 mm. Evaluate any equation at the table means first, and use the refit coefficients in this note or refit from Tables 1 and 2 with the cranial body as x (Inference, my computation). Table 4, p. 382 (PDF 8).
12. Never extrapolate a cubic beyond the fitted range (VBHA 13.9-20.8 mm, VBHP 14.4-23.1 mm). Fig. 9b, p. 386 (PDF 12), shows a 17 percent error at T12 on Todd and Pyle's data; Inference: their T12 VBHA (about 24 mm read from Fig. 9c) lies outside the fitted range.
13. Expect living, loaded discs on standing or sitting films to be somewhat thinner than these cadaveric values, and severely degenerated discs or bodies with anterior osteophytes over 2 mm to fall outside them. pp. 379, 384-385 (PDF 5, 10-11).
14. Before using a radiographic disc height, check that the end-plates are seen edge-on and the beam is centred on the disc, as the paper did; the paper names disc orientation to the central beam as a main source of radiographic ambiguity. pp. 377, 383 (PDF 3, 9). Inference: an oblique end-plate gives a double contour and shortens the projected disc space.

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of transitional vertebrae, rib anomalies or numbering variants and does not say how levels were counted (p. 377, PDF 3).

(b) Measures:
- Body heights: the generator's posterior body height (Panjabi's VBHp, dry bone, 3D digitiser) matches this paper's VBHP in landmarks. Inference: this paper's radiographic VBHP runs about 0.2-2.0 mm (mean about 1.0 mm) above Panjabi 1991's thoracic VBHp level by level (Panjabi T1 14.1 to T12 22.7, from the panjabi1991t note in this set, against 15.28 to 23.12 here), as Fig. 5b also shows; one possible reason is that a corner-to-corner height on film spans the end-plate rims while a digitiser point may sit elsewhere on the wall. Panjabi's frame is built on the posterior wall of the body with heights along its cranial axis (panjabi1991t note); a CT mask measured in that frame matches VBHp, while a lateral-film corner height does not exactly. VBHA (anterior body height) is not among Panjabi's thoracic measures in this set's notes; this paper offers it for C7-T12 and it could be added, with VBHP/VBHA as a size-free wedge ratio.
- Disc heights: the generator holds none (Panjabi measured dry bone). This paper gives ADH, MDH and PDH for C7-T1 to T11-T12 with SDs, plus I1, I2 and I3. These are pair features between consecutive instances and suit an HMM emission on transitions.
- Definitions that bias a mask value: the anatomical heights include cartilaginous end-plates (bone to bone on a fresh section), which a CT bone-mask gap also includes (Inference), so mask ADH, PDH and MDH should be compared with the anatomical columns, not the radiographic ones. A segmentation that eats into the end-plate cortex lengthens the gap; end-plate osteophytes (grade iii-iv) shorten ADH. I1 here uses the cranial body only (Inference from recomputation): compute it the same way. The paper's mid-sagittal plane is set by rods on the section, not by an anatomical frame (p. 377, PDF 3).
- Size-free features worth carrying (all from Tables 1-3 or derived): I1 by level, I3 by level, I2 by level, VBHP/VBHA by level, the ratio of consecutive body heights (derived: caudal over cranial average height 0.988-1.075 per step, Inference). None comes with a per-level SD; take the variance from the component SDs or from other data.

(c) Measurement error to use: radiographic ADH bias about -9 percent (level range -1.7 to -19.2), PDH about -37 percent (-28.9 to -50.1); intra-observer about 2-6 percent for ADH and body heights, 17-26 percent for PDH; between-specimen SDs ADH 0.30-1.74, PDH 0.45-1.26, MDH 0.45-1.18, VBHA 1.07-2.12, VBHP 1.12-2.44 mm, disc SDs each from 6 specimens. Add a voxel-size term for CT (Inference). The Table 4 SE values are residuals of fits to level means, not per-patient errors (Inference).

(d) Covariates: none. No sex, age, stature or population effect is analysed; the sample is 15 women and 15 men aged 37-80 of unstated origin. The regressions are across level means, so their R^2 is not a size-scaling coefficient and must not be used as a patient-size factor (Inference, confirmed by recomputing the printed P values from 12 or 13 points).
