## What the paper is

- Design: a cross-sectional radiographic morphometry of one measure, the lumbar interpedicular distance (IPD),
  in normal adults, with a comparison table against five earlier series. p. 33-35 (PDF 1-3).
- Aim as stated: to give normal IPD values for the lumbar canal and to learn whether it shows regional, ethnic
  or racial variation (abstract, p. 33, PDF 1); and, in the Discussion, to set a standard normal "minimum
  interpedicular distance" as a preliminary to investigating transverse lumbar canal stenosis (p. 35, PDF 3).
- Population and denominators: plain AP lumbar radiographs of 300 adults, 150 men and 150 women, of known sex
  and age, aged 25 to 40 years, from Sonepat district, Haryana, North India, taken from the radiology department
  of the authors' institution (B.P.S.G.M.C. for women, Khanpur Kalan, Sonepat). p. 33 (PDF 1, abstract); p. 34
  (PDF 2, Methods). Inference: living subjects (hospital films); why they were imaged, whether they were
  patients, whether they were consecutive and the dates of collection are not given. One film per subject and
  150 values per sex at every level are implied, never stated; no per-level n is printed.
- Inclusion: films reported normal by "experienced radiologists". Exclusion: any film with an "obvious
  abnormality". p. 34 (PDF 2). No exclusion list beyond that; no clinical history (back pain, claudication)
  is used.
- Modality and instrument: AP radiograph of the lumbar spine; a scale (ruler) calibrated to 0.5 mm, applied to
  the film. p. 34 (PDF 2). Tube-film distance, patient position (supine or erect), centring and any
  magnification correction are not stated. Who measured, how many times, and blinding are not stated.
- Levels: L1 to L5, both sexes separately (Table I, p. 34, PDF 2). Methods says the dimensions of "all lumbar
  vertebrae" were studied (p. 34, PDF 2). Nothing at T12, at the sacrum or above L1.
- How levels were identified and counted: not stated. Transitional segments: not mentioned; whether an LSTV
  counted as an "obvious abnormality" is unknown.
- Statistics: mean, SD, a "calculated range" of mean +/- 3 SD, and a male-female p-value per level (all
  < 0.001); the test is not named (Table I, p. 34, PDF 2). Inference: a two-sample t from the printed means and
  SDs (n 150 each) gives t = 7.3, 5.3, 7.0, 3.9 and 4.2 at L1 to L5, so p < 0.001 holds at every level. No
  test is reported for the between-series comparison of Table II.
- Ethics: clearance from the ethical committee of B.P.S.G.M.C. for women, Sonepat (p. 36, PDF 4).
  Acknowledgement "nil" and conflict of interest "nil" (p. 36, PDF 4); funding is not stated.

## Anomalies, numbering and transitional segments

The paper says nothing about lumbosacral transitional vertebrae, sacralisation, lumbarisation, L6, T13, rib
counts, block vertebrae or how L1 and L5 were identified; no prevalence of any variant is given. The only
related steps are the exclusion of films with any "obvious abnormality" (p. 34, PDF 2) and a generic mention,
in the Discussion, of congenital malformations among the spinal disorders whose diagnosis CT and MRI have
advanced (p. 35, PDF 3), with no count.

The paper names two conditions an abnormal IPD may signal, with no prevalence: canal stenosis (IPD below the
sex- and level-specific lower limit) and an intraspinal tumour (above the upper limit) (text C, p. 34, PDF 2).

Inference: the male L5 SD (5.9 mm) is 1.5 to 2.7 times the SD at L1 to L4 in men and 1.7 times the female L5 SD
(Table I, p. 34, PDF 2); an unrecognised transitional L5 (sacralised, with pedicles of altered shape) or a
miscount at the lumbosacral junction could inflate it, as could the difficulty of reading L5 pedicles through
the sacral ala and iliac crest on an AP film. The paper offers no explanation.

## Measures, each as a recipe

### Interpedicular distance (IPD), which the paper equates with the transverse diameter of the spinal canal

- Definition: the "minimum distance between the shadows of pedicles of same vertebra" on the AP lumbar
  radiograph; the authors take this as the canal's transverse diameter (p. 34, PDF 2). One value per vertebra.
  The paper does not say "inner margin", "medial", "cortex" or at which height; it says only "minimum" and
  "shadows". Inference: the minimum distance between the two pedicle ring shadows is the gap between their
  medial (inner) cortical margins where the rings bulge most medially, near mid pedicle height. Inference
  supporting this: Amonoo-Kuofi 1982, which the paper tabulates (Table II), defines the same film measure as the
  shortest distance between the medial surfaces of the two pedicles (Amonoo-Kuofi p. 226, Fig. 1), and Hinck
  1966 uses the same wording (see the hinck1966 note).
- Projection: AP plain radiograph. Instrument: ruler calibrated to 0.5 mm (p. 34, PDF 2). Magnification: not
  stated and not corrected; all values are film millimetres.
- Levels: L1 to L5.
- Values, Table I, p. 34 (PDF 2), mm, mean (SD), printed range = mean +/- 3 SD:
  - Men (150): L1 24.06 (2.2) 17.6-30.54; L2 24.73 (2.2) 18.1-31.33; L3 26.01 (2.4) 18.66-33.36;
    L4 27.15 (3.9) 15.15-39.15; L5 31.34 (5.9) 13.34-49.34.
  - Women (150): L1 21.64 (3.4) 11.4-31.8; L2 23.31 (2.4) 16.11-30.5; L3 24.02 (2.5) 16.52-31.52;
    L4 25.71 (2.4) 18.5-32.9; L5 29.01 (3.4) 18.8-39.2.
  - p < 0.001 for the sex difference at every level (Table I).
- From a 3D CT instance mask (Inference throughout; the paper is radiographic): (1) Put the instance in its own
  frame: cranio-caudal axis normal to the end-plates, antero-posterior axis from the body centroid toward the
  spinous process, left-right axis orthogonal to both. (2) Take the pedicles as the arch voxels between the
  posterior body wall and the front of the articular processes, in the slices where the bony ring around the
  canal is closed on both sides (SPINEPS gives the arch, pedicles and laminae together, as one subregion, so the
  pedicles must be cut out by position). (3) Project these voxels along the antero-posterior axis onto the
  coronal plane, as the AP beam does. (4) For each cranio-caudal row that crosses both pedicles, take the
  left-right gap between the most medial voxel of the left silhouette and the most medial voxel of the right
  silhouette; IPD is the smallest gap over those rows. This is the film-equivalent "minimum distance between
  the shadows". (5) The result is true size; multiply by an AP film magnification before comparing with Table I,
  or compare level-to-level ratios, which cancel magnification. (6) Do not use the width of SPINEPS's
  spinal-canal label, the width at the laminae, or the lateral recess: they are other quantities.
- From a radiograph alone: AP lumbar radiograph (or AP fluoroscopy), the medial borders of the pedicle ovals;
  not obtainable from a lateral (Inference: IPD is a transverse dimension). Values carry an unknown film
  magnification (p. 34, PDF 2). Inference: on a supine AP film at about 100 cm the factor is of the order of
  1.1; Eisenstein 1977 measured 1.14 for the AP view at 110 cm (Eisenstein p. 175), and on a C-arm it depends on
  the source-to-detector and patient-to-detector distances. Axial rotation of the vertebra shortens the
  projected IPD (Inference; not discussed).
- Across levels: rises from L1 to L5 in both sexes (Table I and text A, p. 34, PDF 2; abstract p. 33, PDF 1;
  conclusion p. 36, PDF 4). Smallest at L1, largest at L5. Inference (arithmetic from Table I):
  - Steps, men: L1-L2 +0.67, L2-L3 +1.28, L3-L4 +1.14, L4-L5 +4.19 mm; women: +1.67, +0.71, +1.69, +3.30 mm.
  - Step ratios (lower/upper), men 1.028, 1.052, 1.044, 1.154; women 1.077, 1.030, 1.070, 1.128. L5/L1 is
    1.30 in men and 1.34 in women; L5 is 7.3 mm (men) and 7.4 mm (women) wider than L1.
  - Step divided by the mean SD of the two levels (between-person separation), men 0.30, 0.56, 0.36, 0.86;
    women 0.58, 0.29, 0.69, 1.14. Only L4 to L5 separates well; L1 vs L2 in men and L2 vs L3 in women barely
    separate by absolute value across people.
- Sex, age, size, population: men wider at every level by 2.42, 1.42, 1.99, 1.44 and 2.33 mm at L1 to L5
  (Inference: arithmetic from Table I); female/male ratio 0.90, 0.94, 0.92, 0.95, 0.93. The sex difference is
  significant at every level (p < 0.001, Table I, p. 34, PDF 2) and the authors call it sexual dimorphism
  (p. 36, PDF 4). Age: only 25-40 years, no age analysis. Body size: no height, weight or body-width data; the
  authors attribute the smaller values than in North Indians (Chhabra 1991) to a difference in build, with no
  data (p. 35, PDF 3). Population: the authors claim regional, ethnic and racial variation and call for
  separate male and female ranges for each region (p. 33, PDF 1; p. 35, PDF 3; p. 36, PDF 4).
- Error: none reported. Instrument resolution 0.5 mm (p. 34, PDF 2). No intra- or inter-observer data, no
  repeat measurement, no magnification correction. The SDs (2.2 to 5.9 mm) mix biological spread and
  measurement error.

How Eisenstein is cited (special attention):
- Both Eisenstein rows are attributed to reference 2, Eisenstein S. (1977), J Bone Joint Surg 59-B:173-180,
  whose printed title names "South African Negroes and Caucasoids" (p. 36, PDF 4); Table II relabels the
  Negro group "Zulus" and omits Eisenstein's third group. The introduction cites Eisenstein 1977 (with Hinck
  1966) among workers who used plain radiography and names his group only as South African Caucasoids
  (p. 34, PDF 2). A separate Eisenstein paper, 1976 (Clin Orthop 115:42-46, reference 4), is cited only for the
  canal's change of shape (p. 33, PDF 1).
- Checked against Eisenstein 1977 itself (Table I, p. 174 of that paper, read from its page image): the four
  Eisenstein rows of Chaudhary's Table II are his Caucasoid and Zulu "Canal I.P." rows exactly; his Sotho rows
  (male 21 21 22 23 25, female 20 20 21 22 24) are left out. Eisenstein's values are vernier-caliper readings
  on 2,166 dry lumbar vertebrae of 443 skeletons of the Raymond Dart Collection (Caucasoid 78 men and 35
  women; Zulu 108 men and 54 women; Sotho 106 men and 62 women; age at death 16 to 96, mean 50), with the
  transverse diameter taken as "the greatest interpedicular distance", in whole millimetres (Eisenstein p. 173).
  He radiographed only 55 vertebrae of 11 skeletons, to derive magnification factors (AP 1.14 at 110 cm;
  Eisenstein p. 174-175). Inference: Chaudhary therefore (a) presents a dry-bone series as radiographic, (b)
  sets true-size skeletal values beside magnified film values with no correction, (c) sets a greatest-distance
  definition beside a minimum-distance one, and (d) prints Eisenstein's male whole millimetres with two zero
  decimals (23.00), a precision the source never had. Eisenstein's skeletons were older (16 to 96) than the
  Sonepat subjects (25 to 40).
- Checked against Amonoo-Kuofi 1982 itself (Table 1, p. 228 of that paper): the Nigerian rows match exactly;
  those are film values from 150 men and 140 women at an anode-film distance of 100 cm with no correction
  (Amonoo-Kuofi p. 226), the same kind of value as Chaudhary's.
- Inference: dividing Chaudhary's male film values by a factor of 1.1 to 1.14 gives about 21.1 to 21.9 mm at
  L1, 21.7 to 22.5 at L2, 22.8 to 23.6 at L3, 23.8 to 24.7 at L4 and 27.5 to 28.5 at L5: below Eisenstein's
  Caucasoid dry-bone male means at L1 and L2, about equal at L3 and L4, above only at L5, where Table II shows
  the Sonepat men 0.73 to 5.34 mm wider at every level. Not a statement of the paper.
- The authors read Table II as minimal variation among ethnic groups, highest in North Indians and lowest in
  Zulus, with Sonepat between and comparable to Nigerians (p. 35, PDF 3). Inference (Table II): Zulus are
  lowest at every level in both sexes except male L5, where Zulu and Caucasoid tie at 26.00.

## Level by level and at the junctions

- L1: narrowest in both sexes (men 24.06, women 21.64). Largest sex difference (2.42 mm) and the largest female
  SD (3.4, equal to L5) of the female series; the female lower limit (11.4 mm) is the lowest of all printed
  limits. Table I, p. 34 (PDF 2).
- L2: men only 0.67 mm above L1; women 1.67 mm above L1. Smallest sex difference (1.42 mm). Table I.
- L3: men 26.01, women 24.02; the women's L2 to L3 step (0.71 mm) is the smallest female step. Table I.
- L4: men's SD jumps to 3.9 (from 2.4 at L3) while women's stays 2.4. Table I.
- L5: widest in both sexes (men 31.34, women 29.01); the L4 to L5 step is the largest (+4.19 men, +3.30 women).
  The male SD of 5.9 gives a printed normal range of 13.34 to 49.34 mm, wider than any other cell; the male L5
  lower limit lies below the male L1 lower limit. Table I.
- Junctions: the paper says nothing about T12/L1 or L5/S1, nor about the thoracic or sacral canal except in the
  introduction (next item).
- Canal shape by region (introduction, citing Eisenstein 1976): the upper lumbar canal is nearly circular and
  becomes triangular in the lower lumbar spine, a transition between the circular thoracic canal and the
  triangular sacral canal; a trefoil configuration may predispose to lumbar and sacral root compression. p. 33-34
  (PDF 1-2). Boundaries of the lumbar canal, which the paper calls triangular (citing Gray's Anatomy 35th
  edition): in front the vertebral bodies, discs and posterior longitudinal ligament; laterally the pedicles;
  posterolaterally the laminae and ligamenta flava; behind, the junction of the laminae with the spinous
  processes. p. 33 (PDF 1).

## Measurement error and reliability

- Instrument: ruler calibrated to 0.5 mm (p. 34, PDF 2). Inference: reading resolution about +/-0.25 mm, small
  beside the SDs.
- No observer study, no repeat readings, no named observer, no statement of blinding (p. 34, PDF 2).
- Magnification: not stated, not corrected (p. 34, PDF 2).
- Biological plus measurement spread, as SD: men 2.2, 2.2, 2.4, 3.9, 5.9 mm; women 3.4, 2.4, 2.5, 2.4, 3.4 mm at
  L1 to L5 (Table I, p. 34, PDF 2). Inference: coefficient of variation men 9, 9, 9, 14, 19 percent; women 16,
  10, 10, 9, 12 percent, larger than Amonoo-Kuofi 1982's 6.1 to 9.4 percent (his Table 1) and Hinck 1966's 8.8
  to 12.5 percent at L1 to L5 (hinck1966 note), which suggests more measurement scatter here, or a less selected
  sample.
- Internal arithmetic (Inference): in men the printed ranges are not mean +/- 3 x the printed SD at L1 (implies
  SD 2.16), L3 (2.45), L4 (4.0) and L5 (6.0); the women's ranges and the male L2 range agree with their SDs.
  Differences are small but show the SDs were rounded after the ranges were computed, or the reverse.

## Clinical pearls, indications and pitfalls

- Clinical frame (introduction, p. 33, PDF 1): the lumbar region is the commonest seat of backache; the lumbar
  canal holds the cauda equina; narrowing of the canal, developmental or acquired, can compress the cauda equina
  and cause pain with or without neurological signs, which is why canal dimensions matter.
- Indication: normal ranges of IPD by sex and level as a preliminary step in investigating transverse lumbar
  canal stenosis (p. 35, PDF 3), and in detecting isolated segmental changes (p. 34, PDF 2).
- Threshold rule as stated: an IPD below the lower limit of mean - 3 SD for that sex and level may suggest canal
  stenosis at that level; above the upper limit of mean + 3 SD may suggest an intraspinal tumour at that level
  (text C, p. 34, PDF 2).
- Uses claimed in the conclusion: radiological detection of bony canal stenosis, some intraspinal tumours,
  vertical compression of vertebral bodies and prolapsed discs (p. 36, PDF 4). Inference: the paper measured
  only the IPD, which shows none of the last two directly, and it reports no "indices" though the conclusion
  speaks of them.
- Mechanism cited: thickening of the laminae narrows the canal and reduces the IPD, called the second most common
  cause of canal narrowing, citing Sachs and Frankel (p. 35, PDF 3). Inference: laminar thickening narrows the
  sagittal canal and lateral recess; the IPD is set by the pedicles, so this attribution is doubtful, and the
  cited 1900 paper (reference 10, p. 36, PDF 4) is titled on ankylosing rigidity of the spine.
- Context: plain radiography is cheaper for the patient and remains the mainstay in rural settings despite CT
  and MRI (p. 34, PDF 2; p. 35, PDF 3); careful reading of plain films gives more than ultrasound (citing Hawkes
  and Roberts, p. 34, PDF 2); the canal can be studied in the living by plain radiography, transverse axial
  tomography, ultrasound, CT and MRI (p. 34, PDF 2).
- Pitfall (Inference): mean +/- 3 SD (about 99.7 percent coverage under normality) with SDs of 3.4 to 5.9 mm
  gives limits so wide (male L5 13.34 to 49.34 mm; female L1 11.4 to 31.8 mm) that almost no stenosis or tumour
  would fall outside them. Hinck 1966 used 90 percent tolerance ranges and Amonoo-Kuofi 1982 mean +/- 2 SD;
  every one of Chaudhary's ten lower limits (11.4 to 18.66 mm) lies below Eisenstein 1977's radiological lower
  limit of normal for the IPD, 20 mm on AP films at 110 cm (Eisenstein p. 175, Table II).
- Pitfall (Inference): uncorrected film values must not be compared with dry-bone or CT values (Table II does).
- Pitfall (Inference): a "minimum distance between shadows" and Eisenstein's "greatest interpedicular distance"
  are different definitions and should not be pooled.
- Pitfall: the authors stress that norms are sex- and region-specific (p. 35, PDF 3; p. 36, PDF 4).

## Instructions for the autonomous anatomist

1. On an AP lumbar radiograph or AP fluoroscopy frame, find both pedicle rings of a vertebra and measure the
   smallest horizontal gap between them, one value per vertebra; that is the IPD as this paper takes it
   (p. 34, PDF 2). Take the gap between the rings' medial borders (Inference, as Amonoo-Kuofi 1982 states it).
   Do not try to get it from a lateral view.
2. Expect the IPD to rise from L1 to L5 with the biggest step from L4 to L5 (about +4.2 mm men, +3.3 mm women,
   about 13 to 15 percent; Table I, p. 34, PDF 2). Use the widest IPD and the L4 to L5 jump as a caudal anchor
   for naming L5 (Inference); do not rely on IPD to tell L1 from L2 in men (0.67 mm apart, SD 2.2) or L2 from L3
   in women (0.71 mm apart, SD 2.4 to 2.5) (Table I).
3. Work in ratios along the column (each level's IPD over the patient's own L1 or L3 IPD) rather than absolute
   millimetres, because this paper's values are uncorrected film values with unknown magnification (p. 34,
   PDF 2) and fluoroscopic magnification varies with the C-arm (Inference).
4. If absolute values are needed, convert a CT or calibrated value to film scale with a stated factor (for
   example Eisenstein's 1.14 for AP at 110 cm, Eisenstein p. 175), never by assuming the Table I values are true
   size (Inference).
5. Condition on sex: women are 1.4 to 2.4 mm narrower at every level (Table I, p. 34, PDF 2). Inference: a sex
   error moves a value by about one adjacent-level step from L1 to L4 (steps 0.67 to 1.69 mm), less than the
   L4 to L5 step.
6. Flag a level whose IPD lies outside the mean +/- 3 SD range for its sex as possible stenosis (low) or an
   intraspinal mass (high) (p. 34, PDF 2), but compare it with its neighbours too: a single level wider than the
   one below it breaks the normal caudal rise (Inference). Treat the +/- 3 SD limits as loose (see pitfalls).
7. Treat a male L5 IPD with caution: its SD (5.9 mm) is far larger than at other levels (Table I); check the
   lumbosacral junction for a transitional segment before trusting the L5 label (Inference; the paper does not
   discuss transitional segments).
8. When drawing on a published comparison table, check whether each source is dry bone, CT or film and which
   definition (minimum or greatest) it used before pooling; Table II of this paper mixes them (p. 35, PDF 3;
   Eisenstein 1977 p. 173).
9. Use population-matched norms where they exist; this series is North Indian (Haryana), 25 to 40 years
   (p. 34, PDF 2); do not apply it to children or the elderly (Inference: no data outside 25 to 40).

## For bordercall's generator and classifier

(a) Anomaly priors for the sequence decoder: none. The paper gives no prevalence of LSTV, L6, T13 or any other
variant, and does not say how levels were counted.

(b) Measures:
- Spinal canal width (Panjabi's SCW): the generator holds a canal width; Panjabi 1992 measured it on cadaveric
  vertebrae as the canal's lateral width, true size, L1 23.7 to L5 27.1 mm; Panjabi does not say whether it is
  the interpedicular distance, and the panjabi1992 note infers from his Figure 1 that it lies between the
  medial pedicle walls. Chaudhary's IPD differs in three ways that bias a mask value against Table I:
  magnification (film values larger than true by an unstated factor, about 10 to 15 percent; Inference),
  definition (minimum gap between projected pedicle shadows, which is no larger than the axial-slice width at
  any height through the pedicles; Inference), and population (North Indian, 25 to 40 years). A mask-derived IPD
  should be computed as the projected minimum gap (recipe above) and compared with these values only after
  scaling.
- Size-free cue the generator could add: the IPD ratio of adjacent levels and L5/L1 (men 1.30, women 1.34;
  Inference from Table I). The canal/body-width ratio is not in this paper (Amonoo-Kuofi 1982 gives it).
- Use for level likelihoods: within-person rise L1 to L5 with a large L4-L5 step; between-person separation of
  adjacent levels is weak except at L4-L5 (standardised separation 0.3 to 0.7, and 0.86 men, 1.14 women at L4-L5;
  Inference).

(c) Measurement error: none reported. Use 0.5 mm as instrument resolution (p. 34, PDF 2); the total SD per level
and sex (Table I) is an upper bound on measurement error plus biological spread. Inference: treat the male L4
and L5 SDs (3.9, 5.9) as inflated relative to other series (Hinck adult 3.0 and 3.7; Amonoo-Kuofi men 1.7 and
2.3).

(d) Covariates: sex, with women 0.90 to 0.95 of men (Table I); age 25 to 40 only, no age effect estimated; no
body-size measure; population North Indian (Haryana). The paper gives no regression on stature or weight.

## Gaps and conflicts

- Magnification, tube-film distance, patient position, observer, repeatability and level-counting method are
  all missing (p. 34, PDF 2). The indication for the films and how "normal" was judged beyond the radiologists'
  report are not given.
- "Calculated range" is mean +/- 3 SD; in men four of the five printed ranges disagree slightly with 3 x the
  printed SD (L1, L3, L4, L5; Inference from Table I, p. 34, PDF 2).
- Table II's heading on p. 35 (PDF 3) says males only, but the table lists both sexes; the caption under the
  table does not restrict it to males.
- The Results text on p. 35 (PDF 3) says the present values are comparable with "Nigergians" but cites reference
  7, which is the Saudi study; the Discussion on the same page cites reference 6 for Nigerians. Inference
  (arithmetic from Table II): Sonepat men lie closest to the Saudi men (within 0.05 to 1.04 mm) and 1.15 to
  2.64 mm above the Nigerian men; Sonepat women lie closer overall to the Nigerian women (mean absolute
  difference 0.48 mm against 0.99 mm for the Saudi women), though the Saudi women are closer at L2 and L5.
- The Results text calls the variation among ethnic groups minimal (p. 35, PDF 3), while the Discussion and
  Conclusion call the differences significant at all levels (p. 35, PDF 3; p. 36, PDF 4); no between-study test
  is reported.
- The abstract says North Indians (Chhabra 1991) have greater IPD than Haryanvi subjects (p. 33, PDF 1); Haryana
  is itself in North India. Inference: Chhabra's male L4 (35.5) and L5 (37.4) and the 5.8 mm L3-L4 step are far
  above every other series in Table II, which suggests a different method or magnification rather than
  ethnicity.
- Eisenstein 1977 is presented as radiographic (p. 34, PDF 2); its tabulated values are dry-bone caliper values,
  and its radiographs served only to derive magnification factors (Eisenstein p. 173-175). Its greatest-distance
  definition and true-size values are compared directly with minimum-distance film values here.
- Hinck 1966 (white Americans) and Piera 1988 (Spanish) are named in the introduction (p. 34, PDF 2) but not
  tabulated in Table II.
- References: Hawkes and Roberts is dated 1980 with initials G. M. in the text (p. 34, PDF 2) and 1930 with
  initials G.H. in the list (p. 36, PDF 4). Sachs and Frankel is spelled "Saches" in the Discussion (p. 35,
  PDF 3); their 1900 paper, cited for laminar thickening, is titled on ankylosing rigidity of the spine (p. 36,
  PDF 4).
