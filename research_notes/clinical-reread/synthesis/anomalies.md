# Anomalies, numbering and the junctions

Source: synthesis of the 43 notes of the thread "Reread every paper for clinical detail" (project symphony_4,
2026-10-28), /home/user/bordercall/research_notes/clinical-reread/notes/. The notes are the authority. The
Eisenstein counts were also checked against the PDF text (p. 177). Measure names follow bordercall's
schwingcheck (README, "The measures").

How to read this file:

- A citation such as (eisenstein1977 p. 177) names the note and the page of the paper.
- "Inference:" marks anything this synthesis or a note derived: a pooled rate, an interval, a recommended prior,
  a reading of a figure.
- Every 95% interval given as "Wilson" is Inference: my arithmetic on the printed numerator and denominator.
- A count is given as numerator/denominator with its unit (skeletons, patients, pedicles, level measurements).
  Population and material are always named: living, cadaver or dry bone.
- Where papers disagree, both sides are given with their populations and methods. None is averaged silently.

The short answer for the decoder:

- Exactly one paper counts lumbosacral transitional anatomy in a general population: eisenstein1977, on 443
  South African dry skeletons.
- One counts it in a syndrome: ahn2001, 32 US Marfan patients.
- One counts rib anomalies: duman2026, 517 Turkish adults on CT.
- No paper counts T13, 11 or 13 thoracic vertebrae, rib pairs, block vertebrae or a Castellvi type.
- Almost every morphometry paper is silent on how it numbered levels.

## 2. Recognising each variant, and what it does to the measures and to counting

The set gives few recognition criteria. Where it gives one, it is cited. Everything else in this section is
Inference and is marked.

What the CT instance masks give, according to the notes:

- SPINEPS gives no ribs (duman2026 note, rib section, citing bordercall's scanimport/schemes.py).
- SPINEPS has a corpus label (49) with end-plate labels (52, 53), an arch label (41), costal-process labels (43,
  44) and a canal label (61). These numbers are in the duman2026, panjabi1992 and abbas2021 notes (measure sections). It has no pedicle
  label (many notes, measure sections). Several notes give the label set "from memory"; check it against the
  code before relying on it.
- Rib evidence must come from bordercall's independent rib arm or from the costal-process subregions
  (duman2026 note, rib section).

### 2.1 Lumbarisation of S1 and a true L6 (six lumbar-type segments)

What the set says:

- It reports the two only as counts (eisenstein1977 p. 177).
- It never says how an L6 was told from a lumbarised S1.
- It calls persistent S1-S2 facet joints a lesser form of lumbarisation (eisenstein1977 p. 177).

On a CT instance mask (Inference):

1. Count the free lumbar-type instances between the last rib-bearing vertebra and the sacral mass.
2. Six such instances, each with a disc above and below, means L6 or a lumbarised S1.
3. Tell the two apart by what the sacrum below holds:
   - an L6 sits above a sacrum of five fused segments;
   - a lumbarised S1 leaves a sacrum of four.
   Count the sacral foramina pairs or the fused segments in the sacral instance.
4. If the sacrum is not segmented by level, report "six lumbar-type, sacral count unknown" rather than choosing.

On an AP radiograph (Inference):

- Count from the last rib down. The first sacral segment is the one whose lateral masses merge into the ala;
  robertson2000 describes the S1 pedicle as a triangle whose lateral side merges into the ala (p. 713).
- A sixth free segment shows a pedicle oval with a lateral border on the body edge, as L5's does
  (robertson2000 p. 711, p. 713).
- An absent or hypoplastic 12th rib makes the top of the count unreliable (duman2026 pp. 12-13).

On a lateral radiograph (Inference):

- Count disc spaces above the sacral promontory.
- yadav2020 gives the strongest sign that a body is S1: its lower surface collapses and a "disc" of about 3.5 mm
  sits below it (Tables 2-3, pp. 177-178). A lumbar body has a full disc below.

Effect on measures (Inference):

- The extra segment carries L5-like pedicles, IPD and wedge.
- The true L5 then sits one level higher and reads as an L4-L5 intermediate.
- Every caudal-signature measure (IPD jump, pedicle width over height, anterior wedge, coronal facets) is
  displaced by one level.

Effect on counting:

- Counting up from the sacrum names every vertebra above one level too low.
- Counting down from C2 names them correctly but leaves a sixth lumbar label.
- Inference: run the decoder from both ends and flag disagreement.

### 2.2 Sacralisation of L5, the true transitional vertebra and the sacro-transverse joint

What the set says:

- Counts only (eisenstein1977 p. 177).
- The one structural observation: on the side of a sacro-transverse joint or ankylosis, the lumbosacral facets
  were notably smaller (eisenstein1977 p. 177).
- No paper in the set uses Castellvi's grades or any grading. The sacro-transverse joint is the nearest the set
  comes. Inference: it corresponds to the joint or fusion of a transverse process with the sacrum that Castellvi
  grades, without side or type.

On a CT instance mask (Inference):

1. Look for contact or bony continuity between an enlarged costal process (labels 43, 44) of the last lumbar-type
   instance and the sacral ala.
2. Record it per side:
   - contact with a joint gap;
   - fused bone;
   - no contact.
3. Compare the two inferior facets' areas. A smaller facet on the side of the contact fits Eisenstein's
   observation.
4. If the last lumbar instance is fused to the sacrum across the body, SPINEPS may merge it into the sacral mask.
   Check whether the sacral instance is one segment taller than usual.

On an AP radiograph (Inference):

- Look for a broad, wing-like transverse process reaching the ala, on one or both sides.
- olsewski1990 shows that AP films already underestimate L5 pedicle width (16.6 against 20.1 mm) and height
  (13.8 against 16.2 mm), probably because the lateral pedicle border is hard to see there (p. 548; Table II,
  p. 544).
- chaudhary2015's note adds that L5 pedicles are read through the ala and iliac crest on an AP film (note,
  anomalies section).

On a lateral radiograph (Inference):

- A small or absent L5-S1 disc and a squared lumbosacral angle.
- Sacralisation cannot be told from a degenerated L5-S1 disc on the lateral alone. Pfirrmann V collapse at
  L5/S1 occurred in 3/54 young men in tang2016 (p. 4117).

Effect on measures (Inference):

- Transverse process width at the last lumbar level rises.
- The inferior facets shrink on the fused side (eisenstein1977 p. 177).
- The disc below falls toward the S1-S2 value of yadav2020 (3.55 mm, Table 3, p. 178).
- The "L5" row gains spread.

Effect on counting:

- With four free lumbar segments, a count up from the sacrum names every vertebra above one level too high.
- Inference: treat a fully sacralised L5 as the decoder's "missing L5" path, not as a missing body.

### 2.3 Persistent S1-S2 zygapophysial joints

What the set says:

- 16/443 (eisenstein1977 p. 177), with no description beyond the name.

On a CT mask (Inference):

- Facet joints with a gap between S1 and S2, and possibly a separate S1 instance.
- Expect SPINEPS either to split S1 from the sacrum or to keep one sacral mask. Inspect the posterior elements at
  the S1-S2 level.

On AP and lateral radiographs (Inference):

- Usually not visible. The lateral may show a rudimentary S1-S2 disc.

Effect on counting:

- None if S1 stays in the sacral mask.
- If S1 is split off, it can be read as an L6. The yadav2020 S1 signature (collapsed lower surface, Tables 2-3,
  pp. 177-178) is then the check.

### 2.4 T13 and the lumbar rib (a rib on the first lumbar-type vertebra)

What the set says:

- No paper counts T13.
- duman2026 counts 12/517 lumbar ribs without naming the vertebra (Table 5, p. 10).
- It gives no criterion to separate a lumbar rib from an unfused transverse process or from a T13 (duman2026
  note, anomalies section).
- Its Fig. 5B shows the lumbar rib as a separate bony element jointed to a transverse-process tip (duman2026
  p. 10).

On a CT instance mask (Inference):

1. A separate bone touching the costal-process tip of the first lumbar-type instance, with a joint gap, is a rib.
   A continuous long process is an elongated transverse process.
2. Then test the vertebra's own form:
   - Lumbar facets: sagittal superior facets, panjabi1993f p. 1304.
   - Absent costovertebral body facets.
   - Lumbar pedicle shape.
   If all three hold, call it L1 with a lumbar rib.
3. A vertebra with thoracic facets, a costovertebral body facet and a rib is a T13 candidate, and the count from
   C2 must show 13 thoracic levels.

On an AP radiograph (Inference):

- A short rib below the 12th. A projected rib length does not prove anything, because an oblique rib is
  foreshortened (duman2026 note, rib section).

On a lateral radiograph:

- Rib overlap hides the thoracic levels. gilad1985 measured no thoracic level for that reason (p. 117).

Effect on measures (Inference):

- A rib-bearing L1 keeps lumbar measures; only the rib arm changes.
- A true T13 carries thoracic measures one level lower, which shifts every lumbar label down by one if the
  lumbar count stays at five.

Effect on counting:

- Counting from the last rib calls the rib-bearing lumbar vertebra T12, and every lumbar label shifts one level
  too low.
- duman2026 warns that top-down and bottom-up counts disagree when the lowest ribs are abnormal, leading to
  wrong-level surgery (pp. 12-13).

### 2.5 Absent or hypoplastic 12th rib (and 11 rib pairs)

What the set says:

- 22/517 absent and 55/517 hypoplastic in Turkish adults, laterality not reported (duman2026 p. 10).
- The authors advise:
  - never rely on AP radiographs alone for level localisation;
  - trace the whole rib cage on CT before any thoracolumbar procedure, especially in women (duman2026 pp. 12-13).

On a CT mask:

- SPINEPS gives no ribs (duman2026 note), so use the rib arm.
- Inference: identify T12 by its own form rather than by its rib. T12 has a costovertebral facet on the body and
  no costotransverse facet (panjabi1991t Table 6, p. 895), and its inferior facets are lumbar-type
  (panjabi1993f p. 1304).

On an AP radiograph:

- The rib may be short or absent (duman2026 Fig. 4B, p. 9).
- Inference: a projected length under 4 cm does not prove hypoplasia.

On a lateral radiograph:

- Not usable (rib overlap; gilad1985 p. 117).

Effect on counting (Inference):

- The vertebral count is unchanged. Only the rib-based count moves: counting the 11th rib as the 12th names every
  lumbar vertebra one level too high.
- Do not model an absent 12th rib as a missing vertebra.

### 2.6 Cervical rib

What the set says:

- 7/517, female excess not significant on Fisher's test (duman2026 p. 10; recomputation in the note).
- Inference (panjabi1991c note): a cervical rib or an elongated C7 transverse process would sit exactly on C7's
  transverse process width, the strongest single C7 cue (66.6 against 46.4-52.6 mm above; panjabi1991c Table 5,
  p. 864).

On a CT mask (Inference):

- A separate bone jointed to the C7 costal-process tip. Measure C7's transverse process width with and without
  it, and keep the bone out of the width.

On an AP radiograph (Inference):

- A short rib from C7. Count rib pairs from the top to tell it from a first thoracic rib.
- C7 is obscured by the clavicle, humerus and ribs on about 10% of lateral cervical films (gilad1985 p. 117).

Effect on counting:

- Counting ribs from the top names C7 as T1 and shifts every thoracic label up by one.

### 2.7 Spondylolysis and spondylolisthesis

What the set says:

- L5 bilateral in 9/443 and L4 left in 1/443 (eisenstein1977 p. 177).
- The unilateral L4 defect came with an enlarged ipsilateral inferior facet (eisenstein1977 p. 178, Fig. 7).
- One lytic L5 had a bifid spinous process (eisenstein1977 p. 177).

On a CT mask (Inference):

- A gap across the pars, between the superior and inferior articular processes, within the arch label (41).
- The arch splits into an anterior part attached to the body and a loose posterior part.
- Check that the instance's posterior elements are complete before measuring the pars or the inferior facets.

On a lateral radiograph (Inference):

- A lucency across the pars. A slip of the body on the one below (ahn2001 measured slips on the lateral, p. 68).

On an AP radiograph (Inference):

- Usually not seen.

Effect on measures (Inference):

- Facet and interfacet measures of the lytic level are unreliable.
- A slip moves the body forward and changes disc heights and the foramen at that level. masharawi2021 measured
  foramen height lower by up to 3.9 mm with degenerative slips at L4-L5 (masharawi2021 Tables 4-5, pp. 4-8).

### 2.8 Spina bifida occulta

- What the set says: 7/443, lumbar, level not given (eisenstein1977 p. 177, p. 180).
- On a CT mask (Inference): the two laminae fail to meet in the midline; the arch label has a midline gap and the
  spinous process may be absent or split.
- Effect on measures (Inference): spinous process length and the arch's midline landmarks fail at that level. Use
  body and pedicle measures there.

### 2.9 Trefoil canal

What the set says:

- 66/443, almost always at L5 and sometimes at L4, caused by laminar bars (eisenstein1977 p. 177, p. 178,
  p. 179).
- Nigerian CT has trefoil shape at L4-L5 and mixed shape at L3 (alonge2021 pp. 74-75).
- griffith2016's low canal area over depth x width at L5 (0.58-0.60) fits a triangular or trefoil outline. That
  reading is the note's Inference from Tables 2-4 (p. 675); the paper does not describe shape.

On a CT mask (Inference):

- In the canal outline at mid-pedicle (the bony foramen, not SPINEPS's canal-contents label 61), look for lateral
  recesses that make the outline three-lobed.
- Take area over (depth x width): a round canal gives about 0.79 and a triangle 0.5.

Effect on measures and counting:

- Trefoil shape is a level cue, not an anomaly to reject: it favours L5 and allows L4.
- Inference: do not let a trefoil canal at L3 force an L5 label. It occurs at L3 in about a third of Nigerian
  adults (alonge2021 pp. 74-75).

### 2.10 Facet orientation variants

What the set says:

- Flat coronal superior facets in 6/443 and flat sagittal in 8/443, level not given (eisenstein1977
  pp. 176-177).
- At the thoracolumbar junction, the SEM of facet orientation at T12-L1 is 8.2-9.5 deg against 0.9-2.8 in the
  mid-thorax (panjabi1993f Tables 6-7, p. 1303). Inference: three to ten times the spread.
- Malmivaara's superior T12 angle of 98 deg sits against Panjabi's 70 deg (panjabi1993f p. 1308, Table 10).

Inference: the facet switch may fall one joint high or low in some spines.

On a CT mask (Inference):

- Fit a plane to each superior and inferior facet's articular surface.
- Report the angle to the mid-sagittal plane.
- The thoracic-type facets are coronal-ish at about 70 deg on Panjabi's scale; the lumbar-type are near 135-140
  deg (panjabi1993f Tables 6-7, p. 1303).

Effect on counting:

- If the decoder uses the facet switch to name T12, a switch one joint off moves the T12 label by one.
- Weigh it against ribs, costal facets and the count from C2; never let it decide alone.

### 2.11 Block vertebrae and hemivertebrae

What the set says:

- No block vertebra is reported or counted anywhere in the set.
- hinck1966 Case IV has a hemivertebra at T2 and a ventral hemivertebra at T10, with a focal IPD trough and a
  wider mid-sagittal diameter near T10 (pp. 152-153).

On a CT mask (Inference):

- A block vertebra is one instance about twice the usual height, with a vestigial disc or none. SPINEPS may give
  it one or two labels.
- A hemivertebra is a wedge-shaped half body with one pedicle.

Effect on counting (Inference):

- A block vertebra is the decoder's "two levels in one instance" case.
- Allow a transition that consumes two level states for one instance only when the instance's height is about
  twice its neighbours'.

### 2.12 Pedicle variants

What the set says:

- Caudad L5 pedicles: 12/83 in elderly cadavers against 2/94 in young living adults on lateral films
  (olsewski1990 pp. 544-546). Both pedicles of a vertebra pointed the same way (olsewski1990 p. 545).
- Zindrick's L5 is the only level with a caudal mean sagittal angle, -1.8 deg (zindrick1987 p. 162).
- Narrow upper lumbar pedicles (yu2015 pp. 1640-1641).

Effect on measures (Inference):

- The pedicle sagittal inclination at L5 is bimodal, so a Gaussian likelihood centred on the mean
  underestimates the caudad mode.
- Widen it at L5, or use a two-component mixture.

### 2.13 Marfan syndrome and dural ectasia

What the set says:

- Marfan widens the IPD at every lumbar level. The Marfan L1 mean (29.5 mm) equals a normal L5 (29.7 mm), and
  the steps grow (Marfan L3-L4 +2.7 and L4-L5 +4.8 mm against normal +0.9 and +2.8) (ahn2001 p. 68).
- LSTV in 5/32 (ahn2001 p. 68).
- Dural ectasia is a major Marfan criterion (italianmri p. 2, p. 5).

On a CT or MRI (Inference):

- Scalloping of the posterior body walls, a wide canal, a sac that does not taper (S1 diameter above L4).
- The Italian norms take S1/L4 below 1.2 in men and below 1 in women as normal (italianmri p. 6).
- italianmri warns that Oosterhof's cut-offs call 18-49% of normal adults ectatic (p. 6).
- italianmri's Fig. 3 caption may have the sexes reversed (italianmri note, gaps, on Fig. 3, pp. 3-4).

Effect on measures and counting (Inference):

- IPD and canal measures cannot name levels in a Marfan patient: a widened L1 reads as L5. Drop the canal and
  IPD terms when Marfan is known or the IPD sits above the normal L1 cut-off at the top of the column.
- Raise the transitional priors (section 3).

### 2.14 Achondroplasia, osteopetrosis, trisomy 21 and congenital stenosis

What the set says:

- Achondroplasia narrows the IPD caudally, reversing the L4 to L5 rise (hinck1966 pp. 150-153).
- Osteopetrosis narrows it at every level through massive pedicles (hinck1966 p. 153).
- In trisomy 21 the IPD may fall caudally (amonookuofi1982 p. 232).
- Congenital stenosis shortens the sagittal canal while the transverse stays normal (italianmri pp. 1, 4-5).

Effect (Inference):

- Every rule that names L5 by the widest IPD fails in these patients.
- Weight body, pedicle and facet terms over canal terms when the IPD falls or stays flat caudally.
- Eisenstein's stenosis limits are 13 mm AP and 18 mm interpedicular true size, and 15 and 20 mm on film at
  110 cm (eisenstein1977 p. 175). Use them to flag a canal that should not be trusted as a level cue.

### 2.15 Anterior wedge compression and degenerated discs

- Wedge compression in 17/443 skeletons, level not given (eisenstein1977 p. 177). Inference: an anterior wedge
  at L1 or T12 mimics the normal thoracolumbar wedge and an L5 wedge mimics the normal lumbosacral one. Do not
  name a level from wedging alone.
- Pfirrmann V discs (tang2016 p. 4117) and Eisenstein's osteophytosis, which worsens from L1 to L5
  (eisenstein1977 p. 175), lower disc heights. Inference: a collapsed L4-L5 or L5-S1 disc can imitate the S1-S2
  "disc" of yadav2020 (3.55 mm, Table 3, p. 178). Require the S1 body-surface collapse as well before calling
  an instance S1.


## 3. Priors for the sequence decoder

### 3.1 From prevalence to transition

Inference: each variant below occurs at most once per spine and at a fixed place in the sequence. So the
per-spine prevalence is the probability of the corresponding transition at that place:

| Variant | Transition |
|---|---|
| L6 | L5 -> L6, rather than L5 -> sacrum |
| Sacralised L5 | L4 -> sacrum |
| T13 | T12 -> T13 |

Treat these separately from the decoder's "missing body" transition, which stands for an instance the
segmentation or the field of view lost (section 3.4).

### 3.5 What the set does not give

- No count of T13, of 11 or 13 thoracic vertebrae, or of 11 or 13 rib pairs.
- No Castellvi type or any graded LSTV, and no side of any transitional joint.
- No prevalence of block vertebrae or hemivertebrae.
- No LSTV prevalence in any living general population. Eisenstein is dry bone; Ahn is Marfan only.
- No population rate for any variant outside South Africa (lumbosacral) and Turkey (ribs).
- No joint distribution of rib and lumbosacral variants within one person. So the decoder cannot learn from the
  set whether a lumbar rib and an L6 travel together.
- No rule, in any morphometry paper, for numbering a spine with a transitional segment, and no measure reported
  separately for transitional and typical spines.
- No measured effect of any variant on any morphometric measure, except Eisenstein's smaller facets beside a
  sacro-transverse joint (p. 177) and the syndromic IPD changes.
- No error rate of SPINEPS instance splitting or merging at the sacrum, and no rate of vertebrae lost from a CT
  field of view.


## 4. The junctions: every criterion the set gives

Each criterion names its material:

- dry bone: dry or cadaveric bone;
- CT: living CT;
- AP: AP radiograph;
- LAT: lateral radiograph;
- MRI.

Then:

- Steps are given as upper level -> lower level.
- Values are means in mm or deg as printed.
- "Inference" marks a note's or this synthesis's reading.

Status of the schwingcheck pool (schwingcheck README, "The measures"):

- It covers C3 to L5 only, so C2 and S1 are outside it.
- The transverse process width and the spinous process length are in the pool but have no measurer yet.
- Those two are the strongest C7/T1 and T12/L1 cues below.

### 4.1 C2/C3

Body and canal:

- C2 has the deepest and largest canal of the neck: SCD 21.0 and SCA 374.5, with the lowest width/depth ratio,
  1.17, a round canal (dry bone; panjabi1991c Table 3, p. 864).
- From C2 to C3 the canal depth falls 4.8 mm, which is significant. The canal area falls 126 mm2, which is not
  significant (panjabi1991c Tables 3-4, p. 864).

Pedicles:

- C2 pedicles are much larger: width 7.7/8.3, height 9.4/11.1 and area 32.3/51.8 (right/left). The left area is
  60% larger than the right (dry bone; panjabi1991c Table 4, p. 864; p. 866).
- Pedicle size falls at C3 in three of four dimensions (panjabi1991c p. 866).

Uncovertebral surfaces:

- C2 has a lower uncovertebral surface and no upper one (dry bone; panjabi1991c Table 6, p. 865).
- Inference: on a CT mask, an instance with uncinate-type contact only on its lower side is C2, provided the dens
  confirms it.

Facets:

- C2's superior facets are the largest of the column: area 211.3/205.1 (panjabi1993f Tables 2, 4,
  pp. 1301-1302).
- They are oriented unlike any other cervical facet: S 114.1/118.5 and T 40.7/33.5 (dry bone; panjabi1993f
  Table 6, p. 1303).
- At C2/C3 (dry bone):
  - the superior sagittal-plane angle falls from about 116 to 75 (panjabi1993f p. 1304);
  - the superior CAY changes sign (panjabi1993f p. 1305);
  - the interfacet height falls from 20 to 12-13 (panjabi1993f Table 5, p. 1302);
  - IFW/IFH rises from 1.64 to 2.93 (panjabi1993f Table 5, p. 1302).

Lateral body and spinous shape:

- On lateral films, C2 is taller than deep: b 16.6 and d 19.0 (LAT; gilad1985 Tables 1-2, p. 118).
- The C2 spinous process is longer than C3's: L 51.3 against 45.2, e 40.0 against 34.4, f 36.6 against 30.6
  (gilad1985 Tables 1-2, p. 118).
- Inference (gilad1985 note, from Tables 1-2, p. 118): d falls 1.82 pooled SD and f 2.14 SD from C2 to C3, the
  clearest step in the neck.

Visibility:

- C1 is never measurable on the lateral film (LAT; gilad1985 p. 117).
- About 10% of C2s are unclear on the lateral film, n 130 of 141 (LAT; gilad1985 p. 117).
- C1 and C2 are not measurable on AP films (AP; hinck1966 p. 142).

C3 against C4 and C5:

- No measure separates any two of C3, C4 and C5 significantly (dry bone; panjabi1991c Tables 2-6, pp. 863-865).
- Adjacent differences stay below 0.6 pooled SD (LAT; gilad1985 note, Inference from Tables 1-2, p. 118).
- In Singaporean Chinese specimens, body widths, depths and heights are constant from C3 to C5 (dry bone;
  tan2004 p. 139). There the levels are ordered mainly by spinous process length (25.6, 30.3, 33.6) and
  transverse process width (41.4, 44.9, 47.6) (tan2004 Table 1, pp. 140-141; ordering is the note's
  Inference).
- Inference: name C3 to C5 by the count from C2 and the sequence, never by their own measures.

Not covered: tan2004 and busscher2010 start at C3 (tan2004 p. 137; busscher2010 p. 1105). No other paper
measures C2.

### 4.2 C7/T1

Ribs and costal facets:

- T1's upper costovertebral facet (rib 1's, Inference) is 1.6 to 1.9 times T2's: 45.3/56.6 against
  29.0/29.5 mm2 (dry bone; panjabi1991t Table 6, p. 895).
- The total rib-articulation area falls from 132.0 at T1 to 95.9 at T2 (panjabi1991t p. 893).
- T1 to T10 carry upper and lower costovertebral surfaces (panjabi1991t p. 893).
- A cervical rib in 1.35% of Turkish adults would add rib evidence at C7 (CT; duman2026 p. 10).

Transverse processes:

- C7's width is 66.6 against 46.4-52.6 above (dry bone; panjabi1991c Table 5, p. 864).
- T1's is the longest of the thorax at 75.3, falling to 69.4 at T2 (dry bone; panjabi1991t Tables 3-5,
  pp. 892-894).
- In Singaporean specimens it steps from 53.8 to 63.8 from C7 to T1 (dry bone; tan2004 Table 1, pp. 140-141).
- In Dutch specimens the transverse process length steps from about 64 to 75 at C6/C7 and peaks at T1 at about 81
  (CT of specimens; busscher2010 Figs. 6-7, pp. 1110-1111).
- Inference: keep any cervical-rib bone out of C7's width (2.6).

Spinous processes:

- C7's is 45.7 against 34.2 at C6 (dry bone; panjabi1991c Table 5, p. 864).
- It is the longest cervical spinous process at 46.9 in tan2004 (Table 1, pp. 140-141).
- C7 is the longest cervical vertebra on lateral films: L 62.3, e 49.6 (LAT; gilad1985 Table 1, p. 118).

Body:

- C7's upper end plate jumps: EPWu 18.5 -> 21.8 and EPAu 221.2 -> 278.3 from C6 (dry bone; panjabi1991c Table 2,
  p. 863).
- C7's lower end-plate depth falls 18.5 -> 16.8, which the authors call a distinct transition (panjabi1991c
  p. 867).
- At C7/T1, in Singaporean specimens (dry bone; tan2004 Table 1, pp. 140-141):
  - EPWu 19.0 -> 24.7 and EPWl 20.3 -> 27.1;
  - VBHp 11.8 -> 14.0;
  - the lower end-plate inclination changes sign, +5.1 -> -2.9.
- At C7/T1, in Dutch specimens (busscher2010):
  - VBHa 15.7 -> 17.3 and VBHp 15.9 -> 18.8 (Table 3, p. 1107);
  - the lower end-plate width rises 30.7 -> 35.4 while the upper falls 31.5 -> 28.8 (Figs. 3-8, pp. 1109-1112).
- C7 has the smallest body of kunkel2011's series: VBHA 13.89, VBHP 14.38 (donor segments; Table 2, p. 379).
- The C7-T1 disc is the only unwedged disc: ADH = PDH = 4.5 and I3 1.00 (kunkel2011 p. 380; Table 3, p. 379).

Pedicles:

- T1's pedicle is nearly round: PDH/PDW 1.1, with PDW 8.5 and PDH 9.6 (dry bone; panjabi1991t Tables 3-7,
  pp. 892-895).
- Its transverse inclination is only 7.6 deg, jumping to 17.9 at T2 (panjabi1991t Tables 3-7, pp. 892-895).
- At C7/T1 in Singaporean specimens, PDW 5.65 -> 7.45 and PDH 6.3 -> 8.5 (dry bone; tan2004 Table 1,
  pp. 140-141).
- In Dutch specimens, pedicle height 7.5 -> 9.6 and pedicle width peaks at T1, about 8.4 (busscher2010 Figs. 5, 8,
  pp. 1110-1112).
- In Taiwanese bone, T1 has PW 7.65, PH 8.65, TA 28.2 and SA 11.6, against SA 18.9 at T2 (dry bone; lien2007
  Tables 1-2, p. 1218).
- T1 has the largest thoracic transverse angle, 26.6 deg, and the narrowest sagittal width, 9.9 mm (dry bone and
  CT; zindrick1987 pp. 161-162).

Uncovertebral surfaces:

- C7 has both an upper and a lower uncovertebral surface (dry bone; panjabi1991c Table 6, p. 865).
- Inference: T1 carries uncinate processes in these spines.
- The uncovertebral frontal angle rises 104.0 -> 115.6 from C6 to C7 (panjabi1991c Table 6, p. 865).

Facets (dry bone; panjabi1993f Table 5, p. 1302):

- The superior transverse-plane angle rises: C7 59.4/51.6, T1 62.2/57.7, T2 66.5/66.6.
- The superior interfacet width falls: 38.6, 33.2, 28.2.
- IFW/IFH falls: 2.09, 1.68, 1.25.
- Inference (note): the paper's own cervical rule, IFW/IFH above 1.5, calls T1 cervical. The facet threshold
  falls between T1 and T2, not between C7 and T1.

Canal:

- C7 has the smallest cervical canal: area 223.8, width/depth 1.61 (dry bone; panjabi1991c Table 3, p. 864).
- T1's canal is the second widest of the thorax: SCW 21.8, against 19.5 at T2 (dry bone; panjabi1991t Tables
  3-4, pp. 892-893).
- The canal narrows 19.7 -> 17.7 in Singaporean specimens (tan2004 Table 1, pp. 140-141) and 27.3 -> 23.9 in Dutch ones
  (busscher2010 Figs. 3-8, pp. 1109-1112).
- The IPD makes the largest drop of the profile, 28.0 -> 24.0 at C7/T1 and 24.0 -> 20.5 at T1/T2. The adult
  ranges are C7 24-32, T1 20-28 and T2 17-24 (AP, Portland adults; hinck1966 Table IA, p. 142; Table III,
  p. 146).

Visibility:

- C7 is obscured in about 10% of lateral films (LAT; gilad1985 p. 117).
- Upper thoracic posterior landmarks are hidden on films (kunkel2011 p. 383).
- The AP-PA difference in IPD is largest at T1-T3, 1.1 mm, so chest films are least reliable there (hinck1966
  pp. 148-149).

Disagreements:

- How big the body-width jump at C7/T1 is depends on the sample. Panjabi's implied EPWu step is 21.8 -> 24.5
  against tan2004's 19.0 -> 24.7 (tan2004 note, Inference, from Tables 1-2, pp. 140-143).
- The Dutch absolute widths are larger than both (busscher2010 Figs. 3-8, pp. 1109-1112). Busscher's 6 men were tall (mean
  182 cm; busscher2010 p. 1105).
- Inference: use the direction and relative size of the steps, not absolute thresholds, across populations.

### 4.3 T12/L1

Ribs and costal facets: the strongest rule.

- The authors state that from T1 to T10 each rib articulates with its own vertebra and the one above. T11 and
  T12 are uniarticular, with no lower costovertebral surface (dry bone; panjabi1991t p. 893; Table 6, p. 895).
- The costotransverse facet is present T1-T10 (40.0-65.0 mm2), small at T11 (16.7/11.7) and absent at T12
  (panjabi1991t Table 6, p. 895).
- T10 still carries lower costovertebral surfaces (30.0/26.9) (panjabi1991t Table 6, p. 895).
- Ribs 11 and 12 float (panjabi1991t pp. 897-898).
- Inference: on a CT mask with a rib arm, name T12 as the lowest vertebra with a costovertebral facet on its body
  and no costotransverse facet. Name T11 by a small costotransverse facet.

Facet orientation:

- The switch happens on average at the T12-L1 joint (dry bone; panjabi1993f p. 1304; Tables 6-7, p. 1303):
  - T12 superior S is 72.5/67.4 against L1 superior 139.1/134.2;
  - T11 inferior S is 72.2/70.5 against T12 inferior 139.7/138.8.
- T12 is identified by the abrupt change between its own superior and inferior facets (panjabi1993f p. 1307).
- T12's inferior facets change to lumbar type, which is why its pars area drops (panjabi1991t p. 893).
- Caution: the SEM of S is 8.2-9.5 deg at the T12-L1 joint against 0.9-2.8 at T1-T10 (panjabi1993f Tables 6-7,
  p. 1303). Inference (note): spreads of about 28-33 against 3-10 deg, so the switch may sit one joint off.
  Malmivaara's superior T12 angle is 98 against Panjabi's 70 (panjabi1993f Table 10, p. 1308).
- By size the T12-L1 joint is still small: inferior T12 width 9.5/9.8 and area 90.8/90.0. The size jump comes at
  L1-L2 (panjabi1993f Tables 3-4, pp. 1301-1302).
- Qualitative lumbar traits: mammillary processes on the superior articular process, curved sagittal facets and a
  triangular foramen (literature review; bonczar2024 p. 6, p. 1).

Transverse processes:

- T12 has the narrowest transverse process width of the thoracolumbar spine: 41.0 in tan2004 and 46.9 in Panjabi
  (tan2004 Table 1, pp. 140-141; panjabi1991t Tables 3-5, pp. 892-894).
- The width jumps at L1:
  - 41.0 -> 53.6 in Singaporean specimens (tan2004 Table 1, pp. 140-141);
  - Panjabi's L1 is 71.2 (dry bone; panjabi1992 Tables 2-5, pp. 301-304), on the same 12 spines as his T12, a
    step of about 24 mm. Inference: this assumes the two papers define the width alike, which the notes do not
    confirm;
  - in Dutch specimens the transverse process length jumps about 54 -> 79 (busscher2010 Figs. 6-8,
    pp. 1110-1112).

Pedicles:

- Transverse angle (dry bone and CT; zindrick1987 Tables 1, 3-5, pp. 161-165):
  - T12 -4.2 deg (lateral), with SD 9.5 and range -17.0 to +14.5;
  - L1 10.9.
  The text says the angle returns to neutral by L1, which contradicts its own table (zindrick1987 p. 164).
- Other measures across T12 -> L1 in zindrick1987 (same tables): sagittal angle 11.6 -> 2.4, depth along the
  pedicle axis 38.6 -> 50.7, transverse width 7.1 -> 8.7.
- In Chinese bone the transverse width falls 8.3 -> 7.0 (dry bone; hou1993 Tables 1, 3, pp. 1852-1853):
  - L1 is narrower than both neighbours in every one of 40 columns (hou1993 pp. 1851-1852);
  - the pedicle length LI steps up from 42.9 to 49.5;
  - the inclination turns from about -2.5 to 3 deg.
- In Taiwanese bone: PW 7.55 -> 6.45, PH 15.1 -> 13.65, SA 10.7 -> 6.1 (dry bone; lien2007 Tables 1-2,
  p. 1218).
- In Singaporean specimens: PDW 7.55 -> 5.55, PDH 14.1 -> 13.1, and the transverse inclination changes sign
  (dry bone; tan2004 p. 143; Table 1, pp. 140-141).
- In Dutch specimens the pedicle angle jumps 0 -> 14 deg (busscher2010 Figs. 6-8, pp. 1110-1112).
- In living CT the transverse angle jumps from about 0 to about 12 deg (CT; krag1988 Fig. 6, p. 31, Inference from the
  note). The text keeps L1 with T9-L1 at 0-10 deg (krag1988 p. 30).
- Pedicle length jumps 31.5 -> 41.4 (tan2004 Table 1, pp. 140-141).

Body:

- T12's superior and inferior end-plate widths are equal: 38.0 against 37.6 in men, P 0.674 (CT; chen2011
  p. 1817; Table 3, p. 1818).
- L1 widens caudally by 4.3 mm in men, 38.4 -> 42.7 (chen2011 Table 3, p. 1818).
- In North Indian CT, the upper width jumps 3.2 mm in men (36.99 -> 40.15) and the interfacet distance 2.2 mm
  (15.12 -> 17.27) (CT; yadav2020 Tables 2-3, pp. 177-178).
- L1 is taller behind than in front in several series:
  - frontphys2026: 27.2 against 25.1 (CT, Chinese; p. 5);
  - moncadahabib2025: PH 26.9 against AH 24.3 (CT, Mexican; Tables 1-2, pp. 4-5);
  - bonczar2024: 26.57 against 24.54 (pooled; Table 2, p. 5);
  - gilad1985: b 25.4 against d 27.1, anteriorly wedged on lateral films (LAT, Israeli men inferred; p. 119).
- Inference: the L1 posterior-taller wedge is the lumbar end of the thoracolumbar kyphosis. An anterior
  compression (eisenstein1977 p. 177) imitates it.

Disc:

- T12/L1 is the smallest of the six discs measured in every disc height (CT, Tampa; bach2019 Table 6,
  p. e113).
- In Dutch specimens the disc below T12 is 9.3 against 6.1 below T11 (busscher2010 Table 3, p. 1107; the disc
  attribution is the note's Inference).

Canal:

- The canal enlarges sharply from T11 to T12: SCW 19.4 -> 22.2 and SCA 220 -> 280 (dry bone; panjabi1991t
  Tables 3-4, pp. 892-893).
- From T12 to L1, SCA 177.8 -> 200.0, the maximum of the column (tan2004 Table 1, pp. 140-141).
- The adult IPD at T10, T11, T12 and L1 is 18.7, 20.2, 23.2 and 25.0 (AP, Portland; hinck1966 Table IA, p. 142).
  - The T12/L1 step is only 0.6 mm at ages 3-5, 6-8 and 13-14.
  - The T11/T12 step is +2.0 to 3.0 mm at every age.
  - Inference (note): in children the T11/T12 rise is the steadier thoracolumbar signal.
- L1 has the widest mid-sagittal canal of the lumbar spine in:
  - Indian MRI (ahmed2019 pp. 22-24);
  - Nigerian dry bone (amonookuofi1985 p. 76);
  - Bosnian CT (cizmic2023 pp. 202-203);
  - Italian MRI (italianmri Tables 2-5, pp. 4-5).
- Against that, L1 is not the widest in:
  - Chinese CT, where L5 is widest (cited in cizmic2023 p. 203);
  - Nigerian CT in women, where L5 is widest (alonge2021 pp. 73-74).
  So this cue depends on the population.

No step:

- In Turkish CT every body height, pedicle diameter and pedicle angle rises smoothly from T11 to L2, and nothing
  marks T12/L1 (CT; duman2026 Tables 1-2, pp. 7-8).
- Inference: body and pedicle size alone will not place the junction. Use ribs, costal facets, facet orientation
  and transverse-process width.

Visibility:

- No thoracic level was measured on lateral films because of rib overlap (gilad1985 p. 117).
- With an absent or hypoplastic 12th rib, top-down and bottom-up counts disagree on planning images and on
  fluoroscopy (duman2026 pp. 12-13).

Disagreements:

- duman2026's pedicle transverse angle (21-28 deg at T11-L2) is 15-28 deg larger at every level than Zindrick's,
  whose landmarks it claims to follow, and it shows no lateral T12 (duman2026 note, Inference against
  zindrick1987 Table 3, p. 163). Do not mix the two series.
- hou1993's Discussion calls L1 the smallest pedicle, though T9 and T10 are narrower in its own Table 1
  (hou1993 pp. 1851-1852; Table 1, p. 1852; hou1993 note, gaps).

### 4.4 L5/S1

What makes an instance S1:

- In North Indian CT (yadav2020 Tables 2-3, pp. 177-178):
  - the lower body width and depth collapse: 46.93 -> 30.78 and 31.66 -> 22.78 in men;
  - the "disc" below S1 is 3.55 mm;
  - S1 has the minimum canal depth (11.00 men, 11.19 women), the maximum interfacet distance (27.91, 26.89) and
    the minimum foramen (5.77, 5.80);
  - S1's upper width, 49.08, barely exceeds L5's, 48.79.
  - Inference (note): the S1 lower surface and the disc below are the strongest single sign that an instance is
    S1.
- In Italian MRI every measure but the dural sac ratio drops at S1 (italianmri Tables 2-5, pp. 4-5):
  - body depth 29.6 -> 22.4;
  - canal 17.3 -> 14.9;
  - sac 12.9 -> 9.9.
  The S1 lines are drawn obliquely, following the sacral inclination (italianmri Fig. 2, p. 3).
- On radiographs of dry bone, the S1 pedicle is a triangle whose lateral side merges into the ala, and its lateral
  border is never seen on coaxial views (robertson2000 p. 713).
- In Dutch CT, S1's superior end plate (41.2) is narrower than L5's inferior one (44.1) (vanderhouwen2009
  Fig. 2, p. 37).

What makes an instance L5:

- Pedicle shape:
  - The L5 pedicle is an oblique ellipse, tilted 55.0/50.3 deg, whose lateral border projects onto the lateral
    border of the L5 body (dry bone radiographs; robertson2000 Table 1, p. 711).
  - The pedicle is wider than tall in several series:
    - olsewski1990, the only lumbar level so (pp. 544-545);
    - zindrick1987, 18.0 against 14.0 (Tables 1-2, pp. 161-162);
    - yu2015, PW 18.33 against PH 13.67 (Tables 2-5, pp. 1641-1645);
    - makino2012, TPW 12.86 against SPW 8.95 (p. 1519);
    - lien2007, ratio 1.41 (Table 1, p. 1218).
  - Disagreement: hou1993 gives L5 width 12.9 and height 19.8, taller than wide (Tables 1-3,
    pp. 1852-1853). panjabi1992 gives both near 19 (Tables 2-5, pp. 301-304).
  - Inference: the shape depends on where height is measured. Define it on the mask and calibrate on your own
    data.
- Pedicle convergence jumps at L4 -> L5:
  - 14 -> 35 deg (busscher2010 Figs. 5-8, pp. 1110-1112);
  - 17.47 -> 28.23 (arockiaraj2025 Table 2, p. 316);
  - about 18.5 -> 30 (hou1993 Tables 1-3, pp. 1852-1853);
  - about 28 at L5 (krag1988 Fig. 6, p. 31);
  - 29.8 (zindrick1987 p. 161);
  - 29.33 direct (mitra2002 p. 456).
- The sagittal pedicle angle at L5 is bimodal (olsewski1990 pp. 544-546).
- Anterior wedge, anterior height above posterior:
  - tan2004: 22.0 against 20.0 (Table 1, pp. 140-141);
  - busscher2010: VBHp -3.1, VBHa +1.7 (Table 3, p. 1107);
  - frontphys2026: +3.1 mm (p. 5);
  - moncadahabib2025: 26.2 against 22.5 (Table 1, p. 4);
  - bonczar2024: 27.44 against 24.11 (Table 6, p. 9);
  - gilad1985: b 28.3 against d 25.7 on lateral films (pp. 119-120).
- IPD jumps at L4 -> L5. The jump is absent or reversed in achondroplasia (hinck1966 pp. 150-153) and trisomy 21
  (amonookuofi1982 p. 232):
  - +2 to 3 mm (eisenstein1977 Table I, p. 174);
  - normal adults 26.9 -> 29.7 (AP; hinck1966 Table IA, p. 142; ahn2001 p. 68);
  - +4.19 men and +3.30 women (AP, North India; chaudhary2015 Table I, p. 34);
  - +2.7 and +3.0 (AP, Nigeria; amonookuofi1982 Table 1, p. 228);
  - 27.32 -> 31.94 (CT; cizmic2023 Table 5, p. 202).
- Facets:
  - L5 has the most coronal superior facets (TFA about 43-47 deg) and the largest inferior interfacet width, about
    46-49 mm (CT; masharawi2021 Tables 2-5, pp. 4-8).
  - L5's inferior facets articulate with the sacrum and copy no measured superior facet (panjabi1993f p. 1306).
  - L5's inferior facets stand closer to the sagittal plane than L4's: |S| 127.6/125.1 against 112.7/114.7
    (panjabi1993f Table 7, p. 1303). Inference (note): the lumbar trend reverses at the last joint.
- Canal:
  - The trefoil canal is almost always at L5 (eisenstein1977 p. 177, p. 179).
  - On a lateral film the posterior canal limit at L5 lies just anterior to the radiolucent lake of the spinous
    process (LAT; eisenstein1977 p. 175, p. 178).

The L5/S1 disc:

- It has the largest anterior height (11.92/11.36) and a low posterior height (5.27/5.23), which makes it the most
  wedged disc. Its foramen is the smallest (14.30/14.36) (CT, Tampa; bach2019 Table 6, p. e113).
- In Loma Linda CT it has the tallest anterior height (10.76) but falling middle and posterior heights (8.14,
  3.80) and the smallest foramen (shin2024 Tables 2, 4, pp. 3-5).
- The L5-S1 wedge is about 15 deg supine (CT, Groningen; vanderhouwen2009 Fig. 3, p. 39).
- It is lower than L4-5 in all four groups of twomey1985 (cadavers; Table 2, p. 497).
- The L5/S1 disc and end-plate areas are 5-7% smaller than the discs above (MRI; tang2016 p. 4126).

Radiographic pitfalls:

- AP films underestimate L5 pedicle width, 16.6 against 20.1 mm (olsewski1990 p. 548).
- L5 pedicles are read through the ala and the iliac crest (chaudhary2015 note, Inference on the male L5 SD of 5.9 mm, Table I, p. 34).
- On coaxial views, the L5 pedicle's lateral border is sometimes missing (robertson2000 p. 713).
- The CT gantry could not always be set parallel to L5-S1 (olsewski1990 p. 543).

Transitional anatomy at this junction:

- See sections 1.1 and 2.1-2.3 (eisenstein1977 p. 177, p. 180; ahn2001 p. 68).
- Inference: before naming the last free instance L5, check three things:
  - its disc below is full height and not 3.5 mm;
  - its transverse processes do not touch the ala;
  - the sacral instance holds five segments.

### 4.5 Rules for the decoder at the junctions

All rules are Inference, built from the criteria above.

1. Anchor at both ends when both are in view:
   - At the top, take C2 by the dens and its lower-only uncovertebral surface.
   - At the bottom, take S1 by the collapsed lower surface and the ala.
   Decode from both anchors. Report a disagreement as a candidate numerical variant, not as noise.
2. Name T12 from vertebral form when the rib arm is uncertain:
   - a costovertebral facet on the body;
   - no costotransverse facet;
   - superior facets thoracic-type and inferior facets lumbar-type.
   Use the rib only as one term. An absent or short 12th rib occurs in about 15% of Turkish adults (duman2026
   p. 10).
3. Name C7 against T1 by:
   - transverse process width (C7 wide, T1 widest);
   - the round T1 pedicle;
   - the rib-1 facet on T1;
   - the IPD drop at C7/T1.
   Do not use IFW/IFH above 1.5, which calls T1 cervical (panjabi1993f Table 5, p. 1302).
4. Do not name C3, C4 or C5, or T4 to T9, from their own measures. Carry them by count from a named neighbour
   (panjabi1991c Tables 2-6, pp. 863-865; panjabi1991t p. 897; gilad1985 Tables 1-2, p. 118).
5. At L5/S1, require at least two independent cues before naming a level:
   - pedicle shape;
   - wedge;
   - IPD step;
   - facet orientation;
   - the disc below.
   Each cue alone fails in a known condition: achondroplasia and trisomy 21 for the IPD, Marfan for every canal
   cue, compression for the wedge, and disc degeneration for the disc.
6. On radiographs, divide by magnification before comparing with dry-bone or CT norms: 1.23 lateral and 1.14 AP
   at 110 cm tube-film distance (eisenstein1977 p. 175). Prefer ratios, which are free of scale.
